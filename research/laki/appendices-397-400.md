# Avasin Standard — Laki Appendices 397–400
**Status:** Test-branch research content, not yet production-published.

## Scope
- 397: causal conjunctions — `ki`, `čü(n) (ki)`, `ařā (ya ki)`, `bi xātir (ya ki)`.
- 398: purpose clauses — `ki`, `tā`, infinitive `-(i)n`.
- 399: temporal clauses — `ki`, `tā`, `iska (ki)`, `baʕd (a ya) ki`, `vaxtī (ki)`, `zamānī (ki)`.
- 400: conditional clauses — `a(ga)r`, Protasis/Apodosis, realizable and counterfactual conditionals.

## Research safeguards
- Main reference: Sara Belelli (2021), *The Laki Variety of Harsin: Grammar, Texts, Lexicon*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.20378/irb-51703.
- Harsini is a specific variety; do not generalize these findings to all Laki varieties.
- Preserve all Latin-script examples exactly as transcribed in the source.
- Appendix 397: no independent corpus example was found for `ařā (ya ki)` in the reviewed passage; do not invent one.
- Appendix 398: Belelli §3.14.2.3, pp. 205–206. Purpose clauses use `ki` or `tā`; the documented dependent verbs are Subjunctive in examples (444)–(445). The source also notes some clauses with infinitives, examples (446)–(448). The previously removed `ma-č-in ʕaxdömaxd kird-in` is authentic source example (447), not an invented form.
- Appendix 398–399: `tā` is polyfunctional; distinguish purpose, temporal and degree readings by context.
- Appendix 400: the source leaves open whether Imperfective can replace Past Subjunctive in all counterfactual protases.
- This test page is a research appendix index, not a complete published Laki curriculum.


## Appendix 398 — source-checked examples (Belelli 2021, pp. 205–206)
Purpose clauses introduced by `ki` or `tā`:
- (444) `šöso kwiř pādišā m-āy ki čirāwā āw d-e` — “The next morning, the king’s son comes to give water to the horse.” [1:41]
- (445) `āyl-al-a b-īl-ø=a jā tā bi-č-īm bi-gīrd-īmin` — “Leave the children here so that we may go (and) have a walk.” [4:120]

The source notes that some purpose clauses seem to involve an Infinitive:
- (446) `ma-nīš-ī=a hin šāna kird-in` — “She sits down, what-do-you-call-it, combing.” [1:63]
- (447) `ma-č-in ʕaxdömaxd kird-in` — “they go sign the marriage contract and such” [laki_conv_1, 3:21–3:27]
- (448) `piyāz āyšt-in=a. wiž=im dī čī-m=as=a piyāz kan-in` — “They have planted onions, I myself went to pick onions” [laki_conv_1, 9:03–9:06]


## Appendix 397 — source examples (Belelli 2021, pp. 206–207)
- (451) `čün gi šü kird-ī=a bi-č-im a šü=y=a bi-kwiš-im` — “Since she got married, I shall go (and) kill this husband of hers.” [laki_conv_1, 15:25–15:28]
- (452) `bi_xātir ya gi mardim pīšraft kird-in=a šöw hanābandān=īš ī hanā tazīn=a ma-ka-n dī iska baɫed=in tazīn=a ma-ka-n` — “Since people have improved, on the night of the henna-ing ceremony they decorate this henna too; now they are able to decorate it.” [laki_conv_1, 5:33–5:41]
- `ařā (ya ki)` is listed as a causal conjunctional phrase, but no independent corpus example is provided in the reviewed passage.

## Appendix 399 — temporal clauses (Belelli 2021, pp. 202–205)
- (432) `šöw ki ma-xaf-e xāw=a mö-ün-ī` — “when he sleeps at night, he dreams.”
- (433) `tā ī haštād řuž=a tamām ni-ma-ke-ym ʕarusī` — “Until these eighty days are over, we won't get married.”
- (435) `iska gi pyā=m kird-ī=a sar āyl-il-a biřī=a` — “As soon as he found me, he slaughtered the kids.” [1:134]
- Temporal clauses may also be paratactic; example (443) uses the Pluperfect for an event ideally preceding the main-clause event.
- `tā` also expresses degree (example 434); not every occurrence is temporal.

## Appendix 400 — conditional clauses (Belelli 2021, pp. 208–210)
- Realizable present or immediate-future condition: normally Present Subjunctive in the protasis and Present Indicative or Imperative in the apodosis.
- If a realizable future condition is ideally anterior to the main event, Preterite or Pluperfect can occur in the protasis with Past Subjunctive function (examples 457–458).
- Counterfactual past condition: Past Subjunctive in the protasis and Imperfective in the apodosis (example 459: `a(ga)r bi-zānis(t)-ā-m=a ma-hāt-im`).
- Belelli explicitly leaves uncertain whether Imperfective can replace Past Subjunctive in the protasis, as in Persian.
