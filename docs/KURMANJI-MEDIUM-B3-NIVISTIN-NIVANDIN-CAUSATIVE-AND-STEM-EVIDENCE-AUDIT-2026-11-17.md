# Kurmancî Medium B3 — nivistin and nivandin: causative derivation, lexical homographs, and stem-evidence audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only lexical and grammatical audit. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, seed, database, migration, PR, or deployment changes.

## Executive findings

Haig & Öpengin explicitly cite **nivistin ‘sleep’ → nivandin ‘put to sleep’** as an example of the Kurmancî causative suffix **-and**, which derives a transitive verb from an intransitive present stem.

- **nivistin**: intransitive, “to sleep”. Thackston's Kurdish–English vocabulary records **nivistin -niv- v.i.**; the present stem is therefore **-niv-** in that source.
- **nivandin**: transitive causative, “to put someone to sleep / cause someone to sleep”. The derivation is from the base present stem **niv-** plus **-andin**.
- **Important evidence limit:** the sources inspected explicitly attest the lemma pair and the causative meaning, but do not supply a full independent conjugation table for **nivandin**. Do not present a complete present or past paradigm as directly attested.
- The spelling distinction is essential: **nivistin** “sleep” is not **nivîsîn** “write”; **nivîsandin** is also listed as “write”, not as the causative of sleeping.

## 1. Base verb: nivistin

Haig & Öpengin's grammatical overview gives **nivistin ‘sleep’** as the intransitive base of the causative pair.

Thackston's *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, in the Kurdish–English vocabulary, independently lists:
- **nivistin -niv- v.i.** — to sleep.
- **nivîn -niv- v.i.** — to lie down, sleep.
- **nivîn** (noun) — bed.

This is lexical evidence for the intransitive meaning and present stem **niv-**. The inspected sources do not provide a complete finite paradigm for **nivistin**, so no person-by-person forms are supplied here.

## 2. Derived verb: nivandin

Haig & Öpengin explicitly give:
**nivistin ‘sleep’ → nivandin ‘put to sleep’**.

This is a causative relation:
- Base event: a person sleeps.
- Causative event: an agent causes another person to sleep / puts that person to sleep.
- Valency changes from intransitive to transitive.

The same source explains that the suffix **-and** is attached to an intransitive present stem to derive a transitive verb. The example **gerîn → gerandin** is given alongside **nivistin → nivandin**, so the latter is not an extrapolation from the suffix alone; it is a directly cited lexical pair.

## 3. Stems: what is directly attested and what is only predicted

| Item | Evidence | Editorial status |
|---|---|---|
| Base present stem **niv-** | Thackston vocabulary explicitly prints **nivistin -niv-** | Directly attested |
| Causative lemma **nivandin** | Haig & Öpengin explicitly list it | Directly attested |
| Causative meaning “put to sleep” | Haig & Öpengin explicitly gloss it | Directly attested |
| Causative present stem **nivîn-** | Thackston states that factitive verbs in **-andin** have present stems in **-în-**; applying this general rule predicts **nivîn-** | Morphological prediction; label as inferred, not independently listed |
| Causative past stem **nivand-** | Plausible segmentation of the cited infinitive, but no independent paradigm or past example located in the sources inspected | Not verified; do not teach as a sourced principal part |
| Full person/number paradigm | Not found in the inspected sources | Do not invent |

Thackston §28 states that the factitive infinitive is formed from the base present stem plus **-andin**, and that the present stem of these factitive verbs ends in **-în-**. His examples include **mirin → mirandin mirîn-**, **şewitan → şewitandin şewitîn-**, and **tirsîn → tirsandin tirsîn-**. Applying that regular pattern to **niv-** predicts **nivandin, present stem nivîn-**. The predicted stem should remain marked as an inference until a direct lexicon or finite-text attestation is found.

## 4. Do not confuse the sleep and writing families

Thackston's vocabulary makes the distinction explicit:
- **nivistin** — intransitive, to sleep; present stem **-niv-**.
- **nivîsîn** — transitive, to write.
- **nivîsandin** — transitive, to write.

Thackston §28 further notes that **nivîsîn** and **nivîsandin** both mean “to write” in the variety described, without an apparent difference in meaning. This is a lexical exception and must not be used to infer that **nivîsandin** means “cause to sleep” or that it belongs to the **nivistin → nivandin** pair.

Keep the orthographic contrast intact:
- **i** in **nivistin / nivandin** (sleep / put to sleep);
- **î** in **nivîsîn / nivîsandin** (write).

## 5. Ergative and agreement implications

The causative **nivandin** is transitive. Under the general Standard Kurmancî pattern described by Haig & Öpengin:
- in present tenses, the finite transitive verb indexes its agent/subject;
- in past tenses, the finite transitive verb indexes its direct patient/object, while the agent is oblique.

This is a general grammatical rule, not a **nivandin**-specific attested paradigm. Since no direct finite past examples for **nivandin** were located in the inspected materials, do not invent sample sentences such as a complete singular/plural object contrast. Such forms should be added only after corpus or native-speaker verification.
