## 1. Method and status meanings

This register breaks the current lesson claims into reviewable units. **SUPPORTED** means the source supports the central claim at the stated scope; **SUPPORTED WITH SCOPE** means it applies only to a stated variety, register or construction; **PARTIAL** means the principle is supported but the exact example or gloss is not; **EDITORIAL REVIEW** means a language-editor decision is needed; **UNRESOLVED** means do not teach as a universal rule until a source or specialist settles it.

The register follows the existing lesson review. It does not claim every phrase has been checked against a pinpoint page. No page numbers are invented.

## 5. Lesson 08 — writing versus pronunciation

**C1-L08-01 — Letter, phoneme and pronunciation are distinct.**  
Evidence: basic linguistic distinctions; S2/S4 provide Kurmanji context. **Status: CONCEPTUALLY SUPPORTED.** Action: cite an introductory phonetics reference for definitions and standardize Sorani terminology.

**C1-L08-02 — e/ê, i/î and u/û are distinct written vowel letters.**  
Evidence: S4 vowel inventory; S2 cross-check. Scope: Hawar-based Latin orthography. **Status: SUPPORTED.** Action: preserve graphemic distinction; cite phonetic values separately with variety scope.

**C1-L08-03 — r and rr may correspond to a tap/trill contrast in some descriptions.**  
Evidence: S4 and S2, with the prior audit noting inconsistency in S4's table/prose. **Status: PARTIAL / ORTHOGRAPHY UNRESOLVED.** Action: do not infer universal spelling from the ambiguous table. Retain only as a qualified phonetic note if an independent source confirms it. Do not add rr as an extra Hawar alphabet letter.

**C1-L08-04 — Some varieties distinguish aspirated and unaspirated voiceless stops.**  
Evidence: S4 as recorded in the prior review; S1/S3 support dialect-scope caution. **Status: SUPPORTED WITH SCOPE.** Action: do not present as a contrast in every Kurmanji variety; identify locality/variety where possible.

**C1-L08-05 — Pharyngealization is not aspiration.**  
Evidence: phonetic terminology and source description of unaspirated stops sometimes accompanied by pharyngealization. **Status: CORE DISTINCTION SUPPORTED; WORDING NEEDS TECHNICAL EDIT.** Action: define aspiration (post-release breath) separately from pharyngealization (constriction in the pharyngeal region). Avoid making learners infer they are the same feature. Keep the caveat that this is not universal and has no separate standard Latin letter.

**C1-L08-06 — Standard spelling does not encode every dialectal phonetic detail.**  
Evidence: S1/S2/S4 and S3. **Status: SUPPORTED.** Action: retain as a central principle; distinguish orthographic conventions from phonetic transcription.

## 8. Lesson 11 — personal pronouns

**C1-L11-01 — Direct paradigm ez, tu, ew, em, hûn, ew.**  
Evidence: S1/S2/S4/S6, as recorded in the source review. **Status: CORE PARADIGM CORROBORATED.** Action: identify the reference variety; explain that ew may be singular or plural depending on context and agreement.

**C1-L11-02 — Oblique forms min, te, wî/wê, me, we, wan.**  
Evidence: S1/S2/S6. **Status: CORE PARADIGM CORROBORATED.** Action: present wî/wê within their grammatical constructions; do not label them simply as universal possessive equivalents “his/her.”

**C1-L11-03 — Direct is not simply subject; oblique is not simply object.**  
Evidence: S1/S2, S4 on case/agreement and split alignment, and S6. **Status: SUPPORTED; IMPORTANT SAFEGUARD.** Action: retain the caution. Explain that in past transitive constructions, the transitive agent may be oblique while the direct object remains direct, with agreement patterns not reducible to a simple subject/object mapping. Do not universalize one dialect's detailed analysis.

**C1-L11-04 — Copular forms im, î, ye and in.**  
Evidence: S1/S2/S6 and lesson examples. **Status: GENERAL PARADIGM SUPPORTED; WORDING NEEDS REFINEMENT.** Action: do not suggest selection merely by the final letter of a noun/adjective. Explain person/number and predicate structure; verify every example.

**C1-L11-05 — Reflexive xwe.**  
Evidence: S1/S2/S6. **Status: SUPPORTED WITH SYNTACTIC SCOPE.** Action: retain only with a verified example; explain that xwe is not a free substitute for every personal pronoun and its antecedent is structurally constrained.

**C1-L11-06 — Sorani glosses and example sentences.**  
Evidence: grammar sources support Kurmanji paradigm, not every Sorani gloss. **Status: EDITORIAL REVIEW.** Action: check glosses, hevalên min construction and all copular examples separately.
