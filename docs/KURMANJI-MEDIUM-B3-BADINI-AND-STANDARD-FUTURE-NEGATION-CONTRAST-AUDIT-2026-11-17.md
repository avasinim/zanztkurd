# Badini and Standard Kurmanji: Future Negation Contrast Audit

Date: 2026-11-17  
Scope: documentation-only source reconciliation. No lesson, curriculum, runtime, or production changes.

## Research question

Does Badini use the same negative future construction as Standard Kurmanji, and how should apparently different descriptions of Badini negation be reconciled?

## Findings

### 1. Standard Kurmanji

Unger (2014) contrasts the Standard Kurmanji particle set *wê/dê/ê* with Badini *dê*. In Standard Kurmanji, the future particle can combine with a subjunctive verb form. Since the subjunctive negator is *ne-*, a negative future construction is possible. Thackston's Kurmanji grammar independently gives affirmative and negative future forms of *çûn*, e.g. *ezê biçim* / *ezê neçim* and *ewê biçe* / *ewê neçe*.

### 2. Badini

Unger (2014), “Badini Kurdish modal particles dê and da,” describes Badini future reference as:

- modal particle *dê*;
- present-stem verb inflected for person and number;
- no modal or aspectual prefix, specifically no subjunctive *bi-*.

Unger argues that this Badini *dê*-construction does not license tense/mood information on the verb in the way Standard Kurmanji *wê/dê/ê* does. Since negation prefixes encode tense/mood distinctions, the construction does not permit the negative prefix in the same future-marked construction. Unger therefore distinguishes Badini from Standard Kurmanji: the dedicated negative future construction found in Standard Kurmanji is absent in the Badini system he analyzes.

### 3. Reconciliation with descriptions that show a negative present form for future meaning

Some descriptive material on Bahdini/Bahdīnī says that to negate a future-oriented sentence, *dê* is dropped and the ordinary negative indicative is used; future interpretation is then recovered from context. Example reported in the cited dissertation:

- affirmative: *ez dê nanî çêkim* — “I will make bread.”
- negative: *ez nanî çênakim* — “I won’t make bread.”

This does not establish a dedicated *dê* + negative future construction. It is compatible with Unger's analysis if described precisely as a contextually future-oriented negative indicative, rather than a morphologically marked future negative.

### 4. The particle *da* is separate

In Badini, *da* occurs in a construction syntactically parallel to *dê* but does not itself mark future time. Unger describes uses including distant-past habitual and counterfactual eventualities. It is not an interchangeable spelling or all-purpose variant of *dê*, and it is absent from the Standard Kurmanji variety described in the paper.

## Safe conclusions

1. Do not transfer the Standard Kurmanji negative-future pattern mechanically to Badini.
2. Distinguish a dedicated future construction (*dê* + present-stem verb) from a negative indicative whose future interpretation comes from context.
3. Keep *da* distinct from future *dê*.
4. Do not generalize one Badini description to every southern Kurmanji locality without locality-specific corpus evidence.
5. This is a source audit only. It does not select an Academy standard or authorize changes to lessons.

## Evidence limitations

The sources differ in terminology and in the exact dialect labels they use (Badini, Behdini, Bahdini/Bahdīnī). They support a contrast between Standard Kurmanji and the Badini systems discussed, but do not establish that every locality or speaker follows one uniform pattern. Further claims require locality-tagged examples from a primary corpus or elicitation.
