# Kurmanji conditional particle contrast: ê / dê / wê (2026-11-17)

## Purpose
Clarify the status of the particle in examples such as `Min ê bixwenda` and `Minê nexwenda`, and prevent conflating this construction with the ordinary future.

## Source 1: Geoffrey Haig & Ergin Öpengin (2018)
“Introduction to Special Issue: Kurdish: A critical research overview,” section “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî),” *Kurdish Studies* / overview chapter.

The grammar sketch describes ordinary future marking with a clause-second modal particle `(d)ê` or `wē`, followed by a verb based on the present stem and generally carrying subjunctive `bi-`. It notes that high-frequency verbs such as `gotin` and `bûn` may omit `bi-`. It also distinguishes Standard Kurmanji negative future, where the future particle is retained and the verb uses subjunctive negation `ne-`, from Behdinî, which uses negative indicative forms.

## Source 2: Stefan Georg Unger (2014)
“Future Tense in Kurmanji: The Use of wê/dê/ê in Kurmanji,” *Kurdish Studies Archive* 2 (2014), pp. 213–241.

Unger describes `wê`, `dê`, and enclitic `=ê` as future/modal particles. In Standard Kurmanji, `wê` is predominant, while `dê` also occurs in written/standard Kurdish; `wê` can be shortened/cliticized as `=ê` after pronouns. The particle can also occur with negation in the future construction. Crucially, Unger separately describes a construction Bedir Khan & Lescot call “first conditional”: subject + modal particle + verb inflected with the subjunctive prefix `bi-`, but based on the **past stem**. His example is `Lezgîn dê bi-hat-a` (“Lezgin would have come”). He distinguishes this from a second conditional based on a participle and auxiliary.

## Analysis for the Academy ledger

1. Do not describe `ê/dê/wê` as merely spelling variants of one universal particle without dialect/register qualification. The sources describe related forms, distribution, cliticization, and variation.
2. Do not equate the future construction with the past-stem conditional:
   - Ordinary future: particle + present-stem subjunctive (normally `bi-` + present stem).
   - First conditional described by Unger: particle + `bi-` + **past stem** + conditional/person morphology.
3. The directly attested xwendin examples `Min ê bixwenda` and `Min ê nexwenda` fit the documented past-stem conditional pattern, not the ordinary future paradigm.
4. Preserve source-specific labels. Unger reviews Bedir Khan & Lescot's terminology and explicitly notes the analytical caution around importing tense labels from European languages.
5. The exact particle choice in a learner-facing Academy standard remains unapproved. Record `ê`, `dê`, and `wê` as source-attested variants with distribution caveats; do not normalize lessons automatically.
6. Do not infer that the particle is freely interchangeable in all dialects, persons, syntactic positions, or conditional subtypes.

## Evidence limits
Haig & Öpengin's grammar sketch addresses Northern Kurmanji and notes regional variation. Unger's article compares Standard Kurmanji and Badini and documents a distinct contrast between them. These findings support a careful distinction between future and past-stem conditional morphology, but they do not establish one Academy-approved variety.
