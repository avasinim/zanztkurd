## 3. Lesson 11 — word order and sentence structure

### Existing content confirmed
The lesson covers:
1. Identifying subject, verb and (where present) object, then checking transitivity and case.
2. Noun phrases built with determiners, number, possessors and adjectives connected through ezafe.
3. Information structure and focus, including the possibility of moving constituents.
4. A basic subject–object–verb explanation and the example `Wî mirov-î çay anî`.

### Critical citation correction
The seed currently says that Haig §2.7.1 presents the unmarked SOV order and gives `Wî mirov-î çay anî` as its example. That attribution is wrong:
- Haig §2.7.1 concerns neutral word order. It characterizes neutral clauses as SOVG where a goal argument is present; G denotes a goal such as a spatial goal, recipient or addressee. The order is not rigid, and direct objects can be fronted for pragmatic purposes.
- The exact example `Wî mirov-î çay anî` is Haig (2018), example (32), printed p.131, §2.7.2, in the discussion of alignment and related issues.
- In that example, `Wî mirov-î` is the oblique-marked subject, `çay` the direct object, and `anî` the past verb. It illustrates ergative alignment in a past transitive clause. It is not a standalone neutral word-order example establishing a universal SOV template.

The source-section reference and pedagogical framing should therefore be corrected together. Do not change the Kurmanjî spelling or morphology of the quoted example as part of this documentation task.

### Terminology alignment
- “subject” → بکەر; “object” → بەرکار; “verb” → کردار.
- “word order” → ڕیزبەندیی وشەکان.
- “focus” → جەخت (provisional; retain one approved term consistently).
- “goal” in Haig’s SOVG label is a syntactic/semantic argument role, not merely a generic “purpose”; define it clearly if SOVG is introduced to learners.
- “ergative alignment” should remain distinct from ordinary subject–object order. The working equivalent ڕێککەوتنی ئەرجەتیڤ remains provisional and should be used consistently.
- “ezafe” remains ئەزافە, consistent with the register.

### Remaining instructional gaps
1. The lesson does not explain the G component in SOVG, despite the source's neutral-order description.
2. The cited example is currently assigned to the wrong section and its ergative function is not explained.
3. The information-structure section has no source-checked paired examples showing neutral order versus focus/fronting.
4. The noun-phrase section recaps ezafe but does not provide a fully segmented phrase or model analysis.
5. The review questions have no answer guidance or scoring criteria.

### Recommended acceptance criteria
- Correct the Haig citation for `Wî mirov-î çay anî` to §2.7.2, example (32), p.131.
- Present §2.7.1 separately as the neutral SOVG discussion; explain G and qualify the order as non-absolute.
- Keep the example `Wî mirov-î çay anî` explicitly labelled as a past transitive ergative-alignment example.
- Add a source-attested SOVG illustration only after checking its exact form and context; do not invent one.
- Add one worked sentence analysis covering constituent roles, case, verb and information structure, with a source citation.

### Terminology alignment
The lesson's summary categories broadly align with the working register. Keep the following distinctions intact:
- root (ڕەگ) versus base (بنەڕەت);
- suffix `-ek` versus ezafe versus oblique case;
- direct versus oblique pronoun forms;
- tense (کات) versus aspect (provisional: ڕوانگەی کردار);
- transitive versus intransitive;
- verbal negation versus nominal-predicate negation;
- basic word order versus information-structure variation.
