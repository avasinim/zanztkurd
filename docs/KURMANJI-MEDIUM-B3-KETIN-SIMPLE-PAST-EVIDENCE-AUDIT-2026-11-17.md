# B3 Kurmanji: Ketin Simple-Past Evidence, Agreement, and Aspect Audit
Date: 2026-11-17
Branch: fix/kurmanji-ci-current-migration
Scope: research documentation only; no lesson or application changes.

## Executive finding
`ketin` is an intransitive verb with the Standard Kurmanji simple-past stem `ket-`. The regular direct-subject paradigm is `ez ketim`, `tu ketî`, `ew ket`, and `em/hûn/ew ketin`. This pass located first- and third-person singular tokens in literary/narrative material, and multiple independent grammar sources for the full paradigm. It also found older dialectological material recording variants such as `kewt-` and `kaft-`; these must not be silently normalized into the Standard Kurmanji teaching form.

## 1. Direct literary and narrative attestations
### First-person singular: `ketim`
The Kurmanji song/poetry collection page includes the line `Çeko bilindo heramo!, ser çil û çar çiya ketim` and continues with `Bilind firîm û nizim ketim`. Both occurrences are first-person singular past forms in a poetic first-person voice. The text is presented in a collected song/poetry compilation; the page does not provide a secure author/date attribution for this particular stanza, so cite it as a collected poetic text, not as a dated author's edition.
Source: https://www.scribd.com/document/57114849/Strana-kurmanci
### Third-person singular: `ket`
In the Kurmanji Readings narrative, the sentence begins `Gava timobîl bi rê ket, bê hemd çend hêsir ji çavên min herikîn...` (“When the car set off, a few tears flowed from my eyes involuntarily…”). `ket` is third-person singular past with `timobîl` as its intransitive subject. The passage is in a narrative text reproduced in *Kurmanji Readings*, p. 87.
Source: https://www.yumpu.com/tr/document/view/19679065/kurmanji-readings
### Additional literary use of the compound: `bi rê ketin`
The Wiktionary entry for `bi rê ketin` classifies it as intransitive and lists its simple-past forms `bi rê ketim`, `bi rê ketî`, `bi rê ket`, `bi rê ketin`. Its lexical meanings include setting out / beginning a journey. This is useful for the compound construction but is a lexicographic entry, not a new primary text quotation.
Source: https://ku.wiktionary.org/wiki/bi_r%C3%AA_ketin

## 2. Full paradigm: independent reference cross-check
Samî Tan's *Rêzimana Kurmancî* (p. 126) explicitly lists:
- Ez ketim.
- Tu ketî.
- Ew ket.
- Em ketin.
- Hûn ketin.
- Ew ketin.
The same source contrasts transitive `dîtin` examples, where the subject pronoun is oblique, with intransitive `ketin`, where direct pronouns are used.
Source: https://ansiklopediyakurdistane.com/uploads/urunler/pdf--Rezimana%20Kurmanc%C3%AE-Sam%C3%AE%20Tan.pdf--155-161630878363ce5eb1917950.95951801.pdf
Kanîzar, issue 16 (2018), p.17, independently prints the same six forms under `Bûhêrk` and explicitly says this direct-pronoun pattern is used with intransitive verbs. This is a published grammar article, not literary corpus evidence.
Source: https://portal.arsivakurd.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/kanizar_16.pdf
Kurdish Lessons gives the same past-stem derivation `ketin → ket` and contrasts it with present `bikeve` / present stem `kev-`. Kurdish Academy-style grammar references also distinguish the past stem from the present stem.
Source: https://kurdishlessons.com/simple-present-tense-in-kurdish/

## 3. Negative forms and spelling
The regular negative simple past is formed with `ne-` before the past stem: `ez neketim`, `tu neketî`, `ew neket`, `em/hûn/ew neketin`. Samî Tan's grammar examples give the positive and negative six-person forms. The Kurmanji grammar PDF *Bi Hîndarî Rêzimana Kurdî* also prints the full negative conditional paradigm `heke ez neketim ... heke ew neketin`; because that section is conditional, do not cite it as a simple-past indicative example.
Sources: Samî Tan, above; https://www.kurdishlo.org/pdf/learning/R%C3%AAzimana%20Kurmanc%C3%AE%20%28%DA%A9%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7lKurdpirtuk%29%20.pdf
Editorial caution: write the Standard Kurmanji negative as one orthographic word `neketim` etc. A spaced form `ne ketim` appears in some teaching pages, but should not be mixed into the project’s chosen spelling convention without an explicit orthographic policy.

## 4. Do not confuse simple past with present perfect / pluperfect
`ketim` and `ketî` are simple-past forms in the paradigm above. `ketime`, `ketiyî`, `ketîye`, `ketine` are present-perfect forms, not alternate spellings of the simple past. A Kurmanji Lesson 8 handout gives `Ez ji darê ketime`, `Tu ji darê ketiyî`, `Ew ji darê ketiye`, `Em ji darê ketine` as present-perfect examples, then explicitly distinguishes the simple past. Another beginner grammar gives pluperfect examples `Ez keti bûm`, `Tu keti bûyî`, `Ew keti bû`.
Sources: https://www.kurdipedia.org/files/books/2023/474430.PDF?ver=133232623527294231 ; https://agorism.dev/book/kurdish/kurmanji-basic.pdf
Therefore B3 must not put `ketime` in the simple-past paradigm. Preserve the tense/aspect label with every example.

## 5. Dialect and historical-form caution
An older comparative grammar, *Kurdish Grammar — The Verb*, pp. 64–65, records multiple regional forms for the preterite, including `ketim/ket`, `ketit`, `keti/ket`, `ketin`, alongside variants with `kewt-` and `kaft-`. It explicitly associates some forms with different northern dialect areas. This is valuable dialectological evidence, but its historical transcription conventions and variety labels differ from contemporary Standard Kurmanji orthography.
Source: https://kurdshop.net/content/upload/1/root/grammar-of-the-kurmanji-or-kurdish-language1.pdf
Haig's overview of Northern Kurdish varieties notes that certain intransitive predicates can show non-canonical oblique subjects in some southeastern varieties, while most other dialects use canonical direct subjects. Its cited `çav ... ket` construction is a lexicalized 'catch sight of' expression and should not be used to overturn the ordinary direct-subject paradigm for physical falling.
Source: https://www.academia.edu/17650650/Kurmanji_in_Turkey_structure_varieties_and_status

## 7. B3 editorial recommendations
1. Teach the Standard Kurmanji intransitive paradigm exactly as `ez ketim`, `tu ketî`, `ew ket`, `em/hûn/ew ketin`.
2. Use the Kurmanji Readings sentence as the clean third-person narrative example, preserving the full context and source page.
3. The collected poem can illustrate first-person `ketim`, but label its source provenance as a collected text with uncertain stanza-level attribution.
4. Teach `neketim` etc. as negative simple past and keep the prefix attached according to the project's orthographic convention.
5. Keep `ketime` and related forms in a separate present-perfect section; do not conflate them with simple past.
6. Record `kewt-` and `kaft-` as dialect/historical variants in research notes only. Do not replace the Standard Kurmanji `ket-` forms or mix regional forms into the core B3 pathway.
7. Existing lessons remain unchanged pending explicit approval.
