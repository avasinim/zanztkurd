# Kurmancî Medium B3 — mirin/mirandin and şewitan/şewitandin: factitive derivation, stems, and attested past forms

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only grammatical audit. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, seed, database, migration, PR, or deployment changes.

## Executive findings

The two pairs are established examples of Kurmancî factitive/causative derivation. W. M. Thackston's reference grammar explicitly sets out the formation rule and provides the stems:

1. **mirin** “to die” → **mirandin** “to cause to die; kill”
   - Base: intransitive; present stem **mir-**; past stem **mir-**.
   - Derived factitive: transitive; present stem **mirîn-** (directly stated by Thackston).
   - Past stem **mirand-** is the regular morphological result of the factitive infinitive **mirandin** (infinitive minus **-in**); this is a rule-based stem analysis, not a separate finite example in the grammar excerpt inspected.

2. **şewitan** “to burn (intransitively)” → **şewitandin** “to make burn; set fire to”
   - Thackston's base lemma is spelled **şewitan**, with present stem **şewit-**.
   - Derived factitive: transitive; present stem **şewitîn-** (directly stated).
   - The past form **şewitand** is directly attested in the sentence **Wan mala wî şewitand** “They burned his house down.”
   - In other Standard Kurmancî materials, the intransitive lemma may be written **şewitîn**. Preserve the source spelling in quotations and settle the project's headword convention separately; do not silently normalize quotations.

The pairs illustrate the regular factitive construction, but **-andin** is not a universal guarantee of causative meaning across all dialects or all infinitives. Haig & Öpengin document dialectal forms in which -andin has spread as a general infinitive base without making the verb transitive.

## 1. The grammatical rule

Thackston §28 defines the factitive infinitive as a construction that renders an intransitive base verb transitive. It is formed from the base verb's present stem plus **-andin**. The present stem of the derived factitive verb ends in **-în-**.

The grammar's explicit derivation table:

| Base infinitive | Base present stem | Factitive infinitive | Factitive present stem | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mirin “die” | mir- | mirandin | mirîn- | cause to die; kill |
| şewitan “burn (intr.)” | şewit- | şewitandin | şewitîn- | make burn; set fire to |
| tirsîn “be afraid” | tirs- | tirsandin | tirsîn- | scare |

This is direct, explicit grammatical evidence, not a reconstruction from spelling alone.

## 2. mirin → mirandin

### Base: mirin

Haig & Öpengin list **mirin** with past stem **mir-** and present stem **-mir-**, meaning “die”. Thackston independently gives the base present stem **mir-** in the factitive derivation table.

The base is intransitive: the subject is the entity that dies. Its ordinary past is intransitive and therefore does not use the transitive ergative pattern.

### Factitive: mirandin

Thackston explicitly lists **mirandin mirîn-** and glosses it “to make die, kill”. It is transitive because an agent causes a patient to die.

The present stem **mirîn-** is directly attested in the grammar's table and reinforced by the general rule in §14 that factitive infinitives in **-andin** form their present stem by replacing that ending with **-în-**.

The derived past stem is analyzable as **mirand-** (mirandin minus infinitival -in). This follows the regular two-stem morphology, but the source excerpt inspected does not give a finite simple-past sentence with **mirandin**. Accordingly, the audit records **mirand-** as a regular morphological analysis, not as a directly quoted finite form.

No complete present or past paradigm for **mirandin** is asserted here.

## 3. şewitan → şewitandin

### Base: şewitan

Thackston's table prints the base as **şewitan**, with present stem **şewit-**, and glosses it “to burn” (intransitive). The paired example is:

**Mala wî şewita.**  
“His house burned down.”

This sentence shows the intransitive event: the house is the subject and undergoes burning; there is no agent expressed as the transitive subject.

Some modern teaching sources use the spelling **şewitîn** for the intransitive lemma. The reference grammar's actual headword spelling **şewitan** is preserved in the derivation table here, rather than silently changing the citation.

### Factitive: şewitandin

Thackston lists **şewitandin şewitîn-**, glossed “to make burn, set fire to”. Its example is:

**Wan mala wî şewitand.**  
“They burned his house down.”

This is direct evidence for:
- transitive causative meaning;
- past form **şewitand**;
- oblique agent **wan**;
- direct patient **mala wî**;
- singular-patient agreement with no overt plural ending on the verb.

The contrast between **Mala wî şewita** and **Wan mala wî şewitand** is particularly useful pedagogically: the first says the house burned; the second says “they burned the house”.

## 4. Past transitive agreement

In Kurmancî simple past, transitive clauses use the ergative-absolutive pattern: the agent is oblique and the verb agrees with the direct patient. Thackston's **Wan mala wî şewitand** illustrates the pattern with an oblique plural agent and a singular patient; the verb appears as **şewitand**.

Do not infer a complete object-agreement table from this single example. A plural-patient form for **şewitandin** or the full person-number paradigm for **mirandin** should be added only when supported by directly attested forms.

## 5. Important limit on the suffix -andin

Haig & Öpengin describe **-and** as a moderately productive derivational suffix used to form transitive causatives from intransitive present stems. They also explain that in some dialects the suffix has been extended to serve as a general infinitive base, without causative meaning and sometimes without changing an intransitive verb's valency.

Their examples of this non-causative extension include dialectal forms such as:
- **štexlandin** “to speak”;
- **çelimandin** “to learn”;
- **řazandin** “to sleep”;
- **šewit'andin** “to burn (intransitively)”;
- **mešandin** “to walk”;
- **hišandin** “to listen”.

These are not to be conflated with the Standard Kurmancî factitive pair **şewitan → şewitandin** in Thackston's table. The surface ending alone does not determine valency or meaning: dialect, lemma, and actual syntax must be checked.
