### 2.1 C1-A01 — reference variety and orthographic house policy

**Priority:** P0  
**Evidence status:** a defensible written-standard recommendation is directly supported; the owner's choice remains unresolved.  
**Current action status:** OPEN — owner decision required.

Geoffrey Haig (2018), “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî),” §2, especially printed pp. 107–109, describes a reasonably widely accepted written Standard Kurmanjî, loosely based on Cizre/Botan, using a modified Roman alphabet and Hawar-derived conventions. Haig explicitly treats regional variation separately; the standard is not a universal spoken dialect and should not be projected automatically onto Behdinî/Northern Iraq.

**Recommendation already documented, not approved:** use Standard Kurmanjî in Hawar-based modified Roman orthography as the provisional *written* teaching target. Do not designate one spoken variety as universal. Label regional alternatives and require variety-matched review.

**Closure test:** owner/editor explicitly approves or amends the written target, spoken/listening reference policy, regional-variant labelling and Behdinî transfer rule. A source-backed recommendation does not equal owner approval.

### 2.2 C1-A02 — R/RR: source conflict narrowed; universal rule rejected

**Priority:** P0  
**Evidence status:** the limited phonetic and orthographic facts are directly supported; a universal spelling contrast is not.  
**Current action status:** EVIDENCE RECONCILED; EDITORIAL HOUSE-STYLE APPROVAL STILL OPEN.

The earlier integrated closure report and Lesson 26 audit were too broad in different directions. The current evidence supports this narrower account:

1. **Phonetics — direct evidence:** Haig (2018), printed p. 114, says word-initial written `r` is trilled; elsewhere the distribution of trill and flap is not predictable in the described Standard Kurmanjî phonology.
2. **Orthographic practice — direct but limited evidence:** Thackston, *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, “Alphabet and sounds,” describes the trill as only sporadically indicated by `rr`, giving `pir` / `pirr` as an example. This is evidence that an occasional `rr` spelling exists, not that it is obligatory, productive or consistently applied.
3. **Alphabet inventory:** the Hawar inventory remains 31 independent letters; `rr` is not a 32nd letter.
4. **Initial spelling:** initial `r` may be trilled while remaining single `r`; preserve `roj`, not `rroj`.
5. **Unsupported generalization:** do not teach a predictable one-to-one rule `r = /ɾ/`, `rr = /r/` across all words, environments, writers or varieties.

**Important source limitation:** Haig's phonetic description does not itself establish an `rr` spelling rule. Thackston's example is explicitly sporadic and comes from a reference grammar describing a written norm, not a frequency survey of contemporary publishers. The Lesson 04 cross-lesson audit and the dedicated R/RR reconciliation record slightly different page numbering for Thackston's phonology passage across PDF copies; cite the section heading and verify the physical/printed page in the chosen copy before quoting it.

**Closure test:** retain the distinction between alphabet, phonetic realization and occasional spelling convention; approve any final table placement and wording with the owner/editor and a qualified editor of the selected reference variety. No learner-facing change is authorized by this addendum.

## 5. Evidence and source limitations

### 5.1 Primary scholarly sources

1. **Haig, Geoffrey. 2018.** “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî).” In Geoffrey Haig & Geoffrey Khan (eds.), *The Languages and Linguistics of Western Asia: An Areal Perspective*, pp. 106–158. Berlin: De Gruyter Mouton. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110421682-004. Relevant: §2, especially pp. 107–109 (Standard Kurmanjî and Cizre/Botan scope); p. 114 (initial /r/ trill and non-predictable distribution elsewhere); §§2.2–2.3 (nominal morphology/case/ezafe); §2.6 (verb stems/TAM); §2.7.2, pp. 130–132 (past transitive alignment and regional deviations).
2. **Haig, Geoffrey & Ergin Öpengin. 2018.** “Kurmanjî Kurdish in Turkey: Structure, varieties, and status.” In Christiane Bulut (ed.), *Linguistic Minorities in Turkey and Turkic-speaking Minorities of the Peripheries*, pp. 157–230. Wiesbaden: Harrassowitz. Relevant: phonological inventory and trill/flap discussion, printed pp. 170–171; regional and standardization discussion elsewhere in the chapter. This source supports phonetic and variety distinctions; do not cite it as proof of an obligatory `rr` spelling.
3. **Thackston, W. M. 2006.** *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*. Relevant: “Alphabet and sounds” (flap/trill and sporadic `rr`); §18.2, printed p. 48 (`Min ew dît` and the transitive past paradigm). Thackston describes a pedagogical written norm and acknowledges regional variation; do not treat every paradigm as invariant across all varieties.
4. **Baker, Mark & Ümit Atlamaz. 2014.** “On the Relationship of Case to Agreement in Split-Ergative Kurmanji and Beyond.” Relevant: canonical split alignment and dialect-specific double-oblique patterns. The Lesson 25 register identifies Adıyaman and Muş data; label the dialect scope when using those examples.
5. **Haig, Geoffrey & Ergin Öpengin. 2015.** “Gender in Kurdish: Structural and socio-cultural dimensions.” *Gender Across Languages*, vol. 4, pp. 248–275. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1075/impact.36.10hai. Relevant: §2.1.1, pp. 250–251 (case and split alignment); §2.1.2 (ezafe).
6. **Gündoğdu, Songül. 2011.** *The Phrase Structure of Two Dialects of Kurmanji Kurdish: Standard Dialect and Muş Dialect*. MA thesis, Boğaziçi University. Relevant: pp. 37–40 for interrogatives in the studied varieties.
7. **Gündoğdu, Songül. 2015.** “Negation in Kurmanji.” In Stela Manova (ed.), *Affix Ordering Across Languages and Frameworks*, pp. 154–172. Oxford University Press. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780190210434.003.0007. Important limitation: the cited elicited negation data are Muş; do not generalize them unlabelled to all Standard Kurmanjî.

### 5.2 Evidence categories and limits

- **Direct evidence:** exact form/claim is present in a cited source at the cited locator (example: `Min ew dît`, Thackston §18.2, p. 48).
- **Descriptive support:** a source supports a general rule but not necessarily the exact learner-facing example.
- **Inference:** a form or consequence follows from a rule but is not directly attested in the cited passage; label it as inference, not quotation.
- **Dialect/variety alternative:** a form or rule is documented for a named variety; do not universalize it.
- **Unresolved:** source scope, exact transcription, lexical sense, naturalness or reviewer decision remains insufficient.
- **Human review:** no native approval is recorded unless the reviewer, variety, date, exact item and decision are actually entered in the native-review ledger.
