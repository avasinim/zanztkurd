## 1. Lesson identity and scope

**Current lesson title:** کاتی ئێستا، ڕابردوو و داهاتوو

**Intended scope:** A controlled introduction to present, past and future reference in Standard Kurmanjî, with a focused contrast between simple past and imperfective past and a brief source-attested future construction.

This lesson must not become a complete verb-conjugation chapter. It should teach learners how to read the cited examples and what conclusions are justified, while directing them to a fuller paradigm source for forms not covered here.

## 4. Required conceptual distinctions

### 4.1 Tense and aspect are not synonyms

Teach the distinction explicitly:

- **Tense (`کات`, current working label):** grammatical location of an event in time, such as present or past.
- **Aspect (Sorani equivalent remains provisional):** how the event is viewed internally, for example as bounded/completed or ongoing/habitual, where the cited construction supports that interpretation.

Do not teach “simple past” and “imperfective past” as though they were merely two unrelated tense labels. Explain that the comparison concerns both past-time reference and the way the event is presented.

The working Sorani label `ڕوانگەی کردار` for aspect remains provisional under the course terminology register. Do not freeze it in learner-facing text until the course owner approves it.

### 4.2 Teach the two past comparisons as pairs

Present the forms in two aligned rows:

- `ez hatim` ↔ `ez dihatim`
- `min xwar` ↔ `min dixwar`

For each pair, learners should be guided to:
1. recognize the shared lexical verb form family;
2. identify the contrast recorded by the cited source;
3. describe the simple/imperfective distinction using the approved terminology;
4. avoid claiming that the prefix alone is a universal aspect marker;
5. keep the intransitive and transitive clause patterns distinct.

Do not add morpheme-by-morpheme segmentation unless the exact segmentation is independently supported by the approved source. A visual highlight of `di-` may be used to direct attention, but it must be labelled as a visible form element in these examples, not as a complete universal rule.

### 4.3 Keep transitivity and case visible without overclaiming

The existing lesson correctly contrasts:
- `ez hatim`: past intransitive example;
- `min xwar`: past transitive example.

Explain why the pronoun form and verbal pattern differ in the cited past-transitive construction. Do not say that `min xwar` contains an overt direct object: it does not. Do not infer object agreement or a full argument structure from this two-word example.

If a lesson objective requires learners to identify an overt object, use a separately source-verified complete clause only after approval; otherwise remove that objective from Lesson 09 and leave it to the later case/verb lessons.

### 4.4 Qualify the present `di-` observation

The seed currently says that forms with `di-` occur with many verbs in the present, while some verbs have different forms. Retain this qualification. Avoid:
- “all present verbs take `di-`”;
- “`di-` always means present”;
- a universal segmentation or productivity claim not established by the cited examples.

### 4.5 Treat the future example as a source-context item

Preserve the exact example:

`ew dē mesel-ē ji te re bēž-e`

The seed currently gives the Sorani gloss «ئەو کێشەکە بۆ تۆ دەڵێت» and describes `dē` as a future marker and `bēž-e` as a subjunctive form. The revision must not silently retain or replace the gloss as if it were independently confirmed.

Before learner-facing use:
1. reopen Haig §2.6.4 and inspect the full surrounding paragraph/example context;
2. check the exact segmentation, especially `mesel-ē` and `ji te re`, against the source's own analysis;
3. confirm the contextual meaning and the appropriate Sorani translation;
4. confirm the description of `dē` and the verb form;
5. record the exact source page/example reference in the lesson notes.

If any component cannot be verified, retain the Latin form only in an explicitly source-quoted note or omit the teaching analysis pending review. Do not invent a word-by-word gloss.

## 5. Recommended instructional sequence

1. **Present reference:** Introduce `ez dibêjim` as the cited present example. State the limited observation about `di-` and its exceptions.
2. **Past is not one undifferentiated pattern:** Introduce `ez hatim` and `min xwar` as the source's intransitive/transitive contrast. Keep the case distinction concise and refer learners to the relevant case lessons.
3. **Aspectual comparison:** Display `ez hatim` / `ez dihatim` and `min xwar` / `min dixwar` as two pairs. Define tense and aspect separately.
4. **Future construction:** Present `ew dē mesel-ē ji te re bēž-e` only after the source-context gate in §4.5 is closed.
5. **Method note:** State that these are selected examples, not a complete conjugation paradigm and not an exceptionless rule for every verb.
6. **Applied review:** Ask learners to compare only the forms actually taught and to state what the examples do not prove.

## 6. Practice design and model-answer criteria

### Task A — Classify the cited forms

Learners classify the cited forms as present, past intransitive, past transitive or future construction, using only the source labels taught in the lesson.

**Model-answer criteria:**
- identifies `ez dibêjim` as the cited present form;
- identifies `ez hatim` as the cited past intransitive form;
- identifies `min xwar` as the cited past transitive form;
- identifies the `ew dē … bēž-e` clause as the cited future construction only after its source-context check;
- does not generalize these forms to every verb.

### Task B — Explain the paired past contrast

Learners compare:
- `ez hatim` / `ez dihatim`;
- `min xwar` / `min dixwar`.

**Model-answer criteria:**
- names the source-described simple/imperfective contrast;
- keeps tense separate from aspect;
- notes the visible `di-` in the imperfective examples without asserting a universal prefix rule;
- does not invent additional forms or translations.

### Task C — State the limits of the evidence

Learners answer:
- Does `min xwar` show an overt object? **No.**
- Does one `di-` example prove all present forms take `di-`? **No.**
- Does the lesson provide a complete conjugation paradigm? **No.**
- Can the future gloss be generalized without its full source context? **No.**

Assessment must reward accurate limits, not memorization of overbroad rules.

## 12. Direct-source recheck — 2026-11-23

The tense/aspect examples and future construction were checked again against the Haig (2018) chapter text hosted by the University of Bamberg. This closes the direct-source check for the specific examples below; it does not close the owner/editor terminology and translation gates.

### 12.1 Table 14 — paired past examples

The source's Table 14 lists the simple-past intransitive `ez hatim` and the imperfective-past intransitive `ez dihatim`; it separately lists the simple-past transitive `min xwar` and the imperfective-past transitive `min dixwar`. The source's English renderings distinguish the simple-past event (“I came”; “I ate (sth.)”) from the past-progressive/imperfective reading (“I was coming”; “I was eating (sth.)”). The paired forms therefore support a past-time aspectual contrast in these examples. They do not establish a universal rule that `di-` alone always marks imperfective aspect across every verb or construction.

The same table also places `ez dibêjim` in the simple-present transitive paradigm. Retain the existing qualification that `di-` appears in many present forms but is not a universal present marker; do not infer from the shared string that present `di-` and past imperfective `di-` have one identical function in every environment.

### 12.2 Future — §2.6.4, example (27), printed p.129

The source gives `ew dē mesel-ē ji te re bēž-e` and an interlinear gloss identifying `dē` as FUT and `bēž-e` as say.SUBJ.PRS-3SG. Its English translation is “He will tell you the issue.” This verifies the future construction and the source's grammatical labels. It does not by itself approve the existing Sorani translation in the seed; the owner/editor must approve an idiomatic Sorani rendering that makes the future reference explicit.

### 12.3 Editorial consequences

1. Keep the two past comparisons paired exactly as listed in §4.2.
2. Describe the contrast as source-specific simple past versus imperfective/past-progressive, not as a universal prefix rule.
3. Keep the transitive and intransitive pairs separate; do not infer an overt object in `min xwar` or `min dixwar`.
4. Retain the future form unchanged and identify `dē` and `bēž-e` only as the source does.
5. Do not finalize the Sorani label for aspect or the Sorani future translation until owner/editor approval.
