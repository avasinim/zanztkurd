## Source-backed omissions and correction required

The following points are not sufficiently represented in the current authored lesson and should be restored before treating Lesson 26 as reconciled:

1. **Turkish U qualification (p. 406):** the source says Turkish U is closer to Kurmanji Û, especially in Arabic loanwords. The lesson gives the general correspondence but omits this qualification.
2. **Vowel-harmony examples (p. 406):** the source illustrates disallowed mixed front/back-vowel combinations in native Turkish with *beran*, *erzan* and *guherandin*. The lesson explains the rule and includes *anne* as an exception, but omits the three illustrative forms.
3. **K articulation scope (p. 407):** the source specifically locates the Turkish K articulation difference before **A and O**. The authored lesson broadens this to all back vowels (A, I, O, U), which is not what this source passage states. Narrow this statement to A/O rather than generalizing.
4. **European consonant-cluster examples (p. 410):** the source lists **BL, BR, FL, FR, KL, KR, PS, KST** and gives *blond, Brîtanya, flora, Fransa, klasîk, krîz, psîkolojî, tekst*. The lesson discusses expansion through European loans but omits this concrete inventory and examples.
5. **Armenian loan examples (p. 413):** the source explicitly lists *karîn/kanîn, law, savar, aman, xaç, çilmisîn* as Kurdish words of Armenian origin. The lesson mentions Armenian loanwords in general but does not give these examples.
6. **Historical footnote qualification (p. 413, footnote 301):** the source notes that significant Kurdish acquisition of Armenian is associated with the later establishment of the Armenian state and settlement of some Kurds there; before that, Armenians in the region were generally the minority in shared interaction. The main text is represented, but this footnote qualification is missing.
7. **Native words affected by aspiration (p. 416):** besides *çav, kî, pênc, te*, the source gives *çi, ker, ti/tu* as old native words now pronounced with aspiration in some northern speech. These examples are absent.
8. **Historical possibility in other Kurdish varieties (p. 417):** the source explicitly leaves open the possibility that Sorani, Southern Kurdish and Hawrami once had word-initial unaspirated variants, which may have disappeared under Persian (and perhaps Arabic) influence or because those varieties were farther from Armenian/Caucasian models. It concludes that the lack of historical evidence makes this impossible to prove either way. The authored lesson says the contrast is absent there but omits this historical uncertainty.

## Source inconsistencies retained

- On p. 407, the source first describes the emphatic contrast as P and T (excluding K and T), then says K and P in the next sentence. The authored lesson flags the inconsistency and limits the comparison to the P/T examples actually supplied.
- The author’s historical explanations are presented as hypotheses, not established facts. The lesson should retain that epistemic distinction.
