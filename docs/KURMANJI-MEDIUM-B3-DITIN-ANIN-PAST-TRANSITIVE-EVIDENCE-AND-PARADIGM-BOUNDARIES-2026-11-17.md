## 1. Research question

The previous four-verb audit deliberately left two gaps open: a complete finite present paradigm for `dîtin`, and a complete past-transitive paradigm for `anîn`. This note separates what the primary reference grammar directly attests from what can be derived by applying a general rule, and from what remains unverified.

Principal source: W. M. Thackston, *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings* (2006), especially §14, pp. 32–34; §18.2, pp. 47–49; and §20.1, pp. 52–54. The accessible text reproduces the printed page labels. Secondary online teaching pages are used only as corroboration, not as the basis for a canonical paradigm.

## 2. Dîtin — present: a stem is confirmed, but a complete table is not directly printed in the consulted section

Thackston §14 explicitly lists `dîtin` ‘to see’ with the irregular present stem `bîn-`. The same section gives the general present formation: stressed `di-` + present stem + person ending, and prints the consonant-stem endings:

| Person | Ending |
|---|---|
| 1sg | `-im` |
| 2sg | `-î` |
| 3sg | `-e` |
| 1pl | `-in` |
| 2pl | `-in` |
| 3pl | `-in` |

This provides a transparent morphological basis for the commonly reported forms `dibînim, dibînî, dibîne, dibînin`. A contemporary Kurmanji teaching reference prints all six forms and identifies `bîn-` as the present stem; another reference grammar-oriented lesson also prints the full set. These are corroborating secondary sources, not substitutes for a source-specific primary paradigm table.

**Evidence classification:**
- `bîn-` as the lexical present stem: directly attested by Thackston.
- `di-` + consonant-stem person endings: directly attested as the general rule by Thackston.
- Six finite forms of `dîtin`: morphologically predictable and independently corroborated online, but **not directly printed as a dedicated six-person table in the consulted Thackston passage**.
- Negative six-person forms: not independently established from the consulted primary paradigm section; do not add a full negative table to the project evidence register without a suitable source and variety check.

For editorial work, the six affirmative forms may be recorded as **rule-derived candidates with independent secondary corroboration**, not as a verbatim Thackston table. Native-editor review and reference-variety alignment remain necessary.

## 3. Dîtin — simple past: directly attested object-controlled agreement

Thackston §18.2, pp. 47–48, is unusually useful because it explicitly gives two controlled series for transitive `dîtin`.

### 3.1 Different agents, stable third-person singular patient

| Oblique agent | Direct patient | Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `min` | `ew` | `dît` | I saw him/her/it |
| `me` | `ew` | `dît` | we saw him/her/it |
| `te` | `ew` | `dît` | you (sg.) saw him/her/it |
| `we` | `ew` | `dît` | you (pl.) saw him/her/it |
| `wî` | `ew` | `dît` | he saw him/her/it |
| `wan` | `ew` | `dît` | they saw him/her/it |

The form remains `dît` because the patient is third-person singular; changing the agent does not change the agreement ending in this canonical pattern.

### 3.2 Stable third-person singular agent, changing patients

| Oblique agent | Direct patient | Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wî` | `ez` | `dîtim` | he saw me |
| `wî` | `em` | `dîtin` | he saw us |
| `wî` | `tu` | `dîtî` | he saw you (sg.) |
| `wî` | `hûn` | `dîtin` | he saw you (pl.) |
| `wî` | `ew` | `dît` | he saw him/her/it |
| `wî` | `ew` | `dîtin` | he saw them |

This is direct source evidence for object/patient-controlled person and number marking. The last two rows differ in the number of the third-person patient, which English translation alone may not make visually obvious without contextual annotation. Keep the intended patient number explicit in any teaching record.

**Critical boundary:** this is not a six-person conjugation of the verb with six different agents. It is a controlled object-agreement set with the agent fixed or varied. A learner chart that labels these rows merely “I, you, he, we, you-plural, they” would misrepresent the grammar.

Thackston also gives `wî ez nedîtim`, `wî em nedîtin`, `wî tu nedîtî`, `wî hûn nedîtin`, `wî ew nedît`, and `wî ew nedîtin` as corresponding negative examples. This directly supports the negative pattern for these patient features in this source variety; it does not by itself establish every possible dialectal or discourse variant.

## 4. Anîn — directly attested past examples and the limits of the evidence

Thackston §18.2, p. 48, gives two independent transitive past examples:

- `Jinikekê çaya me anî.` — ‘A woman brought our tea.’
- `Wî mirovî çay anî.` — ‘That man brought tea.’

Both contain the past form `anî` with a third-person singular patient. They establish real usage of the past stem `anî-` and the 3sg finite form `anî` in the cited construction. They do **not** provide a six-person `anîn` paradigm.

The same section gives a coordinated past example with a plural patient:

- `Havalan destên xwe bi hustiwên hev re bir û anîn.` — ‘The friends reached out and put their hands on each other’s necks.’

Thackston explains that in a coordinated sequence with a plural patient, only the last transitive verb may show the plural marking. Therefore, `anîn` here is evidence for plural patient agreement in a specific coordinated construction, not a stand-alone plural conjugation table.

**Do not infer:** a full `anîn` past table, or six agent-indexed forms, from these examples. For a complete teaching paradigm, obtain a source that varies patient person/number systematically and specify the target variety.

## 5. Agreement and case: minimum safe descriptive model

For the canonical transitive simple past described in Thackston §18.2:

1. The agent is marked Oblique.
2. The patient/direct object is Direct (nominative in Thackston’s terminology).
3. The verb agrees in person and number with the patient.
4. The past form is built from the past stem, not the present stem.

Thus:
- `dîtin`: present stem `bîn-`; past stem `dît-`.
- `anîn`: past stem `anî-`; cited past finite form `anî`.
- Do not use present `bîn-` to form the simple past of ‘see’.
- Do not interpret the person of the Oblique agent as the ending on the verb.

Thackston §18.5 also notes stylistic loss of ergativity in some subordinate-clause contexts such as ‘I said that…’ and ‘I saw that…’, and reports sporadic loss in some eastern dialects. Therefore, the canonical model is a teaching baseline, not a claim that every attested past transitive clause in every variety must look identical.

## 7. Editorial recommendations (proposal only)

1. Use `dîtin` as the clearest controlled example for introducing the difference between present subject agreement and canonical past patient agreement, because Thackston explicitly prints both a present stem and patient-controlled past series.
2. Teach the past examples with role labels (agent/subject, patient/object, case, agreement controller), not as a memorized list of “I/you/he” conjugations.
3. Keep `anîn` past examples in the evidence bank as attested sentences, but do not turn them into a full paradigm.
4. Mark the present affirmative `dîtin` forms as rule-derived until a primary source-specific table and editor confirmation are obtained.
5. Do not merge the Thackston paradigms into the proposed Cizre/Botan-leaning project standard without owner approval and native Kurmanji review.

This is an evidence and curriculum-design proposal only. It does not approve or alter any learner-facing content.
