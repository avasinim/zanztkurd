## 2. Teaching distinction to establish

### Pattern A — Indefinite noun with oblique case (Lesson 04)
`gund-ek-î`
- `gund`: noun, ‘village’ (masculine singular)
- `-ek`: singular indefinite marker
- `-î`: oblique-case ending in the forms listed in Haig Table 7
- Whole form: indefinite oblique form of the noun

Feminine comparison:
`jin-ek-ê`
- `jin`: noun, ‘woman’ (feminine singular)
- `-ek`: singular indefinite marker
- `-ê`: feminine oblique ending in the Table 7 paradigm

### Pattern B — Indefinite noun linked to an adjective by ezafe (Lesson 05)
`bajar-ek-î mezin`
- `bajar`: head noun, ‘town’
- `-ek`: singular indefinite marker
- `-î`: masculine ezafe marker linking the head noun to the following adjective in this example
- `mezin`: adjective, ‘big’
- Whole phrase: ‘a big town’

**Critical point:** the final `-î` in Pattern A and Pattern B is not assigned the same grammatical function. The form must be interpreted in its construction, not by its spelling alone.

### Pattern C — Ezafe and a possessor (Lesson 05 bridge / Lesson 06 preview)
`mal-a me`
- `mal`: head noun, ‘house’
- `-a`: feminine ezafe agreeing with the head noun
- `me`: first-person plural oblique pronoun, ‘us/our’ in this possessor construction
- Whole phrase: ‘our house’

This illustrates that the ezafe marker belongs to the head noun, while the possessor is expressed after it. The possessor's oblique form is a separate fact from ezafe agreement.

## 3. Proposed lesson architecture

### Lesson 04 — ناوی دیار و نادیار؛ پاشگری -ek
Recommended sequence:
1. Briefly define definite/indefinite interpretation in context; do not imply a bare noun is always definite.
2. Introduce the Table 7 contrast for masculine and feminine singular nouns:
   - `gund` / `gund-ek`
   - `gund-î` / `gund-ek-î`
   - `jin` / `jin-ek`
   - `jin-ê` / `jin-ek-ê`
3. Label the three components separately: noun + indefinite marker + oblique ending.
4. Add a clearly separated comparison box showing `bajar-ek-î mezin` from Haig example (4). Explain that its `-î` is ezafe, not the oblique ending in `gund-ek-î`.
5. Include one guided identification task using only these source-verified forms.
6. Include a short answer key or marking rule: learners must name the construction and grammatical function, not merely identify the letters.

Suggested Sorani explanatory copy (editorial draft, not inserted into lesson):
> لە `gund-ek-î`دا، `-ek` نیشانەی ناوی تاکی نادیارە و `-î` نیشانەی حاڵەتی ناڕاستەوخۆیە. بەڵام لە `bajar-ek-î mezin`دا، `-î` ئەزافەیە و ناوەکە بە هاوەڵناوی `mezin`ەوە دەبەستێتەوە. بۆ ناسینەوەی کارکردی `-î`، پێویستە پێکهاتە و شوێنی وشەکە لە دەربڕینەکەدا ببینین.

### Lesson 05 — ئەزافە و پەیوەندیی ناو و هاوەڵناو
Recommended sequence:
1. Define the head noun as the element whose gender/number controls the ezafe form.
2. Present `bajar-ek-î mezin` as the adjective construction.
3. Present `mal-a me` as a possessor construction.
4. In both examples, visually label:
   - head noun;
   - ezafe marker;
   - following adjective or possessor.
5. State that adjectives do not inflect for case in the cited construction, while possessors in ezafe constructions take oblique case.
6. Explain that the ezafe marker agrees with the head noun, not with the following adjective or possessor.
7. Include a contrast question asking learners why `-î` has different functions in the two phrases.

Suggested Sorani explanatory copy (editorial draft, not inserted into lesson):
> ئەزافە پەیوەندیی نێوان ناوی سەرەکی و وشەی دوای خۆی دروست دەکات. نیشانەی ئەزافە لەگەڵ ڕەگەز و ژمارەی ناوی سەرەکی ڕێکدەکەوێت. ئەگەر وشەی دوای ئەزافە هاوەڵناو بێت، ئەو هاوەڵناوە لە نموونەی ئێمەدا حاڵەتی ناڕاستەوخۆ وەرناگرێت؛ بەڵام خاوەندار لە پێکهاتەی خاوەنداریدا بە فۆڕمی ناڕاستەوخۆ دێت.

## 4. Accuracy notes and guardrails
- Haig Table 7 supports the cited masculine/feminine direct and oblique singular forms.
- Haig §2.3.1, example (4), supports `bajar-ek-î mezin` and analyzes `-î` as masculine ezafe in that phrase.
- Haig §2.3.1, example (5), supports `mal-a me`; `-a` is feminine ezafe and `me` is an oblique first-person plural possessor.
- Haig notes that oblique marking may be suppressed when a noun is followed by ezafe. Do not teach a simplistic rule that every noun in an ezafe phrase must display a separate oblique ending.
- Haig also notes weakening/variation in gender distinctions after indefinite `-ek`. Present the cited paradigm as the source's Standard Kurmanjî paradigm, not as an exceptionless description of every regional variety.
- Do not invent new Kurmanji forms or ask learners to generate forms that have not been taught and source-checked.
- Keep `ڕەگ`, `بنەڕەت` and any future agreed term for stem distinct; this proposal does not alter that course-wide terminology decision.

## 5. Suggested exercises and answer criteria

### Lesson 04
Task: classify the final ending in each cited form:
1. `gund-ek-î`
2. `jin-ek-ê`
3. `bajar-ek-î mezin`

Expected criteria:
- Learner identifies `-ek` as the indefinite marker in all three.
- Learner identifies the final `-î` in (1) as oblique case.
- Learner identifies `-ê` in (2) as feminine oblique case in the cited paradigm.
- Learner identifies the final `-î` in (3) as ezafe linking the head noun to `mezin`.
- Learner does not claim that identical spelling proves identical grammatical function.

### Lesson 05
Task: label the head noun, ezafe marker and following element in:
1. `bajar-ek-î mezin`
2. `mal-a me`

Expected criteria:
- In (1), `bajar` is the head noun, `-î` is ezafe, and `mezin` is the adjective.
- In (2), `mal` is the head noun, `-a` is ezafe, and `me` is the oblique possessor.
- Learner explains that ezafe agreement follows the head noun, while possessor case is a separate grammatical relation.
