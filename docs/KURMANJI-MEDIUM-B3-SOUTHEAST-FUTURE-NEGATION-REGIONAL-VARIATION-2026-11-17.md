# Regional variation in Kurmanji future marking and negation (2026-11-17)

## Key new finding
Geoffrey Haig's regional overview in *The Languages and Linguistics of Western Asia: An Areal Perspective* (De Gruyter Mouton, 2018), §2.5.2, explicitly distinguishes Standard Kurmanji from southeastern Kurmanji varieties.

## 1. Future affirmative morphology
The overview states:
- General/standard pattern: preverbal particle `(d)ê` or `wê` followed by a verb with subjunctive prefix `bi-`.
- In southeastern Kurmanji dialects, the verb lacks `bi-` in future tenses.

This aligns with Christoph Unger's distinction between Standard Kurmanji and Badini:
- Standard Kurmanji: `wê/dê/ê` + present-stem subjunctive with `bi-`.
- Badini: `dê` + present-stem person/number form without `bi-`; Unger gives `vêca ez dê ç-ime malê` (“I will go home now”).

## 2. Future negation
Haig's regional overview states:
- Standard Kurmanji can retain the future particle `dê/wê` with a negated subjunctive form (`ne-`).
- In Kurmanji varieties spoken in Şemzînan and northern Iraq, this construction is not available; instead, a negated indicative form expresses future.
- Therefore, do not automatically transfer Standard Kurmanji negative future examples such as `ez ê ... neçim` to Şemzînan or northern-Iraqi varieties.

Unger independently identifies Badini as predominantly spoken in Duhok Governorate, Iraq, and documents its future affirmative construction with `dê` plus an unprefixed present-stem verb. Haig's regional overview separately names northern Iraq and Şemzînan for the negative-future contrast. Keep these geographic descriptions source-specific rather than assuming every speaker in all of northern Iraq or every Badini subvariety behaves identically.

## 3. Distinguish Badini `da` from future `dê`
Unger (2014), “Badini Kurdish Modal Particles dê and da: Procedural Semantics and Language Variation,” pp. 215–217, states:
- Badini future `dê` combines with present-stem person/number inflection without `bi-`.
- Badini also has a separate particle `da`, used with the present stem without modal/aspectual prefixes for remote-past habitual or counterfactual meanings.
- Unger says `da` is absent from Standard Kurmanji.

Do not treat `da` as a spelling variant of future `dê`, or as a universal Kurmanji conditional marker.

## 4. Implications for Academy documentation
1. Keep Standard Kurmanji and Badini future paradigms separate.
2. Keep the Şemzînan/northern-Iraq negative-future exception visible in the grammar ledger.
3. Do not build one combined paradigm that mixes `bi-`-marked Standard Kurmanji with unprefixed southeastern future forms.
4. Do not use the Standard Kurmanji negative future construction as a universal Kurmanji rule.
5. Do not select a target variety or particle for the Academy without explicit approval.
6. The finding concerns ordinary future marking; it does not overturn the independently documented past-stem first conditional examples `Min ê bixwenda` / `Min ê nexwenda`. Those must remain a separate construction in the evidence ledger.

## Scope and confidence
This is a source-backed regional contrast, not a complete dialect atlas. Haig's chapter provides a regional synthesis; Unger gives a focused comparison of Standard Kurmanji and Badini. More primary elicited examples from the exact Şemzînan and northern-Iraqi communities would be needed before writing dialect-specific learner paradigms.
