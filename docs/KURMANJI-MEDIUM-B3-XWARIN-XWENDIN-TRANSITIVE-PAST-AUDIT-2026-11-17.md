# Kurmancî B3 — xwarin û xwendin: transitive simple-past evidence audit (2026-11-17)

## Scope and safety
Documentation-only source audit. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, migrations, production data, PR #3, or deployment changed. Course inventory remains 26 + 12 lessons. The target learner variety remains Standard Kurmancî in Hawar-based Latin orthography; regional forms are not silently mixed into the core.

## Executive findings
- **xwarin** (“to eat”) has past stem **xwar-** and present stem **xw-**. Haig & Öpengin's Northern Kurdish reference gives a full transitive simple-past paradigm for a third-person singular object: **min xwar, te xwar, wî xwar, wê xwar, me xwar, we xwar, wan xwar**. The oblique agent changes, while the verb remains unmarked because the patient is third-person singular.
- The same reference contrasts simple past **min xwar** (“I ate [something]”) with past progressive **min dixwar** (“I was eating / used to eat”). Do not teach these as interchangeable.
- **xwendin** (“to read/study”) has past stem **xwend-** and present stem **xwîn-**. A learner-facing example **Me pirtûk xwend** (“We read the book”) is attested in the Kurdîzan teaching material. This is a pedagogical example, not an independently verified literary quotation.
- Both are transitive in the relevant uses. In the canonical Standard Kurmancî simple past, the agent is oblique and the direct patient controls verbal agreement. The readily verified examples have a third-person singular patient; they therefore show the unmarked verb form, not the complete person/number agreement paradigm.
- Negative simple past is formed with **ne-** in the reference grammar's general rule: **min nan nexwar** (“I did not eat bread”). The exact complete negative paradigm for xwendin was not independently verified here.
- No independently page-checked literary token for either verb was confirmed in this pass. Do not label the teaching examples as literary evidence.

## 1. Xwarin — stem, alignment and aspect
**Infinitive:** xwarin  
**Past stem:** xwar-  
**Present stem:** xw-  
**Present illustration:** **em dixwin** (“we eat”), from the learner grammar's stem inventory and present formation.

### Simple past with a third-person singular patient
| Agent | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg oblique | Min nan xwar. | I ate bread. |
| 2sg oblique | Te nan xwar. | You ate bread. |
| 3sg masculine oblique | Wî nan xwar. | He ate bread. |
| 3sg feminine oblique | Wê nan xwar. | She ate bread. |
| 1pl oblique | Me nan xwar. | We ate bread. |
| 2pl oblique | We nan xwar. | You (plural) ate bread. |
| 3pl oblique | Wan nan xwar. | They ate bread. |

Haig & Öpengin's Table 14 prints the pronoun + verb forms (without repeating a noun phrase in every row) and glosses the 1sg example “I ate (something).” The table establishes that changing the agent does not add agent agreement when the patient is third-person singular.

### Aspect contrast
- **Min nan xwar** — simple past, “I ate bread.”
- **Min nan dixwar** — past progressive/imperfective, “I was eating bread / I used to eat bread.”

The prefix **di-** marks the progressive/imperfective past in this contrast. Do not interpret **dixwar** as a simple-past form.

### Negation
- **Min nan nexwar** — “I did not eat bread.”
- The general Northern Kurmanji rule described by Haig & Öpengin adds **ne-** to negate simple and progressive past forms (e.g. **ne-hatin**, **ne-di-hatin**). Regional negative progressive patterns differ; Western Kurmanji sources report **na-de-** in some varieties. Keep such regional variants out of the Standard core unless the course explicitly teaches them.

## 2. Xwendin — stem and verified transitive example
**Infinitive:** xwendin  
**Past stem:** xwend-  
**Present stem:** xwîn-

The accessible beginner grammar lists the past and present stems separately. Kurdîzan's ergative lesson gives **min xwend** (“I read”) and **me xwend** (“we read”) for a third-person singular implicit object; its past-stem lesson lists **xwendin → xwend / xwîn**. A later teaching example gives **Me pirtûk xwend** (“We read the book”).

### What this confirms
- **Me** is the oblique agent (“we”) in the past transitive construction.
- **Pirtûk** is the direct third-person singular patient.
- **Xwend** is unmarked for person/number because the patient is third-person singular.
- The present stem **xwîn-** must not be substituted for past stem **xwend-** in a simple-past clause.

### What remains unverified
- A full xwendin agreement table with first-/second-person direct objects was not independently checked against a primary reference in this pass. Do not manufacture forms by copying the dîtin table without verifying phonological/morphological details.
- A full negative xwendin paradigm and a page-checked literary quotation were not confirmed. **Ne-xwend** is a regular-rule prediction, not presented here as a separately sourced literary token.

## 3. Agreement principle and dialect guardrail
The University of Arizona's Northern Kurdish overview describes the past-tense ergative alignment and distinguishes nominative from oblique pronouns. Haig & Öpengin's table provides the concrete xwarin paradigm. This Standard Kurmancî pattern is the core teaching model; research on regional varieties documents variation, including non-canonical agreement in connected discourse and regional differences in negation. Such variants should be explicitly labelled rather than blended into the core paradigm.
