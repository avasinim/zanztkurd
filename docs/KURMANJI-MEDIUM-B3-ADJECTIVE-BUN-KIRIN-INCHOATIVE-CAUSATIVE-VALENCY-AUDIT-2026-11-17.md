# Kurmanji Adjective + Light Verb Alternations — Inchoative/Causative, Valency and Dialect Audit — 2026-11-17

## 1. Purpose and scope

This audit examines adjective-based Kurmanji complex predicates, especially pairs in which **bûn** forms an intransitive/inchoative predicate and **kirin** forms a transitive/causative predicate. It distinguishes:

- adjective + **bûn**: a state or change into a state (often inchoative);
- adjective + **kirin**: causing an entity to enter or have that state (often causative);
- ordinary copular adjective predication;
- resultative/passive interpretations, which require separate syntactic evidence.

The evidence includes Haig (2002), Songül Gündoğdu's study of noun-verb complex predicates and the Muş Kurmanji analysis in her dissertation. These sources support a constructional contrast, not a license to generate every possible adjective + bûn/kirin combination.

Documentation only. No course lesson, course inventory, seed, code, schema, migration, CI, database, production system, PR, deployment or main branch is changed.

|---|---|---|---|

| **eşkere bûn** | **eşkere kirin** | ‘become obvious/clear’ / ‘make obvious/clear’ | Explicit pair in the Muş Kurmanji discussion of adjective + light-verb predicates |
| **nexweş ketin** | — | ‘get sick’ | Attested adjective + intransitive light-verb example; not a bûn/kirin pair |
| **rast hatin** | — | ‘encounter’ | Haig's adjective + intransitive verb type; lexicalized meaning, not a productive ‘become right’ pattern |

The first two pairs are useful for demonstrating the semantic contrast, but their exact inflection, argument marking, information structure and dialectal distribution must be checked before they become lesson paradigms.

## 3. The core contrast: inchoative versus causative

The pair **acis bûn / acis kirin** is listed with the meanings ‘get bored’ and ‘bore somebody’. The semantic relation is:

- inchoative: the experiencer/theme enters the state of boredom;
- causative: an external causer brings about that state in an affected participant.

The pair **eşkere bûn / eşkere kirin** is described as ‘become obvious/clear’ versus ‘make obvious/clear’. It provides a second adjective-based illustration of the same broad alternation.

A cautious semantic representation is:

| Predicate type | Event structure (informal) | Typical argument roles |
|---|---|---|
| ADJ + bûn (inchoative) | BECOME [STATE(ADJ)] | one affected theme/experiencer |
| ADJ + kirin (causative) | CAUSE [BECOME [STATE(ADJ)]] | causer + affected theme |

This is a semantic diagnostic, not a claim that every pair is derived by a single productive syntactic operation. Lexical meaning, conventional argument structure and variety-specific usage must be recorded per predicate.

## 4. Do not conflate inchoative, copular and resultative readings

An adjective near **bûn** can occur in more than one construction:

1. **Copular/stative:** the subject is characterized as being in a state.
2. **Inchoative:** the subject comes to be in that state.
3. **Lexical complex predicate:** adjective + light verb forms a conventional predicate with a stored meaning and argument frame.
4. **Resultative/passive candidate:** a state is interpreted as the result of an event; this requires an eventive source, argument mapping and contextual evidence.

The existence of **acis bûn** ‘get bored’ or **eşkere bûn** ‘become obvious’ does not establish that adjective + bûn is a passive construction. Nor does a translation such as English ‘be clear’ prove a passive reading. The analysis must ask whether there is a corresponding transitive event, whether the affected participant corresponds to its object, and whether an external agent is suppressed, optionally expressed or unavailable.

## 5. Valency and agreement must be tested independently

Gündoğdu's analysis of adjective/preposition complex predicates uses the intransitive/transitive contrast in examples such as:

- **eşkere bûn** — ‘become obvious’;
- **eşkere kirin** — ‘make obvious/clear’;
- **vebûn** — ‘get opened’;
- **vekirin** — ‘open’;
- **bi rê ketin** — ‘set off on a journey’;
- **bi rê kirin** — ‘see off’.

The source's analysis treats adjective and prepositional predicates as predicative structures that can have an intransitive form and a transitive counterpart. It proposes a structural account in which the transitive light-verb configuration introduces the external causer and the intransitive form expresses the change of state.

For instructional/editorial use, do not derive case and agreement from the English gloss. Verify for each predicate:
- whether the intransitive subject is direct case and controls agreement;
- whether the transitive agent is oblique in past tense;
- whether the affected object is direct case and controls past agreement;
- whether the predicate has any additional PP or oblique complement;
- whether the pattern is stable across the intended standard and regional varieties.

The general Kurmanji past transitive alignment does not remove the need to establish the lexical predicate's valency first.

## 6. What the sources do and do not prove

### Haig (2002)

Haig's article establishes that Kurdish complex predicates are structurally heterogeneous. It identifies adjective + verb, noun + verb, particle + verb and PP + verb patterns; no single analysis explains all types. The paper's examples include **rast hatin** and **pîroz kirin**, and its passive discussion shows that complex predicates can themselves enter passive constructions.

It does not establish that every adjective can freely combine with bûn or kirin, or that every adjective + bûn sequence is passive.

### Gündoğdu (2016; dissertation material)

Gündoğdu's work provides the directly relevant adjective pairs **acis bûn / acis kirin** and **eşkere bûn / eşkere kirin**, alongside **nexweş ketin**. The dissertation's detailed syntactic discussion is based on Muş Kurmanji data. This is valuable independent evidence for the inchoative/causative contrast, but Muş findings should not be silently relabelled as universal Standard Kurmanjî.

The available paper text and dissertation excerpts support the construction types and listed examples. Full paradigms and broad regional distribution have not been established in this audit.

## 7. Editorial policy for the Kurmanji-medium pathway

1. Teach **bûn** and **kirin** as light verbs whose contribution depends on the full complex predicate.
2. Introduce **acis bûn / acis kirin** as a documented semantic pair, with the source variety clearly labelled and native-editor review before learner-facing publication.
3. Treat **eşkere bûn / eşkere kirin** as a second documented inchoative/causative pair, again with dialect/source labelling.
4. Do not state that all adjectives form productive pairs with bûn and kirin.
5. Do not label ADJ + bûn as passive without independent event-structure and argument-promotion diagnostics.
6. Do not infer the passive pattern reported by Mizban (2026) from these ordinary adjective + light-verb pairs. That report concerns a distinct regional claim involving noun/adjective + bûn and compound verbs in northeastern/northern Iranian Kurmanji; its full chapter still requires inspection.
7. Keep Muş examples distinct from the chosen target standard until a native Kurmanji editor confirms the forms and their acceptability in that standard.
8. No course content or seed data is updated by this research note.

## 9. Conclusion

The available Kurmanji evidence supports adjective-based complex predicates in which **bûn** may express an inchoative change of state and **kirin** may express a corresponding causative event. The attested pairs **acis bûn / acis kirin** and **eşkere bûn / eşkere kirin** are useful examples, but they are lexical/constructional evidence, not proof of unrestricted productivity. Copular, inchoative, causative and resultative/passive readings must be distinguished by syntax and context. Muş data must remain labelled as Muş evidence until independently confirmed for the target standard.

**Decision:** document the attested pairs as source-labelled research examples; do not modify lessons or generalize a productive adjective + bûn/kirin rule without further dialect and native-editor verification.
