## 4. Consolidated P0 academic/editorial decisions

### P0-A — choose and document the reference variety

The sources use different scopes:
- Haig (2018) describes Standard Kurmanjî loosely based on Cizre/Botan, then discusses regional variation.
- Thackston's reference grammar describes a written norm adopted by many writers while acknowledging extensive dialect differences.
- Haig & Öpengin (2015) use a widely accepted written standard in their examples.
- Baker & Atlamaz's case/agreement study includes Adıyaman data and contrasts regional patterns.

The owner/editor must decide which spoken reference variety the course teaches. Until then:
- describe standard forms as standard/pedagogical forms, not universal facts;
- label regional alternatives explicitly;
- do not mark an attested dialect variant as simply wrong;
- keep advanced variants in teacher notes if they overload beginner lessons.

### P0-B — approve Sorani house style and terminology

The Kurmanji source review cannot certify Sorani writing. A native Sorani editor must review:
- all lesson explanations and glosses;
- grammar terminology for Direct/Oblique, split alignment, ezafe, flap/trill, phoneme/grapheme;
- spelling, punctuation, sentence fluency and pedagogical clarity.

Do not run global spelling substitutions before the Avasin house style is approved.

### P0-C — preserve orthographic category distinctions

Across lessons 01–08 and 26, consistently distinguish:
1. alphabet letter/grapheme;
2. phoneme;
3. phonetic realization;
4. orthographic convention.

For r/rr specifically:
- the Hawar alphabet inventory remains 31 letters;
- word-initial r is trilled phonetically;
- roj remains written with one r;
- rr is only sporadically used to mark a trill in some contexts, not an extra letter and not a universal doubling rule.

### P0-D — exact-example provenance

Every example in the course should be tagged internally as:
- source-attested (exact form and gloss located);
- adapted (source model adjusted, with adaptation disclosed);
- instructor-created (not presented as a source quotation).

The exact Min ew dît example is now source-attested in Thackston §18.2, p. 48. This supersedes the earlier register's unresolved status. Other examples remain subject to their own evidence checks.

## 5. Cross-lesson grammar consistency requirements

### 5.1 Direct and Oblique case
- Keep Direct/Oblique as the course terms.
- Do not equate Direct with subject or nominative, or Oblique with object or accusative.
- Present transitive clauses in the canonical pattern: Direct subject, Oblique object.
- Past transitive clauses in the canonical pattern: Oblique agent/subject, Direct patient/object, with verb agreement to the Direct patient.
- Keep the documented double-oblique and agreement deviations as dialect-specific notes.

### 5.2 Ezafe and nominal morphology
- Separate ezafe gender/number agreement from external case of the whole NP.
- Distinguish indefinite -ek, oblique case suffixes, linking ezafe and free/demonstrative ezafe.
- Avoid treating visually similar suffixes as the same morpheme without syntactic analysis.
- Explain xwe for same-clause subject coreference where relevant.

### 5.3 Verbs, negation and interrogatives
- The present di- pattern is a generalization, not a universal template for every irregular or compound verb.
- Teach karîn and other irregular patterns separately.
- Separate present verbal negation, copular/existential negation and negative imperative constructions.
- Distinguish kî (Direct) and kê (Oblique); do not flatten çend and çiqas into one gloss.
- Treat wh-in-situ as a common pattern, not an absolute ban on fronting.
- Do not teach ma as a neutral, universal yes/no marker without its marked pragmatic/discourse conditions.

### 5.4 Word order and information structure
- SOV is the pragmatically neutral baseline, not an invariant order.
- Explain recipient/goal placement with sourced examples where appropriate.
- Fronting/focus examples need discourse context and native-speaker validation.

### 5.5 R/rr and pronunciation
- Do not add rr as a 32nd alphabet letter.
- Keep phonetic tap/trill distinction separate from whether orthography marks it.
- Do not change roj to rroj.
- Do not invent minimal pairs from an internally inconsistent reference table.
