# Kurmancî B3 — şandin û kişandin: stem alternations, transitive past and agreement (2026-11-17)

## Scope and safety
Documentation-only linguistic audit. No lesson content, course inventory, runtime, CI, migrations, production database, PR #3, or deployment was changed. The existing course inventory remains 26 + 12. The target remains Standard Kurmancî in Hawar-based Latin orthography; regional and historical forms are labelled rather than silently mixed into the core.

## Executive findings
- **şandin** (“to send”) is transitive. Its present stem is **şîn-** and its past stem is **şand-**. Present forms: **ez dişînim, tu dişînî, ew dişîne, em/hûn/ew dişînin**. The simple-past stem is **şand**; with a third-person singular patient the published paradigm gives **min şand, te şand, wî/wê şand, me/we/wan şand**. When the direct patient is second person singular, the academic example is **min tu şandî** (“I sent you”), where the verb agrees with the direct patient.
- **kişandin** (“to pull, draw, attract; also smoke, photograph, endure depending on construction”) is transitive in its central lexical use. Its present stem is **kişîn-** and past stem **kişand-**. Present: **ez dikişînim, tu dikişînî, ew dikişîne, em/hûn/ew dikişînin**. A dictionary gives a simple-past example **Wî şeşar li min kişand** (“He presented/aimed a gun at me”). Thackston gives a plural extended-patient example **Wêneyên min bala rojname û hunermendan kişandin** (“My pictures attracted the attention of newspapers and artists”), where the plural final element of the extended patient triggers plural agreement.
- Both verbs follow the Standard Kurmancî transitive past pattern: oblique agent, direct patient, verb agreement with the patient. For a third-person singular direct patient, the verb is unmarked **şand / kişand**. Do not read the oblique pronoun as the direct object.
- A key caution: Karabax's **kişandin** table on p.171 is explicitly the **past progressive/continuous**, not the simple past: **Min tu dikişandî / Te ez dikişandim / Wî(wê) hûn dikişandin**. Do not copy these **di-** forms into a simple-past paradigm.
- The Kurmancî Wiktionary entry for **şandin** records a classical citation attributed to Melayê Cizîrî (c.1640), but the syntax of the quoted verse is not transparent enough to use it as a clean, standalone simple-past paradigm example. A modern reader also supplies **Nameyek ji aliyê wî ve hat şandin** (“A letter was sent by him”), which is passive, not active simple past.

## 1. Şandin — “to send”
**Infinitive:** şandin  
**Present stem:** şîn-  
**Past stem:** şand-  
**Transitivity:** transitive.

### Present paradigm
| Person | Standard present |
|---|---|
| ez | dişînim |
| tu | dişînî |
| ew (sg.) | dişîne |
| em | dişînin |
| hûn | dişînin |
| ew (pl.) | dişînin |

The present stem **şîn-** is documented in the Kurmancî verb entry and in the standard pedagogical rule for verbs ending in **-andin**: replace **-andin** with **-în** to obtain the present stem.

### Simple past
The Kurdish Wiktionary conjugation table lists **şand** as the past form with a third-person singular direct patient for each oblique agent:
- **min şand**
- **te şand**
- **wê/wî şand**
- **me şand**
- **we şand**
- **wan şand**

I. I. Tsukerman's linguistic article provides a direct object-agreement contrast:
- **ez te dişînim** — I send you (present; nominative agent).
- **min tu şandî** — I sent you (past; oblique agent, direct patient, verb agrees with 2sg patient).

This example is especially valuable because it shows the alignment shift in one verb across present and past.

### Other forms: do not conflate
- **Te tiştek ji min re neşand!** — You did not send anything to me. The Wiktionary entry cites this as a negative simple-past sentence; the direct patient is an indefinite “anything”.
- **Wê ev name ji min re şandiye.** — She has sent this letter to me. This is a perfect form, not simple past.
- **Name tên şandin.** — Letters are sent / a letter is being sent. This is passive present, not active present.
- **hatibûn şandin** — had been sent; passive pluperfect construction, with **hatin** as the finite auxiliary and **şandin** remaining infinitive.

### Classical evidence
The Kurmancî Wiktionary entry quotes a verse attributed to Melayê Cizîrî (circa 1640) containing **... li ser canî ve şandin**. This is a genuine historical literary citation for the lexical form, but the quoted line's syntactic analysis is not sufficiently clear in the entry to classify it confidently as a simple-past finite form. Preserve it as a literary occurrence, not as a conjugation proof.

## 2. Kişandin — “to pull, draw, attract”
**Infinitive:** kişandin  
**Present stem:** kişîn-  
**Past stem:** kişand-  
**Transitivity:** transitive in the core “pull/draw” sense; the verb also has extended senses such as attracting attention, taking a photograph, smoking, or enduring suffering.

### Present paradigm
| Person | Standard present |
|---|---|
| ez | dikişînim |
| tu | dikişînî |
| ew (sg.) | dikişîne |
| em | dikişînin |
| hûn | dikişînin |
| ew (pl.) | dikişînin |

The present stem **kişîn-** is independently listed by the pedagogical verb-stem table, Kurdolingo, and the Kurmancî–Turkish dictionary. Example: **Şano gelekî bala min dikişîne** (“Theatre attracts my attention a lot”). The lexical dictionary also gives **Wêneyekî ... bikişînim** (“let me take a photograph ...”) as subjunctive, not present indicative.

### Simple past and extended patient agreement
A published Kurmancî–English dictionary gives:
- **Wî şeşar li min kişand.** — He presented/aimed a gun at me.

Here **wî** is the oblique agent; **şeşar** is the direct thing presented/aimed; **li min** is a prepositional complement, not the direct object. The finite past **kişand** has third-person singular agreement with the direct patient.

Thackston's reference grammar provides an extended-patient example:
- **Wêneyên min bala rojname û hunermendan kişandin.** — My pictures attracted the attention of newspapers and artists.

The grammatical discussion explicitly explains that **kişandin** agrees in the plural with the final element **hunermendan** inside the extended patient phrase. This is a useful advanced example of agreement with a complex patient, not a basic one-word object paradigm.

### Past progressive — a separate form
Karabax, *Rêzimana Kurdî ya Kurmancî*, example 224, labels the following as past progressive/continuous:
- **Min tu dikişandî**
- **Te ez dikişandim**
- **Wî/wê hûn dikişandin**
- **Me ew dikişandin**
- **We ez dikişandim**
- **Wan em dikişandin**

The negative forms add **ne-** to the progressive construction:
- **Min tu nedikişandî**
- **Te ez nedikişandim**
- **Wî/wê hûn nedikişandin**

These forms are not simple past. The **di-** prefix and object-agreement endings belong to the progressive paradigm shown in this source. Keep them separate from simple-past **kişand**.

### Lexical and syntactic caution
The dictionary entry records several meanings of **kişandin** and illustrates them with different constructions. Do not assume every use has the same argument structure:
- literal pulling/drawing: direct patient;
- aiming/presenting a weapon **li** someone: direct weapon plus oblique/prepositional target;
- attracting attention: extended patient phrase, whose final plural element may control agreement;
- smoking or enduring suffering: idiomatic/semantic extensions requiring their own examples.
The simple-past agreement rule applies to the direct patient, not automatically to every oblique phrase.

## 3. Comparison and teaching recommendations
| Feature | şandin | kişandin |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | send | pull/draw/attract |
| Present stem | şîn- | kişîn- |
| Past stem | şand- | kişand- |
| Present 1sg | dişînim | dikişînim |
| Present 3sg | dişîne | dikişîne |
| Simple past with 3sg patient | min şand | wî şeşar kişand |
| Clear object-agreement example | min tu şandî | plural extended patient: ... hunermendan kişandin |
| Past progressive evidence | not needed here | dikişand- forms in Karabax; not simple past |

Recommended instructional order:
1. Teach both infinitives with the two stems as separate lexical facts.
2. Teach present paradigms using **dişînim / dikişînim**.
3. Teach transitive simple past with the oblique agent and nominative direct patient.
4. Use **min tu şandî** to show direct-object agreement with a 2sg patient.
5. Keep the **kişandin** past-progressive examples in a later aspect lesson; never label them simple past.
6. Teach extended-patient agreement in an advanced note, with the full phrase underlined so learners can see which noun controls plural agreement.
