## 1. Scope and method

This audit narrows the previous B3 stem inventory to four high-value verbs:

- `hatin` — come
- `anîn` — bring
- `gotin` — say
- `dîtin` — see

The purpose is not to manufacture four complete paradigms from analogy. It is to record exactly what the consulted grammars attest, distinguish directly attested forms from morphological projections, and identify the missing evidence that must be obtained before any paradigm is used in a learner lesson.

The principal references are:

1. Haig (2018), “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî),” especially §2.6, Tables 12–14, pp. 124–126.
2. Haig & Öpengin (2018), “Kurmanji Kurdish in Turkey: Structure, varieties and status,” especially §3.6, Tables 9–11, pp. 183–185, and the discussion of past-transitive agreement.
3. Thackston (2006), *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, §14, pp. 33–34, and §18, pp. 43–48.

The source descriptions are not interchangeable: Haig’s “Standard Kurmanjî” sketch is loosely based on Cizre/Botan, while Thackston documents a reference-grammar variety whose exact alignment with that target must be checked before adopting its paradigms. Haig & Öpengin explicitly document dialect variation. The proposed project reference variety is still not owner-approved.

| Infinitive | Past stem | Present stem / form | Meaning | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `hatin` | `hat-` | `-(h)ê- / -wer-` in Haig & Öpengin; special finite forms in Thackston | come | Directly attested; alternative stems require variety labeling |
| `anîn` | `anî-` in Haig & Öpengin’s stem inventory | `-tîn-` is not presented as a simple regular stem; Thackston gives special finite forms | bring | Stem inventory and finite paradigm need to be kept distinct |
| `gotin` | `got-` | `-bêj-` | say | Directly attested in both stem inventory and finite present paradigm |
| `dîtin` | `dît-` | `-bîn-` | see | Directly attested in stem inventory; full six-person finite table not found in the consulted excerpts |

**Key distinction:** a lexical present stem is not necessarily the visible string in a finite form. In particular, the finite forms of `hatin` and `anîn` are described as exceptional in Thackston. The source’s stem notation and its finite conjugation table must therefore be stored separately rather than forced into one oversimplified formula.

## 3. Directly attested six-person present paradigms

### 3.1 `hatin` — Thackston’s affirmative and negative present

The following six-person forms are transcribed from Thackston §14, p. 34. They are source-attested forms, not forms reconstructed for this report.

| Person | Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | `ez têm` | `ez nayêm` |
| 2sg | `tu teyî` | `tu nayeyî` |
| 3sg | `ew tê` | `ew nayê` |
| 1pl | `em tên` | `em nayên` |
| 2pl | `hun tên` | `hun nayên` |
| 3pl | `ew tên` | `ew nayên` |

Haig (2018, Table 14, p. 126) independently attests the simple past and past progressive of `hatin` (see §4). The Haig & Öpengin stem inventory lists alternative present stems `-(h)ê-` and `-wer-`; this should not be collapsed into a claim that every regional form is freely interchangeable.

### 3.2 `anîn` — Thackston’s affirmative and negative present

| Person | Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | `ez tînim` | `ez naynim` |
| 2sg | `tu tînî` | `tu naynî` |
| 3sg | `ew tîne` | `ew nayne` |
| 1pl | `em tînin` | `em naynin` |
| 2pl | `hun tînin` | `hun naynin` |
| 3pl | `ew tînin` | `ew naynin` |

These forms are directly transcribed from Thackston §14, p. 34. The reference explicitly describes `hatin` and `anîn` as having irregular present conjugations and explains the visible `t-` pattern as an assimilation involving the indicative/modal marker. This is a source-specific analysis; it should not be converted into a universal phonological rule without further evidence.

### 3.3 `gotin` — Haig’s six-person simple present

Haig (2018, Table 14, p. 126) directly gives all six affirmative present forms:

| Person | Attested form |
|---|---|
| 1sg | `ez dibêjim` |
| 2sg | `tu dibêjî` |
| 3sg | `ew dibêje` |
| 1pl | `em dibêjin` |
| 2pl | `hûn dibêjin` |
| 3pl | `ew dibêjin` |

Haig & Öpengin’s Table 9 independently lists `got-` as the past stem and `-bêj-` as the present stem. This is a useful example of why learners need to record both principal parts: the finite present forms are based on `bêj-`, not on a mechanical deletion of the infinitive ending from `gotin`.

### 3.4 `dîtin` — present stem is attested; full finite paradigm remains unverified

Haig & Öpengin Table 9 and Haig Table 12 both list:

- infinitive: `dîtin`
- past stem: `dît-`
- present stem: `-bîn-`
- meaning: “see”

Thackston §14, p. 33, independently identifies `bîn-` as the present stem of `dîtin`. However, the consulted passages do not provide a complete six-person finite present table specifically for this verb.

A mechanically composed candidate such as `di- + bîn- + person ending` may look predictable under the general present-stem pattern, but this audit deliberately does **not** certify the resulting six forms as source-attested. They remain **not yet verified** until checked in a primary paradigm source and by a qualified native Kurmanji editor.

## 4. Directly attested simple past of intransitive `hatin`

Haig (2018, Table 14, p. 126) and Thackston (§18, p. 43) attest the following simple past forms:

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1sg | `ez hatim` |
| 2sg | `tu hatî` |
| 3sg | `ew hat` |
| 1pl | `em hatin` |
| 2pl | `hûn hatin` |
| 3pl | `ew hatin` |

This is an intransitive past paradigm. It is therefore not a model for the past of transitive `gotin`, `dîtin`, or `anîn`. The past-tense agreement controller changes with transitivity.

## 5. Why the past transitive paradigms must not be guessed

Haig & Öpengin (2018, pp. 184–185) explain that finite agreement indexes:

- the intransitive subject in all tenses;
- the transitive subject in present tenses;
- the transitive object in past tenses, in the canonical pattern.

Their Table 11 contrasts present `gotin` with intransitive past `hatin` and transitive past `xwarin`. This is a crucial structural distinction: the six-person present table for `gotin` does not license a six-person past table by simply replacing `dibêj-` with `got-` and reusing the same person labels.

Haig & Öpengin also discuss past-transitive subject agreement under specific discourse and dialect conditions. Their example (35) contains `got-in` with plural agreement even though the direct object `doz-a xwe` (“their case”) is singular; the plural agreement reflects a preceding plural subject in a particular discourse configuration. The authors explicitly state that the extent of such subject agreement varies by dialect and by semantic and syntactic factors.

Consequently:

1. Do not publish a single six-person past-transitive paradigm for these verbs without specifying the object’s person/number, the construction, and the variety.
2. Do not label every plural past-transitive verb form as an error.
3. Do not infer a complete `gotin`, `dîtin`, or `anîn` past table from the intransitive `hatin` paradigm.
4. For each transitive example, identify the agent/subject, the patient/object, their case forms, and the element controlling agreement.
5. Obtain source-attested minimal examples and native-editor review before creating a learner-facing paradigm.

## 6. Morphological segmentation: what is safe to teach internally

The following are safe descriptive distinctions for the editor’s evidence records:

- `gotin`: present stem `bêj-`; past stem `got-`.
- `dîtin`: present stem `bîn-`; past stem `dît-`.
- `hatin`: the present forms require special treatment; the simple past `hat-` is independently attested.
- `anîn`: the present forms require special treatment; do not assume the finite `tîn-` pattern is a regular stem derived by deleting the infinitive ending.

A generic internal segmentation such as `TAM/NEG + stem + agreement` is only a heuristic. It must not obscure lexical suppletion, contractions, allomorphy, or the transitivity-sensitive past agreement system.

## 8. Recommended B3 teaching sequence (proposal only)

1. Teach the concept of two lexical principal parts with `gotin` and `dîtin`, using only source-confirmed stems.
2. Teach the complete present paradigms of `gotin`, `hatin`, and `anîn), with `hatin` and `anîn` explicitly marked as special forms in the chosen reference grammar.
3. Teach the intransitive simple past of `hatin`.
4. Introduce the distinction between present-transitive agreement and past-transitive agreement with a separately sourced, controlled example set.
5. Add `dîtin` finite forms only after a full source-attested paradigm is located and independently checked.

This sequence is a design proposal, not a lesson plan approved for publication and not a certification of B3 CEFR alignment.
