# Course 2 Lesson 10 — Question Structure Source Review (2026-11-23)

## Scope
This is a documentation-only review of the question portion in Course 2, Lesson 10 seed (lesson 10 HTML). No lesson text, SQL, application code, database, Supabase, main, or production content was edited.

## Seed claims checked
- Yes/no questions can be identified by intonation/context.
- Information questions use question words (the seed gives Sorani gloss examples: who, what, where, when).
- Question word order should be checked in the Standard Kurmanji frame and context.

## Evidence found
1. A source accessible via Bogazici Digital Archive, section “Pro-drop” (source excerpt discussing Kurmanji), states that Kurmanji yes/no questions use intonation without a Turkish-style question particle; it gives the example “Te vê pirtûkê xilas kir?” and footnote describing emphasis/intonation. It also notes subject/object omission depends on discourse context.
2. Windfuhr, *The Iranian Languages*, section 4.3.3 “Questions” (Kurdish chapter) says question words appear in the positions normally occupied by the corresponding grammatical constituents; it describes yes/no questions with terminal interrogative intonation and mentions the particle âyâ in mostly literary Kurdish. Because this is a broad Kurdish source, its descriptions must not be automatically treated as universal Kurmanji-specific rules.
3. Haig (2018), *Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî)* is the principal grammar reference already used in the course review, but the search result excerpt examined here does not establish a dedicated question chapter or a full question-word paradigm.

## Review outcome
- **Supported at a cautious level:** yes/no questions may be formed without a dedicated question particle, using interrogative intonation; question words can remain in their corresponding constituent positions.
- **Not fully verified for the course's exact Standard Kurmanji scope:** exact question-word forms, full paradigm, intonation details, and variation by dialect/register. Avoid expanding this seed paragraph into a categorical rule without a Kurmanji-specific passage.
- **No confirmed error** in the seed's high-level description from this limited check.

## Recommendation
Keep the question section brief and explicitly scoped to standard Kurmanji examples. Use verified examples only, and do not introduce an âyâ particle as a general Kurmanji feature based solely on the broader Kurdish source. A dedicated Kurmanji source check is still required before adding a paradigm or detailed word-order rule.
