# Kurmanji Passive Variation in Northeastern and Northern Iran — 2026 Source-Critical Audit

## 1. Purpose and change boundary

This audit evaluates a newly located 2026 chapter reporting variation in passive constructions in Kurmanji spoken in northeastern and northern Iran. It is a source-critical supplement to the general Kurmanji passive documentation.

This is not a revision of the course grammar or learner-facing lesson text. No lesson, seed data, code, schema, migration, CI, production database, PR #3, main branch, or deployment is changed.

## 2. New source and reported dataset

Elham Mizban, “Passive Structure in Kurmānji and Its Morphological and Sociological Changes,” in *Caspian Languages and Linguistics* (Springer, 2026), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-95-1390-1_9.

The accessible abstract reports:
- 1,258 sentences collected from radio programs, interviews, and semi-structured questions;
- 100 sentences excluded as incomplete or irrelevant;
- 1,158 sentences categorized and analyzed;
- data from Kurmanji in northeastern and northern areas of Iran;
- two reported passive strategies in that dataset.

The abstract calls the first strategy the historical construction: conjugated **hātin** plus an infinitive, noun, or adjective, and reports it remains used with simple verbs. It calls the second a “New Analytic” strategy: noun/adjective plus **bûn**, reported for compound verbs. The abstract reports frequencies of 36.45% for the historical strategy and 63.54% for the New Analytic strategy, and associates the latter with higher education and, slightly, women speakers.

## 3. Critical limitation: this is not yet a verified Kurmanji-wide rule

The available public page exposes the abstract and bibliographic metadata, not the complete chapter, its full tables, elicitation materials, exact examples, coding manual, or sampling details. The percentages therefore cannot be independently recalculated from the material currently accessible.

The reported result is geographically and sociolinguistically bounded. It must not be generalized to all Kurmanji varieties, all speakers in Iran, or the Standard Hawar/Cizîrî/Botan teaching target without additional evidence.

The chapter's “New Analytic” label and analysis need close inspection in the full text:
- What exact noun/adjective + **bûn** forms are included?
- Are they productive passive constructions, resultative/adjectival predicates, or lexicalized complex predicates?
- How is the agent represented or suppressed?
- What is the relation between the nominal/adjectival component and the lexical verb?
- How are simple verbs separated from compound predicates?
- Are the two percentages calculated over all 1,158 sentences, only passive tokens, or a restricted subset?
- Are radio, interview, and elicited data reported separately?
- How are speaker age, locality, gender, education, and register controlled?

Until these questions are answered from the full chapter, the reported frequency contrast is an abstract-level claim, not an independently audited corpus statistic.

## 4. Reconciliation with established descriptions

The established descriptive baseline remains:
- Haig (2004, §4.3.1.4): Northern Group/Kurmanji passive formed with finite **hatin** plus lexical infinitive; restricted to transitive predicates; active object advances to passive subject.
- Haig (2002): passivization of noun-plus-verb complex predicates depends on the argument structure of the complete predicate; only a direct object advances to subjecthood.
- Daneshpazhouh & Karimi Doostan (2016): comparative analysis describes Sorani as morphological and Kurmanji as periphrastic (**hatin + infinitive**).
- The 2026 Garrusi study explicitly distinguishes multiple Kurdish varieties and cannot establish Kurmanji morphology.

Mizban's 2026 result does not automatically falsify the established baseline. It may document an innovative, contact-influenced, geographically restricted strategy in a particular northeastern/northern Iranian Kurmanji sample, especially in complex predicates. The correct scholarly response is to retain the established construction while recording the new result as a serious regional hypothesis requiring full-text verification.

## 5. Do not confuse three different structures

### A. Canonical periphrastic passive
Finite **hatin** + lexical infinitive. This is the well-established Kurmanji baseline and should remain the primary teaching model unless the course target is explicitly changed after editorial review.

### B. Noun/adjective + bûn
The 2026 abstract reports this as a newer analytic passive for compound verbs in its regional dataset. Do not yet treat every noun/adjective + **bûn** sequence as passive. It must be tested for argument structure, event interpretation, agent suppression/licensing, aspect, and whether the nominal/adjectival element is part of a complex predicate or a resultative predicate.

### C. Resultative, adjectival, or inchoative predicate
A noun/adjective with **bûn** may describe a state or change of state rather than a passive event. A translation such as “has been cleaned” is not enough to determine the syntax. Require an active counterpart, agent diagnostics, event structure, and the full source context.

## 6. Required full-text verification protocol

Before incorporating the reported strategy into any course reference:
1. Obtain lawful access to the complete Springer chapter.
2. Extract every example of noun/adjective + **bûn** and preserve original transcription and context.
3. Identify the exact locality and speaker metadata for each example.
4. Determine whether the predicate is a simple verb, noun-verb complex, adjective-verb complex, or resultative.
5. Record whether an overt agent phrase is possible, attested, rejected, or untested.
6. Establish the patient/undergoer and any active counterpart.
7. Distinguish passive event readings from result-state and anticausative readings.
8. Reconstruct the denominators behind 36.45% and 63.54%.
9. Check whether examples were spontaneous, broadcast, interview, or elicited.
10. Compare the findings with a separate Standard Kurmanji corpus and native-editor judgments outside the Iranian regional sample.

## 7. Course and pathway policy

For the current fully Kurmanji-medium pathway:
- Retain **hatin + infinitive** as the core passive construction.
- Do not introduce noun/adjective + **bûn** as a productive general rule.
- Add a research note that a 2026 study reports a possible newer regional strategy in northeastern/northern Iranian Kurmanji.
- Keep any verified examples in a regional-variation evidence bank, not in the core paradigm, until full-text review and native-editor approval.
- Never label the strategy “Persianized” solely from the abstract's contact interpretation; inspect the evidence and distinguish structural resemblance from demonstrated causal transfer.
- Do not modify existing lessons or course inventory without explicit authorization.

## 8. Evidence status

| Claim | Status | Editorial treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Kurmanji canonical passive uses finite **hatin + infinitive** | Established descriptive evidence | Core reference |
| Only direct objects advance under canonical passive | Supported by Haig's Kurmanji analysis | Use as syntactic diagnostic |
| Iranian regional sample reports N/ADJ + **bûn** | Abstract-level 2026 report | Provisional regional finding |
| Reported 36.45% / 63.54% distribution | Abstract-level statistic; denominator and tables not inspected | Do not generalize or recalculate |
| New structure is a productive passive across Kurmanji | Not established | Do not teach as general rule |
| New structure is caused by Persian contact | Author's abstract interpretation; causal details not independently assessed | Attribute, do not assert as settled fact |
