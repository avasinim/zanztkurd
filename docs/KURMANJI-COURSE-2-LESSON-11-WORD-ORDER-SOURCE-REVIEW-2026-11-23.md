## Seed claims inspected
- Identifying subject, verb, and object is a useful starting point for sentence analysis.
- Noun phrases may include determiners, number, possessors, and adjectives; ezafe is one connection used in such phrases.
- The order of constituents can shift for information structure or emphasis.
- The seed presents `Wî mirov-î çay anî` as a source example.

## Source-verified findings
Haig & Öpengin (2018), §3.7.1, describes pragmatically neutral Kurmanji clause order as **SOVG** (subject–object–verb–goal), with G covering spatial goals, recipients, and addressees. In the seed's simpler SOV description, the goal constituent is omitted from the mnemonic because not every clause contains one.

The cited example `Wî mirov-î çay anî` is source-present; the source gloss is “That man brought tea.” The example supports the lesson's subject/object/verb identification and verb-final placement in this clause.

The source also explicitly says word order is not rigid: direct objects may be fronted for pragmatic purposes. Goal-argument placement can vary by construction and dialect. Therefore SOV should be taught as the neutral/basic pattern—not as an exceptionless rule.

## Findings / limits
- **Verified:** neutral SOV(G) baseline; the source example; flexible ordering and pragmatic object-fronting.
- **Not fully verified here:** the seed's general statements about noun-phrase constituents, adjective/possessor placement, and ezafe as a full instructional rule. Those need dedicated source checks before adding new examples.
- **No confirmed grammar error** in Lesson 11 from this review.
- **Teaching improvement opportunity:** annotate the cited sentence word-by-word and add a contrasted, source-verified fronted-object example before expanding the lesson.
