## 1. Scope and evidence rules

This register audits the six existing lesson topics: (13) cardinal numbers, (14) colours and everyday objects, (15) family vocabulary, (16) weekdays and time, (17) present-tense verbs, and (18) negation.

Evidence labels:
- **SUPPORTED — scope-limited:** a cited source directly supports the stated grammatical or lexical generalization, but not necessarily every lesson example.
- **PARTLY SUPPORTED:** the general topic is documented; the exact form, gloss, dialect distribution, or sentence still needs checking.
- **UNRESOLVED:** current accessible evidence does not justify a firm editorial decision.
- **EDITORIAL:** consistency, sequencing, translation, or pedagogy decision rather than a linguistic fact.

A grammar reference can support a pattern without validating a particular sentence in the course. A vocabulary list cannot establish that every inflected example is grammatical. Sorani glosses require independent Sorani editorial review. No claim below authorizes a silent correction.

## 2. Source register

**S1.** Haig, Geoffrey (2018). “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî).” In *The Languages and Linguistics of Western Asia*, pp. 106–158. Open author-hosted chapter: https://multicast.aspra.uni-bamberg.de/data/pubs/Haig2018_Northern-Kurdish_Kurmanji.pdf  
Use: core descriptive grammar, numerals, nominal morphology, verb stems, TAM, negation and documented regional variation. Limitation: a grammar sketch is not a validation corpus for every course sentence.

**S2.** Haig, Geoffrey & Öpengin, Ergin (2018). “Kurdish: A critical research overview.” *Language and Linguistics Compass* 12(9), e122 Kurdish overview / Northern Kurdish section. Author-hosted material: https://www.uni-bamberg.de/fileadmin/aspra/06_Team/Publications_and_CV/HaigOepengin2018_Kurmanji_Kurdish_.pdf  
Use: standard Kurmanji morphology, case and agreement patterns; regional qualifications. Limitation: dialect coverage is necessarily selective.

**S3.** Thackston, W. M. (2006). *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*. Harvard University. Accessible copy: https://sites.fas.harvard.edu/~iranian/Kurmanji/kurmanji_complete.pdf  
Use: paradigms, cardinal numbers, present tense and irregular verbs. Limitation: older pedagogical reference; compare with later descriptive work and the chosen house variety.

**S4.** *Kurmanji Kurdish for the Beginners* (2008). Course material associated with Harvard Faculty of Arts and Sciences / Uppsala University, lessons 1–4. Accessible PDF: https://www.kurdipedia.org/files/books/2023/474430.PDF  
Use: beginner vocabulary, family terms, weekdays/time, present tense and negative forms. Limitation: instructional material, not a peer-reviewed descriptive grammar; use as corroborating teaching evidence, not sole authority.

**S5.** Gündoğdu, Songül (2015). “Negation in Kurmanji.” In Stela Manova (ed.), *Affix Ordering Across Languages and Frameworks*, pp. 154–172. Oxford University Press. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780190210434.003.0007  
Use: analysis of the negative prefixes n(a)-, n(e)- and ni-, and their interaction with TAM morphology. Limitation: theoretical chapter; examples and variety coverage should be checked against the course's target variety.

**S6.** Haig, Geoffrey & Öpengin, Ergin (2015). “Gender in Kurdish: structural and semantic dimensions.” Author-hosted offprint: https://www.uni-bamberg.de/fileadmin/aspra/06_Team/Publications_and_CV/haig_opengin_2015_gender_kurdish_offprint.pdf  
Use: kinship terms and their regional variation; distinguishes lexical meanings such as wife/husband, mother/father, grandmother/grandfather. Limitation: a regional overview does not select the project's preferred house forms.

**S7.** Amryan, Tereza (2026). “‘Black Day, White Face’: Color Terms in Kurmanji Kurdish Phraseology.” *Journal of Iranian Linguistics* 3(1), published 2026-07-31. DOI: https://doi.org/10.46991/jil/2026.01.01  
Use: recent specialist work on Kurmanji colour terms and cultural/phraseological semantics. Limitation: it studies idioms and symbolism, not a complete elementary colour lexicon; its Yezidi Armenian community material must not be generalized to all Kurmanji varieties.

**S8.** University of Arizona, “Kurmanji Kurdish,” Iranian Languages teaching resource: https://iranian-languages.arizona.edu/node/7  
Use: concise explanation of nominal case and present simple formation. Limitation: teaching overview, not an exhaustive grammar; confirm with S1/S2.

**S9.** Haig & Öpengin (2014). “Regional variation in Kurmanji: A preliminary classification of dialects.” Use for explicit variety-aware treatment. Bibliographic landing page: https://www.academia.edu/  
Limitation: do not use as proof for an individual word or sentence unless the relevant passage and data are inspected.

## 4. Cross-lesson decisions and release blockers

1. **Reference variety:** choose the written house standard and the spoken/listening reference variety before finalizing variant labels.
2. **Controlled lexicon:** establish a versioned dictionary/terminology register for numbers, colour semantics, kinship, weekdays and time expressions.
3. **Example provenance:** tag each example as directly attested, adapted from a cited source, or instructor-created and independently reviewed.
4. **Morphology sequencing:** coordinate lessons 13–14 with nominal case/indefiniteness; lesson 15 with ezafe/possessives; lesson 17 with stem alternations and complex predicates; lesson 18 with TAM and non-verbal negation.
5. **Sorani editorial gate:** review every Sorani explanation and translation independently.
6. **Pathway B gate:** re-author all learner-facing material in Kurmanji; do not translate Pathway A line by line.
7. **Assessment gate:** test forms in context, not only word-list recall; include sentence production, comprehension and correction tasks.
