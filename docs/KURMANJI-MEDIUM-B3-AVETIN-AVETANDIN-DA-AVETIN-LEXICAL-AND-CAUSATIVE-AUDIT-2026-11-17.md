# Kurmancî Medium B3 — avêtin, avêtandin, and da-avêtin: lexical status, stems, and causative audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only lexical and grammatical audit. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, seed, database, migration, PR, or deployment changes.

## Executive conclusion

The reliable Standard Kurmancî evidence establishes **avêtin** as a transitive simplex verb meaning “throw, cast, hurl, toss”. Its stems are:
- past stem: **avêt-**
- present stem: **-avêj-**
- 1sg present: **ez davêjim**
- 2sg present: **tu davêjî**
- 3sg present: **ew davêje**
- plural present: **em/hûn/ew davêjin**

The exact lemma **avêtandin** was not verified in the authoritative dictionaries, academic stem inventory, or grammar sources consulted. Do not teach it as a standard causative, assign it a stem pair, or fabricate a paradigm.

A distinct prefixed/compound form **da-avêtin** is attested in Chyet's *Kurmanji Lexicon* with the meaning “put together, throw together”. This is not the same spelling or lexical analysis as **avêtandin**. Keep these three strings distinct.

## 1. avêtin: meaning, stems, and present paradigm

Haig & Öpengin (2018), Table 12, explicitly lists:
- infinitive: **avêtin**
- past stem: **avêt-**
- present stem: **-avêj-**
- meaning: **throw**

The same stem pair is reproduced in the Boğaziçi University-hosted academic reference extract. This is strong evidence that avêtin is a simplex transitive verb with a suppletive-looking present/past stem contrast.

Wîkîferheng's Northern Kurdish entry classifies **avêtin** as transitive and gives the meanings “throw, cast, hurl, toss”. Its paradigm is:
- **ez davêjim**
- **tu davêjî**
- **ew davêje**
- **em davêjin**
- **hûn davêjin**
- **ew davêjin**

The singular imperative is **bavêje** and plural imperative **bavêjin**. The Kurmancî grammar *Rêzimana Kurdî ya Kurmancî* independently gives **bavê / bavên** as imperative forms and records the present root **-avê-** in its analysis, while identifying **avêtin/avêjtin** and noting that avêjtin is an older form and avêtin is the active/current form in that source's editorial tradition. This illustrates a small difference in stem segmentation across references; for the course's stem inventory, retain the academically listed **-avêj-** and document the surface imperative separately.

## 2. Simple past and alignment

The past stem is **avêt-**. Verbix and the Wiktionary entry list the simple past citation form **avêt**. In Kurmancî, avêtin is transitive, so simple past clauses follow the general split-ergative pattern:
- the agent is oblique (min, te, wî/wê, me, we, wan);
- the patient is direct/absolutive;
- the verb indexes the patient.

A directly attested past-perfect example in *Kurmanji Kurdish for the Beginners* is:
**Beydayê kilîla malê avêtibû nav deryayê.**
“Beyda had thrown the key of the house into the sea.”
This confirms the past participial base **avêt-** in a full clause, but it is a past-perfect construction, not a simple-past object-agreement paradigm.

Do not mistake Verbix's six rows “min avêt / te avêt / wî avêt ...” for agreement with six different agents. In the transitive past these are oblique-agent pronouns and the unmarked verb form corresponds to a third-person-singular patient. A full set of non-3sg patient-agreement examples for avêtin was not independently verified in the sources inspected; do not invent one.

## 3. avêtandin: exact-lemma status

Exact searches for **avêtandin** did not yield a reliable Kurmancî dictionary headword, academic stem entry, independently checkable literary example, or complete conjugation. The major academic inventory explicitly lists **avêtin**, but not avêtandin, among frequent simplex verbs. The reference grammar describes **-and** as a moderately productive causative suffix used to derive transitive verbs from intransitive present stems.

This derivational rule is important but does not license the form **avêtandin**:
1. **avêtin is already transitive** (“throw”).
2. The academic account describes -and as deriving transitive verbs from intransitive verbs.
3. No inspected source establishes avêtandin as the causative of avêtin or gives it a separate lexical meaning.
4. No reliable present/past stem pair or conjugation for avêtandin was found.

Therefore:
- Do not infer “make someone throw” or “cause to be thrown” as a dictionary meaning of avêtandin.
- Do not fabricate present stem *avêjîn-* or past stem *avêtand-*.
- Do not silently normalize avêtandin to avêtin; preserve the source spelling and request contextual evidence if the form occurs in a manuscript or regional speech.
- Until an independent primary attestation is found, mark **avêtandin** “unverified as a Standard Kurmancî lemma”.

The suffix -and is real and moderately productive, but its productivity is constrained and must be demonstrated for each lexical formation. Haig & Öpengin's examples are **gerîn → gerandin** (“walk/stroll” → “lead”) and **nivistin → nivandin** (“sleep” → “put to sleep”). These are not evidence for *avêtin → avêtandin*.

## 4. da-avêtin: a separate prefixed/compound verb

Chyet's *Kurmanji Lexicon* lists **da-avêtin** as a transitive verb meaning “put together, throw together”. The same lexical source lists **avêtin** separately as “throw, throw away, throw out; lay (foundation)”, and **davêtin** as a cross-reference/variant for da-avêtin.

This is evidence for a preverb/compound construction **da- + avêtin**, not for the suffixed form **avêtandin**. Keep the spelling distinctions:
- **avêtin** — simplex “throw”.
- **da-avêtin / davêtin** — compound “put/throw together”.
- **avêtandin** — exact lemma not verified.

Do not collapse the compound prefix **da-** and the derivational suffix **-and**. They occupy different morphological positions and have different functions.

## 6. Editorial note for the Kurmancî-medium pathway

The learner-facing distinction should be concise:
- **avêtin**: tiştek avêtin — to throw something.
- **da-avêtin**: to put/throw things together (lexicalized compound; use only with source/editor confirmation).
- **avêtandin**: not included in the standard verb list until an attested regional or literary source is supplied and reviewed.

The first example is an illustrative phrase built from the verified lexical meaning, not a direct corpus quotation. The second should be supported by a source-specific example before it is added as a lesson exercise.
