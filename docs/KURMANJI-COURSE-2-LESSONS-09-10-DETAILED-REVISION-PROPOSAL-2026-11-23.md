# Course 2 — Lessons 09–10: Detailed Revision Proposal
Date: 2026-11-23
Status: Documentation-only proposal; no lesson-content changes authorized or performed.

## 1. Purpose and boundaries
This proposal turns the existing Lesson 09 instructional audit and Lesson 10 negation/question source reviews into a conservative, testable revision plan. It covers tense/aspect, negation, yes/no questions and information questions.

It does not rewrite lesson text, invent Kurmanji forms, or authorize changes to lesson HTML, seed SQL, application code, database records, migrations, Supabase, main, deployment or production.

## 2. Lesson 09 — کاتی ئێستا، ڕابردوو و داهاتوو

### Verified content to retain
- The lesson cites Haig (2018), Table 14 for `ez dibêjim`, `ez hatim` and `min xwar`.
- It contrasts simple past and imperfective past pairs: `ez hatim` / `ez dihatim`, and `min xwar` / `min dixwar`.
- It cites Haig (2018), §2.6.4 for the future construction `ew dē mesel-ē ji te re bēž-e`.
- Its cautious wording that `di-` occurs with many present forms, not every verb, should remain. Do not turn this into an exceptionless rule.

### Required instructional improvements
1. **Separate tense from aspect.** Define tense as the temporal location of an event and aspect as how the event is viewed (for example, ongoing/habitual versus completed), using terminology approved for this course. Do not treat “past imperfective” as simply another tense label without explanation.
2. **Work through the past pairs.** Put `ez hatim` beside `ez dihatim`, and `min xwar` beside `min dixwar`. Explain the contrast in the construction and identify `di-` only as supported by the cited forms; avoid claiming that one prefix alone explains every aspectual distinction.
3. **Keep transitivity and case visible.** Explain why the lesson compares an intransitive past form with a transitive past form, but do not use `min xwar` alone as proof of an overt object or object agreement: the cited example has no overt object.
4. **Gloss the future example.** Add a word-by-word gloss and a cautious explanation of `dē` and the subjunctive verb form only after checking the full source context. Preserve the exact Latin transcription.
5. **Recheck the Sorani translation.** The current gloss “ئەو کێشەکە بۆ تۆ دەڵێت” must be checked against the cited passage and context before it is retained as a general template. Do not silently substitute a new translation.
6. **Add a controlled comparison task.** Ask learners to identify which pair contrasts simple and imperfective past, identify the form carrying `di-`, and explain what can and cannot be concluded from the transitive example. Provide a model answer based only on the taught examples.

### Acceptance criteria
- Tense and aspect have separate definitions.
- Both past pairs are segmented or glossed accurately.
- `di-` and `dē` are not generalized beyond source-supported constructions.
- Future example and Sorani translation are checked in context.
- Exercise answers do not claim that `min xwar` contains an overt object.
- All Kurmanji spellings remain unchanged unless a specific source-backed correction is approved.

## 3. Lesson 10 — نەفی و پرسیار

### A. Negation: retain the supported core
The Haig (2018) review supports the existing qualified claims:
- Present indicative negation commonly uses `na-` in place of `di-`.
- Subjunctive negation uses `ne-` in place of `bi-`.
- The cited lexical forms include `nizanim` and `nikarim`.
- Past examples include `ne-hatin` and `ne-di-hatin`.

Keep the qualifications. Do not present these patterns as exceptionless or apply them to every verb and dialect without evidence.

### B. Resolve the ambiguous heading with the author
The heading «نەفیی ڕستەی ناوەکی» remains unresolved. Badıllı (1965) documents negation in nominal clauses, but that does not establish what the lesson author intended by this exact heading. It could refer to more than one grammatical concept.

**Do not rename, delete or reinterpret this heading until the author clarifies its intended meaning.** Once clarified, select a Standard Kurmanjî source that directly supports the chosen concept and align the explanation and examples with it.

### C. Yes/no questions: distinguish particle and intonation
Badıllı (1965), §195 (printed p. 134), documents a preverbal `ma` particle and also describes conversational questions in which the particle may be omitted and intonation/lengthening helps mark the question.

This is useful historical/regional evidence, not sufficient by itself to prescribe the current course's Standard Kurmanjî spelling and usage.

Proposed treatment:
1. Keep the current point that intonation can signal a conversational yes/no question, with careful wording.
2. Add `ma` only after its form and use are confirmed in a current Standard Kurmanjî reference appropriate to the course.
3. Distinguish spoken intonation from written question punctuation.
4. Do not imply that context alone is the grammatical question marker.

### D. Information questions: add only checked forms
The existing outline mentions “who, what, where, when” but lacks a full Kurmanji paradigm and examples. Badıllı lists forms such as `kî`, `kê`, `çi`, `kîjan`, `ç’awa` and `çend`; Kurdîzan provides beginner corroboration for `kî`, `çi` and `li ku`.

Because Badıllı is a 1965 regional grammar, do not mechanically copy all forms into the current Standard Kurmanjî lesson. Before adding a table:
- verify each form in a current Standard Kurmanjî source;
- preserve distinctions such as `kî` / `kê` only where supported for the chosen variety;
- use complete source-checked sentence examples;
- do not translate Sorani question words mechanically into Kurmanji.

### Suggested controlled exercises
- Identify the negative marker in already-taught source examples and state which construction it belongs to.
- Distinguish a yes/no question from an information question using verified lesson examples.
- Match each approved question word to its meaning only after the forms are source-checked.
- Include model answers and a note where dialect or source variation exists.

### Acceptance criteria
- Negation examples and qualifications remain source-faithful.
- The ambiguous heading is author-confirmed before editorial change.
- Any `ma` particle content is confirmed for the course's Standard Kurmanjî variety.
- Information-question forms and full examples have identifiable sources.
- Spoken intonation and written punctuation are not conflated.
- Applied exercises assess only content actually taught and include answer guidance.
