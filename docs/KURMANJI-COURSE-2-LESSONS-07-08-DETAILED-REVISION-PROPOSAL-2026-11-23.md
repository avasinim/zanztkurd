## 2. Lesson 07 — جێناوە کەسییەکان و حاڵەتی ڕاستەوخۆ و ناڕاستەوخۆ

### 2.1 Keep the verified paradigm, but make it easier to read
Haig Table 9 confirms the Standard Kurmanjî paradigm:

| کەس | ڕاستەوخۆ | ناڕاستەوخۆ |
|---|---|---|
| یەکەمی تاک | `ez` | `min` |
| دووەمی تاک | `tu` | `te` |
| سێیەمی تاکی نێر | `ew` | `wî` |
| سێیەمی تاکی مێ | `ew` | `wê` |
| یەکەمی کۆ | `em` | `me` |
| دووەمی کۆ | `hûn` | `we` |
| سێیەمی کۆ | `ew` | `wan` |

Important teaching note:
- Direct `ew` is syncretic: the same form is used for third-person masculine singular, feminine singular and plural.
- Oblique forms distinguish masculine singular `wî`, feminine singular `wê` and plural `wan`.
- Do not say that the direct form alone identifies gender or number. Syntax, agreement and discourse context help interpret it.

### 2.2 Teach case separately from sentence role
Recommended explanation:
> «بکەر» و «بەرکار» ئەرکی ڕێزمانیین؛ «ڕاستەوخۆ» و «ناڕاستەوخۆ» حاڵەتی ڕێزمانیین. ئەم دوو جۆرە زانیارییە یەک شت نین. بۆ دیاریکردنی حاڵەت، دەبێت کات، تێپەڕی/ناتێپەڕی کردار و پێکهاتەی ڕستە بپشکنین.

This prevents the false shortcut “subject = direct” or “object = oblique” as a universal rule.

### 2.3 Add a small source-based case map
According to Haig §2.2.1, oblique forms occur in several environments, including:
- object of a present-tense transitive verb;
- certain goals and recipients;
- complements of adpositions;
- possessors in ezafe constructions;
- subject of a past-tense transitive clause.

Direct case is used in other environments described by the grammar. This list should be presented as a guided map, not an exhaustive rule for every construction or dialect.

### 2.4 Exercises and expected criteria
Exercise A: match each pronoun pair in the table.
- Expected: all six person/number sets are paired accurately.

Exercise B: explain why `ew` appears in more than one row.
- Expected: learner identifies syncretism and says that gender/number must be interpreted from syntax, agreement and context.

Exercise C: given a complete, source-verified sentence selected by the course editor, identify the pronoun, its case and its grammatical role separately.
- Expected: learner does not infer case from “subject” or “object” alone.

Do not add an unsourced sentence just to complete Exercise C. If no verified sentence is approved, keep the exercise at the paradigm-identification level until a source is found.

## 3. Lesson 08 — کردار، ڕەگ، کەس و ژمارە

### 3.1 Use the existing three examples as a controlled contrast
Haig Table 14 confirms the three examples already present in the seed:

| Example | Clause type | Subject form | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ez dibêjim` — «من دەڵێم» | Present transitive paradigm | Direct `ez` | Present-tense transitive subject appears in direct form in this paradigm. |
| `ez hatim` — «من هاتم» | Simple past intransitive | Direct `ez` | Intransitive past subject remains direct. |
| `min xwar` — «من خوارد» | Simple past transitive | Oblique `min` | Past transitive subject appears in oblique form. |

Critical qualification:
- `min xwar` has no overt direct object in the cited example. It illustrates the oblique subject in the past transitive paradigm, but by itself does **not** show the form of an overt object or prove object agreement.
- Do not claim that it demonstrates all parts of the ergative construction.

### 3.2 Keep grammatical roles visible
For each example, ask learners to label:
1. tense/aspect label used by the cited source;
2. transitive or intransitive;
3. subject pronoun;
4. direct or oblique case of that pronoun;
5. whether an overt object is present in the example.

This prevents students from assuming that every past subject is oblique: `ez hatim` is the intransitive counterexample.

### 3.3 Link to Lesson 11 for a complete past-transitive example
Haig (2018), §2.7.2, example (32):
`Wî mirov-î çay anî`
‘That man brought tea.’

This is a complete past-transitive clause with an overt direct object:
- `Wî`: masculine singular oblique subject;
- `mirov-î`: oblique masculine noun in the subject phrase;
- `çay`: direct object;
- `anî`: past verb form.

The source places this example in the discussion of ergative alignment, not in §2.7.1's neutral word-order discussion. It is therefore best used as a cross-reference to Lesson 11 or as an explicitly labeled advanced example, not silently reassigned to Lesson 8.

### 3.4 Do not fabricate the missing present-transitive object example
Haig §2.2.1 supports the generalization that the object of a present-tense transitive verb is oblique. However, the current checked material does not supply a fully verified, overt-object sentence that this proposal can safely add to Lesson 8. The course editor should retrieve and verify such an example from the grammar before inserting it. Until then:
- retain `ez dibêjim` as the Table 14 paradigm example;
- explain the source rule in prose with its scope;
- do not invent an object or alter the existing sentence.

### 3.5 Exercise and expected criteria
Exercise A: classify the three Table 14 examples.
- Expected: present transitive / past intransitive / past transitive labels are correct.

Exercise B: identify the subject form and case in each.
- Expected: `ez` direct in the first two examples; `min` oblique in the third.

Exercise C: explain why `min xwar` alone cannot demonstrate the case of an overt object.
- Expected: learner notices that no object is overtly expressed in that example.

Exercise D: read the cross-reference to Lesson 11 example (32) and label subject and object.
- Expected: learner identifies `Wî mirov-î` as the oblique subject phrase and `çay` as the direct object, without confusing this example with neutral SOVG word order.

## 4. Shared terminology and design rules
- Use `حاڵەتی ڕاستەوخۆ` and `حاڵەتی ناڕاستەوخۆ` consistently.
- Keep `بکەر` and `بەرکار` for grammatical roles; do not use them as synonyms for direct/oblique.
- Keep `تێپەڕ` and `ناتێپەڕ` consistent throughout both lessons.
- Preserve all Kurmanji Latin forms and diacritics exactly as in the verified source.
- State the scope as Standard Kurmanjî and qualify dialectal variation.
- Do not broaden the lesson into a full theory of ergativity; teach the specific tense-conditioned contrast supported by Haig.
- Any new example or paradigm must have a precise source location and a checked Sorani gloss.
