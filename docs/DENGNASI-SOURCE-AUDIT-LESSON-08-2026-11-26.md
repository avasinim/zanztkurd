# Dengnasî source audit — Lesson 8 (2026-11-26)

## Scope and source evidence

Source: Husein Muhammed, *Dengnasî di kurdî de*, printed pages 68–103, corresponding to physical PDF pages 79–114 in the supplied PDF. The 36 printed pages were reviewed against Lesson 8 authoring and its generated JSON/HTML previews.

## Findings

The main lesson already contained the core B, C, Ç and D phonetic descriptions and much of their historical discussion. The source comparison found omitted footnotes, comparative forms, and several table entries. These have been restored in a supplementary source-notes section, retaining the source's qualifications rather than turning examples into universal rules.

### Details restored

1. B-related footnotes and examples: the source's scope for “Southern Kurdish” (Feyli, Kelhuri and Kirmashani), expanded *berf/befr* and *bîst* comparisons, the additional Beluchi/Sanskrit/Avestan forms for *av/sêv/ewr*, and the chronology caveat that initial V/W→B is no longer productive while medial B/V/W variation persists in some dialects.
2. The distinct *ḥeb/ḥev/ḥew* variants and their source gloss, and the dialectal clitic examples *di kitêbê de → t’kitêbê da* and *ji malê → ş’malê*.
3. Additional B/D and B/F evidence: *derî/dergeh* vs Zazaki *ber*, *din/dî* vs *bîn*, the *dîtin/bînan* present stem, and Latin *ferre* / Greek *fêro* from PIE *bher-* as distinct from the B→V→F path in *hefs/zeft*.
4. C's full Y→C comparison table across Kurmanji, Persian, Pahlavi, Avestan and Sanskrit; the source's Hebrew/Aramaic and Yêşûa historical chains; and the Turkic *yedi/yıl/yılan/yirmi* comparison.
5. Additional G→C examples and qualifications, including *camûs/gawmêş*, the preserved-G forms, and the Kurmanji–Zazaki–Persian–Avestan J/C/Z/Ç comparison table.
6. The Mêrdînî *diya min → ca min* example and the source's C→J examples *wijdan/ûjdan* and *îcar/îca → îjar/îja*.
7. Ç orthographic comparisons across writing systems, Arabic dialect realizations, the derived forms of *pênc* that distinguish final C from perceived Ç, and the Ç/Ş, Ç/T, Ç/J/Z examples, including *cent/cento/centum*.
8. D historical comparison tables for *dil/dest* and *bad/abad/bê(d)*, the N+D word comparisons across Kurdish varieties, the eastern *înadin/înad* note, and the source's explanation of *dê* with *divê/xwastin*, Zazaki *do* and English *will*.
9. The D→H/Y/W tables (*badem/bahîv*, *daden/dayîn/dan*, *bûden/bûyîn/bûn*, *zaden/zayîn/zan*), D epenthesis examples, and the distinction between *ferz*, *fer* and *qer* in footnote 76.

The supplementary notes are synchronized in the authoring source, JSON payload and standalone HTML preview. The course validator now checks selected source terms and both historical comparison tables.

## Limits and caution

This is a source-alignment audit, not independent peer review of the book's historical etymologies. Where the source itself presents a proposal or dialect-specific form, the lesson attributes it to the author and retains the qualification. No additional examples, quizzes, tests, questions or exercises were introduced.

The validator was updated but has not been executed locally or by a newly observed GitHub Actions run. No merge to main, deployment, production database change, migration or course activation was performed.
