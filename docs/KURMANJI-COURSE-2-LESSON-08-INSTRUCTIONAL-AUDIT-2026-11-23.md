# Course 2 — Lesson 08: Instructional Audit (2026-11-23)

## Scope
Reviewed the current Lesson 08 payload in `docs/kurmanji-content-seed-part-05.sql`, titled **کردار، ڕەگ، کەس و ژمارە**. This is an instructional audit of the stored content, not a full validation of all verbal morphology.

## Current content (verified)
- Introduces the distinction between a verb's basic form and its stem, and asks learners to identify markers of person, number, tense, and mood.
- Explains that verb forms relate to person and number, but the pattern varies with tense and clause type; an overt subject pronoun does not necessarily mean verbal person marking is absent.
- Defines transitive and intransitive verbs at an introductory level and links the distinction to case and agreement, especially in past clauses.
- Compares three cited forms from Haig (2018), Table 14: `ez dibêjim` (present, transitive ‘I say’), `ez hatim` (past, intransitive ‘I came’), and `min xwar` (past, transitive ‘I ate’).
- Includes a methodological caution not to generalize one verbal pattern to all verbs, review questions, and references.

## Findings
1. **The lesson promises root analysis but does not demonstrate it:** no verb is segmented into stem/root and inflectional material. Learners are asked to identify markers without a worked model.
2. **The three forms are useful but not parallel enough for beginner comparison:** tense and transitivity both change between examples, so learners cannot isolate which factor accounts for each difference from this set alone.
3. **No person/number paradigm:** the lesson describes person and number marking but provides no sourced mini-paradigm for one verb. This is a pedagogical gap, not evidence that the statement is false.
4. **Overt pronoun explanation needs an example:** the note that a subject pronoun can coexist with verbal person marking is abstract without a paired example or explanation of when pronouns are retained.
5. **Transitivity is simplified:** “can take a direct object” is a useful entry definition, but the lesson should avoid implying that every verb's argument structure is determined by a simple object/no-object test.
6. **Review questions test concepts, not analysis:** none asks learners to segment a form, identify person/number, or compare two clauses while holding tense or transitivity constant. No model answers are provided.

## Conservative recommendation
Keep the lesson unchanged for now. In a later approved revision, add one source-checked worked segmentation and a small paradigm for a single verb; then compare examples in controlled pairs (same tense with transitivity varied, or same transitivity across tenses). Label root/stem and inflectional endings only where the cited source supports the segmentation. Do not infer a complete conjugation paradigm from the three Table 14 examples.
