# Kurmanji B3 — Derketin and Daketin: Attested Forms, Stem Evidence, and Limits

Date: 2026-11-17  
Status: source-critical lexical audit; not lesson copy  
Scope: review compound-verb examples for derketin and daketin, compare attested present/imperative and past forms, and prevent overgeneralization across varieties.

## 1. Executive result

This pass finds useful examples for **derketin** and **daketin**, but not a full, independently corroborated principal-parts record for both verbs.

- **derketin**: a beginner grammar gives the imperative **derkeve** and the finite present example **Ez ji vir derdikevim** (‘I am leaving from here’). A separate past-tense teaching page lists **derket** as its past stem. This supports a working teaching record, but the web teaching sources are not equivalent to a scholarly dictionary or corpus concordance.
- **daketin**: a beginner grammar gives **dakeve** as the imperative/headword form and glosses it ‘to come down, descend’. The inspected passage does not supply a complete finite present paradigm or an explicit past form. Do not manufacture these by copying the derketin paradigm.
- The same compound-verb discussion gives **deranîn** and **dagirtin** nearby; these are separate lexical items and must not be confused with daketin.

The evidence upgrades both verbs beyond “no attestation found”, but does not justify a complete lesson-ready paradigm.

## 2. Direct evidence for derketin

### 2.1 Imperative and lexical analysis

A Kurmanji beginner grammar lists under the preverb der-:

- **derketin, derkeve** — ‘to leave, to emerge’.

It identifies der- as a preverbal element and says that the present indicative prefix di- is normally infixed between the preverbal element and the verbal stem.

Source: *Kurmanji Basic*, §4.7 “Compound Verbs with Preverbals”, p. 11, accessible PDF:
https://internationalistcommune.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/kurmanji-basic.pdf

### 2.2 Finite present example

The same section gives:

- **Ez ji vir derdikevim.** — ‘I am leaving from here.’

This is direct clause-level evidence for the sequence der-di-kev-im in the source's analysis: preverb der-, inflectional present prefix di-, verbal stem kev-, first-person ending -im.

This is a single attested person/form, not a full conjugation table. It demonstrates prefix placement in this example, not every tense or dialect.

### 2.3 Past stem

A separate Kurmanji teaching page on the simple past lists:

- **derketin → derket** — ‘to leave’.

It presents the pedagogical rule that for verbs ending in -in, -in is removed to identify the past stem. This supports derket- as a past stem in that source's teaching variety. It does not independently establish every past agreement form or the distribution of the form across Kurmanji dialects.

Source: “Simple Past Tense in Kurdish”, Kurdish Lessons, 18 January 2023:
https://kurdishlessons.com/simple-present-tense-in-kurdish/

## 3. Direct evidence for daketin

The same beginner grammar lists under preverbals:

- **dakêtin, dakeve** — ‘to come down, descend’.

The spelling of the infinitive in the source contains ê (dakêtin), while the project candidate has been written daketin. Preserve this as a source-specific spelling variant pending confirmation in an authoritative dictionary; do not silently normalize it or assume the two spellings are interchangeable in every variety.

The listed imperative dakeve supports the present-stem segment kev- in the dictionary/imperative analysis used by this grammar. However, the inspected passage does not provide:
- a finite present indicative clause for daketin;
- a past-stem form;
- a complete person-number paradigm;
- a locality label sufficient to map the form to a narrower dialect.

Therefore no finite present or past forms should be generated for daketin from the derketin example. The shared -ketin sequence is not enough to establish identical prefix placement or a complete paradigm.

Source: *Kurmanji Basic*, §4.7, p. 11:
https://internationalistcommune.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/kurmanji-basic.pdf

## 4. Cross-check: compound verbs and the subjunctive

A Kurmanji lesson PDF lists derketin among one-word compounds such as wergirtin and vekirin, and says the subjunctive prefix bi- is often omitted in such compounds. It gives:

- **Divê ez derkevim.** — ‘I need to leave.’

This independently supports the compound-subjunctive form derkevim in the teaching material. It is not a past-tense attestation and should not be confused with the indicative example derdikevim.

Source: *Kurmanji Kurdish, Lesson 5*, PDF hosted by Kurdipedia:
https://www.kurdipedia.org/files/books/2023/474430.PDF

## 6. Important distinction: stem extraction is not a license to invent forms

Some Kurmanji teaching grammars derive the present stem from the imperative by removing bi- (when present) and the final -e. On that pedagogical analysis:

- derkeve → der-kev-e → kev- as the verbal base after the preverb;
- dakeve → da-kev-e → kev- as the verbal base after the preverb.

This is an analysis of the cited imperative forms. It does not supply an attested finite paradigm, past stem, or dialect-wide rule for either compound. The preverb and inflectional prefixes must remain separately represented in the lexical record.

A further source on compound verbs notes that the present prefix di- is normally inserted between the preverbal element and verbal stem, and gives derdikevim; do not automatically extrapolate that exact form to every dialect or to daketin without a finite example.

## 7. Editorial decisions

1. Record derketin with the attested imperative derkeve, the finite present example derdikevim, and the source-supported past stem derket; mark the evidence type and source for each field.
2. Record daketin with a spelling note: the inspected source prints dakêtin; it supplies dakeve, but not a finite present or past stem in the inspected passage.
3. Do not fill the missing daketin forms by analogy with derketin or ketin.
4. Keep indicative derdikevim distinct from subjunctive derkevim.
5. Keep Standard Kurmanji teaching examples separate from Şemzînan/Midyat incorporation patterns described by Haig.
6. Do not alter existing lessons, quizzes, course inventory, seed, runtime, CI, database, migrations, production deployment, or PR #3.
