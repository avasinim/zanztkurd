# Kurmanji PP + Verb Complex Predicates — Valency Alternation, Endpoint Semantics and Dialectal Word Order Audit — 2026-11-17

## 1. Purpose and scope

This audit examines Kurmanji complex predicates whose non-verbal element is a prepositional phrase (PP), with particular attention to the distinction between:

- PP + light/simplex verb as one lexical predicate;
- intransitive/inchoative versus transitive/causative alternations;
- ordinary spatial PP adjuncts and selected PP complements;
- post-predicate placement of a constituent;
- preverbs/particles that are not full PPs.

The principal examples are **bi rê ketin** ‘set off on a journey’, **bi rê kirin** ‘see off’, **ji bîr kirin** ‘forget’, and **li xwe kirin** ‘dress oneself’. A separate Behdinî/Duhok phenomenon—certain light-verb complements occurring after **kirin** with a directional clitic—is documented but must not be conflated with the PP + V lexical pattern.

This is research documentation only. No lessons, course inventory, seed, code, schema, migration, CI, database, production system, PR, deployment or main branch are changed.

## 2. PP + verb examples in the Kurmanji inventory

Gündoğdu's overview of Kurmanji complex predicates lists non-verbal elements from several categories: noun, adjective, particle/adposition and PP. Her PP + V examples include:

| PP + verb | Literal structure | Lexical meaning | Source category |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bi rê ketin** | with road + fall | ‘set off (on a journey)’ | PP + V |
| **ji bîr kirin** | from mind + do | ‘forget’ | PP + V |
| **li xwe kirin** | to/on self + do | ‘dress oneself’ | PP + V |

These are source-attested lexical predicates, not examples generated by a general rule that any prepositional phrase may combine with any verb. Their idiomatic meanings and argument frames must be stored individually.

Gündoğdu also contrasts **bi rê ketin** with **bi rê kirin**:
- **bi rê ketin**: non-agentive/intransitive ‘set off on a journey’;
- **bi rê kirin**: agentive/transitive ‘see off’.

The paper argues that, for the adjective/particle/PP patterns it discusses, the choice of light verb contributes to agentivity and valency: the first member is intransitive/non-agentive and the second transitive/agentive. This is a constructional alternation, not merely a change in English translation.

## 3. Argument structure: a useful alternation, not a universal template

The pair **bi rê ketin / bi rê kirin** is analyzed as an intransitive/transitive alternation. A cautious semantic representation is:

| Form | Broad event structure | Typical role pattern |
|---|---|---|
| PP + **ketin** | GO / BECOME involved in a path or departure | one moving/affected participant |
| PP + **kirin** | CAUSE another participant to move along or enter the path | causer + moved/affected participant |

This is an explanatory abstraction of the cited pair, not a fully established derivation for every PP + verb predicate. The source's gloss ‘see off’ should not be expanded into an invented set of example sentences or inflectional paradigms.

The same PP string can participate in a different lexical predicate with a different meaning and frame. **ji bîr kirin** ‘forget’ is not simply a transparent locative phrase plus a fully lexical ‘do’; its lexicalized meaning is stored as a predicate. **li xwe kirin** ‘dress oneself’ also needs its own reflexive/argument analysis. The PP must not automatically be tagged as a freely movable locative adjunct.

## 4. PP phrase versus particle/preverb

Kurmanji complex predicates include both full phrases and particles. Gündoğdu distinguishes:
- PP + V: **bi rê ketin**, **ji bîr kirin**, **li xwe kirin**;
- particle/adposition + V: **derketin**, **jêkirin**, **daçûn**.

The distinction matters because the forms and their inflectional behavior differ. The paper notes that preverbal particles such as **da**, **ra**, **jê**, **lê**, and **der** combine with the verb; when negation or imperfective prefixes are added, these inflectional prefixes may intervene between the particle and the verb (e.g. **der-na-kev-e**, **der-di-kev-e**). The paper therefore does not analyze these as ordinary incorporated PP phrases.

Editorial rule:
- annotate full PP + V separately from particle + V;
- do not segment a particle as though it were always an independent PP complement;
- preserve the source's orthography and segmentation in citations;
- record inflectional placement from the relevant variety rather than imposing a single spacing rule.

## 5. Selected PP complement versus free locative adjunct

A PP that is part of a complex predicate is not equivalent to every ordinary spatial PP in a clause.

Compare the analytical questions:
1. Is the PP lexically selected and part of the predicate's conventional meaning?
2. Can the PP be omitted without changing the lexical sense?
3. Does the PP have a stable idiomatic meaning with the verb?
4. Does it participate in an attested intransitive/transitive alternation?
5. Does it behave as a constituent in word order and inflection?
6. Is the same string also used compositionally as a spatial phrase elsewhere?

For **bi rê ketin**, the source explicitly lists a lexical complex predicate meaning ‘set off’. For a normal clause containing a motion verb and a destination phrase, do not assume the destination is part of an LVC merely because it is a PP. The distinction must be made at the level of the lexical entry and its sense.

## 6. Behdinî/Duhok post-predicate complements: a separate dialectal phenomenon

Haig (2022), in *Post-Predicate Constituents in Kurdish*, reports that Duhok Kurmanjî requires post-predicate position for a small set of complements of **kirin** ‘do/put’. Examples from an educated native speaker include:

- **Ez yê di-ke-m=e xar** — ‘I am running’;
- **Ez yê di-ke-m=e kenî** — ‘I am laughing’;
- **Ez yê di-ke-m=e girî** — ‘I am crying’.

The directional clitic is attached to the finite verb in these examples. Haig also reports a related Sersink example with **gazî kirin** ‘call’, where the addressee follows the verb and the verb bears the directional clitic; in most Kurmanji varieties described there, the addressee is pre-predicate.

Critical limits:
- This is not evidence that all PP complements in Kurmanji occur after the verb.
- The post-predicate items in these examples are described as light-verb complements, not as the same full PPs in **bi rê ketin** or **ji bîr kirin**.
- Haig identifies the extension to certain light-verb complements as rare and apparently not encountered outside Behdinî in the data he surveyed.
- The broader post-predicate system is areally uneven; Haig describes a southeast-to-northwest gradient and notes that the Duhok variety extends the pattern to some light-verb complements.

The Duhok and Sersink evidence must therefore be labelled by locality and construction type. It is not a word-order rule for the Academy's target Standard Kurmanjî.

## 7. What the evidence supports—and what it does not

### Supported by the sources inspected

1. Kurmanji complex predicates can have a PP as their non-verbal component.
2. **bi rê ketin / bi rê kirin** is explicitly presented as an intransitive/transitive pair, with ‘set off’ versus ‘see off’.
3. **ji bîr kirin** and **li xwe kirin** are listed as PP + V complex predicates.
4. Particle + V constructions are a separate structural type.
5. Some Behdinî/Duhok and Sersink light-verb complements have dialect-specific post-predicate placement associated with directional morphology.

### Not established by the inspected sources

1. That every PP + verb combination is a lexical complex predicate.
2. That every PP + **ketin** has a corresponding causative PP + **kirin**.
3. That the **bi rê** pair has one invariant argument/case/agreement paradigm across all Kurmanji dialects.
4. That Duhok post-predicate light-verb complements generalize to Standard Kurmanjî or all Kurmanji.
5. That the post-predicate complements in Duhok are syntactically identical to the full PP components of **bi rê ketin**, **ji bîr kirin**, or **li xwe kirin**.
6. That any of these patterns is a passive construction.

## 8. Editorial policy for the Kurmanji-medium pathway

- Store **bi rê ketin**, **bi rê kirin**, **ji bîr kirin**, and **li xwe kirin** as individually attested lexical predicates with source and variety metadata.
- Use **bi rê ketin / bi rê kirin** to illustrate a documented intransitive/transitive alternation only after a native editor confirms the forms and intended meanings for the target standard.
- Do not create productive PP + verb templates or invent unattested pairs.
- Keep full PP + V separate from preverbal particle + V.
- Keep Duhok/Sersink post-predicate complement placement as a regional syntax note; do not teach it as the default Standard Kurmanjî word order.
- Do not infer passive voice from PP + bûn/kirin/ketin or from post-predicate position.
- No lesson text or seed data is modified by this research note.

## 10. Conclusion

PP + verb complex predicates in Kurmanji include attested lexical items such as **bi rê ketin**, **ji bîr kirin**, and **li xwe kirin**. The pair **bi rê ketin / bi rê kirin** is explicitly analyzed as an intransitive/transitive alternation, showing that the light verb contributes to agentivity and valency. This does not establish a productive rule for arbitrary PPs. Full PP predicates must also be kept distinct from particles/preverbs and from the rare, geographically restricted post-predicate light-verb complements reported for Duhok/Sersink.

**Decision:** preserve the attested lexical pairs as source-labelled evidence; teach no generalized PP + verb rule or Duhok post-predicate placement in the target standard without further variety-specific verification and native-editor approval.
