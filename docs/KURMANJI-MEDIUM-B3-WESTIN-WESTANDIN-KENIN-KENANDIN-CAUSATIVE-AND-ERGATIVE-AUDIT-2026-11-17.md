# Westîn / westandin and kenîn / kenandin: causative contrast and stem audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only lexical and grammatical audit for the Kurmanji-medium research track. No lesson content is changed by this file.

## Executive findings

This audit compares two useful intransitive/transitive pairs. The evidence is not equally strong for every form, so the entries below distinguish direct dictionary evidence from general morphological expectations.

1. **westîn → westandin**
   - *westîn*: “to get tired, become weary”; intransitive. Chyet records the present forms with stems **-west- / -westa-** and the past forms **westîme** “I am tired” (literally “I got tired”) and **westiya** in the entry's dialectal/literary examples. The same dictionary lists *westan* and *westiyan* as related/variant forms.
   - *westandin*: “to tire someone out, exhaust”; transitive. Chyet explicitly marks it **vt**, gives the present stem **-westîn-**, past stem **-westand-**, and an attested literary example **westand**. The Kurmanji Wiktionary conjugation page independently lists *ez diwestînim*, *tu diwestînî*, *ew diwestîne*, imperative *biwestîne*, and past stem *-westand-*.
   - This is a clear lexical transitive counterpart, but the relation is best described as a causative/valency pair rather than assuming that every regional form of *westîn* derives identically.

2. **kenîn → kenandin**
   - *kenîn*: “to laugh, smile”; intransitive. The Kurmanji Wiktionary entry lists present stem **-ken-**, forms **dikenim, dikenî, dikene, dikenin**, and past stems **-kenî- / -keniya-**, with past forms **kenîm / keniyam**, **kenî / keniyayî**, **kenî / keniya**, **kenîn / keniyan**.
   - *kenandin*: “to make someone laugh”; transitive and causative. Its entry gives present stem **-kenîn-**, present forms **dikenînim, dikenînî, dikenîne, dikenînin**, imperative **bikenîne / bikenînin**, and past stem **-kenand-**. It includes the attested example **Wî bi henekên xwe em hemû kenandin** (“With his jokes he made us all laugh”).
   - The pair is directly linked in the dictionary, which identifies *kenîn* as the intransitive counterpart and derives *kenandin* from *ken + -andin*.

### westandin
- ez **diwestînim**
- tu **diwestînî**
- ew **diwestîne**
- em / hûn / ew **diwestînin**
- imperative: **biwestîne**, plural **biwestînin**

### kenîn
- ez **dikenim**
- tu **dikenî**
- ew **dikene**
- em / hûn / ew **dikenin**
- imperative: **bikene**, plural **bikenin**

### kenandin
- ez **dikenînim**
- tu **dikenînî**
- ew **dikenîne**
- em / hûn / ew **dikenînin**
- imperative: **bikenîne**, plural **bikenînin**

The forms are reproduced from the individual verb entries. Do not normalize away the distinction between the *ken-* present stem of *kenîn* and the *kenîn-* present stem of *kenandin*.

## 3. Past tense and ergative caution

The dictionary entry for *kenandin* gives the past stem **kenand-** and the invariant third-person singular citation **kenand** across the listed oblique agents. Its literary example **Wî bi henekên xwe em hemû kenandin** shows an oblique agent (*wî*) and a plural patient (*em hemû*), with the verb form **kenandin** agreeing with the plural patient.

The *westandin* entry gives past stem **westand-** and lists **westand** as the simple past citation form. The example in Chyet's dictionary, **Boîx k’uta, nav garanê gerand, westand**, confirms the lexical use “tired [it] out.” This is useful evidence for the verb and its transitive meaning, but it does not by itself establish a complete object-agreement paradigm.

Do not manufacture a full past paradigm for either verb from the third-person citation forms. For *kenîn*, use the separately listed intransitive past paradigm; do not confuse it with the transitive *kenandin* forms.

## 4. Dialect and lexicalization caution for westîn

Chyet records several related forms around *westîn*: *westan*, *westiyan*, and *westîn*, with dialect/source labels and differing stems. The entry for *westandin* also records a regional form *wistandin*. This variation means the teaching entry should name the chosen Standard Kurmanji lemma and identify variants separately, rather than presenting all forms as interchangeable.

Haig and Öpengin caution that in some Southern Kurmanji varieties, infinitives ending in **-andin** can occur without causative meaning and may remain intransitive. Thus **-andin** is not a mechanical guarantee of causative semantics in every dialect. For the two lexical pairs here, the dictionary evidence directly marks *westandin* and *kenandin* as transitive.

## 5. Teaching-safe summary

> **Kenîn** واتە پێکەنین و کردارێکی ناتێپەڕە؛ **kenandin** واتە پێکەنینی لە کەسێکدا دروستکردن و کردارێکی تێپەڕە.  
> **Westîn** واتە ماندوو بوون؛ **westandin** واتە ماندووکردنی کەسێک.

Use the examples below only with the stated meanings:
- **Wî bi henekên xwe em hemû kenandin.** — بە گاڵتەکانی خۆی هەموومان پێکەنی.
- **Boîx k’uta, nav garanê gerand, westand.** — نموونەی ئەدەبیی Chyet بۆ *westandin*؛ وشەبەوشەی وەک سەرچاوەکە بهێڵرێتەوە و بۆ فێرکاریی سەرەتایی ڕێکبخرێتەوە تەنها دوای پشکنینی ڕەسەنەکە.
