# Kurmanji Principal Parts — Haig 2018 Table 12 Cross-Source Audit — 2026-11-17

## Purpose and scope

This is a documentation-only audit of the principal-parts evidence for frequent Kurmanji verbs. It cross-checks the forms printed in Geoffrey Haig (2018), §2.6, Table 12, against the relevant descriptive statements in W. M. Thackston, *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, especially §14. It does not implement a lexicon, create learner lessons, or authorize changes to existing course content.

The central pedagogical and editorial rule is to store and teach the infinitive, past stem and present stem as separately evidenced principal parts. The relation between the two stems is not fully predictable; do not generate one mechanically from the other.

## 1. Primary evidence: Haig (2018), §2.6, Table 12

Haig explicitly states that Kurmanji verbs have a past stem and a present stem, and that the formation of one from the other is not fully predictable. Table 12 lists the following frequent verbs. The table below preserves the source's spellings and hyphen orientation; it is a source transcription, not a claim that every item is irregular in every variety.

## 2. Cross-source checks and non-equivalences

### 2.1 Thackston §14: principal-part principle

Thackston independently identifies present stems of several common verbs as irregular and says they must be learned as principal parts; examples include dîtin → bîn- and xwestin → xwaz-. This corroborates the need to record a present stem rather than derive it from the infinitive.

### 2.2 Do not silently normalize lexical headwords

Haig prints **xwastin**, while Thackston's cited example prints **xwestin**. Keep each source's literal form in its own attestation. A normalized display headword may be proposed only as an editorial decision with its own stated orthographic convention; do not overwrite the source form or imply the two spellings are universally identical.

### 2.3 Multiple present stems are evidence, not noise

Haig lists alternatives for hatin, çûn and xistin. Preserve the alternatives as source-attested variants, with source, variety/scope and context recorded where available. Do not flatten them into a single canonical stem without further dialect-specific evidence.

### 2.4 Similar forms do not establish identical paradigms

Haig lists bûn → -b- and birin → -b-. The shared present-stem shape does not make the lexemes or their paradigms identical. The same caution applies to other apparent stem similarities.

### 2.5 Do not infer a full conjugation from a principal-part row

A principal-parts table establishes the listed stem relationship and gloss, not all tense, mood, person, negation or alignment forms. Full paradigms require their own directly checked tables or examples. This is particularly important for transitive past forms, where agreement and oblique-agent marking cannot be reduced to a simple stem substitution.

## 3. Recommended lexical-record fields

For each verb, keep at least:

- stable lexeme identifier;
- literal infinitive form and source;
- literal past stem and source;
- literal present stem or stems and source;
- gloss and sense identifier;
- source edition, page/section/table and exact variety scope;
- source spelling versus editorial display spelling;
- whether the form is directly printed, corroborated, normalized or unresolved;
- evidence for transitivity/valency in a separate field;
- example sentence and its provenance, if available;
- review status and reviewer notes.

A slash-separated alternative must be stored as separate attestations or variant records, not as an unqualified single string in a production learner dictionary.

## 4. Evidence status for this audit

- **Direct primary-table evidence:** the 25 rows transcribed above from Haig (2018), Table 12.
- **Cross-source corroboration:** the principal-part principle and examples dîtin → bîn- and xwestin → xwaz- in Thackston §14.
- **Editorial comparison:** the xwastin/xwestin spelling contrast; it is not resolved here.
- **Unresolved:** the exact locality distribution and usage conditions of the alternatives in Haig's slashed entries; these need dialect-specific evidence and, ideally, native-editor review.
- **Not established by this audit:** complete paradigms, pronunciation, frequency ranking beyond Haig's label “frequent”, or suitability for a particular course lesson.

## 5. Editorial and teaching recommendations

1. Teach three principal parts as a memorized lexical package: infinitive, past stem, present stem.
2. Mark a form as source-literal or editorially normalized; never mix the two silently.
3. Keep regional variants visible and scoped. Do not import Amêdî or Sincarî forms into a Standard Kurmanji record without an explicit variety label.
4. Teach productive patterns only after they have been separately established; an irregular-verb list must not be used to invent rules for unseen verbs.
5. Require an independent second-person check of all diacritics and stem boundaries against the PDF page before learner-facing publication.
6. Do not turn this audit into a lesson or seed-data import without the project owner's explicit approval.
