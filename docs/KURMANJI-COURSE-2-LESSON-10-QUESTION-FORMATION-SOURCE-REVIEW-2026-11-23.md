# Course 2 — Lesson 10 question-formation source review

## Existing seed claims
The lesson says yes/no questions can use intonation/context; information questions use interrogative words; it lists “who, what, where, when” as examples and notes word order should be source checked.

## Source-verified findings
1. **Yes/no questions:** Songül Gündoğdu (2011), *The Phrase Structure of Two Dialects of Kurmanji Kurdish: Standard Dialect and Muş Dialect*, §1.3.4 and footnote 6 (p. 40), states that Kurmanji yes/no questions do not use a question particle; intonation is used and the verb is stressed. The thesis provides the yes/no example `Te vê pirtûkê xilas kir?` (“Did you finish this book?”).
2. **Interrogative words and in-situ:** The same thesis §1.3.2 (p. 37) says Kurmanji wh-words remain in their base position, rather than being moved as in English. It lists `kî` (who), `çi` (what), `ku/ku(der)` (where), `kîngê` (when), `kîjan` (which), `çima` (why), `çan(kî)` (how), `çend` (how many), and `çi qas` (how much).
3. The source examples include `Tu di-c-î ku?` (“Where are you going?”), `Kî pirtûkê di-xwîn-e?` (“Who is reading the book?”), `Zana či di-xwîn-e?` (“What is Zana reading?”), and `Zana kîngê pirtûkê di-xwîn-e?` (“When is Zana reading the book?”). These are preserved exactly as presented in the source.
4. A current learning resource (Kurdîzan, “Asking questions”) independently presents question words as replacing the missing information in the sentence, keeping the remaining order, and illustrates `Tu kî yî?`, `Ew çi ye?`, `Tu li ku yî?`, `Ew li ku ye?`. This is a pedagogical corroboration, not a substitute for the thesis/reference grammar.

## Corrections to the seed's framing
- “Question via intonation” is source-supported for yes/no questions in the cited Kurmanji study.
- The information-question claim should be refined: wh-words are generally described as **in situ** in the source reviewed; don't imply that all interrogatives share one universal word-order rule.
- The seed's words “kêy”/“when” etc. should be checked for spelling against the course's chosen standard before lesson edits. The source uses `kîngê`; another modern teaching resource uses `kengî`. This review does not normalize spelling.
- The seed's unexplained heading “نەفیی ڕستەی ناوەکی” is still unresolved. The question-source check does not define it or validate it as a distinct category. It should not be retained as a technical label without a clear source and definition.
