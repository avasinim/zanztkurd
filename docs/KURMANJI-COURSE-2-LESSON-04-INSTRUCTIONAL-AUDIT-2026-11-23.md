# Course 2 — Lesson 04: Instructional Audit (2026-11-23)

## Scope
Reviewed the exact current seed entry `kurmanji-course-2-lesson-04.html`, title **ناوی دیار و نادیار؛ پاشگری -ek**. The review checks instructional clarity and internal distinctions; it is not a complete source verification of every noun, case, dialect, or definiteness context.

## Current outline (verified)
1. Singular indefiniteness with `-ek`: compares `gund` (‘village’) with `gundek` (‘a village’) and flags direct/oblique and ezafe forms as relevant.
2. Nouns without `-ek`: cautions that absence of the suffix does not automatically mean definite; interpretation depends on context.
3. Indefinite noun and oblique case: shows `gund-ek-î` and `jin-ek-ê`; distinguishes the oblique endings from ezafe in `bajar-ek-î mezin`.
4. Method note: gender, case, and context affect forms.
5. Review questions: function of `-ek`, context without `-ek`, and case effects.

## Strengths
- The lesson explicitly avoids equating the bare noun with definiteness in every context.
- It connects indefiniteness to case rather than presenting `-ek` as a form that never changes.
- It flags a useful contrast between similar-looking endings in different constructions.
- The review questions target the core concepts.

## Gaps / items to address
- **No simple first example:** `gund/gundek` appears but is not displayed as a labeled segmentation/translation table.
- **Morphological boundaries could be clearer:** the hyphenated forms are not accompanied by a compact breakdown and gloss.
- **The contrast is dense:** `gund-ek-î`, `jin-ek-ê`, and `bajar-ek-î mezin` are introduced in the same section, which may overload a learner before the basic contrast is consolidated.
- **No practice:** learners are not asked to identify `-ek`, distinguish the case ending, or decide when context makes a bare noun definite/indefinite.
- **No model answers:** review questions have no answer guide.

## Conservative recommendation
Keep the current lesson unchanged for now. If a future approved content pass adds teaching examples, order them from simple to complex: `gund / gund-ek` with a gloss, then one sourced oblique example, then the contrasting ezafe phrase `bajar-ek-î mezin`. Label each morpheme's function and avoid suggesting that one surface ending has a single grammatical role in every construction. Confirm the examples and claims against the referenced source before expanding.
