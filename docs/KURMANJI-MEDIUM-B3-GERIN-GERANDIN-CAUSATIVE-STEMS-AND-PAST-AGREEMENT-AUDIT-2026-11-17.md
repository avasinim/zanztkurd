# Kurmancî Medium B3 — gerîn and gerandin: causative derivation, stems, lexical extensions, and ergative evidence audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only lexical and grammatical audit. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, seed, database, migration, PR, or deployment changes.

## Executive findings

**gerîn** and **gerandin** are a well-attested intransitive/transitive pair, explicitly cited by Haig & Öpengin as an example of the Kurmancî causative suffix **-and**:

- **gerîn**: walk, stroll, wander around; intransitive.
  - present stem: **ger-**
  - simple-past forms recorded in sources: **gerî- / geriya-**
  - 3sg simple past: **geriya**
  - 1sg present: **ez digerim**
- **gerandin**: cause someone/something to move around; take someone for a walk; lead/guide; turn/rotate; distribute, wrap, manage, and in some contexts deceive.
  - transitive.
  - present stem: **gerîn-**
  - past stem: **gerand-**
  - 3sg simple past: **gerand**
  - 1sg present: **ez digerînim** (paradigm consistent with the dictionary's present stem; direct full paradigm is recorded in the Qafqaz dictionary and should be treated as source-specific pending editorial standardization).

The pair illustrates a derivational pattern, but the broader sources warn that the suffix **-and** is only moderately productive and can also occur in citation forms without causative meaning in some dialects. Do not use the suffix alone to guess the meaning or conjugation of an unattested verb.

## 1. gerîn: intransitive base verb

Kurdolingo records **gerîn** as a Kurmancî A1 verb meaning “to walk around”, with present stem **ger-** and the example:
**Em carinan li kolanan digerin.**
“Sometimes we stroll in the streets.”

The *Kurmanji Kurdish for the Beginners* lesson glossary records:
**Siyabend li ber mala wê geriya.**
“Siyabend wandered around her home.”
It explicitly labels **geriya** as the third-person-singular simple-past form of **gerîn/geriyan** (“to wander around”).

The Chyet *Kurmanji Lexicon* records **geriyan** as an intransitive verb meaning “to go around, roam around”, and **gerîn** appears as the related form in compound entries. The Kurmancî Wiktionary page for the compound **lê gerîn** gives the intransitive present paradigm:
- ez lê digerim
- tu lê digerî
- ew lê digere
- em/hûn/ew lê digerin
and past variants **lê-gerî- / lê-geriya-**.

A dialect/orthography note in *Kanîzar* states that some intransitive verbs are used in paired forms, including **geriyan / gerîn**, and gives the corresponding participial forms **geriyayî / gerîyî**. Therefore preserve the source's form rather than treating geriyan as automatically a typo for gerîn.

## 2. gerandin: transitive derived verb

Haig & Öpengin (2018) explicitly identify **gerîn ‘walk, stroll’ → gerandin ‘lead’** as an example of the moderately productive causative suffix **-and**, which derives transitive verbs from intransitive present stems.

Faik Şahin's *Ferhenga Kurdî–Tirkî* labels **gerandin** transitive and gives its origin as **ger + -andin**. It records these meanings:
1. take someone for a walk / lead someone around;
2. distribute or serve (e.g. coffee/tea);
3. wrap, put around, embrace;
4. guide, direct, manage or administer;
5. deceive or fool (in the reciprocal expression **hevûdu gerandin**).

Its examples include:
- **Min zozana bigerîne.** — “Take me around the summer pastures.” (imperative/subjunctive form; not a past-tense example)
- **Keça malê çay li mêvanan gerand.** — “The girl of the house served tea to the guests.”
- **Destê xwe li stoyê min gerand.** — “She/he put their arm around my neck / embraced me.”
- **Dema ez ji leşkeriyê vegeriyam, dayika min destên xwe li stûyê min gerandin û giriya.** — “When I returned from military service, my mother put her arms around my neck and cried.”
- **Ji dema ku bavê wan miriye, xwişka mezin karê malê digerîne.** — “Since their father died, the elder sister has been running the household.”

Chyet's *Kurmanji Lexicon* independently lists **gerandin gerîn-** as a transitive verb meaning “to turn, rotate; to take for a walk; to produce”. This is direct lexical evidence for the present stem **gerîn-**.

## 3. Past stem and patient agreement

The past stem is **gerand-**, confirmed by the past forms **gerand** and **gerandin** in the Faik Şahin examples and by the Kurdish-Kurdish Qafqaz dictionary, which explicitly gives **di gerand / di gerandin** and **gerandbû**.

The examples are especially useful because they show patient agreement:
- **Keça malê çay ... gerand** — singular patient “tea”, verb **gerand**.
- **Dayika min destên xwe ... gerandin** — plural patient “her arms”, verb **gerandin**.

This aligns with the general Kurmancî transitive-past pattern described by Haig & Öpengin: in past transitive clauses the verb indexes the direct patient, while the agent is oblique. The example with **dayika min** (singular agent) and **destên xwe** (plural patient) is particularly clear evidence that the plural ending is not simply agreement with the agent.

Do not confuse:
- **gerand**: past form agreeing with a singular patient.
- **gerandin**: past form agreeing with a plural patient in the cited example.
- **gerandin** as the infinitive lemma: same spelling, but a different grammatical function in dictionary citation form.

The complete six-person patient-agreement paradigm has not been directly established in the sources inspected. The cited singular and plural examples are sufficient to teach the contrast, but not to invent the remaining forms.

## 4. Morphological analysis and semantic range

The derivation is:
**ger- (present stem of intransitive gerîn) + -and → gerandin (transitive causative)**.

The semantic bridge is “walk/stroll” → “make someone walk / take someone for a walk / lead”. The verb has developed additional conventional lexical senses: distribute/serve, wrap, embrace, manage, and deceive in a reciprocal construction. Those extended senses should be taught as lexical meanings supported by examples, not all mechanically translated as “cause to walk”.

The *Kurdish-English Dictionary* by Chyet records the lemma **gerandin gerîn- v.t.** and its transitive uses; the major *Kurdish Etymological Dictionary* records gerandin as transitive with the same stem and documents its senses with literary/corpus-style citations. These sources support both the causative relationship and lexicalization beyond the most literal causative.

## 5. Crucial warning: -andin is not always causative

Haig & Öpengin explain that **-and** is only moderately productive as a causative suffix in Standard Kurmancî, deriving transitive verbs from intransitive stems. They also document dialectal citation forms where -and/-andin has been extended as an infinitive-forming pattern without additional causative semantics. In that variety, examples include intransitive forms such as **štexlandin** “to speak” and **mešandin** “to walk”; their inflection is not the regular causative paradigm.

Therefore:
- **gerandin** is a genuine transitive causative/derived verb; its lexical meaning is independently attested.
- Do not infer that every infinitive ending in **-andin** is transitive or causative.
- Do not assign present stem **-în-** and past stem **-and-** automatically to all such forms.
- Check valency, stem pair, and actual usage for each lemma separately.

## 6. Teaching recommendation and confidence

| Claim | Confidence | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| gerîn is intransitive, “walk/stroll/wander” | High | Teach |
| gerîn present stem ger- and 3sg past geriya | High | Teach with gerîn/geriyan variant note |
| gerandin is transitive, derived from ger- + -andin | High | Teach |
| gerandin present stem gerîn- | High | Teach |
| gerandin past stem gerand- | High | Teach |
| gerand / gerandin contrast with singular/plural patient | High | Teach with sourced examples |
| all meanings of gerandin are transparent causatives | False | Teach lexical extensions separately |
| every -andin lemma is causative | False | Explicitly warn against this inference |
| complete past paradigm for all patient persons | Not established here | Do not invent |
