# Nivistin / nivandin: factitive derivation, stem evidence, and lexical homonymy

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only audit of the Standard Kurmanji pair *nivistin / nivandin*. This note distinguishes the intransitive sleep verb, the transitive factitive “put to sleep”, and a separate lexically attested verb *nivîn*.

## Executive finding

Haig and Öpengin explicitly document:

- **nivistin** — “to sleep”; intransitive base.
- **nivandin** — “to put to sleep”; transitive factitive.

This is a direct Standard Kurmanji example of **-and** deriving a transitive verb from an intransitive present stem. The beginner grammar independently gives **nivistin → niv-**, and illustrates the intransitive verb with **dinivim** (“I sleep”) and **Tu di saet çendan de nivistî?** (“What time did you sleep?”).

The derived verb is directly attested in Chyet's dictionary of verbs as **nivandin**, transitive, “to put to sleep”, with the example **Gulçinê kurê xu nivand** (“Gulçin put her son to sleep”). Glosbe's cited dictionary example also records **min nivand** (“I put her to sleep”) and the imperative **binvîne**.

A separate Chyet lexicon entry lists **nivîn** as an intransitive verb meaning “to lie down, sleep”, in addition to a homonymous noun **nivîn** “bed”. This independent lemma must not be confused with the present stem **nivîn-** of the factitive *nivandin*. The forms look alike, but their grammatical status and lexical entries differ.

## 1. Base verb: nivistin

Haig and Öpengin explicitly use **nivistin** “sleep” as the intransitive base of the factitive pair.

The beginner grammar lists:
- infinitive: **nivistin**
- present stem: **niv-**
- first-person singular present: **dinivim**
- third-person singular present: **dinive**
- past stem: **nivist-**
- example: **Tu di saet çendan de nivistî?** (“What time did you sleep?”)

The same grammar gives intransitive present-perfect forms such as **Ez duh şev zû nivistime** (“I fell asleep early last night”) and **Ew duh şev dereng nivistiye** (“He/she slept late last night”). These examples confirm the intransitive use and should not be mistaken for forms of *nivandin*.

## 2. Derived verb: nivandin

Haig and Öpengin explicitly pair:
> **nivistin** “sleep” → **nivandin** “put to sleep”.

Chyet's *Dictionary of Kurdish Verbs* records:
- **Nivandin** — transitive verb; “to put to sleep”.
- Example: **Gulçinê kurê xu nivand.**

Glosbe, citing a Kurmanji dictionary, independently records:
- **nivandin**
- **binvîne** (imperative)
- **min nivand** (“I put her to sleep”).

These examples establish transitivity and the past form **nivand**. They do not supply a full conjugation paradigm.

## 3. Stems: evidence and confidence

| Verb | Past stem | Present stem | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **nivistin** | **nivist-** | **niv-** | Directly listed in the beginner grammar |
| **nivandin** | **nivand-** | **nivîn-** | Past **nivand-** directly attested; present **nivîn-** follows the documented -andin factitive stem pattern and is consistent with **binvîne**, but a complete paradigm was not located |

Haig and Öpengin describe **-and** factitives as deriving from the intransitive present stem and give *nivistin → nivandin* as an example. Thackston's general description of **-andin** factitives states that the derived present stem is formed by replacing the infinitive ending **-andin** with **-în-**. Applying that documented rule to *nivandin* yields **nivîn-**. The imperative **binvîne** is consistent with this stem analysis.

This is a rule-based morphological analysis, not a claim that a full present-tense table for *nivandin* has been independently attested. Keep that distinction visible in any future learner-facing treatment.

## 4. Lexical homonymy: nivîn

Chyet's *Kurmanji Lexicon* distinguishes:
- **nivîn¹** — noun, usually plural, “bed”.
- **nivîn² -niv- v.i.** — “to lie down, sleep”.

This separate intransitive lemma is relevant because **nivîn-** is also the derived present stem expected for *nivandin*. Do not conflate:
1. **nivîn** as an independent intransitive infinitive/lexeme;
2. **nivîn-** as the present stem of transitive **nivandin**;
3. **nivîn** as the noun “bed”.

The dictionary entry also lists **nivistin -niv- v.i.** “to sleep”, confirming that *nivistin* and *nivîn* are separately recorded intransitive lexical items. Haig and Öpengin's explicit factitive example, however, is specifically **nivistin → nivandin**.

## 5. Transitivity and past agreement

The base *nivistin* is intransitive. The derived *nivandin* is transitive: its meaning introduces an agent who causes another person to sleep.

The attested example **Gulçinê kurê xu nivand** shows an oblique agent and a third-person singular patient with the past verb form **nivand**. The example **min nivand** likewise records a first-person oblique agent with a third-person singular object.

Haig's general Kurmanji agreement description states that transitive past verbs index the object, not the agent. These individual examples are consistent with that pattern. They do not establish all person/number object forms for *nivandin*. Do not extrapolate a full past agreement table from them.

## 6. Teaching-safe conclusion

> **nivistin** بە واتای خەوتنە؛ کردارێکی ناتێپەڕە، ڕەگی ئێستای **niv-** و ڕەگی ڕابردووی **nivist-** ـە. **nivandin** بە واتای خەواندنە؛ کردارێکی تێپەڕ و هۆکارسازییە. فۆڕمی ڕابردووی **nivand** بە نموونەی بەکارهاتوو پشتڕاست کراوەتەوە؛ ڕەگی ئێستای **nivîn-** لە یاسای گشتیی دروستکردنی کردارەکانی **-andin** و فۆڕمی فەرمانی **binvîne** پشتگیری دەکرێت، بەڵام خشتەی تەواوی لێکدانەوەی کرداری بەردەست نییە. هەروەها **nivîn** کردارێکی ناتێپەڕی سەربەخۆیە بە واتای خەوتن/پاڵکەوتن و ناوی «جێخەو»یشە؛ ئەم دوو بەکارهێنانە لە ڕەگی **nivîn-** ی کردارەکەی **nivandin** جیا بکرێنەوە.
