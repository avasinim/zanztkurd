## 2. Key reconciliation decisions

### 2.1 The `r/rr` issue is not a settled universal spelling rule

The earlier integrated report states that the Hawar inventory remains 31 letters, word-initial `r` is trilled phonetically, `roj` remains written with one `r`, and `rr` is sporadically used to mark a trill in some contexts.

The later Lesson 26 audit cautions that the current seed's claim that Standard Kurmanji spelling distinguishes `r` and `rr` “in some words” is not established by the principal Standard Kurmanjî orthographic description reviewed there, and recommends removing that general spelling claim or recasting it as a specifically sourced phonetic observation.

**Reconciled teaching position pending source-level resolution:**

- Do not present `rr` as an additional alphabet letter.
- Do not teach a universal or productive `r` versus `rr` spelling contrast.
- Preserve the ordinary spelling `roj`; do not change it to `rroj`.
- If the course later mentions sporadic use of `rr` in particular orthographic traditions or contexts, it must cite the exact source passage, describe the scope precisely, and distinguish spelling convention from phonetic realization.
- Until that source-level verification and native-editor review are complete, omit the broad `r/rr` spelling statement from learner-facing material.

This resolves the instructional risk without pretending the two earlier reports made identical claims. The exact scope of any sporadic non-initial `rr` notation remains a source-check item, not a universal rule.

### 2.2 Lesson 23: definiteness, demonstratives and case

The later Lesson 23 audit provides the more precise current account:

- Standard Kurmanjî has no dedicated definite article suffix equivalent to Sorani `-eke/-ekan`; bare nouns receive context-dependent definite, generic or other readings.
- Singular indefiniteness commonly uses `-ek`.
- Case and ezafe must be kept distinct even where surface strings overlap.
- `ev/ew` are demonstratives, not articles; oblique forms must be shown in their syntactic context.
- Plural indefinite `-in` must not be generalized beyond the regional scope supported by the source.

Use the Lesson 23 report as the current detailed reference for this lesson, while retaining the earlier integrated report as historical context.

### 2.3 Lesson 24: possession, ezafe and reflexive `xwe`

The later Lesson 24 audit sharpens the distinction between possessor form and ezafe agreement:

- Possessors in ezafe constructions use oblique personal-pronoun forms (`min, te, wî, wê, me, we, wan`).
- Ezafe agrees with the possessed/head noun, not with the possessor.
- Reflexive `xwe` is needed in relevant same-clause subject-coreference contexts.
- Free ezafe forms (`yê, ya, yên`) have both modifier and anaphoric uses and should not be collapsed into ordinary possessive pronouns.

These details should govern any later approved harmonization of Lessons 11, 21, 23, 24 and 25.

### 2.4 Lesson 25: Direct/Oblique alignment

The later Lesson 25 audit should be used for the current instructional model:

- Direct and Oblique are grammatical cases, not semantic synonyms for subject/object.
- Intransitive subjects and present-transitive subjects are Direct in the canonical pattern.
- Present-transitive objects are Oblique.
- In canonical past-transitive clauses, the agent is Oblique and the patient/object Direct; the Oblique agent remains the grammatical subject.
- Oblique also has functions beyond present-transitive objects, including possessors, adpositional complements and some goals/recipients.
- Masculine singular Oblique marking and plural forms require dialect/phonological qualification.
- Ezafe constructions affect the visible external case marking of their head.

The earlier integrated report's broad alignment framework is compatible with this, but the Lesson 25 audit supplies the more detailed qualifications.

## 5. Source hierarchy and validation rule

Use Haig (2018) as a major descriptive source for Standard Kurmanjî, cross-check with Thackston and other relevant published work, and use dictionaries for lexical lookup only. Use Council of Europe CEFR materials for assessment design, not for Kurmanji grammatical claims.

For every disputed form or example, record:
1. exact source and page/section;
2. exact form and gloss;
3. whether it is directly attested, adapted or instructor-created;
4. the named variety/register;
5. native-speaker review status.

Do not turn an attested regional form into a universal Standard Kurmanjî rule. Do not label a dialect form wrong merely because it differs from the selected teaching baseline.
