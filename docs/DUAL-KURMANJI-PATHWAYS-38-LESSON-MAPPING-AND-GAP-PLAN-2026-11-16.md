# Dual Kurmanji Pathways — Mapping of Existing 38 Lessons and Curriculum Gap Plan

**Date:** 2026-11-16  
**Repository:** avasinim/zanztkurd  
**Branch:** fix/kurmanji-ci-current-migration  
**Status:** Proposal-only curriculum map; not an approval to rewrite lessons.

## 1. Purpose and limits

This document maps the existing Northern Kurmanji inventory (Course 1: 26 lessons; Course 2: 12 lessons) into two distinct learning pathways:

- **Pathway A:** Sorani-mediated Kurmanji, with Sorani explanations and Kurmanji target language in Hawar Latin.
- **Pathway B:** independently authored Kurmanji-medium instruction in Hawar Latin.

The 38 lessons are an existing content inventory, not a complete or certified proficiency curriculum. The map preserves their current topics and identifies where they should be reused, split, expanded, moved or newly authored. It does not edit lesson bodies or assert that a topic is already taught completely.

## 2. Recommended learner architecture

### Pathway A

Target learner: a Sorani reader with little or no Kurmanji.

Recommended sequence:
1. Orientation and script bridge.
2. Pronunciation and basic literacy.
3. Everyday vocabulary and interaction.
4. Core sentence patterns.
5. Noun phrase, pronouns and case.
6. Verb systems and negation.
7. Extended communication and discourse.
8. Review, assessment and independent production.

Keep explanations in Sorani, but make Kurmanji input and learner output progressively dominant.

### Pathway B

**Recommendation:** design a complete staged route from absolute beginner to advanced Kurmanji literacy, with an optional bridge for native/proficient speakers who enter at a later stage.

This resolves the two likely audiences without turning the whole course into an advanced grammar reference:
- Beginner route: controlled Kurmanji instructions, simple definitions and short texts.
- Heritage/native bridge: diagnostic placement and accelerated entry to orthography, grammar analysis and writing.
- Advanced route: academic literacy, stylistics, dialect awareness, editing and linguistic analysis.

All learner-facing material remains in Kurmanji. The Kurmanji-medium course must be authored independently, not translated line by line from Pathway A.

## 5. Shared content versus independent authorship

The two pathways should share an approved linguistic fact base, not identical lesson scripts.

| Shared layer | Pathway A expression | Pathway B expression |
|---|---|---|
| Verified Kurmanji forms and paradigms | Kurmanji examples with Sorani explanations | Kurmanji examples with Kurmanji definitions |
| Grammar specification | Sorani terminology plus Kurmanji target forms | Reviewed Kurmanji metalanguage and paraphrases |
| Example bank | Same source/provenance IDs where appropriate | Same source/provenance IDs where appropriate |
| Exercises | Sorani instructions initially, Kurmanji response targets | Kurmanji instructions and Kurmanji responses |
| Feedback | Sorani explanation of Kurmanji error | Kurmanji explanation of Kurmanji error |
| Assessment blueprint | Shared skill construct, pathway-specific task wording | Shared skill construct, pathway-specific task wording |
| Reading texts | Gradually lengthened Kurmanji texts | Independently levelled Kurmanji texts |
| Dialect notes | Sorani explanation of variation | Kurmanji explanation of variation |

Pathway B may reuse verified Kurmanji examples and factual paradigms, but its explanatory prose, exercise prompts, feedback and sequence must be newly authored in Kurmanji.

## 6. Major curriculum gaps not covered by the current 38 lessons

The following areas are absent, underrepresented or too briefly treated to support a complete staged course. They should become new units or carefully designed expansions, not be forced into existing lesson titles.

### Literacy and pronunciation
- Syllable structure, stress and connected speech.
- Listening discrimination and pronunciation practice, once the spoken reference variety is approved.
- Punctuation, capitalization, apostrophes, compounds, clitics and loanword spelling.
- Keyboard use, Unicode, search and proofreading.

### Grammar and communication
- Imperatives and requests.
- Future, subjunctive and modality in more than one introductory comparison.
- Past intransitive versus past transitive paradigms with full person/number contrasts.
- Adpositions, postpositions, spatial relations and case selection.
- Reflexive, reciprocal and interrogative pronouns in broader contexts.
- Demonstratives and determiners across case and number.
- Relative clauses, complement clauses, coordination and subordination.
- Valency, ditransitive patterns, goals and recipients.
- Adverbs, prepositions/postpositions, particles and discourse markers.
- Existential/copular clauses and their negation.
- Reported speech and narrative sequencing.

### Lexicon, pragmatics and discourse
- High-frequency verbs and collocations.
- Food, shopping, health-service navigation, travel, education, work and public life.
- Register, politeness, address forms, requests, refusals and turn-taking.
- Idioms, proverbs and figurative language with source and register labels.
- Paragraph cohesion, reference, topic continuity and information structure.

### Reading and writing
- Graded dialogues, notices, messages, forms, descriptions and narratives.
- Summaries, explanations, opinions and arguments.
- Editing, proofreading and stylistic revision.
- Authentic texts selected for rights, dialect scope and difficulty.

### Advanced and academic literacy
- Morphological analysis and glossing.
- Dialect comparison and standardization debates.
- Orthographic editing and terminology development.
- Critical reading of linguistic descriptions.
- Structured academic exposition and evidence-based argument.

## 7. Proposed staging and placement

| Stage | Pathway A (Sorani-mediated) | Pathway B (Kurmanji-medium) | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — Orientation | Script, sounds, transfer awareness | Literacy bridge and classroom Kurmanji | Read/write basic Hawar forms; follow simple instructions |
| 1 — Foundation | Everyday words, greetings, descriptions | Simple Kurmanji definitions, dialogues and texts | Introduce self, ask/answer basic questions, read short texts |
| 2 — Core | Noun phrases, pronouns, present/past, questions, negation | Structured grammar and coherent communication | Produce connected everyday speech/writing and understand short passages |
| 3 — Independent | Extended texts, narrative, discourse, variation | Advanced grammar, writing, editing and analysis | Summarise, narrate, explain and revise longer texts |
| 4 — Specialist option | Optional contrastive/linguistic studies | Academic literacy and advanced linguistic analysis | Analyse structures, compare varieties and write evidence-based explanations |

These stages are project design levels, not certified CEFR levels. CEFR mapping should be a separate validation project.

## 8. Sequencing and dependency rules

1. Introduce Hawar literacy before asking learners to interpret long written examples.
2. Introduce phonological concepts only as far as needed; keep advanced IPA and phonetics available as optional depth.
3. Teach pronouns before requiring complex direct/oblique case production, then revisit pronouns with case and alignment.
4. Teach noun gender, number, indefiniteness and ezafe as related but distinguishable systems.
5. Teach present verbs before past transitive alignment; do not collapse tense, aspect and case into one beginner rule.
6. Teach negation alongside the relevant verb/clause types rather than as one universal prefix rule.
7. Teach neutral word order before focus/fronting, goals/recipients and complex clauses.
8. Recycle vocabulary across reading, listening, speaking and writing tasks.
9. Every advanced lesson must identify prerequisite forms and return to earlier paradigms.
10. Keep a shared controlled glossary and example bank, but maintain separate pathway lesson IDs and authored texts.

## 9. Assessment map

Each stage should assess more than recognition:
- Orthography: identify, type, read and correct forms.
- Vocabulary: interpret and use words in context.
- Grammar: recognize, complete, construct and explain forms.
- Comprehension: understand dialogues and graded texts.
- Interaction: ask, answer, clarify, request and respond appropriately.
- Writing: produce and revise sentences, paragraphs and longer texts.
- Pragmatics: select suitable forms for context, register and relationship.

Pathway A instructions may begin in Sorani, but the learner must increasingly demonstrate Kurmanji comprehension and production. Pathway B instructions and feedback remain in Kurmanji at every stage. Provide separate scoring rubrics and placement routes for beginner and proficient-entry learners.
