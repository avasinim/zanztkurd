## Verified teaching claims (limited)
1. Lesson 03 gender/case: the source confirms masculine/feminine grammatical gender in Kurmanji, with gender expressed in singular oblique case markers and ezafe/linkers; distinctions are neutralized in plural. Haig & Öpengin 2015, sections 2.1.1–2.1.2.
2. Lesson 04 -ek/case: Haig 2018 Table 7 confirms `gund`, `gund-î`, `gund-ek`, `gund-ek-î`, `jin`, `jin-ê`, `jin-ek`, `jin-ek-ê`; also explains oblique marking environments and suppression where ezafe follows. Section 2.2.1, Table 7.
3. Lesson 05 ezafe: Haig 2018 §2.3.1 examples (4)–(5) confirm `bajar-ek-î mezin` ‘a big town’ and `mal-a me` ‘our house’; the ezafe form depends on gender/number of its head. Note: the presence of an ezafe suppresses the oblique marking on that noun, described in §2.3.1.
4. Lesson 06 reflexive possessive: Haig 2018 §2.3.1 example (9) confirms `Ez li mal-a xwe me` and rejects `mal-a min` in the same-clause coreferential possessive reading.
5. Lesson 07 pronouns: Haig 2018 Table 9 verifies the direct/oblique paradigm `ez/min`, `tu/te`, `ew/wî-wê`, `em/me`, `hûn/we`, `ew/wan`. §2.2.3 states invariant reflexive `xwe` is used for all persons and numbers when coreference with same-clause subject is intended.
6. Lessons 08–10 verb patterns/negation: Haig 2018 Table 14 supports the seed's cited examples `ez dibêjim`, `ez hatim`, `min xwar`, `ez dihâtim`, `min dixwar`. §2.6.1 states present-based forms use `di-`, `bi-`, `na-` or `ne-` according to form; notes `ni-` for `zanîn` and `karîn`; past simple/progressive negation is `ne-`, with a western dialect exception for progressive negation using `na-`.
7. Lesson 11 word order: Haig 2018 §2.7.1 presents SOV as the unmarked order while noting flexibility for information structure and variations in goal/recipient placement.
8. Lesson 09 future: the cited Haig future construction is visible in §2.6.4. The exact source form and the seed's current form are recorded as a separate correction gate; do not alter it from this note alone.
