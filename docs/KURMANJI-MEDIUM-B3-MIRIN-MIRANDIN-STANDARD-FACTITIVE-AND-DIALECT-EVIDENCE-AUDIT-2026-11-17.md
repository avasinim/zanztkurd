# Mirin / mirandin: Standard Kurmanji factitive and limits of dialect evidence

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Supplementary source audit of the Standard Kurmanji pair *mirin / mirandin*, with a strict distinction between documented standard morphology and dialect claims not established by the consulted sources.

## Executive finding

The Standard Kurmanji factitive pair is explicitly documented by Thackston:

- **mirin** — “to die”; present stem **mir-**.
- **mirandin** — “to make die, kill”; present stem **mirîn-**.

This is a direct example of the factitive pattern in which **-andin** derives a transitive verb from an intransitive present stem. Haig's overview likewise describes **-and** as a moderately productive suffix for deriving transitive verbs from intransitive present stems, with examples such as *gerîn → gerandin* and *nivistin → nivandin*.

The consulted sources do **not** provide a specific alternative dialectal infinitive or a dialect-specific conjugation for *mirin / mirandin*. Therefore, no regional variant should be asserted here. The responsible conclusion is “no dialect contrast established by these sources”, not “no dialect variation exists”.

## 1. Direct Standard Kurmanji evidence

Thackston, §28, lists the factitive formation in a table:

| Base infinitive | Base present stem | Factitive infinitive | Factitive present stem | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **mirin** | **mir-** | **mirandin** | **mirîn-** | “to make die, kill” |

The same section states that the factitive infinitive is formed from the base present stem plus **-andin**, and that the present stem of such factitives ends in **-în-**.

This is direct evidence for the base/factitive relationship and both present stems. It is stronger than inferring the pair solely from the surface resemblance of the infinitives.

## 2. Morphological interpretation and limits

Haig's overview describes **-and** as the only moderately productive derivational process for creating new verbs in Kurmanji, deriving transitive verbs from intransitive present stems. The examples include:

- **gerîn** “walk, stroll” → **gerandin** “lead”
- **nivistin** “sleep” → **nivandin** “put to sleep”

This independently supports the general factitive analysis of *mirin → mirandin*. The verb-specific evidence for *mirin / mirandin*, however, is the explicit Thackston table above.

## 3. What the available dialect sources do not establish

The reviewed Haig and Öpengin materials discuss substantial regional variation in Kurmanji, including lexical and morphosyntactic differences among Southeastern, Southern, and Western varieties. They do not, in the passages located for this audit, give an alternative dialect form or a distinct paradigm for *mirin / mirandin*.

Accordingly:
- Safe: the Standard Kurmanji pair and stems listed above.
- Not established: a Mardin, Şemzînan, Behdînî, or Western Kurmanji alternative infinitive for this specific pair.
- Not established: dialect-specific present or past stems for *mirandin*.
- Not safe: extrapolating a local variant from other verbs' dialectal **-andin** patterns.

A dialect form must be supported by a source that actually records this verb in that variety.

## 4. Past tense and agreement

The base verb *mirin* is intransitive. Haig's general description of Kurmanji agreement states that intransitive subjects control agreement in all tenses, while transitive objects control agreement in past tenses. This is a general grammatical rule, not a full *mirandin* paradigm.

The sources reviewed here do not provide a full verb-specific past agreement table for *mirandin*. Do not generate all person/number forms from the single past stem without attested examples or a validated paradigm. The previous project audit's individual examples remain separate evidence and should not be expanded beyond their scope.
