# Dual Kurmanji Curriculum — Topic-by-Topic Evidence and Source Map

**Date:** 2026-11-16  
**Repository:** avasinim/zanztkurd  
**Branch:** fix/kurmanji-ci-current-migration  
**Status:** Research and editorial planning artifact. It is not a substitute for expert review and does not authorize lesson rewrites.

## 1. Purpose

This source map connects the proposed dual-pathway syllabus to scholarly and descriptive resources useful for checking its claims. It identifies source families relevant to each topic, what each source can and cannot establish, where lessons require dialect-specific evidence, which claims need a second independent check, and which examples require direct provenance.

The sources are not interchangeable. A grammar sketch, dialect database, theoretical syntax paper, orthography reference and learner dictionary answer different questions. A source's existence does not automatically validate every example or regional generalization in a lesson.

## 2. Core reference library

### S1 — Haig & Öpengin (2018): structure, varieties and status

Geoffrey Haig & Ergin Öpengin. 2018. “Kurmanjî Kurdish in Turkey: Structure, varieties, and status.” In Christiane Bulut (ed.), *Linguistic Minorities in Turkey and Turkic-speaking Minorities of the Peripheries*, pp. 157–230. Wiesbaden: Harrassowitz.

Bibliographic record: https://glottolog.org/resource/reference/id/572205  
Accessible author-hosted PDF: https://www.uni-bamberg.de/fileadmin/aspra/06_Team/Publications_and_CV/HaigOepengin2018_Kurmanji_Kurdish_.pdf

**Best use:** broad structural orientation; phonological and nominal morphology comparisons; regional variation and sociolinguistic context.

**Limit:** the chapter's geographical and dialect coverage must be respected. It is not a universal paradigm book for every Kurmanji-speaking community. Extract each example with the source's own variety label.

### S2 — Haig (2018): Northern Kurdish grammar sketch

Geoffrey Haig. 2018. “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî).” In *The Languages and Linguistics of Western Asia*, pp. 106–158. De Gruyter Mouton. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110421682-004

**Best use:** structural grammar reference for phonology, morphology and syntax; a secondary cross-check alongside S1 and targeted studies.

**Limit:** do not cite the chapter as evidence for a specific local pronunciation or a particular learner-oriented spelling convention without checking the relevant discussion.

### S3 — Öpengin & Haig (2014): regional variation

Ergin Öpengin & Geoffrey Haig. 2014. “Regional variation in Kurmanji: A preliminary classification of dialects.” *Kurdish Studies* 2(2), 143–176. DOI: https://doi.org/10.67145/ks.v2i2.62

Publisher record: https://kurdishstudies.net/menu-script/index.php/KS/article/view/62  
Bamberg repository record: https://fis.uni-bamberg.de/entities/publication/3228ade5-ef47-4fdc-93df-3e9a1b0ad4ed

**Best use:** dialect-aware wording, lexical/phonological/morphosyntactic comparison, regional variant metadata.

**Limit:** the authors describe their classification as preliminary and draw on a defined set of localities. Do not turn it into a complete map of every Kurmanji variety or a hierarchy of correctness.

### S4 — Baker & Atlamaz (2014): case, agreement and split alignment

Mark Baker & Ümit Atlamaz. 2014. “On the Relationship of Case to Agreement in Split-Ergative Kurmanji and Beyond.”

Author-hosted paper: https://sites.rutgers.edu/mark-baker/wp-content/uploads/sites/199/2019/07/kurmanji-split-erg-distributed-July-2014.pdf

**Best use:** advanced analysis of the relation between case marking, verbal agreement and tense-based split alignment; useful for preventing simplistic subject/object explanations.

**Limit:** the paper's central empirical variety is Adıyaman Kurmanji. Treat its detailed claims as variety-scoped; compare with broader descriptions and dialect evidence before turning them into a general beginner rule.

### S5 — University of Manchester: The Dialects of Kurdish database

Project: https://kurdish.humanities.manchester.ac.uk/about-the-project/dialects-of-kurdish-project/  
Database: https://kurdish.humanities.manchester.ac.uk/database-of-kurdish-dialects/  
Database glosses: https://kurdish.humanities.manchester.ac.uk/database-glosses/

**Best use:** compare attested phrases and structures by location and feature; inspect sound samples, transcriptions and morphological/syntactic tags; identify forms that vary across locations.

**Limit:** check the sample's locality, consultant and transcription system. A database token is not automatically a pan-Kurmanji norm or a ready-made textbook example.

### S6 — Technical romanization references

BGN/PCGN 2007 Kurdish romanization (revised presentation 2022): use as a technical cross-reference for transliteration and names, not as the sole authority for Kurmanji pedagogy or grammar.

**Best use:** compare technical transliteration decisions where relevant.

**Limit:** a romanization standard designed for geographic names or information exchange is not necessarily the same as a community's preferred literary orthography. The project should define its own approved Hawar editorial policy after specialist review.

## 3. Topic-by-topic evidence matrix

| Curriculum topic | Primary evidence | Cross-check / practical evidence | Claims that require special caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hawar alphabet and orthography | Approved project orthography guide; S1/S2 for linguistic context | Specialist-reviewed contemporary Kurmanji publications and digital text | Do not infer phoneme count from letter count; do not add digraphs as alphabet letters without evidence |
| Vowels and vowel sounds | S1 and S2 | S5 sound samples and location-labelled forms | Separate vowel graphemes from phonetic realizations; pronunciation varies |
| Consonants and sound contrasts | S1 and S2 | S5 phonetic samples | IPA must represent a stated variety and transcription convention |
| Syllable, stress and connected speech | S1/S2 where described | S5 recorded speech; targeted phonetic research needed | Do not invent universal stress rules from a few words |
| Spelling versus pronunciation | Orthography policy + S1/S2 | S3 and S5 for variation | Keep orthographic forms distinct from phonetic transcription |
| Greetings, phrases and common vocabulary | No single grammar source is sufficient | S5, reviewed contemporary texts, learner corpus/dictionary with provenance | Label register, locality, frequency and pragmatic context |
| Personal pronouns | S1/S2 | S5 pronoun and case-tagged data | Direct/oblique forms must be taught in syntactic context |
| Demonstratives | S1/S2 | S5 determiner/pronoun examples | Check gender, number, case, deixis and regional variants |
| Numbers and quantification | S1/S2 where relevant | S5; reviewed practical texts and corpus examples | Check counting, age, price, dates and quantifier constructions separately |
| Gender and number | S1/S2 | S5 nominal morphology and determiner examples | Avoid overgeneralizing noun gender or plural formation |
| Indefiniteness and determiners | S1/S2 | S5 definiteness/determiner tags | Bare nouns and suffixes may have context-sensitive interpretation |
| Ezafe | S1/S2 | S5 ezafe-tagged phrases; S3 for variation | Distinguish head-noun agreement, linking ezafe and independent/demonstrative ezafe |
| Adjectives and agreement | S1/S2 | S5 adjective/ezafe examples | Check gender, number, definiteness and case interactions |
| Possession | S1/S2 | S5 possessive and oblique possessor examples | Separate possessive suffixes/pronouns, oblique possessors and ezafe relations |
| Present-tense verbs | S1/S2 | S5 present indicative and agreement tags | Verify verb class, person/number agreement and regional forms |
| Past intransitive | S1/S2 | S5 past intransitive samples | Keep past intransitive and past transitive patterns distinct |
| Past transitive and split alignment | S1/S2 + S4 | S5 past-transitive samples across localities | S4 is Adıyaman-focused; don't universalize one analysis or confuse direct/oblique with subject/object |
| Future and modality | S1/S2 | S5 modality examples; targeted grammar references | Check dialect and register; separate tense, aspect and modality |
| Negation | S1/S2 | S5 negative clause examples | Do not teach one negative marker as universal across all clause and verb types |
| Questions and interrogatives | S1/S2 | S5 question-word and interrogative examples | Distinguish lexical question words and case forms; verify natural discourse use |
| Word order | S1/S2 | S5 clause examples; discourse corpus needed | SOV is a useful baseline, not a rigid rule; track focus, topic, goals and recipients |
| Adpositions/postpositions and spatial relations | S1/S2 | S5 spatial/directional examples | Check case selection and animate/inanimate contrasts |
| Clause linking and subordination | S1/S2 | S5 complement, relative and subordinate clause examples | Build a separate inventory of conjunctions, complementizers and clause types |
| Pragmatics and register | S1 sociolinguistic context; S3 variation | Contemporary dialogues reviewed by speakers from target communities | Grammar sources alone cannot establish politeness, naturalness or frequency |
| Reading and writing genres | No single linguistic grammar source is enough | Rights-cleared contemporary texts and learner-piloted materials | Record author, date, variety, register, rights and difficulty |
| Academic terminology and analysis | S1/S2/S4 as reference metalanguage | Specialist-reviewed Kurmanji terminology glossary | Do not translate technical terms literally from Sorani/English without validation |

## 4. Required evidence record for every lesson claim

Every substantive linguistic claim should have a claim record:

- **Claim ID:** stable identifier, for example C1-L11-CLAIM-03.
- **Exact claim:** the sentence as it appears in the lesson.
- **Scope:** written standard, named regional variety, register or context.
- **Source:** full bibliographic reference and page/section/table.
- **Evidence excerpt/data:** short excerpt or example with source location.
- **Second check:** independent source or specialist review.
- **Counterexample/variation:** whether a documented variant exists.
- **Teaching simplification:** what has been simplified for the learner.
- **Reviewer:** Kurmanji linguist/editor and date.
- **Status:** supported / supported-with-scope / revise / unresolved.

A general citation at the bottom of a lesson is not enough to support a particular inflected example.

## 5. Example provenance and validation

Each example must be one of:

1. **Attested:** transcribed exactly from a traceable source, with locality and context.
2. **Adapted:** derived from an attested source, with changes documented.
3. **Instructor-created:** authored for teaching and reviewed by a proficient Kurmanji editor.

For all three types, check spelling and diacritics, morphological segmentation, naturalness and register, agreement/case/verb form, dialect/locality, translation or gloss accuracy, and consistency with earlier and later lessons.

Never label instructor-created examples as quotations or corpus attestations.

## 6. Priority evidence work by stage

### Stage 0 / B0: alphabet and literacy
- Freeze the approved Hawar orthography and Unicode test set.
- Verify every letter, diacritic and sample word.
- Keep phoneme/phone/letter distinctions explicit.
- Do not finalize audio until the spoken reference variety is approved.

### Stage 1 / B1: communication and vocabulary
- Build a reviewed phrase bank from more than one source type.
- Record locality, register and communicative function.
- Pilot greetings, requests and time expressions with speakers from the intended target community.
- Avoid translating Sorani phrases mechanically into Kurmanji.

### Stage 2 / B2: core grammar
- Create full paradigms for pronouns, demonstratives, nouns, ezafe, possession and present verbs.
- Validate every cell in a paradigm, not just one illustrative form.
- Record exceptions and dialect variants beside the relevant rule.
- Ensure examples cover affirmative, negative and interrogative contexts.

### Stage 3 / B3: past, modality and syntax
- Build separate, fully reviewed past-intransitive and past-transitive paradigms.
- Compare S1/S2, S4 and location-labelled S5 data.
- Label the empirical scope of any split-alignment claim.
- Add goals/recipients, spatial expressions and clause-linking examples.

### Stage 4 / B4: literacy and variation
- Select rights-cleared graded texts with known authorship and variety.
- Add editing tasks with explainable correction criteria.
- Use S3/S5 to teach variation as documented variation, not as a list of mistakes.
- Review genre, register and cohesion with proficient speakers.

### Stage 5 / B5: specialist study
- Teach morphology and glossing using consistent abbreviations.
- Separate descriptive evidence from theoretical analysis.
- Require citations and locality metadata in dialect comparison.
- Have all Kurmanji metalanguage reviewed by specialists and fluent editors.

## 7. Source gaps and commissioning priorities

The current core library is strong for structural orientation, variation and some advanced morphosyntax. It is not sufficient by itself for every pedagogical or lexical task.

Priority additions:
1. A specialist-approved contemporary Hawar orthography guide with Unicode conventions.
2. A reliable Kurmanji dictionary or lexicon with regional labels and usage examples.
3. A reviewed Kurmanji verb reference with full paradigms and source notes.
4. A contemporary corpus of written Kurmanji with licensing and metadata.
5. A spoken corpus/audio bank with named localities, speaker consent and transcription conventions.
6. A pragmatic/discourse resource covering greetings, politeness, requests, turn-taking and register.
7. A specialist-reviewed Kurmanji grammatical terminology list for Pathway B.
8. A Sorani editorial glossary and contrastive policy for Pathway A.

Do not fill these gaps by inventing “standard” forms. Mark unresolved items as HOLD until evidence is found or a specialist explicitly approves a teaching convention.

## 8. Editorial review workflow

For each batch of lessons:
1. Extract all grammatical claims and examples.
2. Assign claim IDs and example IDs.
3. Match each claim to the most relevant source, not merely the most convenient source.
4. Verify the cited page, table, example or database record.
5. Record the source's variety and scope.
6. Compare with at least one independent reference where possible.
7. Send disputed or underdescribed items to a Kurmanji specialist.
8. Obtain Sorani copy-edit for Pathway A.
9. Obtain Kurmanji-only learner-language review for Pathway B.
10. Approve or HOLD each item; preserve a review log.

## 9. Important methodological safeguards

- A grammar sketch is not a dialect atlas.
- A dialect database entry is not a universal standard.
- A theoretical analysis is not automatically a beginner teaching rule.
- A romanization table is not a full literary orthography guide.
- A word list is not evidence of natural pragmatic use.
- One fluent speaker's intuition is valuable but does not settle all regional questions.
- A pedagogical simplification must be identified and revisited at a later stage.
- CEFR-like labels must not be claimed without validated outcome and assessment mapping.
