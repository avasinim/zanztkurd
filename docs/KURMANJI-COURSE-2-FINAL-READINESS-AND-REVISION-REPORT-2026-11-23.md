## 5. High-priority linguistic distinctions that must not be blurred

### A. Case vs ezafe
- `gund-ek-î` and `jin-ek-ê` are analyzed in the reviewed paradigm as indefinite oblique forms.
- In `bajar-ek-î mezin`, the `-î` is the masculine ezafe marker in the adjective construction.
- The surface sequence alone does not identify grammatical function. The full construction and context must be taught.

### B. Direct/oblique case vs grammatical role
Do not teach direct case as always “subject” and oblique case as always “object.” Haig's distribution depends on construction: present transitive subjects and past intransitive subjects are direct, while simple past transitive subjects are oblique. Use complete source examples and explain ergative alignment in scope.

### C. SOVG vs ergative alignment
Haig §2.7.1 describes neutral SOVG order when a goal argument is present; word order is not absolutely fixed, and fronting is pragmatic. The sentence `Wî mirov-î çay anî` is Haig example (32) in §2.7.2, illustrating past transitive ergative alignment. It must not be presented as the source's general SOVG rule.

### D. Tense vs aspect
Simple past and imperfective past are not just two interchangeable past-tense labels. Explain the aspectual contrast with source-checked pairs and avoid deriving a universal rule from one verb.
