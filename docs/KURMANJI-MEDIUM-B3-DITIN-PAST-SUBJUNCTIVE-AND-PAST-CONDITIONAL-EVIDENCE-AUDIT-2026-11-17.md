## 1. Purpose and boundary

This audit isolates two related but distinct forms built on the transitive past stem of `dîtin`:

1. the **past subjunctive** (§22);
2. the **past conditional** (§25.1).

It also records the boundary between these and the **past perfect conditional** (§25.2), and the potentially misleading label **future perfect** (§23). It does not author a lesson, approve a dialect policy, or generalize a paradigm beyond what the cited reference directly prints.

## 2. Primary source and scope

W. M. Thackston, *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, §§19–25.2, printed pp.49–63. The relevant sections are:

- §22, “The Past Subjunctive,” printed pp.57–58;
- §23, “The Future Perfect Tense,” printed p.59;
- §25.1, “The Past Conditional,” printed pp.60–61;
- §25.2, “The Past Perfect Conditionals,” printed pp.62–63.

The source describes a written Kurmanji norm while acknowledging substantial regional variation. These paradigms are therefore evidence for the reference grammar's described norm, not proof that every regional variety uses every form identically.

## 3. Past subjunctive: directly attested transitive paradigm

Thackston §22 states that the past subjunctive is formed like the past perfect, but substitutes the **present subjunctive of `bûn`** for the past tense of `bûn`; the form also takes the subjunctive prefix `bi-` (which may be omitted stylistically) or negative `ne-`. Intransitives remain non-ergative; transitives are ergative.

The source directly prints the following transitive/ergative paradigm for `dîtin`:

| Agent (oblique) | Direct patient | Affirmative verb |
|---|---|---|
| min | — | bidîtibe |
| me | — | bidîtibe |
| te | — | bidîtibe |
| we | — | bidîtibe |
| wî | — | bidîtibe |
| wan | — | bidîtibe |

The table holds the verb form constant because the paradigm is organized around a third-person singular direct patient. Thackston also prints the corresponding negative series with `nedîtibe` for the same six agent forms.

**Interpretation:** the agent is oblique and the patient is direct; the transitive past-subjunctive construction follows the ergative pattern. The repeated verb form is not a six-person agent-agreement paradigm. Do not add agent endings or create a synthetic agreement chart from this table.

### 3.1 Function

Thackston lists four uses:

1. after constructions that take subjunctive complements when the complement is past;
2. in past relative clauses with indefinite antecedents and clauses introduced by indefinite relatives such as “whoever” or “whatever”;
3. after `weke ku` ‘as though’ in past hypothetical contexts and in certain relative clauses;
4. as the normal present-subjunctive form for the verbs `karîn` and `zanîn`.

These functions are not interchangeable with the pluperfect. A lesson must not define the past subjunctive merely as “had seen” or as a tense-only alternative to the past perfect.

## 4. Past conditional: directly attested transitive forms

Thackston §25.1 describes the past conditional as the past stem preceded by `bi-` and followed by person/number endings. In close compound verbs, `bi-` may be omitted. The source explicitly states that intransitive past conditionals are non-ergative, whereas transitive past conditionals are ergative.

For transitive `dîtin`, the source prints an ergative series with the agent `wî` and changing direct-patient agreement:

| Oblique agent | Direct patient value represented by ending | Printed form |
|---|---|---|
| wî | 1SG | bidîtama |
| wî | 2SG | bidîtayî |
| wî | 3SG | bidîta |
| wî | plural | bidîtana |

The printed negative examples include `wî nedîtama` and `wî nedîtana`. The full table in §25.1 provides the other corresponding negative forms.

**Interpretation:** this is a transitive/ergative paradigm, not a nominative-agent conjugation. The endings track the direct patient; `wî` is the oblique agent. The four forms above reproduce the source's table and should not be expanded into unprinted combinations without a separate source.

### 4.1 Functions and constructional limits

Thackston describes the past conditional as a complement after the past perfect of `karîn`, after past forms of `diviya/diva bû`, and in some dialects after past `xwestin`. It is also used for `bûn` in both clauses of a past counterfactual conditional. These are construction-specific uses; the form should not be taught as a universal marker for every “if” clause.

## 5. Keep three categories separate

| Category | Thackston section | Core formation / scope | Teaching caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Past subjunctive | §22 | Past-stem perfect-like form + present subjunctive of `bûn`; `bi-` or negative `ne-` | Subjunctive clause function; transitive forms are ergative |
| Past conditional | §25.1 | `bi-` + past stem + conditional endings | Transitive forms are ergative; intransitives are not |
| Past perfect conditional | §25.2 | Two competing formations based on the past perfect | Thackston says the two variants appear mutually exclusive by dialect; do not merge them into one paradigm |

For the past perfect conditional, §25.2 describes (1) `bi-/ne-` plus the past perfect with `-(y)a`, and (2) `bi-/ne-` plus a form in which `ba-` replaces `bû`. The source says speakers appear to use one variant or the other depending on dialect. This is a dialect-conditioned alternative, not free stylistic variation to be mixed in a single beginner table.

## 6. “Future perfect” is a terminology trap

Thackston §23 forms the so-called future perfect with future markers (`ê/wê`) plus the past subjunctive. However, the author says that effectively only `zanîn` and `karîn` occur in this tense, with modal meanings similar to English “would know” and “would be able,” **not** the ordinary English future perfect “will have known.”

Therefore:

- do not use §23 as evidence for a productive future-perfect paradigm of `dîtin`;
- do not translate the label literally without explaining its modal scope;
- keep it separate from the past perfect and past perfect conditional.

## 8. Editorial and assessment recommendations (not approved lesson text)

1. Teach the **past subjunctive** and **past conditional** as separate mood/construction targets, not as two translations of English “had.”
2. Keep the oblique-agent/direct-patient distinction visible whenever a transitive paradigm is shown.
3. Label the `dîtin` examples as source-attested forms in Thackston's described norm.
4. Do not synthesize unprinted agent–patient combinations or claim the table shows agent agreement.
5. Introduce the two past-perfect conditional variants only with a dialect note and a single coherent reference variety selected by the project owner.
6. Do not introduce §23 “future perfect” as an ordinary future-perfect tense for `dîtin`.
7. Before learner-facing use, obtain review from a qualified Kurmanji native speaker/editor whose variety is identified; check spelling, acceptability, and present-day usage.
