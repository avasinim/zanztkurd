# Course 2 — Lesson 09: Instructional Audit (2026-11-23)

## Scope
Reviewed the current Lesson 09 payload in `docs/kurmanji-content-seed-part-05.sql`, titled **کاتی ئێستا، ڕابردوو و داهاتوو**. This is an instructional audit of the stored lesson, not a full independent re-analysis of every tense/aspect paradigm.

## Current content (verified)
- Present: states that `di-` appears in many present forms, with lexical/special verb exceptions.
- Past: warns that past is not a single uniform pattern and that transitivity and case marking affect clause structure.
- Future: says future meaning can be expressed with verbal constructions and auxiliary material, with use dependent on context and clause type.
- Cites Haig (2018), Table 14: `ez dibêjim` (present), `ez hatim` (simple past intransitive), and `min xwar` (simple past transitive).
- Contrasts simple past `ez hatim` / `min xwar` with imperfective past `ez dihatim` / `min dixwar`.
- Cites Haig (2018), §2.6.4 for future example `ew dē mesel-ē ji te re bēž-e`, identifying `dē` as future marker and the verb as subjunctive.
- Preserves the source's Latin spelling and cautions against inventing a universal paradigm.

## Findings
1. **The lesson combines tense and aspect but does not define the distinction:** simple past vs imperfective past is shown, yet the learner is not told clearly what aspectual contrast the pair illustrates. The title and review questions focus on tense, so the aspect material may feel incidental.
2. **The present marker is over-general only if read carelessly:** wording “many verbs” and the exception warning are appropriately cautious; retain both. The lesson offers no concrete present form beyond the Table 14 example.
3. **Past comparison lacks segmentation and gloss:** `ez hatim` vs `ez dihatim`, and `min xwar` vs `min dixwar`, are useful pairs but no element-by-element explanation shows what `di-` contributes in the imperfective forms.
4. **Future example is not beginner-friendly:** it is a full source example, but no word-by-word gloss or clear breakdown of `dē`, the subjunctive verb form, and the phrase's other constituents is supplied.
5. **Translation may be too compressed:** the Sorani translation “ئەو کێشەکە بۆ تۆ دەڵێت” should be checked against the source's exact context before being used as a standalone general future template.
6. **No controlled exercise:** review questions ask for broad explanations but do not ask learners to compare a simple/imperfective pair, identify the marker, or analyze the future construction. No answer key is supplied.
7. **Terminology:** distinguish tense (زمانی ڕوودان) from aspect (شێوازی ڕوودان/تەواوبوون) consistently in the lesson; do not use “ڕابردووی بەردەوام” as if it were always a direct one-to-one equivalent without explaining the source's aspectual category.

## Conservative recommendation
Keep the stored lesson unchanged pending source verification. In a later approved revision, explicitly introduce tense vs aspect, segment the two past pairs, and add a glossed future example from the cited source. Verify the Sorani translation in context and preserve the exact source transcription. Do not generalize `di-` or `dē` beyond the cited constructions.
