# Course 2 Lesson 11 — Correction Note: Haig Example (32)
Date: 2026-11-23

## Verified source location
The Lesson 11 seed currently introduces `Wî mirov-î çay anî` under its word-order section and attributes it to Haig (2018), §2.7.1.

The exact example is present in Geoffrey Haig (2018), “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî),” example (32), printed page 131, in **§2.7.2 “Alignment and related issues”**, not in §2.7.1 “Word order”.
Source PDF: https://multicast.aspra.uni-bamberg.de/data/pubs/Haig2018_Northern-Kurdish_Kurmanji.pdf
Source lines in extracted PDF: 1905–1937; the example and gloss are at 1929–1937.

## Source analysis
Haig introduces the example after explaining the ergative construction used with past transitive verbs:
- the transitive subject takes the Oblique case;
- the direct object is in the Direct case;
- the verb agrees with the direct object;
- despite the Oblique case on the subject, the order of subject and object remains unchanged.

Example (32):
- `Wî` — demonstrative masculine oblique;
- `mirov-î` — man, oblique masculine;
- `çay` — tea;
- `anî` — bring, past, third-person singular;
- translation: “That man brought tea.”

The example therefore demonstrates **ergative alignment in a past transitive clause**, while also showing the surface sequence subject–object–verb. It is not the best standalone evidence for the general neutral word-order rule, which Haig states separately in §2.7.1 as SOVG (subject–object–verb–goal) and qualifies by construction and dialect.

## Confirmed correction needed in the lesson
The current lesson seed says:
- Haig §2.7.1 supplies this exact example;
- `Wî mirov-î` is the subject, `çay` the object, and `anî` the verb;
- the example is used to illustrate the basic SOV sequence.

The grammatical-role labels and translation align with Haig, but the section citation and teaching purpose are incomplete. If the lesson is revised later, it should:
1. cite Haig §2.7.2, example (32), for this sentence;
2. explicitly note that `Wî mirov-î` is the oblique transitive subject in a past-tense ergative construction, while `çay` is the direct-case object;
3. explain that the example also happens to have subject–object–verb order, but it should not replace the separate §2.7.1 account of neutral SOVG order.

## Separate word-order source
Haig §2.7.1 (printed pp. 130–131) states that pragmatically neutral clauses have SOVG order, with G covering spatial goals, recipients and addressees. It also notes that direct objects may be fronted for pragmatic purposes and that goal placement/marking varies. This is the appropriate source for the general word-order paragraph.
