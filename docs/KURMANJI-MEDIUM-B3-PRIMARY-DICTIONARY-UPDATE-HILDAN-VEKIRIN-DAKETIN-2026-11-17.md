# B3 Kurmanji-Medium: Primary-Source Update — Hildan, Vekirin and Daketin
Date: 2026-11-17
Scope: correction and strengthening of the 2026-11-17 follow-up evidence audit. Documentation only.

## 1. Why this update exists

A new pass located stronger lexical and grammatical evidence than was available to the preceding note. This update supersedes the earlier provisional statements where noted below. It separates (a) dictionary-attested lexical forms and examples, (b) published grammar descriptions, and (c) conjugation tables from educational or collaborative websites. No form is promoted beyond what its source actually establishes.

## 2. Hildan — past stem now corroborated

### Stronger evidence
- Kurdish Academy's “All Verb Forms” table lists **hildidim** (1sg present), **hilda** (singular past), **hildan** (plural past), and past-perfect forms such as **hildabûm**. The site labels the table as a comprehensive set of 80 verbs, but it is an online pedagogical resource, not a critical scholarly edition.
- Michael L. Chyet's *Kurdish-English Dictionary* (Yale University Press, 2003), entry **hil-dan**, gives independent literary/field examples with **hilda** in past clauses, including **Û toximê xwe hilda** (“And he picked up his grain”) and **Xort k’axêza xwe hilda** (“The lad took the paper”). The entry explicitly marks the verb transitive and provides multiple source sigla.
- Thackston's *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, §15, treats **hildan** as a close compound of preverb **hil-** plus **dan**, and gives its present pattern and subjunctive forms. The later sections on past transitive verbs explain the ergative construction.

### Revised assessment
- **hilda** is now supported by both a dedicated conjugation table and independent dictionary-attested clauses. The past stem can be recorded as **hild- / hilda** only with a note that the dictionary's lexical examples attest the surface past form **hilda**; the conjugation table's plural past is **hildan**.
- Do not use the earlier statement “past stem not verified”; it is superseded by this update.
- Because **hildan** is transitive, do not teach a simple nominative-subject past paradigm without explaining oblique agent and object agreement. The conjugation site's row labels should not replace a checked explanation of Kurmanji past ergativity.

## 3. Vekirin — past form corroborated, present structure supported

### Evidence
- Thackston §15 explicitly identifies **vekirin** as a close compound **ve- + kirin**, with present verbal base **k-**. It gives the present forms **ez védikim**, **tu védikî**, **ew védike**, plural **vedikin**, and explains that **di-** is added to the verbal part after the preverbal element. Its negative examples include **venakim**. It also treats close compounds in the subjunctive.
- The Kurmancî Wiktionary entry gives the explicit paradigm **ez vedikim** and past **min vekir**, with oblique-agent past forms **te vekir**, **wê/wî vekir**, and plural **me/we/wan vekir**. This is a collaborative dictionary, so it is not treated as the sole authority.
- The KurdiZan “Lêkerên pêkhatî” page independently presents **Min ew vekir**, lists **ve-di-k-im** as the present structure, and gives **vekir** as the past stem. This is a modern pedagogical source that cites Thackston.
- Chyet's 2003 dictionary independently confirms the lexical entry **ve-kirin**, glossed “to open”, and provides usage examples and dialect/source labels. Its entry confirms the lexical item, not a full conjugation.

### Revised assessment
- **vekir** is no longer merely an uncorroborated Wiktionary form: Thackston's compound analysis plus KurdiZan's explicit past example and Kurmancî Wiktionary's paradigm support it. The independent Chyet entry confirms the verb's lexical status but does not itself provide the complete paradigm.
- Record the indicative present as **ve-di-k-im → vedikim**, with the source's spacing/accent conventions preserved in citations.
- Record negative **ve-na-k-im → venakim**; the negative marker replaces the present **di-** marker in the cited Standard Kurmanji pattern.
- Keep imperative **veke / vekin** distinct from subjunctive **bivekim** and indicative **vedikim**. Do not treat these as interchangeable stem variants.

## 4. Daketin / dakêtin — past evidence now directly attested

### Stronger evidence
- Chyet's 2003 *Kurdish-English Dictionary*, entry **da-k'ev-**, labels the verb intransitive and defines it as “to descend, go down”. It supplies direct past examples:
  - **Gavan û jina xwe daketin nav gund** — “The cowherd and his wife went down to the village.”
  - **Kurê pîrê daket sûkê** — “The old woman's son went down to the market.”
  These clauses attest singular **daket** and plural **daketin** in context. The entry also records an alternate spelling/lexical notation **daketin** and a source form **da-kêtin**.
- *Kurmanji Basic*, §4.7, lists **daketin, dakeve** and gives the compound-preverb rule for present formation.
- Chyet's dictionary entry preserves its own orthographic conventions and source variants. Do not silently flatten **da-kêtin**, **daketin**, and other transcriptions into one form without the project's orthographic policy.

### Revised assessment
- The previous note's claim that the past stem was unverified is superseded. Chyet's clauses directly attest **daket** (singular) and **daketin** (plural) as past forms of this intransitive verb.
- **Dakeve** is attested as the dictionary's second form/imperative-type citation form. The present finite paradigm is still not directly printed in the inspected Chyet entry. The general preverb rule alone does not authorize inventing a learner-facing finite example.
- The spelling **dakêtin** occurs in one teaching source while **daketin** is used in Chyet and *Kurmanji Basic*. Preserve each source's form and defer normalization to an explicit orthographic standard.

## 6. Editorial consequences for the B3 pathway

1. Correct the previous research record: past evidence for **hildan** and **daketin** is now present; **vekir** has multiple independent pedagogical/grammatical supports.
2. For **hildan** and **vekirin**, identify transitivity and explicitly model the oblique agent in past clauses (e.g., **min**, **te**, **wî/wê**) and agreement with the direct object.
3. For **daketin**, identify intransitivity: the past verb agrees with the subject, as in **daket / daketin**.
4. Maintain separate fields for infinitive, present stem, past stem, attested finite example, transitivity, and source/dialect.
5. Do not infer the present form of **daketin** from a general compound rule unless a reliable source directly confirms the chosen form.
6. Do not silently normalize orthographic variants. The source's spelling should remain visible in the evidence register.
7. No existing lesson should be edited on the basis of this audit without the user's explicit approval.
