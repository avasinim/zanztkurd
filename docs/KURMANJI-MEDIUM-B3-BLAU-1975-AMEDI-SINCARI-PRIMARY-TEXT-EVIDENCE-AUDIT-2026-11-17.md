# Blau (1975): Primary-Text Extraction — Amêdî and Sincarî Evidence Boundaries — 2026-11-17

## Executive result

The full scanned 1975 book is now accessible through the Kurdipedia document viewer (260 scan pages). This supersedes the previous access-status note: the Google Books record itself has no eBook, but a separate Kurdipedia-hosted scan is available. The scan's title page identifies Joyce Blau, *Le kurde de ʿAmadiya et de Djabal Sindjar: analyse linguistique, textes folkloriques, glossaires*, Paris, C. Klincksieck, 1975.

This audit records primary-text findings from the introduction, phonology and grammar sections. It does not convert the local material into Standard Kurmanji or propose edits to learner lessons.

## 1. Corpus identity and locality control

Blau explicitly distinguishes two studied speech varieties:
- **amêdî**: the speech of the town of ʿAmadiya (Amêdî).
- **sincarî**: the speech of the Yazidis living on Djabal Sindjâr (Şingal).

The author situates these within the broader northern Kurdish / Badînanî area, but her empirical study is of these two selected local varieties, not a representative sample of every locality in Badinan. She reports two field missions in autumn 1967 and 1968 and recordings of men, women and children, including legends, narratives, poetry, songs and nursery rhymes; she also conducted two intensive linguistic surveys.

The preface thanks Sadiq Bahâ' al-Dîn Amêdî for helping refine the Amêdî texts and states that the book substantially reworks a 1973 third-cycle dissertation. The introduction explains that ordinary-conversation recordings were disappointing and that the author consequently relied heavily on storytellers. This matters methodologically: the book contains rich oral narrative evidence, but the register and genre should not be silently equated with spontaneous everyday conversation.

**Primary-source locations:** scan pp. 5–10; printed pagination approximately pp. 1–6.

## 2. Phonological comparison: a shared count is not an identical system

In §57, Blau states that both Amêdî and Sincarî have eight oral, non-nasalized vowel phonemes and three degrees of aperture. She immediately cautions that the systems are not very similar:
- Amêdî: three front, three central and two back vowels.
- Sincarî: two front, three central and three back vowels.
- The author describes systematic shifts between the systems, including opening of /e/ in Amêdî, raising/velarization of /a/, strong raising of /o/, and fronting of /û/.

This is a direct comparative description by the author, not a license to assign a single “Badini eight-vowel inventory” to all localities. The exact IPA values and charts should be transcribed from the scanned figures and checked visually before any learner-facing use; OCR of the chart is unreliable.

**Primary-source location:** §57, scan pp. 34–36; printed pp. 31–33.

## 3. Verb morphology: the two local systems are not identical

### 3.1 Past and present stems

§80 says the past stem is regularly obtained by removing -(i)n from the infinitive in the described systems, with examples such as:
- Amêdî k'eft'in ‘fall’ → past stem k'eft'
- Sincarî k'etin ‘fall’ → past stem k'et
- both varieties kirin ‘do’ → kir
- Amêdî çon ‘go’ → ço; Sincarî çûn → çû

§81 explicitly warns that the present stem is not automatically predictable from the past stem and that exceptions are not rare. The author gives locality-specific contrasts:
- Amêdî kirin → ke-; Sincarî kirin → k-
- Amêdî birin → be-; Sincarî birin → b-
- Amêdî xarin → xo-; Sincarî xwarin → xw-
- Amêdî hat'in → (h-)ê-, with a second stem wer-; Sincarî hatin → ê-
- got'in → bêj-; dît'in → bîn-

The book also records special present stems for motion verbs: Amêdî çon has a second present stem her-, alongside ç-; hatin has a second stem wer-, which the author says is never preceded by the modal prefix bi-. These are local facts and must not be collapsed into a single cross-dialect paradigm.

**Primary-source location:** §§80–81, scan pp. 60–62; printed pp. 57–59.

### 3.2 Number of verbal paradigms

§88 states explicitly that Blau identified **twelve verbal paradigms in Amêdî and nine in Sincarî**. She says the paradigms comprise five forms, with an additional two-form injunctive imperative, past participle and infinitive. The summary table on scan pp. 67–68 lists categories including present, future, present and past imperfect, preterite, two perfect forms, pluperfect, present subjunctive, perfect subjunctive, conditional perfect and past conditional.

This difference is a central result: do not take an Amêdî conjugation chart and label it “Badini” or “Sincarî”, nor assume every category exists in both systems with the same distribution.

The OCR of the summary table and the detailed paradigm charts on scan pp. 91–96 is badly degraded. The categories and count are legible in prose; individual cells of the scanned tables require visual inspection before exact transcription.

**Primary-source location:** §88, scan pp. 67–68; detailed tables listed at printed pp. 88–96.

### 3.3 Negation

§89 describes several negative prefixes and their distribution:
- na- occurs with present indicative and future in the general pattern described, replacing the relevant present/future prefixes; Blau gives locality-labelled examples such as Amêdî ez nahêm ‘I will not come’ and Sincarî examples with na-.
- ne- is used with the other tenses and replaces positive bi- where present.
- Some verbs have special negative behaviour: siyan/sê- ‘can’, viyan/vê- ‘need/want’, and k'arîn/k'ar- ‘can’; the author distinguishes Amêdî zanîn and Sincarî zanîn in her description.
- Sincarî me- has a prohibitive function and replaces bi- where the positive form has it.

Do not simplify this into one universal negation rule. The source itself gives exceptions and variety-specific examples.

**Primary-source location:** §89, scan pp. 69–70.

### 3.4 Past transitive clause alignment

§94 states that two-actant verbs in past tenses (forms built from the past stem) have what Blau calls a “passive” construction:
- the first actant (agent/logical subject) is oblique;
- the second actant (patient/logical object) is direct;
- the verb agrees in person and number with the second actant;
- the author describes a relatively rigid order: agent, patient, verb.

The label “passive” is Blau's terminology in this section; when incorporating this in modern teaching or analysis, explain the ergative alignment rather than implying that the clause is a morphological passive. Examples are labelled Amêdî and include min ... înay ‘I brought ...’ and te ez mexdor kirim ‘you wronged me’.

**Primary-source location:** §94, scan pp. 74–75; printed pp. 71–72.

### 3.5 Conditional and counterfactual forms

The author distinguishes:
- §111: pluperfect conditional, described as an unreal or impossible past hypothesis. Amêdî examples include forms such as hat'iba and kust'ibama; Sincarî examples include dabaya and bûbaya.
- §112: past conditional, described as an unreal present. The examples are separately labelled Amêdî and Sincarî.

This confirms that the book contains locality-labelled conditional evidence. However, the exact paradigm cells should be taken from the scanned chart only after visual verification; OCR strings are not reliable enough to reconstruct full person paradigms.

**Primary-source location:** §§111–112, scan pp. 88–90; printed pp. 85–87.

## 4. Methodological limits

1. The author herself notes the difficulty and diversity of Kurdish transcription systems (§58 and following). Preserve Blau's transcription in source quotations; any normalized Hawar spelling must be shown separately and explicitly labelled as editorial normalization.
2. The recordings are from 1967–1968 and include narrative genres. Do not claim they directly represent all contemporary speech.
3. Amêdî and Sincarî must remain separate columns in any evidence matrix. The source's broader Badînanî framing is not a warrant to generalize an individual form to Zakho, Duhok city, Barwari, Hakkari or all Badinan.
4. Do not infer missing paradigm cells by analogy. The author explicitly says present stems are not automatically predictable and exceptions are common.
5. The PDF text layer contains substantial OCR corruption, especially in phonetic charts and paradigms. For exact tables, inspect the page image and obtain a second independent transcription before teaching.
6. These findings are a research reference only. Existing learner content remains unchanged pending explicit approval and native-editor review.

## 5. Implications for the Avasin Academy pathway

- Keep the proposed Standard Kurmanji/Hawar-based pathway distinct from both local corpora.
- If a future optional dialect-comparison resource is approved, present Amêdî and Sincarî as two historically documented local varieties with separate provenance, not as a single undifferentiated “Badini” model.
- Teach verb principal parts rather than asking learners to predict present stems mechanically.
- Avoid audio assessment based on this historical text until locality, speaker, transcription and contemporary pronunciation policy have been independently reviewed.
