## 1. Executive finding

The four pilot drafts have a promising shared vocabulary, but the terminology is not yet governed by an approved house style. Some terms are used consistently (`tîp`, `deng`, `peyv`), while other entries are circular, too broad or not clearly distinguished for B0 learners.

A further issue is that Kurmanji orthographic/terminological sources do not all make the same lexical choices. The project must not present one author's terminology proposal as universally settled.

**Recommendation:** retain the current draft's `tîp` provisionally as the course term for “written alphabet letter/grapheme”, because the current 2019 collective *Rêbera Rastnivîsînê* material itself uses `tîp` and presents `dengdêr / nedengdêr` as the vowel/consonant pair. Record the alternative `herf` as a documented proposal in a different guide, not as an error. Obtain explicit owner/editor approval before freezing the house style.

## 3. Source reconciliation: do not collapse distinct guide decisions

### 3.1 The term for “letter”

Two documented choices exist:

- The review of Mistefa Aydogan's *Rêbera Rastnivîsînê* (2012) reports that Aydogan proposes `herf` instead of `tîp`, partly on historical/lexical grounds.
- The online material for the collective *Rêbera Rastnivîsînê* by Komxebata Kurmancîyê (2019) uses the heading `Tîpên alfabeya kurmancîyê` and explicitly lists 31 `tîp`.

These are not the same book or authorial decision. Do not cite the Aydogan review as proof that the 2019 collective guide rejects `tîp`.

**Provisional project rule:** keep `tîp` as the working learner-facing term, since it is already used throughout the B0 drafts and is directly present in the collective 2019 guide's alphabet material. Add a style note recording `herf` as an attested alternative proposal. Owner/editor approval remains required.

### 3.2 The terms for vowel and consonant

The Aydogan terminology proposal, as reported in a scholarly book review, prefers:
- `dengdêr` for vowel;
- `nedengdêr` for consonant;

rather than the pair `dengdar / dengdêr` attributed in that review to Celadet Bedirxan.

The collective 2019 guide's accessible alphabet material also labels the eight vowel letters `Dengdêr` and the consonant letters `Nedengdêr`.

**Provisional project rule:** use `dengdêr` for “vowel” and `nedengdêr` for “consonant” if the owner adopts this source-aligned pair. Avoid using `dengdêr` to mean consonant. This choice is strongly supported as a coherent house pair, but it still requires editorial approval.

### 3.3 Sentence terminology beyond B0

The review reports Aydogan's proposal to revive `komek` for “sentence” in place of the widespread `hevok`. This is a later-stage terminology question and does not need to be introduced in B0. Keep it on the future B2/B5 terminology register; do not silently replace `hevok` throughout the course.

## 5. Exact glossary issues in current drafts

### Lesson 02 — `Tîpên kurmancî`

- `tîp — nîşana nivîskî ya alfabeyê`: useful starting definition, but `nîşan` may be confused with a diacritic. Editor should clarify that a letter is an alphabetic written unit, while its diacritic is not an optional decoration.
- `mezin — forma tîpa mezin` and `biçûk — forma tîpa biçûk`: circular definitions. Use a visible uppercase/lowercase pair as the definition; no abstract paraphrase is needed at B0.
- `rêz — rêzkirina tîpan`: repetitive/circular. Demonstrate the alphabet sequence.
- `klavye — amûra nivîsandinê`: too broad; a keyboard is an input device, not every writing tool. Check the chosen Kurmanji noun and its spelling with the editor.

### Lesson 03 — `Tîp, deng û peyv`

- `tîp`: distinguish what is seen/written from what is heard.
- `deng`: the current definition `tiştê ku em dibêjin û dibihîzin` needs grammatical and conceptual review; a sound is heard, but not every sound is something “we say”.
- `peyv`: the glossary's “meaningful unit of language” concept is a reasonable starting point, but the lesson's main explanatory paragraph incorrectly says a word is made of written letters. Keep the concept; repair the distinction between spoken and written word forms only after editor approval.
- `veqetandin`: avoid using one generic verb for word separation, grapheme-by-grapheme spelling and syllable division. These are different operations.
- `nivîs`: the current gloss is underspecified and may overlap with `nivîsîn` (the act of writing), written text and written form. Do not use it as a technical synonym for grapheme.

### Lesson 04 — `Dengdêr di peyvan de`

- `dengdêr — tîpa dengdêr`: circular. A beginner who does not know the term learns nothing from the definition.
- The lesson should distinguish the **written vowel letters** from vowel phonemes and their phonetic realizations. The objective is grapheme recognition; no B0 learner needs tense/lax or IPA labels.
- `nîşan — tişta ku li ser tîpekê tê danîn`: this may suggest the mark is external or optional. The five marked Hawar letters Ç/ç, Ê/ê, Î/î, Ş/ş and Û/û are alphabet letters in their own right; vowel diacritics are integral to the written distinction.
- `binê xêzê de bîne`: an instruction phrase, not a vocabulary headword. Keep it in a modelled instruction list rather than the glossary.

## 6. Concept boundaries that must remain separate

1. **Letter/grapheme:** visible written unit, e.g. `ş`.
2. **Speech sound:** audible realization in speech; its precise phonetic value may vary by context and variety.
3. **Phoneme:** abstract contrastive unit in a linguistic analysis; not interchangeable with a letter.
4. **Word:** meaningful language unit that can have spoken and written forms; not “a set of written letters” in every mode.
5. **Syllable:** a different unit from a letter; splitting `şev` into `ş-e-v` identifies letters/graphemes, not syllables.
6. **Diacritic:** written mark that distinguishes a letter/form; it must not be stripped during input, storage or assessment.
7. **Spelling/orthography:** conventional written form; not a pronunciation transcription.

The B0 learner-facing explanations should teach only the distinctions required by the task. Technical definitions belong in internal notes or later levels.

## 8. Source register and limitations

1. **Komxebata Kurmancîyê, *Rêbera Rastnivîsînê* (Mezopotamya Vakfı, 2019, 229 pp.).** Publisher catalogue describes it as a collectively prepared guide for unified written Kurmanji. The accessible chapter “Tîpên alfabeya kurmancîyê” lists the 31-letter inventory and uses `tîp`, `dengdêr`, `nedengdêr`.  
   Publisher record: https://www.wmezopotamyaye.org/ku/d/wesan/1-rebera-rastnivisine.html  
   Alphabet/terminology page: https://nlpkurdi.com/rebera-rastnivisine/chapter_1_1.html  
   **Limit:** a guide's chosen terminology is an editorial policy, not proof that all alternatives are invalid.

2. **Review of Mistefa Aydogan, *Rêbera Rastnivîsînê* (2012).** The review reports Aydogan's proposal to prefer `herf` over `tîp`, `dengdêr / nedengdêr` over `dengdar / dengdêr`, and `komek` over `hevok`.  
   Kurdish Studies Archive book review: https://kurdishstudies.net/menu-script/index.php/KS/article/download/137/127/128  
   **Limit:** this is a report of one author's proposals in a different guide; do not attribute these decisions to the 2019 collective guide.

3. **Ergin Öpengin, critical discussion of *Rêbera Rastnivîsînê*.** The review/commentary notes that the guide's technical terminology choices are systematic but should be open to critical scrutiny.  
   https://zimannas.wordpress.com/2020/02/22/pedacuneke-rexneyi-li-ser-rebera-rastnivisine/  
   **Limit:** a critical discussion reinforces the need for transparent house decisions; it does not itself establish a universally accepted replacement vocabulary.

4. **Haig (2018), “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî),” §2.1.1.** Supports the distinction between Hawar spelling, vowel phonemes and phonetic realizations; it also documents a central vowel written `i` in some lexical items.  
   https://multicast.aspra.uni-bamberg.de/data/pubs/Haig2018_Northern-Kurdish_Kurmanji.pdf  
   **Limit:** this is a linguistic description, not a beginner glossary or a ruling on Kurmanji pedagogical terminology.
