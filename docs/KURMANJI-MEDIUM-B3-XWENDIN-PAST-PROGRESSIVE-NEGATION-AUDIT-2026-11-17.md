# Xwendin: past progressive aspect and negation audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only grammar audit for the Standard Northern Kurmanji pathway.

## Executive finding

The sources support a past progressive / habitual pattern built with **di-** on the past-stem construction, and a negative form in which **ne-** is added while the progressive marking remains visible.

Directly attested examples use other verbs, not **xwendin**:
- Thackston: **min ew didît** — “I used to see him/her.”
- Thackston: **wî ez nedidîtim** — “he did not use to see me.”
- Haig & Öpengin: **min dixwar** — “I was eating (something)” (transitive past progressive, third-person singular object).
- Kurdîzan: **Min ew nedidît** — “I was not seeing him/her / did not use to see him/her.”

These support the general pattern, but they do not directly attest the exact forms **dixwend** or **nedixwend**. Do not present those xwendin forms as source quotations or as a verified paradigm.

## 1. Aspect: simple past vs. past progressive/habitual

Thackston (§18.1–18.2) describes the habitual/progressive past with the prefix **dí-** and gives intransitive forms such as **ez díhatim** (“I was coming / used to come”). For transitive verbs, §18.2 gives **min ew didît** (“I used to see him/her”) and **wî ez nedidîtim** (“he didn’t use to see me”).

Haig & Öpengin likewise describe a past progressive/imperfective distinction marked by **di-** and provide a transitive past-progressive paradigm with **xwarin**:
- **min dixwar**
- **te dixwar**
- **wî / wê dixwar**
- **me dixwar**
- **we dixwar**
- **wan dixwar**

The paradigm shown has a third-person singular object and therefore does not itself demonstrate a plural-object ending.

## 2. Negation: prefix order and dialect qualification

The sources show the negative progressive past with both negative and progressive material:
- Thackston's joined orthography: **nedidîtim**, and describes the negative as **nédi-**.
- Haig & Öpengin's morphologically segmented description: **ne-di-hatin** (“they were not coming”).
- Kurdîzan: **nedihatim**, **nedidît**, and explicitly says to put **ne-** before **di-**.
- Haig & Öpengin note a regional Western Kurmanji alternative: **na-de-kir-in** (“they were not doing it”) in Adıyaman–Urfa.

For the Academy's proposed Standard Northern Kurmanji pathway, record the joined surface spelling **nedi-** as the pattern evidenced by Thackston and Kurdîzan, while retaining **ne-di-** as transparent morphological segmentation. Do not mix in the Western regional **na-de-** form as if it were the same standard form.

## 3. Transitivity and agreement

The progressive aspect does not turn a transitive verb into an intransitive one:
- The agent remains oblique in the past transitive construction.
- The direct object remains the agreement controller in the canonical ergative pattern.
- Thackston's **wî ez nedidîtim** shows a first-person singular object reflected by **-im** even under progressive negation.
- Haig & Öpengin's **min dixwar** illustrates the third-person singular object with no overt ending in the transitive progressive.

The sources consulted do not directly supply a progressive **xwendin** pair contrasting singular and plural direct objects. Nor do they provide a directly attested **dixwendin / nedixwendin** paradigm. It would be a reasonable morphological hypothesis to preserve object agreement in the progressive, but the exact xwendin forms and plural-object realization must remain **unverified** until a direct example or full paradigm is located.

## 5. Teaching-safe decision

For the Standard Northern Kurmanji pathway:
1. Explain the past progressive/habitual marker **di-** using source-attested examples from **hatin**, **dîtin**, and **xwarin**.
2. Explain negative progressive order as **ne- + di- + past-based verb form**, with joined spellings such as **nedidîtim** where the source uses them.
3. State that object agreement remains relevant in transitive past-progressive clauses.
4. Do not invent an exact **xwendin** progressive or negative-progressive paradigm.
5. Keep the Western Kurmanji **na-de-** variant separate from the target standard.
6. Do not modify learner-facing lessons until explicit approval.
