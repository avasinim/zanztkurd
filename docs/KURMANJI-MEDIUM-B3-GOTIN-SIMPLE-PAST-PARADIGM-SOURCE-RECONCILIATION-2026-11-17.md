## 1. Research question

Can a six-person simple-past paradigm for Standard Kurmanji `gotin` be presented as directly attested by the academic sources, and how should its forms be distinguished from the special complement construction `Min gotim ku…`?

The answer is qualified:

- A six-person Standard simple-past table is published on the community-maintained Kurmanji Wiktionary conjugation page, with the **Direct patient** varying by person and a list of possible Oblique agents for each row.
- The peer-reviewed/academic grammar sketch by Haig & Öpengin gives the past stem `got-`, the general person suffix system, a direct negative example `Gundi-yan tişt-ek ne-got`, and a discourse-conditioned plural `got-in`; it does **not** print a full six-person past paradigm for `gotin`.
- Thackston's `Min gotim ku…` is a separate complement construction in which ergativity may be lost. It must not be used as if it were the ordinary first-person row of a canonical transitive paradigm.

## 2. What the academic grammar directly provides

### 2.1 Past stem and endings

Haig & Öpengin (2018), Table 9, p. 184, list:

- infinitive: `gotin`
- past stem: `got-`
- present stem: `-bej-` (their literal table spelling)

Table 10 on the same page gives person agreement suffixes. The past forms are -(i)m for 1SG, -(y)î for 2SG, zero for 3SG, and -(i)n for plural persons. The authors explain that simple-past forms are built from the past stem plus appropriate agreement markers, but agreement in past tense varies with transitivity.

### 2.2 What Table 11 does — and does not — show

Haig & Öpengin Table 11, p. 185, includes:
- a six-person **present** paradigm for `gotin` (`ez dibêjim`, `tu dibêjî`, etc.);
- a six-person **intransitive past** paradigm for `hatin`;
- a six-person **transitive past** paradigm for `xwarin`, with the oblique agent changing by person while the patient is a constant singular/unspecified ‘something’ and the verb remains `xwar`.

It does **not** include a past conjugation table for `gotin`. It would be inaccurate to cite Table 11 as if it did.

### 2.3 Directly attested past examples

Haig & Öpengin §3.7.2, p. 190, give:

`Gundi-yan tişt-ek ne-got.`

Gloss: village-PL.OBL thing-INDF NEG-say.PST.3SG.  
Translation: ‘The villagers didn’t say anything.’

This is a directly attested ordinary past-transitive example: plural Oblique agent, singular Direct patient, 3SG verb agreement.

They also give the Cegerxwîn example:

`Herdu çû-n-e cem rovî doz-a xwe jê_ra got-in.`

The transitive verb `got-in` is plural although its direct object `doz` ‘case’ is singular. The plural agreement reflects the plural subject `herdu` (‘the two’), understood from the preceding intransitive clause and not repeated in the second clause. The authors report that this same-subject, preceding-intransitive pattern occurs in spoken and written varieties, while the wider distribution of plural-subject agreement on past transitive verbs varies by dialect, verb semantics and other syntactic factors (pp. 190–191).

**Interpretation:** this is evidence for a conditioned discourse-linked plural agreement pattern, not a replacement for the canonical Direct-patient agreement rule.

## 3. The community-maintained Standard paradigm

The Kurmanji Wiktionary page *Tewandin:gotin* gives a Standard section and labels the simple past as a transitive paradigm with the heading `Biker zêde > Berkar` (multiple possible agents > patient). Its six patient rows are:

| Direct patient | Listed possible Oblique agents | Simple past |
|---|---|---|
| ez | te, wî, wê, we, wan | gotim |
| tu | min, wî, wê, me, wan | gotî |
| ew (SG) | min, te, wî, wê, me, we, wan | got |
| em | te, wî, wê, we, wan | gotin |
| hûn | min, wî, wê, me, wan | gotin |
| ew (PL) | min, te, wî, wê, me, we, wan | gotin |

The same page provides the past stem `-got-` and separately displays Central and Eastern colloquial sections with a different past stem `-go-` and forms such as `min go`, `te go`, `wê/wî go`. Those sections are explicitly labelled as distinct colloquial varieties and must not be merged into the Standard section.

### 3.1 Evidential status

This is a useful candidate reference because it makes explicit what a generic past-transitive paradigm must encode: the Direct patient is the person feature reflected in the verb, while the Oblique agent is listed separately. However:

- Wiktionary is community-maintained, not a peer-reviewed grammar.
- The conjugation page does not, in the displayed table, provide a scholarly source note supporting every cell.
- Its table should therefore be treated as a **secondary candidate paradigm for verification**, not as the sole authority for course content.
- The Standard table must remain separate from its Central, Eastern, Serhedî and Western dialect sections.

## 4. The crucial ambiguity: `gotim` does not identify its argument by itself

The surface form `gotim` can appear in two different grammatical environments:

### A. Canonical past transitive: 1SG Direct patient

In the Wiktionary Standard paradigm, the row with Direct patient `ez` has `gotim`, while the agent is one of the listed Oblique pronouns (for example, `te`, `wî`, `wê`, `we`, `wan`). The 1SG suffix is patient agreement.

The exact interpretation depends on the valency and discourse context of `gotin`; the table is not a licence to translate every `ez gotim` mechanically as ‘I said’.

### B. Special complement construction: 1SG Oblique agent

Thackston §18.5, p. 51, gives:

`Min gotim ku…` — ‘I said that…’

and contrasts it with `min got`. He says that, for stylistic reasons, ergativity may be lost in past transitive verbs normally in expressions such as ‘I said that…’ and ‘I saw that…’ when followed by a subordinate clause functioning as complement.

Here `min` is the 1SG Oblique agent, and the 1SG ending in `gotim` is associated with the special non-ergative complement construction, not with a 1SG Direct patient. The matrix construction and its following finite complement are essential to the analysis.

**Teaching warning:** never teach `gotim` as a one-to-one equivalent of ‘I said’ without identifying the construction and argument roles. Case marking, the presence/type of complement, and the discourse context determine the analysis.

## 6. Editorial recommendation — proposal only

1. Retain the academically established general rule: in canonical past transitive clauses, the agent is Oblique, the patient Direct, and the verb agrees with the Direct patient.
2. Use `Gundiyan tiştek negot` as the directly attested core example for `gotin` with a singular Direct patient.
3. Keep the Wiktionary six-row Standard table as a **review candidate**, with a provenance label and a native-editor check required before any learner-facing use.
4. Do not describe Haig & Öpengin Table 11 as a past paradigm of `gotin`; it is a present paradigm for `gotin` and past paradigms for `hatin` and `xwarin`.
5. Keep the Cegerxwîn plural `gotin` example in an advanced note on discourse-linked agreement, not in the beginner paradigm.
6. Keep `Min gotim ku…` in a separate construction note on complement clauses and loss of ergativity.
7. Require the editor to check every row with controlled contexts that distinguish: (a) 1SG patient, (b) 1SG agent with a 3SG Direct patient, and (c) 1SG agent plus finite complement.
8. Do not change existing lessons, seed content, or the course's approved variety policy on the basis of this audit.
