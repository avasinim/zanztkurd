Source: Husein Muhammed, *Dengnasî di kurdî de* (PDF `Dengnasiya_Zimane_Kurdi_27_Wane.pdf`).
Scope checked: draft Lessons 1–15 against the source's printed page labels and physical PDF pages.

## Confirmed findings

### 1. Lesson 3–4 boundary: corrected in the authoring draft

- Lesson 3 is assigned printed pages 16–28; Lesson 4 is assigned printed pages 29–39.
- The source's T section begins on printed page 28 with the description and classification of T sounds. That material correctly remains in Lesson 3.
- The minimal contrasts `ta`, `tam`, and `tu`, including their meaning differences by aspiration, appear on printed page 29 (physical PDF page 36), after the Lesson 4 divider.
- The authoring draft initially included those minimal contrasts in Lesson 3. This has now been corrected: the contrast examples and the source footnote about `tu/ti` dialectal variation are placed in Lesson 4, while the general T classification remains in Lesson 3.
- The 27-lesson structure and printed-page ranges remain unchanged.

### 2. Lesson 1 heading: corrected in the authoring draft

- Index: “Dergeh: dengnasî û lêkolînên pêşîn”.
- The draft heading was “Dergeh: ev vekolîn û yên berî wê”.
- The draft heading now matches the index. Lesson 3's heading has also been aligned with its index wording.

### 3. Lesson 5: source arithmetic discrepancy retained, not silently repaired

- Lesson 5 follows the source's printed pages 40–43 and preserves the listed letter frequencies and counts.
- The source reports 52,705 letters overall, but the sum of the 31 individual letter counts is 53,001; the printed percentages sum to 100.43%, not 100%.
- The draft records this inconsistency explicitly and keeps the source's individual values unchanged. No replacement totals or corrected frequencies have been guessed.

### 4. Lesson 6: syllable-pattern table restored to the source's three-position structure

- Lesson 6 covers printed pages 44–55.
- The source table distinguishes first, second and third syllable positions. The initial draft had compressed this into a single example column, losing positional information.
- The table has now been restored with all three position columns and the examples shown in the source, including the source's parenthesized rare patterns.
- The lesson heading now follows the source divider: “Dengrêziya kurdî; peyv ji kîteyan pêk tên”.
- The source itself is inconsistent about native Kurmanji onset clusters: printed page 47 includes `xw-`, while printed page 48's summary list omits it and mentions possible `şt-`. The draft preserves and flags this discrepancy instead of choosing one silently.

### 5. Lesson 7: comparison material restored

- Lesson 7 covers printed pages 56–67.
- The first draft summarized the N-to-M assimilation examples and the analogy sections too narrowly, omitting several source examples and the cross-language comparison tables.
- The authoring draft now includes the additional examples `Stembol/Stenbol`, `duşem/duşembî`, `himban/hinban`, and the fast-speech forms `jimbab/jimbav`.
- The comparison tables for the numerals “eight” and “nine”, the prepositions `bi/di/ji/li`, and the postpositions `de/re/ve` have been restored from the source.
- Source caveats and dialect notes are retained; uncertain etymological explanations are not presented as certain facts.

### 6. Lesson 8: B, C, Ç and D — source comparison material restored

- Lesson 8 follows printed pages 68–103 (physical PDF pages 80–115).
- The draft's prose covered the principal sound descriptions and many historical developments, but it did not preserve several of the source's cross-language comparison tables.
- Restored the source comparison tables for C and Y/C developments across Kurmanji, Persian, Pahlavi, Avestan and Sanskrit; the Turkic-language comparisons; the G/C comparisons; and the Kurmanji–Zazaki–Persian–Avestan J/C/Z/Ç set.
- Restored the source's Ç/C, Ç/S, Ç/Ş, Ç/T and Ç/J/Z examples and the K-to-Ç dialect examples.
- Restored the D comparison tables involving Proto-Indo-European, Avestan, Soranî, Hewramî, Zazakî and Persian.
- The source's own alternate forms and dialect distinctions are retained; the tables are transcribed into Kurmanji Latin lesson format rather than copied as page images.

### 7. Lesson 9: F and G — source comparison tables restored

- Lesson 9 follows printed pages 104–115 (physical PDF pages 116–127).
- Added the source's F comparison tables across Kurmanji, Persian, Pahlavi and Avestan; the P-to-F examples; the Arabic loanword table; the FT/WT comparison; and the cross-language forms of the verb “ketin”.
- Added the source's G comparison table for *gav/giran/girtin*, the V/W correspondences, and the Southern Kurdish K/G examples.
- The source's caution that F is not exclusively an Arabic-origin sound is retained.

### 8. Lesson 10: H and J — source comparison material restored

- Lesson 10 follows printed pages 116–130 (physical PDF pages 128–143).

- Added the J comparison table for Kurmanji/Soranî, Zazakî, Persian/Pahlavi and Avestan, including the source note about the Zazakî written standard `roj` versus the widespread dialect form `roc`.
- These additions restore source examples and tables; they do not authorize treating the book's historical etymologies as independently verified modern consensus.

### 13. Lesson 15: Ş and T

- Lesson 15 covers printed pages 186–202 (physical PDF pages 203–219).
