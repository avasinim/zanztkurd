The repository's existing 26 + 12 lesson inventory remains unchanged. The learner target remains Standard Kurmancî in Hawar-based Latin orthography; dialectal forms are not silently merged into the core.

## Executive findings
- **dan** (“to give; also a frequent light/auxiliary verb”) has present stem **d-** and past stem **da-**. Ekici's *Kurmanji Kurdish Reader* gives the present paradigm **didim, didî, dide, didin** and simple-past patient agreement **Te ez dam; Min tu dayî; Me ew da; Wî/wê em dan; Wan hûn dan; We ew dan**. The recipient/goal argument of lexical “give” is separately marked; it must not be confused with the direct patient that controls agreement.
- **anîn** (“to bring”) is exceptional in the present: **ez tînim, tu tînî, ew tîne, em/hûn/ew tînin**. Thackston explains that the ordinary **di-** marker is assimilated and appears as **t-**; the negative paradigm is also irregular (**ez naynim**, etc.). The present stem is therefore best represented pedagogically as the exceptional **tîn-** series, not as a regular *di-an-* formation.
- **anîn** has past stem **anî-**. Ekici gives a direct transitive past example **Kesekî name anî** (“Someone brought the letter”) and the agreement table **Wan tu anî** (“They brought you”), while plural patients yield **anîn** in examples such as **Te em dan** (for dan) and the corresponding **anîn** paradigm in Kurdish Academy's table. For anîn, the full person/object paradigm is shown in the Kurdish Academy conjugation table, which gives **anîm, anî, anî, anîn** for 1sg/2sg/3sg/1pl patients.
- Both verbs are transitive in their ordinary lexical use; their simple past follows the Standard Kurmancî ergative pattern: oblique agent, direct patient, verb agreement with the patient. With a third-person singular patient, the finite verb has no overt person ending: **Me ew da**, **Kesekî name anî**.
- **dan** also functions as a causative auxiliary followed by an infinitive (e.g. **dan + sekinandin**). In this construction, conjugate **dan**; keep the lexical verb in the infinitive. Do not treat the infinitive after dan as a second finite verb.
- The reader supplies grammar examples and news prose, not independently page-verified literary quotations for these two verbs. Do not label the teaching examples as classical/literary evidence.

## 1. Dan — stems, ordinary transitive past and agreement
**Infinitive:** dan  
**Present stem:** d-  
**Past stem:** da-  
**Core meaning:** give; in compounds, a frequent light verb (“show”, etc.) and causative auxiliary.

### Present paradigm
Ekici gives:
- **Ez didim**
- **Tu didî**
- **Ew dide**
- **Em didin**
- **Hûn didin**
- **Ew didin**

The paradigm is based on the present stem **d-** with the present marker **di-** and person endings.

### Simple past: direct patient controls agreement
Ekici's object-conjugation table gives:
| Oblique agent | Direct patient | Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Te | ez | dam | You gave me. |
| Min | tu | dayî | I gave you. |
| Me | ew | da | We gave him/her/it. |
| Wî/wê | em | dan | He/she gave us. |
| Wan | hûn | dan | They gave you (plural). |
| We | ew | dan | You (plural) gave them. |

This table is particularly useful because it shows that the verb suffix tracks the direct patient, not the oblique agent. The **da** form is the third-person singular patient form; **dan** is the plural patient form.

### Recipient versus patient
With the lexical verb “give”, the recipient/goal is a separate argument from the direct thing given. Haig & Öpengin note that recipients with **dan** commonly occur after the verb in the oblique case without an adposition; southeastern varieties may additionally use a directional particle or **bo**. Therefore, a teaching example must label the theme/direct patient and the recipient separately. Do not infer that every oblique noun after **dan** is the agent.

## 2. Anîn — exceptional present and transitive simple past
**Infinitive:** anîn  
**Past stem:** anî-  
**Exceptional present series:** tînim / tînî / tîne / tînin  
**Meaning:** bring.

### Present paradigm and negative
Thackston's *Kurmanji Kurdish* reference grammar identifies **hatin** and **anîn** as exceptional present-tense verbs: the ordinary progressive/habitual marker has assimilated and appears as **t-**.
| Person | Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| ez | tînim | naynim |
| tu | tînî | naynî |
| ew (sg.) | tîne | nayne |
| em | tînin | naynin |
| hûn | tînin | naynin |
| ew (pl.) | tînin | naynin |

Do not construct a regular present such as *dianim* for the core standard paradigm. The exceptional **tîn-** series is explicitly documented. Minor transcription/spelling differences across teaching resources should be recorded as source variants, not mixed into one table.

### Simple past
The past stem is **anî-**. Ekici provides:
- **Kesekî name anî.** — Someone brought the letter.
- **Wan tu anî.** — They brought you.

The first example has a third-person singular direct patient (“the letter”), so **anî** has no overt person ending. The second has a second-person singular direct patient, and the verb shows **-î** agreement.

Kurdish Academy's conjugation table gives this patient-oriented simple-past series:
| Direct patient | Past form |
|---|---|
| ez (1sg) | anîm |
| tu (2sg) | anî |
| ew (3sg) | anî |
| em (1pl) | anîn |
| hûn (2pl) | anîn |
| ew (3pl) | anîn |

Use the agent in the oblique case and the patient in the direct case in example sentences. The series above is the verb's agreement with the patient, not a conjugation according to the agent.

## 3. Simple past, progressive past and perfect: keep the forms apart
- **dan** simple past: **da / dam / dayî / dan**, according to the direct patient.
- **dan** past progressive: Ekici shows **dida** with subject/third-person-singular patient and **didam, didayî, didan** in object-agreement forms. The prefix **di-** marks the progressive past; it is not part of the simple-past stem.
- **anîn** simple past: **anî / anîm / anîn**, according to the direct patient.
- **anîn** present: **tînim / tînî / tîne / tînin**, exceptional and distinct from the past.
- **anîn** future/subjunctive-related forms include **bînim / bîne / bînin** in the cited grammar. Do not confuse these with present **tînim** or past **anîm**.

## 4. Causative dan: a separate construction
Ekici explicitly analyzes the causative as:
**dan + (infinitive of a transitive verb)**

Examples from the reader:
- **Wan ez bi mamostayî dam sekinandin.** — They got the teacher to stop me.
- **Me ew bi mamostayî da sekinandin.** — We got the teacher to stop him/her.
- **Te em bi mamostayî dan sekinandin.** — You got the teacher to stop us.

The auxiliary **dan** carries the tense and agreement; the following verb **sekinandin** remains infinitive. The negative is formed on the auxiliary (**nedam**, **neda**, etc.), not by independently conjugating the infinitive.
