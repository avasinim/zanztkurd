# Kurmanji B3 — Primary Grammar Evidence for Hildan and Vekirin; Limits for Other Preverbs

Date: 2026-11-17  
Status: source-based lexical audit; not lesson copy  
Scope: verify compound-verb structure and the forms directly documented in W. M. Thackston's reference grammar, with a separate check of the Wiktionary entry for vekirin.

## 1. Main finding

The primary grammar evidence materially improves the evidence status of **hildan** and **vekirin**. Thackston explicitly analyses both as close, one-word compound verbs and supplies their present-stem analyses and present forms. He also provides subjunctive and imperative examples. For vekirin, the inspected Wiktionary entry supplies a full paradigm, including past **vekir**, but that user-editable dictionary entry should be treated as corroboration, not as a replacement for the grammar.

The same evidence does **not** establish full paradigms for **derketin**, **daketin**, or **raketin**. A single finite example for raketin remains a single example, not a complete paradigm.

## 2. Thackston §15: direct evidence

Thackston defines compound verbs as a nonverbal element (preverb or complement) plus a verb. He identifies the following close compounds, normally written as one word:

| Infinitive | Analysis in source | Present stem of verbal element | Present indicative example |
|---|---|---|---|
| hildan ‘to lift/raise’ | hil + dan | d- | em hildidin; ew hildidin |
| vekirin ‘to open’ | ve + kirin | k- | em vedikin; ew vedikin |
| rûniştin ‘to sit down’ | rû + niştin | nê- | ez rûdinêm; ew rûdinê |

The source states that the di- prefix is added to the verbal part, after the nonverbal/preverb element. It also gives negative forms in which na- replaces di- in these compounds: venakin and hilnadin.

Thackston explicitly distinguishes close compounds such as hildan and vekirin from open compounds such as behs kirin and alîkarî kirin. He says the close compounds do not take an extended construct in the same way as open compounds; they take normal direct objects before the verb. His example for hildan is silavê ... hildidin (‘raise/send a greeting’), and for vekirin, dibistanên kurdî vedikin (‘open Kurdish schools’).

**Critical parsing point:** the present stem of the verbal base is d- in hildan and k- in vekirin; it is not correct to teach hil- or ve- as though either were the whole present stem. The lexical verb includes the preverb, and the inflectional prefix is placed between preverb and verbal base in the Standard Kurmanji pattern described here.

Source: W. M. Thackston, *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings* (2006), §15, pp. 34–36. Accessible PDF: https://kurdshop.net/content/upload/1/root/kurmanji-complete.pdf

## 3. Subjunctive and imperative evidence

Thackston further supplies these forms:

| Infinitive | Subjunctive forms | Imperative forms |
|---|---|---|
| hildan | ez hildim, tu hildî, ew hilde; plural em hildin, hûn hildin, ew hildin | hilde, hildin |
| vekirin | Thackston's §17 table gives the imperative based on ve-k-: véke, vékin | veke, vekin |

For vekirin, the compound subjunctive discussion says the modal prefix may be omitted with close compounds. The form bivekim is also documented by the independent Wiktionary paradigm, but should be recorded as that source's form rather than silently inserted into Thackston's table.

For hildan, §16 explicitly gives the close-compound subjunctive without bi-: hildim, hildî, hilde, hildin. The source also gives negative subjunctives such as hilnedim/hilnedin in its transcription.

These examples establish more than a bare word-list gloss, but they still do not constitute every tense, aspect, person, dialect, or register.

Source: Thackston, §§16–17, pp. 37 and 43–44, same PDF.

## 4. Vekirin: independent dictionary cross-check

The Northern Kurdish Wiktionary entry analyses vekirin as ve- + kirin, glosses it ‘to open’, and lists:

- Present indicative: ez vedikim, tu vedikî, ew vedike; plural em vedikin, hûn vedikin, ew vedikin.
- Past indicative: min vekir, te vekir, wî/wê vekir; plural agents me/we/wan vekir in the entry's table.
- Negative present: ez venakim, tu venakî, ew venake; plural em venakin, hûn venakin, ew venakin.
- Imperative: vebike, vebikin.
- Subjunctive: ez bivekim, tu bivekî, ew biveke; plural em bivekin, hûn bivekin, ew bivekin.

This is useful cross-checking, especially for the past form vekir, which is not supplied in the Thackston passage inspected above. However, Wiktionary is collaboratively edited; its forms need a second independent dictionary or corpus citation before being elevated to an authoritative course paradigm. The past table should also be reviewed for the exact object-agreement and dialect assumptions before reuse.

Source: Wiktionary, “vekirin,” Northern Kurdish section, accessed 2026-11-17: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/vekirin

## 6. Dialect and orthography cautions

1. Thackston's grammar describes a Standard Kurmanji reference variety; it is not a substitute for a locality-labelled survey of all Kurmanji varieties.
2. Haig documents a different prefix placement pattern in Şemzînan and reports related southernmost Midyat patterns. Keep these labelled separately; do not merge them into the Standard Kurmanji paradigm.
3. Accent marks in Thackston's examples represent stress and/or transcription conventions. Do not carry them into Hawar orthography as if they were ordinary letters.
4. Do not use forms from Sorani, Badini, or a locality-specific variety as interchangeable Standard Kurmanji forms.
5. The English gloss ‘lift/raise’ does not by itself establish the full semantic range or argument structure in every variety.

## 7. Editorial decisions

- Promote hildan from “compound example only” to “grammar-attested close compound with present-stem and clause evidence.”
- Promote vekirin to “grammar-attested close compound with present/negative/subjunctive/imperative evidence”; record vekir as a Wiktionary-supported past form pending independent corroboration.
- Do not claim complete paradigms for either verb from this passage alone.
- Keep derketin and daketin unresolved.
- Keep raketin at the exact evidence level of Haig's one cited finite form.
