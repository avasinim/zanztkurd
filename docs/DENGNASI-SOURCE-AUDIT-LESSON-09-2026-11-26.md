# Dengnasî source audit — Lesson 9 (2026-11-26)

## Scope and source evidence

Source: Husein Muhammed, *Dengnasî di kurdî de*, printed pages 104–115, corresponding to physical PDF pages 115–126 in the supplied 462-page file. The twelve printed pages were reviewed against the authored Lesson 9 and its generated HTML/JSON previews.

## Findings

The main lesson already covered the definitions and major distribution/history of F and G. The targeted source comparison found that several source footnotes, examples and comparative forms were missing or condensed. These have been restored in a supplementary section, preserving the original lesson structure.

### Details restored

1. The comparative F vocabulary table across Kurmanji, Persian, Pahlavi and Avestan: *ferman*, *berf/befr*, *fireh* and *kef*, with the source distinction between *kefa ser avê* and *kefa destan*.
2. Additional historical comparisons for *heft*, including *hêpta, septan, septem* and *septyni*, and the source qualification about initial P in Germanic languages and later Latin loans.
3. The source's additional Arabic loanword examples *funduq* and *felsefe*, with their stated Greek origins; the old Arabic rendering of Iranian P as F; modern P-to-B rendering in new loans; and the Proto-Semitic P / Aramaic *pale* comparison for *fellah*.
4. The source's *kulav, kevn* / Persian *kolah, kohen/kohnê* comparison and its caution that the F/H relation is indirect; footnote 81 on L/R alternation and word-final C/G/D loss, including *rewa* from *rewag/rewac*.
5. The G comparison table for *gav, giran, girtin* across Kurmanji, Sorani, Persian, Pahlavi, Avestan, Sanskrit, Ancient Greek, Latin, Lithuanian and English.
6. Footnote qualifications for Proto-Indo-European *gwem-*, the meaning of *gimti*, and English *come* being pronounced *kam* with G → K.
7. Additional G comparisons *gul/gol/gil/veridhe*, *gu-neh/bi-nas/go-nah/vi-nas*, and *gotin/wutin/gutin/watey/vatene*.
8. Footnotes 87–89: the source gloss on *reh/rih* and Sorani *reg*; the scope of “Southern Kurdish” as Feyli, Kelhuri and Kirmashani varieties; and the source's *qenc/genc* comparison with *ciwan* and *law*.

The supplementary section is synchronized in authoring, JSON payload and standalone HTML preview. The validator now asserts the restored source examples, comparison tables and footnotes.

## Limits

This is a targeted audit of printed pages 104–115, not independent peer review of every historical etymology. Source interpretations are attributed to the book; the audit does not convert them into universal sound laws. The validator has been updated but has not been executed locally or by a newly observed Actions run. No merge to main, deployment, production database change, migration, or course activation was performed.
