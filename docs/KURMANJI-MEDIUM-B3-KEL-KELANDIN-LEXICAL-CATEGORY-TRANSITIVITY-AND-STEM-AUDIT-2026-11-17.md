# Kel / kelandin: lexical category, transitivity, and stem audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only audit following the proposed *kelîn → kelandin* comparison. The key result is a correction: the consulted lexicographic evidence does not establish *kelîn* as an intransitive verb lemma in Standard Kurmanji.

## Executive finding

The proposed pair **kelîn → kelandin** must not be presented as a confirmed intransitive-to-transitive causative pair on the evidence currently available.

Michael L. Chyet's *Kurmanji Lexicon* records:

- **kel** as a noun meaning “boiling” (alongside a separate homonymous noun meaning “bull”).
- **kelandin**, with present stem **kelîn-**, marked **v.t.** (“transitive verb”), meaning “to boil”.

The lexicon does not, in the retrieved entry, list **kelîn** as an intransitive verb. The presence of **kelîn-** as the present stem of *kelandin* does not prove that an infinitive *kelîn* exists with the meaning “to boil / become boiled”. Accordingly, the earlier proposed arrow *kelîn → kelandin* is not verified and should be withdrawn as a teaching claim unless an independent dictionary or corpus citation for intransitive *kelîn* is found.

## 1. Direct lexical evidence

Chyet's *Kurmanji Lexicon* gives the compact entry:

> kel¹ boiling; kel² bull; kelandin kelîn- v.t. to boil

This establishes:
- lemma: **kelandin**
- present stem: **kelîn-**
- valency: transitive
- meaning: “to boil”

It does not establish an intransitive lemma *kelîn*. The distinction between an infinitive and a present stem is essential: a present stem may resemble another possible infinitive, but the two are not interchangeable without lexical evidence.

## 2. Conjugation evidence for kelandin

The Northern Kurdish Wiktionary entry gives:

- infinitive: **kelandin**
- third-person singular present: **dikelîne**
- present forms: **ez dikelînim**, **tu dikelînî**, **ew dikelîne**, plural **dikelînin**
- past stem/form: **keland**
- attested simple past forms: **min keland**, **te keland**, **wê/wî keland**, **me keland**, **we keland**, **wan keland**

The entry labels the verb meaning “to cook”. This is a lexicalized meaning distinct from the narrow English gloss “to boil” in Chyet, though semantically related. The difference should be retained as source-specific glossing, not silently harmonized.

The past forms displayed in the Wiktionary table are third-person singular object examples and do not constitute a full patient-agreement paradigm for all object persons/numbers.

## 3. Causative analysis: what can and cannot be concluded

The general Standard Kurmanji pattern described by Haig and Öpengin allows the suffix **-and** to derive transitive verbs from intransitive present stems. However, their paper also warns that the suffix has dialectal extensions and that infinitives ending in **-andin** are not uniformly causative across all varieties.

For *kelandin*, the dictionary explicitly records a transitive verb and a present stem **kelîn-**. This is compatible with the general morphology of -andin verbs, but it is not sufficient to claim that a separately attested intransitive verb *kelîn* is its base.

Therefore:
- Safe: **kelandin** is a transitive verb, “to boil / cook”, with present stem **kelîn-** and past stem **keland-**.
- Not yet safe: **kelîn** is an intransitive verb “to boil / become boiled”.
- Not yet safe: **kelîn → kelandin** is a proven causative pair.

## 4. Teaching-safe summary

> **Kelandin** کردارێکی تێپەڕە بە واتای کوڵاندن/لێنان. ڕەگی ئێستای **kelîn-** و ڕەگی ڕابردووی **keland-** ـە. بەڵگەی بەردەست پشتڕاستی ناکاتەوە کە **kelîn** خۆی کردارێکی ناتێپەڕی سەربەخۆ بێت؛ بۆیە نابێت وەک جووتی هۆکارسازی وانە بگوترێت.
