## 1. Key finding

The earlier Course 2 register marked the Lesson 10 question-formation examples **UNRESOLVED** because the source review did not yet locate sufficiently precise examples. That status can now be narrowed:

- **Wh-questions:** directly documented for Kurmanji, including the principle that wh-words remain in their base position (wh-in-situ) in the studied Standard and Muş varieties.
- **Yes/no questions:** a 2011 Boğaziçi University linguistics thesis documents yes/no questions formed by intonation and specifically says there is no obligatory yes/no question particle in the studied Kurmanji data.
- **Particles such as `ma` and `gelo`:** they do occur in Kurmanji reference material, but their function is not a single neutral, obligatory yes/no question marker. Thackston's description of `ma` is pragmatic and expectation-sensitive; regional usage must be labelled.

This evidence supports a carefully scoped teaching explanation, but does **not** authorize a universal claim that every Kurmanji dialect uses identical question strategies.

## 2. Claim-level evidence

### Q10-01 — Kurmanji wh-words remain in situ rather than undergoing obligatory overt wh-movement
**Status: SUPPORTED FOR THE VARIETIES STUDIED**

Gündoğdu (2011), *The Phrase Structure of Two Dialects of Kurmanji Kurdish: Standard Dialect and Muş Dialect*, §1.3.2, explicitly states that Kurmanji wh-words remain in their base positions. The thesis lists:
- `kî` — who
- `çi` — what
- `ku(der)` — where
- `kîngê` — when
- `kîjan` — which
- `çima` — why
- `çan(kî)` — how
- `çend` — how many
- `çi qas` — how much

It provides attested examples:
- `Tu diçî ku?` — “Where are you going?”
- `Kî pirtûkê dixwîne?` — “Who is reading the book?”
- `Zana çi dixwîne?` — “What is Zana reading?”
- `Zana kîngê pirtûkê dixwîne?` — “When is Zana reading the book?”

Source: Gündoğdu 2011, pp. 37–38 (thesis pagination), Boğaziçi University institutional PDF:
https://digitalarchive.library.bogazici.edu.tr/bitstreams/c22fcd85-97d8-4c48-98ab-8cc623449fff/download

**Teaching consequence:** the core statement “put the wh-word in the position corresponding to the missing information; the rest of the clause generally keeps its ordinary order” has direct support. The exact forms and glosses still require checking against the chosen reference variety and the lesson's own spelling policy.

### Q10-02 — Yes/no questions can be formed through intonation without an obligatory question particle
**Status: SUPPORTED FOR THE STANDARD/MUŞ DATA IN GÜNDOĞDU (2011)**

Gündoğdu (2011), pp. 39–40, provides:
- `Te vê pirtûkê xilas kir?` — “Did you finish this book?”

A footnote explicitly states that Kurmanji has no question particle required to form yes/no questions and that speakers use intonation, particularly stress on the verb. The thesis later illustrates high pitch followed by a fall on the verb as a yes/no question contour.

**Teaching consequence:** the safest beginner rule is that yes/no interrogative force can be expressed by intonation, with no obligatory particle in the studied varieties. The lesson should not claim that `ma` is mandatory.

### Q10-03 — `ma` is an obligatory, neutral yes/no particle across Kurmanji
**Status: NOT SUPPORTED**

Thackston, *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, §34 “Questions with Ma,” describes `ma` as an expectation-sensitive particle (often equivalent to a question expecting a particular/negative answer, or a tag-like “isn't it?” reading). It can also have a discourse contrastive or wondering function in examples with other interrogatives. This is not a neutral, obligatory yes/no marker.

The thesis by Gündoğdu (2011) explicitly reports no obligatory yes/no question particle in the Standard/Muş data it analyses.

**Teaching consequence:** if `ma` is taught, explain its pragmatic force and provide a source-attested example. Do not present it as the universal or required Kurmanji question particle.

### Q10-04 — `gelo` is a neutral universal yes/no marker
**Status: UNRESOLVED / VARIETY- AND REGISTER-SENSITIVE**

Thackston's contents and discussion recognize `gelo` in wondering/question contexts, but the evidence reviewed here does not justify calling it a neutral, universal question marker across Kurmanji. The Indiana University CeLCAR proficiency structural list includes `ma, gelo` as interrogative particles, but this is a proficiency curriculum reference rather than a dialect-controlled descriptive grammar.

**Teaching consequence:** do not put `gelo` into the core paradigm until its function and register are confirmed for the course's approved reference variety.

## 3. Recommended minimal pedagogical specification for Lesson 10

Subject to owner/editor approval and a Kurmanji specialist's final check, the lesson can teach the following sequence:

1. **Wh-question:** the question word occupies the position of the requested information; it generally remains in situ.
2. **Yes/no question:** intonation, including prominence on the verb, can signal the question; no obligatory particle is needed in the studied standard pattern.
3. **Optional particles:** introduce `ma` only with its pragmatic/expectation-sensitive function and a verified example; keep `gelo` out of the core rule until its scope is established.
4. **Case and agreement:** question words such as “who” may interact with case and verbal agreement; examples should be checked in the same reference variety as the pronoun and case lessons.
5. **Dialect labels:** do not mix Standard, Muş, Behdinī and other regional forms in one paradigm without labels.

Do not add examples by translation from Sorani or English. A Kurmanji editor must confirm the forms.
