# B3 Kurmanji-Medium: Hildan, Vekirin and Daketin — Follow-up Evidence Audit
Date: 2026-11-17
Scope: source-led lexical and inflectional evidence review for the proposed Kurmanji-medium pathway. Documentation only.

## 1. Editorial and evidential rule

This note records attested forms separately from analytical reconstruction. It does not authorize changing any existing lesson, pathway text, runtime, seed, CI workflow, or production data. Standard Kurmanji teaching descriptions are not silently generalized to regional varieties. A missing form is marked unverified rather than generated by analogy.

## 2. Hildan

### Attested material
- The Thackston reference-grammar audit already recorded present forms such as **em hildidin** and **ew hildidin**, together with imperative/subjunctive material **hilde** and **hildin**. These forms show the preverb **hil-** remaining visible while inflectional/aspect material occurs in the verbal complex; they do not establish a complete paradigm.
- The Wiktionary Northern Kurdish entry lists **ez hildidim**, **tu hildidî**, **ew hildide**, **em/hûn/ew hildidin**, and future forms such as **ez ê hildim**. Its past-tense cells are unresolved template placeholders, not evidence for any past form.

### Assessment
- Present **hildidim** is explicitly listed by a collaborative lexicographic source; **hildidin** is also recorded in the previously audited reference grammar. Record source-specific forms rather than forcing a single paradigm from excerpts.
- **Past stem and complete past paradigm: not verified** in the sources inspected for this audit. Do not infer a form solely by stripping the infinitive ending.
- The Wiktionary entry is useful as a discovery lead, but its incomplete past cells make it unsuitable as the sole authority for a learner-facing paradigm.

## 3. Vekirin

### Attested material
- The Thackston reference-grammar audit records **em vedikin**, **ew vedikin**, negative **venakin**, and imperative **veke / vekin**. The description treats **ve-** as the preverbal element and **k-** as the present verbal base.
- The Wiktionary entry lists present **vedikim** and related forms, negative **venakim**, past **vekir**, imperative **vebike**, and subjunctive **bivekim**.

### Assessment
- The present pattern **ve-di-k-** is supported by the reference-grammar examples and is consistent with the general Standard Kurmanji description that present **di-** is inserted between a preverbal element and the verbal stem.
- **Vekir** is currently provisional: it appears in the collaborative Wiktionary entry, but this audit did not establish an independent dictionary or corpus attestation for that exact past form. Do not present it as fully corroborated.
- Keep imperative **veke** distinct from subjunctive **bivekim** and from indicative **vedikim**. The forms belong to different moods/aspects and must not be collapsed into one “stem”.

## 4. Daketin and spelling

### Attested material
- *Kurmanji Basic* lists **daketin, dakeve** with the gloss “to come down, descend”, and gives the general rule that present **di-** is normally infixed between the preverbal element and the verbal stem.
- The previously audited passage prints the compound spelling **dakêtin** in one place, whereas the current *Kurmanji Basic* listing prints **daketin**. A Kurdish lexicon entry also lists **daketin**, but gives lexical glosses rather than a finite paradigm.

### Assessment
- **Daketin / dakêtin** is a spelling discrepancy across consulted sources. Preserve the source spelling in citations and do not silently normalize the circumflex without a chosen orthographic authority.
- **Dakeve** is attested as the listed second dictionary form/imperative. Under the cited *Kurmanji Basic* rule, the corresponding present structure is expected to place **di-** between the preverb and verbal base; however, this audit did not locate a directly printed finite example. Therefore, do not promote a reconstructed finite form to an attested example.
- **Past stem and complete paradigm: unverified** in the inspected sources. The lexical entry is not a past-tense conjugation source.

| Verb | Attested in inspected sources | Provisional / unresolved | Teaching decision |

| hildan | Present **hildidim**, **hildidî**, **hildide**, plural **hildidin** (Wiktionary); **hildidin** forms and **hilde/hildin** (Thackston audit) | Past cells unresolved; no verified past stem | Teach only source-identified present/imperative forms with source labels; no past paradigm |
| vekirin | **vedikin**, **veke/vekin** (Thackston audit); **vedikim**, **vekir**, **vebike**, **bivekim** (Wiktionary) | Past **vekir** lacks independent corroboration in this pass | Present/imperative supported; label past as provisional pending independent evidence |
| daketin / dakêtin | Infinitive and **dakeve**; lexical gloss; preverb placement rule | Orthographic variant; no directly attested finite present or verified past in inspected passages | Preserve spelling per source; do not invent finite/past forms |

## 6. Implications for the B3 pathway

1. Store infinitive, dictionary/imperative form, present stem, finite present example, past stem, and past example as separate evidence fields.
2. Do not derive the past stem mechanically from the infinitive or from a present stem.
3. For compounds, record preverb and verbal base separately only when the source analysis supports that segmentation.
4. Distinguish indicative, imperative, subjunctive, and future forms explicitly.
5. Keep Standard Kurmanji evidence separate from regional incorporation patterns. A pattern reported for Şemzînan, Midyat, or another variety is not automatically a Standard Kurmanji rule.
6. Before adding any of these forms to learner-facing content, seek an independent dictionary, published grammar, or corpus attestation—especially for **hildan** past, **vekir** past, and all finite **daketin** forms.
