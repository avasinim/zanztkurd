## 1. Why this reconciliation is necessary

Earlier Course 1 review notes correctly separated the phonetic distinction between a tap/flap and a trill from the 31-letter Hawar alphabet inventory. However, the Lesson 26 audit and the later integrated addendum went too far when they characterized the spelling use of `rr` as unsupported by the consulted standard-orthography descriptions.

A check of the primary teaching grammars and the University of Arizona Kurmanji reference shows that the more accurate conclusion is narrower:

- A phonetic distinction between a trill and a flap/tap is described.
- The trill is sometimes represented by `rr` in writing, but the spelling is sporadic, not obligatory or consistently applied.
- `rr` is not an additional independent letter in the 31-letter Hawar alphabet.
- Word-initial `r` is described as trilled, but that pronunciation does not require writing an initial double `rr`.

This note qualifies the earlier audit language. It does not silently amend the historical reports or learner-facing material.

## 2. Direct source evidence

### 2.1 Thackston — explicit orthographic statement

W. M. Thackston, *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings* (2006), printed page 2, under “Alphabet and sounds,” distinguishes a flap from a trill. The text says that word-initial `r` is trilled and that the trill is “only sporadically indicated in the orthography by rr,” giving `pir` “very” and the occasional written form `pirr` as its example; otherwise, the distinction is not indicated in writing.

Source PDF: https://kurdshop.net/content/upload/1/root/kurmanji-complete.pdf  
Relevant extracted passage: printed p. 2; PDF text around the “r” entries.

**Assessment:** This is direct evidence that `rr` can occur as a sporadic orthographic indication of the trill. It is equally direct evidence against teaching `rr` as a consistent, obligatory spelling rule.

### 2.2 Haig — phonetic distribution and standard-variety scope

Geoffrey Haig (2018), “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî),” in *The Languages and Linguistics of Western Asia*, pp. 106–158, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110421682-004. In the discussion of Standard Kurmanjî phonology (printed pp. 113–114 in the chapter), Haig states that all word-initial `r` sounds are trilled, while elsewhere the distribution of trilled and flap realizations is not predictable. The examples are transcribed phonetically.

Open chapter: https://www.uni-bamberg.de/fileadmin/aspra/06_Team/Publications_and_CV/haig_2018_ch.2.3_northern_kurdish.pdf

**Assessment:** This supports the pronunciation distinction and the word-initial generalization. It does not, by itself, establish a regular spelling contrast `r` versus `rr`; phonetic transcription must not be mistaken for orthographic spelling.

### 2.3 Haig & Öpengin — phonemic inventory and orthography

Geoffrey Haig & Ergin Öpengin (2018), “Kurmanjî Kurdish in Turkey: Structure, varieties, and status,” in Christiane Bulut (ed.), *Linguistic Minorities in Turkey and Turkic-speaking Minorities of the Peripheries*, pp. 157–230. In the section “Trilled and flapped /r/” (printed pp. 170–171), the authors describe the phonetic contrast and its distribution. Their table treats trill and flap as phonetic categories; it does not list `rr` as an additional alphabet letter.

Open PDF: https://www.uni-bamberg.de/fileadmin/aspra/06_Team/Publications_and_CV/HaigOepengin2018_Kurmanji_Kurdish_.pdf

**Assessment:** This is strong evidence for the phonetic distinction, but it should not be cited as evidence that `rr` is a separate grapheme or a consistently required spelling.

### 2.4 University of Arizona — concise orthography guidance

University of Arizona, Iranian Languages, “Kurmanji Kurdish,” section “Orthography”: https://iranian-languages.arizona.edu/node/7

The page states that the distinction between trill and flap is ignored in many cases in standard Kurmanji orthography, but when it is observed, the trill is indicated by `rr` rather than `r`.

**Assessment:** This agrees with Thackston's qualified description: occasional `rr` marking exists, but it is not a consistently represented contrast.

## 3. Reconciled editorial position

The following wording is the safest evidence-based policy for the Course 1 review corpus, pending selection of the course's reference variety and native-editor sign-off:

1. Explain the difference between a tap/flap and a trill as a pronunciation topic, not as an extra alphabet-letter distinction.
2. State that word-initial `r` is trilled in the descriptions cited by Haig and Thackston; the ordinary written form remains single-`r` in words such as `roj`.
3. State, if pedagogically useful, that some descriptions report occasional `rr` spelling to mark a trill in particular non-initial forms (Thackston's example: `pir` / `pirr`). Mark it as sporadic and source-specific, not universal or obligatory.
4. Do not add `rr` as a 32nd letter to the 31-letter Hawar alphabet.
5. Do not infer that every phonetic trill must be written `rr`, or that every single written `r` is pronounced as a flap.
6. Do not change `roj` to `rroj` merely because its initial consonant is trilled.
7. Before any learner-facing edit, ask a qualified editor of the chosen reference variety to verify the intended house style and the exact example forms.
