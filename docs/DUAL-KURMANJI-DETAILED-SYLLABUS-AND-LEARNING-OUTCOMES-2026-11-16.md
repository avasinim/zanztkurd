# Dual Kurmanji Curriculum — Detailed Syllabus Architecture and Learning Outcomes

**Date:** 2026-11-16  
**Repository:** avasinim/zanztkurd  
**Branch:** fix/kurmanji-ci-current-migration  
**Status:** Design proposal only. No existing lesson text is rewritten or replaced.

## 1. Executive design

The project will maintain two separate learner pathways built on one reviewed linguistic fact base:

- **Pathway A — Sorani-mediated Kurmanji:** Sorani explanations and instructions support learners whose strongest literacy language is Sorani. All Kurmanji target forms use the approved Hawar-based Latin writing policy.
- **Pathway B — Kurmanji-medium Latin:** all learner-facing instruction, explanations, examples, tasks, feedback, glossary definitions and assessments are written in Kurmanji. This pathway is independently authored, not translated line by line from Pathway A.

Both pathways progress from foundation to advanced independent use. Pathway B additionally offers a diagnostic accelerated-entry route for proficient or heritage speakers. The current 26 + 12 lessons are treated as reusable source inventory, not as a complete or certified syllabus.

## 2. Learning outcomes by stage

### Stage 0 — Orientation and literacy bridge

**Pathway A outcomes**
- Identify Kurmanji as the target language and distinguish it from Sorani without treating either as a defective form of the other.
- Recognize and type the approved Hawar Latin letters, including diacritics.
- Distinguish a written letter from a sound and recognize that pronunciation varies by region.
- Read and write carefully selected words and short phrases.

**Pathway B outcomes**
- Follow very short, highly supported classroom instructions in Kurmanji.
- Recognize, name, type and read Hawar Latin letters.
- Understand simple Kurmanji explanations supported by examples, images and repetition.
- Copy and produce basic words and short formulaic expressions.

**Evidence:** alphabet recognition/production, Unicode typing, short-word reading, spelling correction, following basic instructions.

### Stage 1 — Foundation communication

**Pathway A outcomes**
- Exchange greetings and introductions.
- Give and request basic personal information.
- Use common vocabulary for people, family, colours, numbers, days and time.
- Describe familiar people and objects in short phrases.
- Ask and answer simple questions.

**Pathway B outcomes**
- Understand and use common classroom and everyday expressions.
- Introduce oneself and another person.
- Read short dialogues and simple descriptive texts.
- Build short noun phrases and simple clauses from controlled patterns.
- Ask and answer familiar questions using Kurmanji prompts.

**Evidence:** short dialogue, personal profile, labelled description, comprehension of a graded text, short written exchange.

### Stage 2 — Core sentence and noun-phrase competence

**Pathway A outcomes**
- Use personal pronouns and demonstratives in reviewed direct and oblique contexts.
- Build noun phrases with gender, number, ezafe, adjectives and possessive relations.
- Use present-tense verb forms and common negative patterns.
- Express quantity, location, time, possession and simple comparison.
- Understand neutral clause order and form common questions.

**Pathway B outcomes**
- Explain and use core noun-phrase patterns in simple Kurmanji.
- Identify stems and common inflectional elements in taught forms.
- Use present-tense verbs with reviewed person/number patterns.
- Produce affirmative and negative clauses and simple questions.
- Read short texts and identify references, modifiers and basic clause structure.

**Evidence:** information-gap task, description, short message, grammar-in-context task, guided paragraph.

### Stage 3 — Independent everyday communication

**Pathway A outcomes**
- Narrate familiar events using reviewed past and present contrasts.
- Express plans, requests, preferences, ability and obligation.
- Use common spatial expressions and adpositions/postpositions.
- Connect clauses and organize a coherent paragraph.
- Recognize important regional and register variation without assuming that every variant is interchangeable.

**Pathway B outcomes**
- Follow longer Kurmanji instructions and explanations.
- Narrate, describe, explain and compare in connected Kurmanji.
- Use a wider range of verb forms, clause types and discourse markers.
- Read graded narratives, notices, messages and informational texts.
- Revise writing for spelling, grammar, cohesion and register.

**Evidence:** oral narrative, practical role-play, written message, reading summary, paragraph revision.

### Stage 4 — Advanced literacy and analytical competence

**Pathway A outcomes**
- Read and summarize longer Kurmanji texts.
- Explain grammatical contrasts with Sorani support while maintaining accurate Kurmanji examples.
- Edit texts for orthography, morphology, agreement, case and cohesion.
- Compare attested regional forms with explicit variety labels.
- Produce structured explanations and arguments.

**Pathway B outcomes**
- Read extended Kurmanji prose and discuss its structure in Kurmanji.
- Explain advanced grammatical patterns using reviewed Kurmanji metalanguage.
- Write coherent narratives, descriptions, explanations and arguments.
- Edit and proofread texts for orthographic and stylistic consistency.
- Discuss register and documented variation in Kurmanji.

**Evidence:** extended reading response, edited text with rationale, oral presentation, structured essay, source-aware language analysis.

### Optional Stage 5 — Specialist and academic strand

This is an optional advanced module, not a requirement for every learner.

**Pathway A:** Sorani-mediated introduction to Kurmanji linguistics, orthographic standardization, dialect comparison and source evaluation.

**Pathway B:** Kurmanji-medium discussion of grammar, morphology, syntax, dialect variation, terminology, editing and evidence-based linguistic exposition.

**Evidence:** annotated text, morphological analysis, dialect comparison with source citations, terminology note, academic-style short paper.

## 5. Diagnostic and accelerated-entry route for Pathway B

A proficient or heritage speaker should not be forced to repeat beginner lessons solely because the course starts at B0.

The placement process should assess:
- Hawar spelling, diacritics and proofreading;
- reading comprehension;
- grammar-in-context;
- writing quality and cohesion;
- register and pragmatic appropriateness;
- ability to explain or edit forms, if entering the specialist strand.

Possible placement outcomes:
- **B0/B1:** learner needs script or foundational literacy support.
- **B2:** learner communicates but needs structured grammar and writing development.
- **B3/B4:** learner has independent literacy and needs advanced writing, editing or analytical work.
- **B5:** learner seeks specialist linguistic or editorial study.

These are internal placement bands, not CEFR certification. Placement tasks and cut scores must be piloted and validated before use.

## 6. Existing 38-lesson inventory: disposition rules

Use the following action codes during detailed editorial review:

| Code | Meaning | When to apply |
|---|---|---|
| RETAIN | Keep substantially as is | Accurate, well-scoped and appropriately levelled |
| EXPAND | Add missing examples or practice | Core idea is sound but underdeveloped |
| SPLIT | Separate concepts or difficulty levels | One lesson combines beginner and advanced material |
| MOVE | Change its position | Prerequisites or progression are not respected |
| REWRITE | Replace explanation or example set after approval | Wording or linguistic analysis is unreliable |
| MERGE | Combine overlapping instruction | Duplicate learning outcomes create unnecessary repetition |
| NEW | Author a missing unit | Curriculum gap is not covered by current inventory |
| HOLD | Await evidence or editorial decision | Dialect, terminology or source status is unresolved |

No disposition code authorizes changing a lesson until the owner approves the relevant revision batch.

## 7. Standard lesson specification

Every lesson in either pathway should have the following fields:

1. Stable course, unit and lesson IDs.
2. Learner stage and prerequisite lesson IDs.
3. One primary communicative outcome.
4. Two to five measurable supporting outcomes.
5. Required vocabulary and grammatical structures.
6. Target language and instruction-language policy.
7. Carefully scoped explanation.
8. At least three reviewed worked examples, unless the lesson format justifies fewer.
9. Guided practice, controlled production and meaningful use.
10. Reading/listening input appropriate to the stage.
11. Assessment items and answer key.
12. Error-specific feedback, not only “correct/incorrect”.
13. Review/retrieval prompts linking earlier lessons.
14. Source/provenance for each attested or adapted example.
15. Variety, register and orthography metadata.
16. Reviewer names/roles, approval state and version history.

### Pathway-specific fields

**Pathway A:** Sorani explanation; Kurmanji target examples; contrastive note only when evidence supports it; Sorani copy-edit status.

**Pathway B:** Kurmanji-only learner-facing explanation and instructions; controlled vocabulary level; Kurmanji paraphrase/definition; Kurmanji feedback; proficiency-level review.

## 8. Assessment blueprint

A balanced stage assessment should include:

| Domain | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Orthography | Read, type, spell, identify and correct forms |
| Vocabulary | Interpret and use words in meaningful contexts |
| Grammar | Recognize, complete, construct and explain forms |
| Reading | Understand dialogues, notices and graded texts |
| Listening | Understand speech from the approved reference variety and labelled variants |
| Interaction | Ask, answer, clarify, request and respond |
| Writing | Produce and revise sentences, paragraphs and longer texts |
| Pragmatics | Choose appropriate register and interactional forms |
| Editing | Detect and justify orthographic/grammatical revisions at advanced stages |

Listening outcomes must remain provisional until the spoken reference variety and audio policy are approved. Do not create a single supposedly universal pronunciation track.

## 9. Quality and evidence gates

A lesson may be marked release-ready only when:
- linguistic claims have identifiable scholarly or corpus support;
- every example is marked attested, adapted or instructor-created;
- regional forms are labelled rather than silently blended;
- Kurmanji specialist review is recorded;
- Pathway A has Sorani editorial review;
- Pathway B has proficient Kurmanji editorial review confirming learner-facing Kurmanji throughout;
- answer keys and feedback have been tested;
- the lesson has been piloted or otherwise reviewed against its stated learning outcomes;
- orthography and Unicode rendering pass technical checks.

A curriculum stage may be described as proficiency-aligned only after an explicit outcome-to-assessment mapping and validation. Do not claim certification from lesson topic coverage alone.
