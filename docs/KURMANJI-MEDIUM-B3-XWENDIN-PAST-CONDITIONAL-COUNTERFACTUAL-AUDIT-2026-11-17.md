# Xwendin: past conditional, counterfactual meaning, and transitive agreement audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only audit for the Standard Northern Kurmanji pathway.

## Executive finding

Kurmanji has a dedicated past conditional / past counterfactual mood. It is not interchangeable with the pluperfect (past perfect) or with an ordinary possible present/future conditional.

Thackston §25.2 describes two past perfect conditional formations, which appear to be dialectally mutually exclusive:
1. **bí-** (affirmative) or **né-** (negative) + the past perfect form + **-(y)a**.
2. **bí-** or **né-** + a form based on the past stem with **-ba** in place of **-bû**.

For transitive verbs, the source gives forms such as **min bídîtibûya**, **wî bídîtibûya**, **min nédîtibûya**, and **wî nédîtibûya**; and the second pattern **min bídîtiba / wî bídîtiba** and their negative counterparts.

The sources consulted do not directly attest a past conditional paradigm for **xwendin**. Consequently, forms such as **bîxwendibûya**, **nêxwendibûya**, **bîxwendiba**, and **nêxwendiba** are not adopted as verified Academy forms in this audit.

## 1. Distinguish the constructions

| Construction | Source example | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Simple past | **Min helbestek xwend.** | I read a poem. |
| Pluperfect / past perfect | **Min nan xwaribû.** | I had eaten bread. |
| Past perfect conditional | **min bídîtibûya** | counterfactual / past conditional form of “I had seen” |
| Second past conditional pattern | **min bídîtiba** | dialectal alternative past conditional formation |

The pluperfect locates an event before a past reference point. The past conditional marks a hypothetical or counterfactual past situation, often in an unreal conditional sentence. It is therefore incorrect to teach **xwendibû** and a conditional form as interchangeable.

## 2. Two past conditional formations in Thackston

Thackston §25.2 states that two past perfect conditional forms are in use and appear mutually exclusive by dialect.

### Pattern A: past perfect + -(y)a

- Affirmative: **bí-** + past perfect + **-(y)a**.
- Negative: **né-** + past perfect + **-(y)a**.

Attested transitive examples include:
- **min bídîtibûya**
- **te bídîtibûya**
- **wî bídîtibûya**
- **me bídîtibûya**
- **we bídîtibûya**
- **wan bídîtibûya**

Negative forms include:
- **min nédîtibûya**
- **te nédîtibûya**
- **wî nédîtibûya**
- **me nédîtibûya**
- **we nédîtibûya**
- **wan nédîtibûya**

The table is the source's paradigm for **dîtin**, not for **xwendin**.

### Pattern B: -ba instead of -bû

The second form prefixes **bí-** or **né-** and substitutes **-ba** for **-bû** in the past perfect:
- Affirmative: **min bídîtiba**, **wî bídîtiba**.
- Negative: **min nédîtiba**, **wî nédîtiba**.

Thackston notes that the two formations are mutually exclusive depending on dialect. They must not be combined into one invented hybrid paradigm.

## 3. Transitive frame and agreement caution

The past conditional forms occur in transitive ergative clauses:
- The agent is oblique (e.g. **min**, **wî**).
- The verb form is part of the transitive past conditional paradigm.
- The examples shown for **dîtin** use a third-person singular object; they do not establish a complete plural-object paradigm for **xwendin**.

The past conditional table's repeated form across different oblique agents is not evidence that agent number or person is the agreement controller. It is also not enough to reconstruct every direct-object agreement form. Use the paradigm only with its documented verb and the object configuration represented by the source.

## 4. Relation to ordinary possible conditionals

A possible present/future conditional is different. Kurdîzan gives:
- **Heke tu bixwînî, ez dê bixwînim.** — If you read, I will read.

That is a possible condition with subjunctive in the condition clause and **dê** in the result clause. It is not a past counterfactual and must not be used to teach the past conditional.

## 5. Xwendin: evidence status

The earlier lesson source directly attests:
- **xwendin** “read, study”
- past stem **xwend**
- **Min helbestek xwend** (simple past, singular object)
- **Wî çirok xwendin** (simple past, plural object)

Thackston documents the past conditional paradigm using **dîtin**, not **xwendin**. No direct source example for a past conditional of **xwendin** was found in the consulted material.

| Candidate form | Proposed pattern | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| **bîxwendibûya** | Pattern A, affirmative | Unverified extrapolation |
| **nêxwendibûya** | Pattern A, negative | Unverified extrapolation |
| **bîxwendiba** | Pattern B, affirmative | Unverified extrapolation |
| **nêxwendiba** | Pattern B, negative | Unverified extrapolation |

These candidates are listed only to document what remains to be checked. They are not claimed to be ungrammatical, and they must not be copied into learner-facing material as confirmed forms. Prefix spelling, vowel length/diacritics, segmentation, and the relevant dialect must be confirmed before any form is adopted.

## 6. Teaching-safe decision

For the Standard Northern Kurmanji pathway:
1. Teach possible present/future conditions separately from unreal past counterfactuals.
2. Teach the pluperfect separately from the past conditional.
3. Present the two Thackston past conditional formations as dialectally alternative patterns, not as freely interchangeable options.
4. Use **dîtin** examples when illustrating the documented transitive paradigms.
5. Do not present a **xwendin** past conditional until a direct source example or an approved speaker/editor confirms the form for the Academy's target variety.
6. Do not infer a complete direct-object agreement paradigm from the third-person singular-object examples.
7. Do not modify learner-facing lessons until explicit approval.
