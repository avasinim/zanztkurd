# Girîn / girandin / giriyandin: spelling, causative meaning, and stem audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only lexical and morphological audit for the Standard Northern Kurmanji pathway.

## Executive finding

The evidence supports a Kurmanji transitive verb meaning **“make someone cry / cause someone to weep”**, but the spelling is not uniform across the consulted references:

- The *Ferhengên Kurmancî* lexicon (Enstîtuya Kurdî ya Qefqasyayê edition) has a direct **Girandin** entry, glossed as causing someone to begin crying, and supplies inflectional information.
- Wiktionary's Kurmanji entry **giriyandin** gives a complete present/past stem analysis and explicitly lists **girîyandin** and **giryandin** as alternative spellings.
- The sources do not provide a single universally settled orthographic recommendation among **girandin**, **giriyandin**, **girîyandin**, and **giryandin**. A course should select one spelling only after applying its own orthographic policy and should retain the others as documented variants.
- Do not confuse **girîn** “cry” with **gîrîn** or with **girtin** “take/hold”. A spelling/diacritic distinction matters.

## 1. Lexical evidence

### Ferhengên Kurmancî

The dictionary entry **Girandin** defines the verb as making someone cry through words, such that they begin to weep. It supplies present-tense forms and past-tense examples, including **di girand** and **girandin**. This is direct Kurmanji lexicographic evidence for the transitive meaning “cause to cry”.

The entry's present-tense transcription contains forms printed as **di girînîm / di girînîn / di girîne**. Because this representation is not transparently identical to the separate Wiktionary lemma **giriyandin**, do not silently normalize the dictionary's paradigm to another spelling.

### Wiktionary Kurmancî

The entry **giriyandin** labels the verb transitive and gives:
- Present stem: **giriyîn-**
- Past stem: **giriyand-**
- Example present: **ez digiriyînim**
- Singular imperative: **bigiriyîne**
- Past example: **Li kurrê xwe xist û ew giriyand.**
- Meaning: “to cause someone to cry” / “to cause weeping”.

It lists **girîyandin** and **giryandin** as alternative spellings. The Wiktionary page is useful for explicit stem and paradigm data, but its forms should be cross-checked against the project's chosen reference standard before learner-facing publication.

## 2. Base verb and derivational relation

The base **girîn** means “to cry / weep” and is intransitive. The transitive verb **girandin / giriyandin** means “to make someone cry”. The semantic relationship is causative, but the surface spelling and stem representation differ between lexicographic sources.

A useful distinction for the curriculum:
- **girîn** — intransitive: the subject cries.
- **girandin / giriyandin** — transitive: an agent causes another person to cry.

Do not infer a full paradigm for **girîn** from the causative entry. The consulted sources do not establish all of its present and past forms consistently enough for a full conjugation table here.

## 3. Stem audit

| Lemma / source form | Present stem | Past stem | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **giriyandin** (Wiktionary) | **giriyîn-** | **giriyand-** | Explicitly listed |
| **girandin** (Ferhengên Kurmancî) | Printed forms include **di girînîm / di girînîn / di girîne** | Examples include **girand-** | Direct lexicon evidence; orthographic/morphological notation needs careful transcription |
| **girîn** | Not assigned here | Not assigned here | Base meaning is clear; full stem table not established by this audit |

The spelling **giryandin** is listed as a variant of **giriyandin** by Wiktionary. It should not be treated as a separate lexical verb without further evidence.

## 4. Orthographic caution: girîn vs gîrîn

A Kurdipedia article explicitly argues for distinguishing **girîn** and **gîrîn**, and discusses different present stems and meanings. That article is an author's orthographic argument, not by itself a binding standardization decision. For the course:
- preserve the distinction in the source forms;
- do not “correct” one to the other by intuition;
- do not conflate **girîn** with **girtin**;
- record the adopted spelling in the project's orthography register before publishing learner material.

## 5. Teaching-safe decision

**Approved for lexical research, not yet a final orthographic course entry.**

A future learner entry may teach the semantic contrast:
- **girîn** — to cry (intransitive)
- **girandin / giriyandin** — to make someone cry (transitive)

Before publication, choose one headword spelling using the academy's adopted Hawar-based orthographic policy. Preserve the alternatives as variants in metadata or editorial notes. Do not mix the forms from different references into one invented paradigm.

No full set of object-agreement forms, negative forms, or all-person past paradigm is asserted in this audit.
