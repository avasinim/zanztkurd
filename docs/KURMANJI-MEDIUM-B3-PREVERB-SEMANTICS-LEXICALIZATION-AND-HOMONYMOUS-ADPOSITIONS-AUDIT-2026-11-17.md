# Kurmanji B3 — Preverb Semantics, Lexicalization, and Homonymous Adpositions: Source-Critical Audit

Date: 2026-11-17  
Status: research/editorial document only; not approved lesson content  
Scope: Kurmanji preverbs and verb formation, especially ve-, der-, hil-, ra-/řā-, and da-. Distinguishes verbal preverbs from homonymous postpositions/circumpositions and contracted adpositional elements.

## 1. Executive findings

1. Kurmanji preverbs are best learned as parts of complete lexical verbs, not as a productive set of particles with one invariant spatial translation each.
2. Some forms are semantically transparent in particular verbs (for example, der- in derketin ‘go out/leave’), but this does not license a universal rule that der- always means ‘out’ or that every occurrence is synchronically compositional.
3. Standard Kurmanji commonly writes the infinitival preverb+verb as one lexical item, while inflectional prefixes such as di- and na- occur between the preverb and verbal stem: der-di-kev-e, der-na-kev-e. This pattern is described as non-incorporated in the syntactic account cited below.
4. Other varieties, notably Şemzînan (SEK) and some southernmost varieties around Midyat, show preverb incorporation/lexicalization with a different placement of inflectional prefixes. These are dialect facts, not alternative spellings of one uniform rule.
5. The strings ve, da, and ra also occur as postpositions or as parts of circumpositions. Their mere surface identity with a verbal preverb does not establish that they are the same morpheme or have the same meaning.
6. A lexical verb must be recorded with its infinitive, present stem, past stem, inflected examples, dialect/source, and meaning. Do not infer its paradigms or valency from a guessed preverb meaning.

## 2. Evidence and limits

### 2.1 Haig (2018), Northern Kurdish: an areal perspective

Haig describes a set of opaque preverbal particles including hil, řā, and da, which combine with verb stems to create new verbs. In Standard Kurmanji, the infinitive is generally written as one item, but indicative/imperfective and negative prefixes may be inserted between the preverb and stem. His example is ra-di-keve ‘goes to sleep’, associated with raketin.

Haig contrasts this with Şemzînan (SEK), where inflectional prefixes can precede the preverb, reflecting lexicalization/incorporation of the preverb+stem complex. The example di-helē-t ‘the sun rises’ is analysed as a contraction of di-hel-hē-t; the corresponding Standard Kurmanji form is hil-t-ê. This is a dialect-specific morphological analysis.

Source: Geoffrey Haig, “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî): An Areal Perspective,” section 3.4.1.2, “Preverb incorporation,” in The Iranian Languages (2018). Accessible author-hosted chapter: https://multicast.aspra.uni-bamberg.de/data/pubs/Haig2018_Northern-Kurdish_Kurmanji.pdf

### 2.2 Haig & Öpengin (2018), Kurmanji in Turkey

The authors discuss dialectal variation and preverb incorporation. They also note that in some highly lexicalized light-verb constructions the negative marker may occur at the left edge of the verbal complex, before non-verbal material. This reinforces the need to analyse the complete construction and dialect rather than applying a single prefix template to all verbs.

Source: Geoffrey Haig & Ergin Öpengin, “Kurmanji Kurdish in Turkey: structure, varieties, and status” (2018), author-hosted PDF: https://www.uni-bamberg.de/fileadmin/aspra/06_Team/Publications_and_CV/HaigOepengin2018_Kurmanji_Kurdish_.pdf

### 2.3 Thackston, Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings

Thackston describes compound verbs such as hildan ‘lift’, vekirin ‘open’, and rûniştin ‘sit down’, contrasting these with two-word noun/light-verb combinations such as behs kirin and alîkarî kirin. His description places di- on the verbal part after the non-verbal element. This is useful descriptive evidence for lexicalization and orthography, but it should not be converted into a claim that every preverb retains a single literal meaning.

Source: W. M. Thackston, Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings, §15 “Compound Verbs” (2006). Publicly accessible scan/catalogue copies vary; use the cited section and edition details when independently checking a physical or institutional copy.

### 2.4 Noun-verb complex predicates

The syntactic account of Kurmanji noun-verb complex predicates distinguishes preverbs/adpositional elements such as da, ra, jê, lê, and adverbial der from the non-verbal elements in noun+light-verb constructions. It gives der-na-kev-e and der-di-kev-e as examples in which inflectional prefixes occur between der- and the verbal stem. It also notes that jê/lê can be contracted forms involving an adposition and an oblique pronoun. Thus a form that looks like a preverb may have a different syntactic history or function.

Source: “Noun-Verb Complex Predicates in Kurmanji: A Syntactic Account,” Proceedings of ConSOLE XXIII (2015), accessible author-uploaded copy: https://www.academia.edu/7519257/Noun_Verb_Complex_Predicates_in_Kurmanji_A_Syntactic_Account_Proceedings_of_ConSOLEXXIII_

The exact orthography, stem alternations, valency, and dialect label must be checked against a dictionary or primary corpus before use in a lesson.

## 4. Crucial homonymy: preverb vs postposition/circumposition

The same written strings can occur in different grammatical positions:

- Verbal preverb: a bound/lexical element in an infinitive such as vekirin, hildan, raketin.
- Postposition/circumposition: ve, de/da, re/ra can occur after an NP or as the second member of a circumposition, for example bi te re, ji îro ve, di mala wê de.
- Contracted adpositional material: jê and lê may reflect adposition + oblique third-person pronoun in particular constructions.

Therefore:
- Do not teach verbal ve- as identical to postpositional ve.
- Do not equate verbal da- with postpositional da or dialectal variants of de.
- Do not equate verbal ra-/řā- with postpositional re/ra merely because forms resemble one another.
- Analyse position, syntax, stress, lexical meaning, and dialect before assigning morpheme identity.

## 5. Recommended lexical-record schema

For every preverb verb, record:

1. Exact infinitive and orthographic source.
2. Present stem and past stem, with cited inflected forms.
3. Meaning in the source language variety; avoid a guessed literal decomposition.
4. Valency and argument structure, supported by attested clauses.
5. Prefix placement in affirmative, negative, indicative/imperfective, subjunctive, imperative, and past forms where attested.
6. Dialect/variety and source metadata.
7. Whether the analysis is synchronically transparent, partially transparent, opaque, or disputed.
8. Homonymous postposition/circumposition excluded or separately analysed.
9. Confidence label: directly attested, source-described generalization, cross-source corroborated, or unresolved.

## 6. Editorial guardrails for B3

- Teach each frequent verb as a whole lexical entry before introducing any semantic family.
- Present spatial associations (out, up, down, away) as tendencies in selected lexical items, never as invariant translations of preverbs.
- Keep Standard Kurmanji prefix placement separate from Şemzînan/Midyat patterns.
- Do not generalize the SEK incorporation pattern to all Kurmanji varieties.
- Do not infer that a preverb is productive merely because several lexical verbs share its spelling.
- Do not mix verbal preverbs with postpositions/circumpositions or contracted pronominal-adpositional forms.
