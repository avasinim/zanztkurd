# B3 Kurmanji-Medium: Preverb Present Stems and Lexical Contrasts
Date: 2026-11-17
Scope: documentation-only source audit of daketin, derketin, raketin, hildan and hilgirtin. No learner-facing lesson or code changes.

Important distinction: **hildan** and **hilgirtin** are separate dictionary entries with different citation forms and meaning ranges. They must not be treated as interchangeable synonyms or as a single verb paradigm. Baran Rizgar's dictionary explicitly lists **hildan (hilde)** as “raise, lift; roll up” and **hilgirtin (hilgire)** as “shoulder or take on a burden/task; bear, endure; carry.” It also records **hilgavtin** as a variant/cross-reference to **hilgirtin**. Chyet's dictionary had previously supplied an independent lexical attestation for hildan.

## 2. Daketin: citation form vs finite present

Baran Rizgar's *Kurdish-English Dictionary* gives **daketin (dakeve)** and classifies it as intransitive, with meanings “come/go down, climb down,” “decrease,” and “descend.” This is strong evidence for the infinitive and dictionary present citation form.

A separate Kurmanji grammar source lists **daketin, dakeve** among compound/preverb verbs and explains that the present marker **di-** is inserted between the preverbal element and the verbal stem. It also provides the parallel pattern **derketin → derdikevim**. This supports a morphological analysis with the preverbal **da-** retained and **di-** placed before the stem. However, the sources inspected in this pass did not provide a full six-person finite table specifically for **daketin**. Therefore:
- safe to record **daketin (dakeve)** as dictionary form;
- safe to explain that **da-** is a preverbal element;
- do not publish a full finite paradigm or unverified sample clause as a settled B3 teaching form yet;
- continue searching for a direct, finite attestation in an edited grammar or literary corpus.

## 3. Derketin: strong present evidence

Baran Rizgar gives **derketin (derkeve)** and marks it intransitive, with meanings “come out, go out, set out, emerge.”

*Kurmanji Basic* lists **derketin, derkeve** and gives the sentence **Ez ji vir derdikevim** (“I am leaving from here”). It explains that the present indicative marker **di-** is normally inserted between the preverbal element and the verb stem. Thus:
- infinitive: **derketin**
- dictionary present citation form: **derkeve**
- 1sg present example: **Ez ji vir derdikevim**
- lexical class: intransitive

This is a directly usable attestation of the finite present, unlike merely inferring a form from a general rule. A pedagogical source lists **derket** as the past stem, but a primary literary past clause remains a follow-up verification item.

## 4. Raketin: present forms and limits

Three sources converge on the everyday present form:
- *Kurdî 1* / Kurdolingo: **Ez nêzî saet dozdehan radikevim** (“I go to bed around twelve”), and explicitly lists the present stem **rakev**.
- *Kurmanji Kurdish Lessons* gives a full present set: **ez radikevim; tu radikevî; ew radikeve; em/hûn/ew radikevin**.
- The Persian-language *Kurdish self-learning* grammar gives the same present forms in a conjugation table.

This is useful corroboration for the present paradigm, although these are teaching resources rather than a critical scholarly edition. A Japanese Wiktionary entry lists the corresponding simple past forms **ez raketim; tu raketî; ew raket; em/hûn/ew raketin**. A KurdishLessons page likewise identifies **raket** as the past stem by analogy with other intransitives. For B3, retain these as provisional reference forms until confirmed in a scholarly reference grammar or primary narrative.

The verb is intransitive in these sources. The single subject remains direct/nominative in both present and past; it does not follow the transitive past ergative pattern.

## 5. Hildan vs hilgirtin: lexical separation

The Baran Rizgar dictionary gives:
- **hildan (hilde)** — transitive; raise, lift; roll up (e.g. sleeves).
- **hilgirtin (hilgire)** — transitive; shoulder/take on a burden or task; bear, endure; carry.
- **hilgavtin** — cross-reference to **hilgirtin**.

This lexical contrast matters pedagogically. A learner should not be told that **hilgirtin** is simply another spelling or inflected form of **hildan**. Both are transitive, so canonical past-tense examples should be checked for oblique agent and direct object agreement. However, the current evidence set does not justify publishing a full **hilgirtin** past paradigm.

## 6. Safe B3 instructional recommendations

1. Teach the dictionary headword together with its present citation form: **daketin (dakeve)**, **derketin (derkeve)**, **raketin (rakeve)**.
2. Explain preverb placement with an attested example first: **Ez ji vir derdikevim**.
3. For **daketin**, distinguish the attested dictionary citation form **dakeve** from a finite indicative paradigm that still needs direct verification.
4. Teach **raketin** as intransitive, and do not use it as an example of transitive past ergativity.
5. Present **hildan** and **hilgirtin** as two separate lexical entries with distinct semantic ranges.
6. Do not infer an entire paradigm from a single headword, one conjugation app, or the general **di-** placement rule.
7. Keep regional variants and spelling variants visible in research notes; do not normalize them into the project's target standard until the written-standard policy is fixed.
