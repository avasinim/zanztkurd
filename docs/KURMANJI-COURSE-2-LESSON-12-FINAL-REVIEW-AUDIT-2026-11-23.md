# Course 2 — Lesson 12: Final Review & Assessment Audit (2026-11-23)

## Scope
Documentation-only instructional audit of the stored Course 2 Lesson 12 payload in `docs/kurmanji-content-seed-part-05.sql`, titled **پێداچوونەوە و تاقیکردنەوەی کۆتایی**. The audit checks alignment between the title, stated learning coverage, revision prompts, and assessment design. It does not independently re-derive every Kurmanji rule.

## Current content (verified against seed payload)
- Morphology review: morpheme/root, prefixes/suffixes, and the common indefinite-singular use of `-ek`, with a context/case caveat.
- Noun phrase review: gender/number, ezafe, adjectives, and possessive constructions.
- Verb/clause review: direct/oblique pronouns, tense, transitivity, negation, questions, and word order.
- A final practice instruction asks the learner to choose a Kurmanji sentence and identify nouns/verbs, pronoun forms/case, ezafe/suffixes, tense and negation/question structure, supporting decisions with sources and context.
- The review questions ask about `-ek`, ezafe and case; pronoun form vs tense/clause type; and source use in grammatical analysis.
- The methodological note cautions that the review is not a substitute for working with source texts and that new examples need grammatical-source verification.

## Findings
1. **Title–assessment mismatch (high priority):** despite “تاقیکردنەوەی کۆتایی” in the title, the payload contains no actual test paper, scored items, response format, time guidance, marking rubric, or answer key. It is currently a guided review/practice lesson rather than a complete final assessment.
2. **Coverage is broad but not measurable:** the lesson names many domains but does not map each item to a specific prior lesson or define what a learner must demonstrate in each domain.
3. **The final practice task is underspecified:** “choose a Kurmanji sentence” gives no controlled source passage, length, difficulty, or minimum number of target structures. Different learners may therefore receive incomparable tasks.
4. **No model analysis:** the learner is asked to analyze a sentence, but no worked sample demonstrates how to identify a morpheme boundary, ezafe relation, case form, tense/aspect, or clause structure without overgeneralizing.
5. **Review questions are mainly explanatory:** they check conceptual recall but do not directly test identification, segmentation, correction, translation, or sentence analysis across the 12-lesson course.
6. **Source discipline is a strength:** the note explicitly warns against inventing examples and requires source/context support. Preserve this safeguard in any revision.
7. **Terminology needs a consistent cross-lesson check:** terms for direct/oblique case, tense/aspect, ezafe, and transitivity should match the wording used in Lessons 1–11. This audit does not prescribe unverified Kurmanji forms.

## Conservative recommendation
Keep the stored lesson unchanged for now. Before calling it a final exam, decide whether it should remain a review/practice lesson or become a true assessment. If an assessment is approved, prepare a lesson-to-objective blueprint, source-controlled Kurmanji examples, a mix of recognition and production tasks, clear scoring criteria, and an answer key with accepted variants. Do not add fabricated examples or assume one dialect form is universal.

## Change boundary
Documentation-only audit. No lesson HTML/SQL, seed data, application code, migrations, database, Supabase, main, or production changes.
