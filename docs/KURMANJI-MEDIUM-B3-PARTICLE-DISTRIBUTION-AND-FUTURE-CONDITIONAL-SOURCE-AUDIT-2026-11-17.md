# Source audit: distribution of Kurmanji future/conditional particles ê, dê, wê (2026-11-17)

## Scope and terminology
This note records source-attested forms and distribution statements for the particles conventionally written `ê`, `dê`, and `wê`. It distinguishes the ordinary future from the past-stem first conditional. It does not select an Academy standard.

## 1. Unger (2014): Standard Kurmanji and Behdînî contrast
Stefan Georg Unger, “Future Tense in Kurmanji: The Use of wê/dê/ê in Kurmanji,” *Kurdish Studies Archive* 2 (2014), pp. 213–241.

Source:
https://kurdishstudies.net/menu-script/index.php/KS/article/download/64/55

Attested statements and examples:
- In Standard Kurmanji, `wê` is predominant; `dê` also occurs in written/standard Kurmanji.
- `wê` may be shortened to `ê` and cliticized to a subject pronoun.
- Unger's example (31): `Însêlah em-ê qezenc bi-k-in` (“God willing, we will win”).
- Example (33): `Ez-ê sibe bi wan re ne-ç-im` (“I won't go with them tomorrow”): the future particle remains while subjunctive negation `ne-` is used.
- Unger contrasts Behdînî, where the corresponding future particle cannot be used with negation in the same way; the negative indicative is used instead.
- In the first conditional described from Bedir Khan & Lescot, the subject is followed by the modal particle and the verb carries `bi-` on the **past stem**. Example (35): `Lezgîn dê bi-hat-a` (“Lezgin would have come”).

Evidence location: article pp. 234–237 (examples 31–35 and discussion).

## 2. Haig & Öpengin (2018): Northern Kurmanji grammar sketch
“2.3 Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî),” in *The Languages and Linguistics of Western Asia*, section 2.6.4.

Source:
https://multicast.aspra.uni-bamberg.de/data/pubs/Haig2018_Northern-Kurdish_Kurmanji.pdf

Attested examples:
- `ez=ē řožī bi-gir-im` — first-person subject clitic + future particle + present subjunctive.
- `ew dē ... bēž-e` — third-person subject + `dē` + subjunctive.
- `wē ... bi-bi-n` — clause-initial future particle.
- Standard Kurmanji may retain the future auxiliary in negative sentences with `ne-`; Behdînî uses a negative indicative construction instead.

The source presents `(d)ê` or `wē` as particles and explicitly allows clause-second and clause-initial positions; therefore, avoid teaching a rigid “always after the subject” rule.

## 3. Thackston: contracted pronoun forms
W. M. Thackston, *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, §16.2.

Available copy:
https://kurdshop.net/content/upload/1/root/kurmanji-complete.pdf

Thackston gives:
- `ezê` / `ez dê`
- `tê` / `tu dê`
- `ewê` / `ew dê`

He explains contractions such as `tu + ê > tê` and `ew + ê > wê`, and gives affirmative and negative future paradigms for `çûn`. This is a separate description of the ordinary future, not the past-stem first conditional.

## 4. Comparison table

| Form | Source-attested distribution | Safe interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `wê` | Predominant in Standard Kurmanji in Unger's account; also appears clause-initially in Haig & Öpengin | Future/modal particle; not globally interchangeable without dialect/register qualification |
| `dê` | Also occurs in written/standard Kurmanji; examples in Unger and Haig & Öpengin | Future/modal particle; also used in the past-stem first conditional example |
| `ê` / `=ê` | Shortened/cliticized form described by Unger; pronoun contractions in Thackston | Treat as a clitic/contraction in the described environments, not a free universal spelling variant |

## 5. Academy decisions still open
1. Select no canonical particle until the Academy chooses the target written variety and register.
2. Do not conflate the ordinary future (present-stem subjunctive) with the first conditional (past-stem subjunctive morphology).
3. Preserve source examples exactly; do not normalize `ê`, `dê`, or `wê` in learner material automatically.
4. Keep Standard Kurmanji and Behdînî negative-future behavior distinct.
5. These sources do not establish the precise distribution for every Kurmanji dialect or every speaker group.
