## 6. Critical distinctions to protect during revision

### 6.1 Indefinite suffix, oblique case and ezafe
The reviewed forms `gund-ek-î` and `jin-ek-ê` are indefinite oblique forms in the relevant paradigm. In `bajar-ek-î mezin`, `-î` is the masculine ezafe marker linking the head noun to the adjective. Do not identify function from the visible ending alone; teach the whole construction.

### 6.2 Case and grammatical role
Do not teach direct case as always subject or oblique case as always object. The reviewed Haig analysis distinguishes present transitive subjects, past intransitive subjects and past transitive subjects. Past transitive subjects may be oblique; explain this with complete source clauses and the appropriate alignment account.

### 6.3 Tense and aspect
`ez hatim` versus `ez dihatim`, and `min xwar` versus `min dixwar`, are presented as simple versus imperfective past contrasts. They require a clear aspect definition and context-sensitive analysis; do not collapse both into undifferentiated “past tense”.

### 6.4 Negation and questions
Keep `na-`, `ne-` and lexical `ni-` in their source-described constructions. Do not treat them as interchangeable universal negative particles. The question section needs current Standard Kurmanjî evidence before adding particle or interrogative paradigms.

### 6.5 SOVG and ergative alignment
Haig §2.7.1 describes neutral SOVG order where a goal argument is present; G means goal, including spatial goal, recipient or addressee. It is not an absolute word-order rule. `Wî mirov-î çay anî` is Haig (2018), example (32), p.131, §2.7.2, and illustrates past transitive ergative alignment. Correct the citation and the lesson's framing together.
