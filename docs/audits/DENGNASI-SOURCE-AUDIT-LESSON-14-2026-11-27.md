## Source-fidelity observations

1. The payload preserves the source's distinction between initial strong R [r] and the usual medial/final tap [ɾ], while noting that the source describes exceptions and optional spelling conventions.
2. The payload records, rather than silently resolves, the source's internal inconsistency around *birîn*: printed page 169 glosses it as cutting, while the later contrast on the same page distinguishes *birîn* (injury) from *birrîn* (cutting).
3. The payload treats L/R and N/L correspondences as historical or dialectal examples, not as unrestricted sound substitutions.
4. The payload preserves the source's qualification that S's light/heavy realizations are allophonic in Kurmanji, unlike the phonemic emphatic/plain contrast in Arabic.
5. The source's description of S on printed page 175 says “pidûyî-xişok-vizok” and describes a complete closure followed by release. That closure-release description is not a correct description of ordinary [s], which is an alveolar fricative rather than an affricate. The payload explicitly flags the source's articulatory-description inconsistency and gives the correct manner for [s].
6. The payload does not treat the book's historical etymologies as independently proven; it represents the author's proposed correspondences and preserves alternative explanations where the book gives them.
7. The payload includes the source's S chapter through page 186 but stops before the Ş chapter, which starts on page 187. This is consistent with the book's printed section boundary.
