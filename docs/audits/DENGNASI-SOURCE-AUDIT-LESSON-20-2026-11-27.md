## Source coverage

### O — long close-mid back rounded vowel (printed pages 298–303)

The payload includes:
- O as a long, close-mid/back/rounded vowel, IPA [o], with optional length marking [oː];
- Kurdish-Arabic and Cyrillic representations, and distinctions from Persian short O and Turkish/loanword spellings;
- occurrence in initial, medial and final word positions, with examples ode, oxir, ofîs, gore, hol, morîk, gelo, hevalo and Heso;
- the reported 0.95% frequency, its rarity relative to other vowels, and restrictions on initial/final O in inherited vocabulary;
- final O in vocatives, including hevalo, gelo, Ehmedo and zarokino;
- initial O in Turkish and European loans, and dialectal avoidance/repair patterns such as ode/hode/wede, ordek/werdek and oxir/wexer;
- the author's account of ol as a loan from Turkish yol, including semantic broadening;
- historical O correspondences from EW/AW, UF/UV and IW/IWA, with the source examples and comparative forms;
- Turkish Ö and European rounded-vowel adaptations, including ördek/ordek, kötü/kotî, köprü/koprî, coiffeur/kuafor and chauffeur/şofêr;
- dialectal O corresponding to Sorani U/Û, and O arising from UH/UHI/UHU/UHE sequences;
- eastern dialect shifts of O to Ü or Î, with kon, rovî and kovî examples;
- O in inflectional/verb-stem patterns, especially the present stems şo- and doş-.

### U — short near-high near-back rounded vowel (printed pages 304–309)

The payload includes:
- U as a short, near-high, near-back, rounded vowel, IPA [ʊ], distinguished from Kurdish Û [uː] and Turkish U;
- Kurdish-Arabic and Cyrillic representation and comparison with Turkish, Persian and Arabic vowel notation;
- occurrence at initial, medial and final positions, with umêd, umet, Urdin, guh, kurd, quling, du, ku and tu;
- the source's note that initial U in several examples is from borrowed vocabulary, while forms such as Umer/umir begin with Eyn rather than a U vowel;
- the source's reported 1.3% frequency and its observation that U is rare initially and finally in inherited words;
- alternations between U and I, including awur/awir, bulbul/bilbil, cuhû/cihû, dukan/dikan, fulan/filan, huner/hiner, nuha/niha, tu/ti and zuha/ziha/ziwa;
- the restriction against changing U to I after G, K, Q, X and Ẍ, with examples such as guh/gih, kul/kil, qumar/qimar, Kurdistan/Kirdistan and xulam/xilam;
- the distinction between inherited U and U in Arabic-origin loans, and preservation of U after K, Q and X in kufir, qumar and xulam;
- historical and dialectal correspondences, including guh/guş/nguş, gul/gol/gil/vil and gur(g)/gorg/gurg/verg/werg;
- U/I correspondences between Central and Eastern Kurmanji, and alternations among UH/UHI/UHU/UHE and O across Kurmanji and Sorani;
- the limited effect of inflection on U, alongside present-tense stems şo- and doş-.

### Û — long high back rounded vowel and dialectal realizations (printed pages 310–314)

The payload includes:
- Û as a long high/back/rounded vowel, generally [uː] in most Kurmanji and Sorani varieties;
- Kurdish-Arabic spelling with two waw letters and distinctions from Turkish U;
- distribution in medial, final and rare initial positions, including bûk, cûn/cûtin, çûn, dûr, gûn, kûp, Mûş, Nûh, şûr, tûr, zûr, bû, çû and zû;
- the special discussion of dû and the variants Yûsif/Ûsiv, Yûnis/Ûnis, ûr, Urdin, Ukrayna and Uganda;
- the dialectal realization of Û as front rounded Ü [yː], and the use of -wî- in Behdînan and other Eastern Kurmanji varieties;
- examples such as bûk/bwîk/bîk, dûr/dwîr/dîr, zû/zwî/zî, mû/mwî/mî, qanûn/qanwîn and tabût/tabwît;
- the distribution of Ü/-wî- across Behdînan, southern Kurdish varieties (Feyli, Kalhuri, Kirmaşanî and related dialects), Şahzênî/Şêxbizinî, some Zazaki varieties and Western Kurmanji;
- the source's observation that this realization was not identified in Sorani varieties;
- the author's discussion of whether Ü is due to Turkic influence, including the geographic and sociolinguistic counterarguments;
- orthographic recommendations: preserve Û in standard writing despite dialectal realizations as Ü, -wî- or Î, treating Û as a polyphonic letter.

## Source-fidelity observations

1. The payload follows the book's sequence O → U → Û and covers the phonetic descriptions, distributions, historical developments, dialectal patterns and orthographic recommendations across pages 298–314.
2. The lesson boundary is confirmed: the diphthong section begins on printed page 315 and is outside lesson 20.
3. The payload preserves the source's key distinction between O [oː], U [ʊ] and Û [uː], and does not conflate U with Û.
4. The payload retains the author's restriction on U → I after G/K/Q/X/Ẍ and the distinction between within-dialect alternation and correspondences between dialects.
5. The source's broad label “kurdiya başûrî” is qualified as Feyli, Kalhuri, Kirmaşanî and related varieties, not simply all varieties in the south of the Kurdistan Region. The payload preserves this important qualification.
6. **Source typo flagged in payload:** the source paragraph on printed page 305 says “rêjeya herfa Ê ... 1,3%” although the surrounding discussion is explicitly about U. The payload assigns 1.3% to U and records the source's apparent typographical error in a footnote.
7. **Payload heading typo to correct in a future content revision:** in the section on O's dialectal development, the payload uses the heading “Peydabûna devokî ya dengê Î”; the source section is discussing O. This appears to be a payload heading error, not a source claim.
8. The payload includes a source-specific footnote and examples on U's etymological origin and the G/K/Q/X/Ẍ restriction. These historical explanations are represented as the author's analysis, not independently established etymologies.
9. The source's frequency figures (O and U) are reported as given; the corpus and counting method were not independently recreated.
10. This is a structured comparison of source topics, examples and payload text, not a line-by-line collation of every lexical item or independent verification of every reconstruction.
