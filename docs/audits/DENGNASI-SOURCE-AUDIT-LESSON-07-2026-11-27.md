## Specific source-fidelity observations

1. **The main orthographic distinction is preserved.** The source argues for pronunciation-based spelling of lexicalized simple words such as *kuştin*, but retains the roots of connected words and compounds in spelling where the assimilation is limited to fast speech. The payload keeps these as separate cases rather than turning them into one universal rule.

2. **The suffixed-word discussion is not overgeneralized.** The source explicitly says there is no absolute rule for all suffixed forms. The payload preserves the contrast between long-established, lexicalized forms (*dezgeh, dezgir, dezgirtî*) and forms newly created in writing, where preserving the root may be preferable.

3. **The N→M explanation matches the source's place-of-articulation account.** The payload correctly relates M and B to bilabial articulation and contrasts them with the place of articulation of N.

4. **The V/H comparison is presented as the author's analysis.** The payload attributes the comparison to the source and does not present the proposed similarity of manner as an independent universal law.

5. **The analogy section preserves the source's limits.** It treats *heşt* and *neh* as the author's historical explanations, notes that analogy is not obligatory, and keeps *bin/ban* as a tentative prompt rather than a proven case.

6. **Dialect and historical tables are retained with qualifications.** The payload notes missing table entries may reflect lack of documentation rather than proof that a form does not exist, and distinguishes the author's proposed explanation of Zazaki postpositions as an opinion.
