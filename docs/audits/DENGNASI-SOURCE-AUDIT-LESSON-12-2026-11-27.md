## Source coverage

### M: articulation, distribution and historical changes (printed pages 147–149)
The payload includes:
- M as a voiced bilabial nasal [m], with both lips closing and air passing through the nose;
- Kurdish-Arabic and Cyrillic representations and IPA notation;
- word-initial, medial and final examples and the source's reported 2.3% frequency;
- phonetic proximity to N, B/P, W and V/F, distinguishing place of articulation and nasal airflow;
- postvocalic M → Kurmanji V and Sorani W in inherited words, with *nav/naw/nam*, *gav/(hen)gaw/gam*, and *nivêj/niwêj/nimac/nemaz* comparisons;
- the qualification that M after consonants is retained, with *germ* and *genim*; the latter is linked to the historical presence of D/T after M;
- the distinction between true M → V/W and words whose earlier consonant was B or V, such as *ziman*;
- M → B in *mismar/bizmar* and dialectal *misilman/bisilman/bisirman*, and the M/B correspondence in *ra-mûs-an* and Persian *bûsê/bûsîden*.

### N: articulation, distribution, NG and sound changes (printed pages 150–153)
The payload includes:
- N as an alveolar nasal [n], with articulation and script representations;
- initial, medial and final examples and the reported 9.4% frequency, the most frequent consonant in the cited Kurmanji writing sample;
- the distinction between NG as two sounds in most Kurmanji dialects, with a syllable boundary possible between them, and [ŋ] in some Sorani and Southern Kurdish dialects;
- the source's definition of “Southern Kurdish” here as Feyli, Kelhuri and Kirmaşani varieties, not Kurmanji or Central Kurdish/Sorani;
- [ŋg] in Kurmanji when N assimilates in place to the following G/K but the stop remains pronounced, compared with [ŋ] where the stop merges;
- N's historical presence and its phonetic proximity to M and L;
- N/M alternation, especially before B/P, including *neke/meke*, *em dixwin/mâ mîxorîm*, *Istanbul/Stembol*, and *jinbab/jinbav → jimbab/jimbav*;
- N → L dialectal forms such as *nimêj/limêj*, *nivîn/livîn*, and the historical relationship of *li/le* to earlier *ni/ne*;
- epenthetic N in *tac/tanc*, *çok/çong*, *firset/firsend*, *bêder/bênder*, *amanc/armanc*, *reng*, *bang*, *deng*, and *veng*, including the source's observation that N often appears before G, C and D;
- final N addition in *dîsa/dîsan*, *çawa/çawan* and *wisa/wisan*;
- final N deletion in inflected plurals with *-an* in spoken Kurmanji, and in *-ên* in some dialects but not all, with the source's examples.

## Source-fidelity observations

1. The payload correctly separates postvocalic historical M → V/W from M that is retained after consonants, and distinguishes these from words whose earlier sound was B or V rather than M.
2. The explanation of *genim* preserves the source's historical qualification: the following D/T at an earlier stage is relevant to the retention of M.
3. The payload preserves the difference between [ŋ] and Kurmanji [ŋg], and does not incorrectly claim that every written NG sequence is one phoneme in Kurmanji.
4. The “Southern Kurdish” label is explicitly restricted to Feyli, Kelhuri and Kirmaşani varieties, following the book's footnote.
5. N → L examples are presented as dialectal variants; the payload states that N forms remain standard or widespread.
6. Epenthetic N and final N deletion are qualified by lexical, dialectal or morphological environment rather than generalized as universal sound changes.
7. The figures 2.3% for M and 9.4% for N are attributed to the book's cited letter-frequency study, not represented as fresh measurements.
