## Source coverage reviewed

### 1. Classification, scope and method (printed pages 373–376)

The payload includes:
- The distinction between lexical, morphological and phonological differences.
- The examples niha/êsta, kitêbên min/kitêbekanim and çav/çaw.
- The focus on phonological differences between Kurmanji and Sorani in this lesson.
- The study's broader comparative frame, which also includes Southern Kurdish and Zazaki in later sections.
- The synchronic and diachronic approach, with historical changes noted selectively rather than reconstructed in full.
- The author's warning that examples are representative, not universal across all dialects.
- The explicit non-hierarchical stance: no dialect is presented as more correct, modern or advanced.
- The brief review of earlier scholarship and the sources used for Kurmanji, Sorani, Southern Kurdish and Zazaki examples.

### 2. Kurmanji V versus Sorani W/M/V (printed page 377)

The payload preserves:
- The general absence or rarity of V in inherited Sorani words.
- Sorani W corresponding to Kurmanji V in çav/çaw, nav/naw, sêv/sêw and şev/şew.
- Sorani M in dev/dem and ev/eme.
- Retention of V in borrowings such as evîn, mirov, televizyon and vîdyo.
- The exception hevde and the note that dem exists in Sorani but kat is more usual for “time”.
- The historical account of postvocalic V/W deriving from earlier M or B.

### 3. Sorani RD versus Kurmanji R or RT (printed page 378)

The payload includes the main comparison table:
- birdin/birin, kirdin/kirin, mirdin/mirin, pird/pir, sard/sar and xwardin/xwarin.
- Related forms in Zazaki and Persian.
- The author's historical account: earlier RT, retained as RD in Sorani and reduced to R in Kurmanji.
- The counter-pattern bijardin/bijartin, where Kurmanji has RT.
- The qualification that RD is not present in every Sorani word corresponding to Kurmanji R.
- The note clarifying that pird/pir here means “bridge”, not “many”.

### 4. Initial ST clusters (printed page 379)

The payload covers:
- Sorani's general avoidance of initial consonant clusters.
- Initial E insertion in forms such as stêr/estêre, stûr/estûr, sto/esto and Stembol/Estemboł.
- T deletion in standin/stendin versus sendin.
- T deletion at word-final position in dest/des.
- Borrowed forms such as stran and studyo that retain initial ST.
- The examples şkeft/eşkewt and the exceptions spî, şkandin and şkestin.

### 5. Aspiration and emphatic consonants (printed pages 379–380)

The payload includes the Kurmanji contrast between aspirated and unaspirated initial Ç/K/P/T, with the source's examples:
- çar/çav
- kurdî/kirin
- perde/pênc
- têr/te

It also retains the Sorani generalization that these consonants are aspirated initially and usually unaspirated medially/finally, while Kurmanji can have unaspirated forms initially. The separate emphatic/heavy variants of P/S/T/Z in some Kurmanji dialects are not conflated with aspiration.

### 6. H correspondences (printed pages 380–381)

The payload covers:
- Word-final H in Kurmanji and its general absence in inherited Sorani forms: deh/de, neh/no, guh/gwê, reh/rih versus reg/rîş.
- The distinction between the H of heval and the distinct H in Hesen, including the possibility of the latter in Sorani Arabic loans.
- EH to A correspondences: pehn/pan, tehl/tał, mehr/mare, and bêhn/bon.
- Initial H deletion in hesp/esp, hêsan/asan, hejmartin/jimardin and hesin/asin.
- Cases where Sorani has H/HE while Kurmanji has no H or has eyn: war/hewar, jar/hejar, ewr/hewr and erd/herd/’erz.
- The author's decision not to develop the historical origin of H in this article.

### 7. I/E and Î/Ê vowel correspondences (printed pages 381–382)

The payload includes:
- Kurmanji I versus Sorani E in bi/be, li/le, bihar/behar, dikim/dekem and gihandin/gihiştin versus geyandin/geyiştin.
- The verbal progressive prefix di- in Kurmanji versus de-/e- in Sorani.
- The source's dialect qualification: de- is associated with Mukri and Hewlêr forms, while e- occurs in Silêmanî and Kerkûk forms; de- is more widespread in writing.
- Kurmanji Î versus Sorani Ê in Îran/Êran, kî/kê and diçînim/deçênim.
- The -în-/-ên- pattern in present stems of verbs in -andin/-endin.
- The counterexample mêvan/mîwan and the author's historical direction Ê > Î in many cases.
- Footnotes on gehandin/gehiştin, the Kurmanji t- progressive forms, the Sorani prefix variants, and the inflected form kê.

### 8. E/Ê in verbal suffixes (printed page 383)

The payload includes the third-person singular present/future verbal suffix contrast:
- diçîne/deçênê
- distîne/desênê
- dixwîne/dexwênê
- dinivîse/denûsê

### 9. Light and dark L (printed pages 383–384)

The payload includes:
- The light/dark L distinction in both varieties.
- The Kurmanji examples law, lez, lêv, lîmon, lome, lûfik versus silav, welat and sal.
- The difference in phonemic status and spelling conventions between Kurmanji and Sorani.
- Sorani Ł corresponding to Kurmanji L in bełê/belê, dił/dil, guł/gul, hełbijardin/hilbijartin and mał/mal.
- The historical account of inherited RD becoming L in Kurmanji and Ł in Sorani, and the different account for Arabic loans.
- The dialectal Sorani realization of Ł as R, and comparable examples in some Kurmanji dialects.

### 10. Kurmanji Û/UH versus Sorani WÊ (printed page 384)

The payload includes the correspondence after C, D, G, K and N, with guh/gwê, nû/nwê, gûz/gwêz, dijûn/cwên and kû/kwê(we), and the historical connection with earlier Iranian -ew-.

### 11. XWE- versus XO- (printed pages 384–385)

The payload includes:
- xwe/xo, xweş/xoş and xwelî/xoł(emêş).
- The qualification that Sorani does not have XWE-, but not every Kurmanji XWE- corresponds to XO-.
- xwedî/xwedan/xudan/xweyî versus xawen, and xwendin/xwandin versus xwêndin.
- The author's account of XWE- as older and XO- as a later development, including dialectal Kurmanji XO-/XU- variants.

### 12. G and K pronunciation in Sorani (printed pages 385–386)

The payload includes:
- The cross-language analogy for G and C before different vowels, with English, French and Italian examples.
- The Sorani pattern in which G before Ê/WÊ may be pronounced [c] and K may be pronounced [ç].
- The Kurmanji/Sorani comparison table: guh/gwê/[cwê], kî/kê/kê [kê], and kû/kwê(we)/[çwê]we.
- The historical note relating Kurdish çi/çî and kî/kê to the reconstructed Indo-European form *kʷis.
