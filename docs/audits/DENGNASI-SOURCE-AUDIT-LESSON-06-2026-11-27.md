## Specific source-fidelity observations

1. **An internal inconsistency in the book is correctly preserved and disclosed.** On printed page 47, the source lists *xw-* among native Kurmanji onset clusters. On printed page 48, its summary list omits *xw-* and lists *sp-*, *st-*, *şk-* and possibly *şt-*. The payload explicitly records this inconsistency rather than silently choosing one version.

2. **The exceptional *qest* form is correctly distinguished.** The source says the general direction of voicing assimilation is from the later consonant to the preceding one, while *qest* is an exception in which the later consonant changes. The payload keeps *qesd*, *qezd*, and *qest* distinct and labels the last as exceptional.

3. **The payload preserves the important phonetic/orthographic distinction in syllabification.** It includes the source's examples where written division and spoken/morphological division differ, including *saet*, *berav*, and *binavkirin*.

4. **The payload keeps the scope of the corpus claims appropriately limited.** It describes onset and coda patterns as source observations about Kurmanji and distinguishes native forms, dialectal forms, and loans.

5. **The lesson is a structured paraphrase, not a page-for-page transcription.** Several long example lists and comparative passages are summarized in the main sections, while a supplementary section restores many of the source's detailed examples and footnotes. This is a reasonable structure, but final completeness should still be checked against the rendered lesson, especially that supplementary material is visible and not accidentally hidden by the page template.
