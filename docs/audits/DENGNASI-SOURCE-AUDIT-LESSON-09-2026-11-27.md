## Source-fidelity observations

1. The payload preserves the author's central qualification that F is not simply an Arabic sound: it is also inherited or historically present in Iranian and wider Indo-European material.
2. The payload distinguishes inherited P → F developments from Arabic-mediated P → F changes, and does not turn these into a universal rule.
3. F/V is presented as a voicing contrast, while the *ketin* and FT/WT examples are kept as morphophonological or dialectal developments.
4. The payload preserves the source's uncertainty around the etymology of *vala/vale* rather than presenting one origin as certain.
5. G's inherited history and its later correspondences with V/W, K, and Y are distinguished; the examples are not generalized into exceptionless sound laws.
6. The source's frequency percentages are attributed to the author's earlier study, not presented as new measurements.
7. Historical reconstructions and etymologies are reported as the book author's analysis. This audit checks correspondence to the book, not independent validity of every reconstruction.
