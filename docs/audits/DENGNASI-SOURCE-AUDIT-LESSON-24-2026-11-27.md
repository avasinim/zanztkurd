## Source coverage reviewed

### 1. Meaning and scope of “Southern Kurdish” (printed page 387)

The payload preserves the author's definition of “kurdiya başûrî” as a group of dialects spoken in southern parts of Eastern Kurdistan and parts of Southern Kurdistan, rather than a synonym for all Kurdish varieties spoken in Southern Kurdistan. It names Feylî, Kelhurî and Kirmaşanî as parts rather than exhaustive equivalents of the group. It distinguishes this group from Hewramî/Goranî, Lekî and Lurî, and notes the author's claim that it is closer linguistically to Kurmanji and Sorani. The payload also retains the note that Southern Kurdish differs from Sorani in phonology and grammar, including the author's statement about ergativity.

### 2. Kurmanji B versus Southern Kurdish W (printed page 388)

The payload includes the main comparisons:
- baran / waran
- kitêb / kitaw
- xirab / xiraw
- xerîb / xerîw

It retains the author's distinction between inherited Iranian vocabulary and Arabic loans, and the historical account of W becoming B in Kurmanji, Sorani and Persian. It also includes the postvocalic B/P > V/W discussion with sêp/sêb/sêv/sêw, şeb/şev/şew, x(v)ab/xew and ebr/ewr/hewr. The regional Kurmanji exception in footnote 284 (Serhed and Central Anatolia, kitêw/kitêv) is included as a qualification rather than generalized.

### 3. Kurmanji V versus Southern Kurdish W or M (printed pages 388–389)

The payload includes:
- çav / çaw
- mêvan / mîwan
- nav / naw
- zava / zawa
- zevî / zewî, with the regional zeẅ/zeü variants.

It retains the source's distinction between W in most Southern Kurdish dialects and M in some varieties under Persian and/or Hewramî influence, as well as the historical account of inherited Iranian M becoming V in Kurmanji and W in Sorani/Southern Kurdish.

### 4. Kurmanji Û versus Southern Kurdish Ü, Î or retained Û (printed page 389)

The payload includes zû/zû/z ü, dûr/dûr/dür, rû/rû/rü and mû/mû/mü, with the dialectal Î variants zî, dîr, rî and mî. It also retains the Eastern Kurmanji/Behdînî parallels (bük/bwîk, dür/dwîr, zü/zwî, mü/mwî and Dihok forms bîk/dîr/zî/mî), and notes that some Southern Kurdish dialects preserve Û.

### 5. Kurmanji O versus Southern Kurdish Û (printed page 390)

The payload includes roj/rûj, zor/zûr, kor/kûr and dew/do/dû. It summarizes the author's vowel-shift explanation and the Eastern Kurmanji parallels roj > rûj/ruj and got > gût/gut.

### 6. Southern Kurdish RD versus Kurmanji R (printed pages 390–391)

The payload includes birdin/birin, kirdin/kirin, mirdin/mirin, pird/pir, sard/sar and xwardin/xwarin. It preserves the author's distinction that Southern Kurdish and Sorani retain RD in these examples while Kurmanji has reduced the cluster to R.

### 7. Light L versus dark Ł (printed page 391)

The payload includes keçel/keçeł, dil/dił, gul/guł and bela/beła. It retains the dialectal qualification that Ł is absent in some Southern Kurdish varieties, especially Kirmaşan and Mendelî, and the source's separate historical explanations for inherited words and Arabic loans.

### 8. Aspiration of Ç, K, P and T (printed page 391)

The payload correctly records the source's contrast: in Southern Kurdish, as in Sorani and unlike Kurmanji and Zazaki, Ç/K/P/T are aspirated at the beginning of words and generally unaspirated medially and finally, especially in unstressed syllables.

### 9. Kurmanji ST versus Southern Kurdish S/ES/HES (printed pages 391–392)

The payload includes:
- standin/stendin, sendin, sendin
- stêr, estêre, hesare
- dest, dest/des, des
- şêst, şêst/şest, şeṣ
- zivistan, zistan, zimsan
- Kurdistan, Kurdistan, Kursan

It preserves the difference between Sorani, which may delete T or insert E to yield EST, and Southern Kurdish, which in the described dialects may both delete T and add E or HE. It also includes the source's historical note that ST occurred in initial, medial and final positions in Iranian languages.

### 10. Kurmanji A/EH versus Southern Kurdish YE (printed page 392)

The payload includes kanî/kehnî, kanî, kyenî; tehl/tal, tał, tyeł; and nizanim, nazanim, nyezanim. It retains the footnote's historical comparison of nazanim with nakim, naxwim and nabêjim.

### 11. Kurmanji NG versus Southern Kurdish Ň (printed page 393)

The payload includes deng/deň, reng/reň and bang/baň, and correctly explains Ň [ŋ] as the result of N and G merging. It retains the qualification that some Sorani dialects also have Ň, while NG remains dominant in Sorani.

### 12. Sorani XO- versus Kurmanji/Southern Kurdish XWE- (printed page 393)

The payload includes xwe/xo, xweş/xoş and the historical explanation that XWE- is older and became XO- in Sorani. It retains the source's note that Southern Kurdish accepts XWE-/XUE- like Kurmanji.
