# Dengnasî — Source audit, Lesson 25
Date: 2026-11-27
Source: Husein Muhammed, *Dengnasî di kurdî de*, printed pages 394–401 (PDF pages 422–429).

## Boundary
Printed p.394 begins “Cudahiyên dengnasî di navbera kurmancî û zazakî de”; printed p.402 begins the next Kurmanji–Turkish comparison. The manifest range 394–401 is correct.

## Source sections read
The source pages reviewed cover:
- Introductory distinction: the two varieties share phonetic inventories but use some shared sounds differently in cognate words; the point is phonetic values, not merely the Latin alphabet.
- Kurmanji B versus Zazaki V in native word-initial position: ba/va, baran/varan, berf/vewre, bîst/vîst.
- Kurmanji V versus Zazaki M/W after vowels: nav/name, gav/gam, av/aw, şev/şew; footnote 287 notes dialectal forms awe/awi/awk and highlights W.
- Zazaki RD versus Kurmanji R: berdene/birdin/birin, kerdene/kirdin/kirin, merdene/mirdin/mirin, pird/pir and werdene/xwardin/xwarin; footnote 288 distinguishes “bridge” from “many/full”.
- Zazaki E versus Kurmanji I: berdene/birin, kerdene/kirin, merdene/mirin, wesar/bihar and berz/bilind.
- Kurmanji XW versus Zazaki W: xweş/weş, xwarin/werdene, xwendin/wendene, xweh-way; the pronoun xwe is an explicit exception, with Zazaki xo/xwu.
- Kurmanji J versus Zazaki C/Z: jin/cenî, roj/roc, jîn-jiyan/ciwayene and jî/zî; footnote 289 explains the Vate team's standardization choice for roj.
- Kurmanji G versus Zazaki V: gul/vilî, gur(g)/verg, gotin/vatene, alongside related Iranian-language comparisons.
- Kurmanji H versus Zazaki Ø or S: hestî/este, hejmar/amar, hêstir-hesp/estor-istor, havîn/amnan; and deh/des, bihar/wesar, including the numbered forms yewndes, diyes, hiryes and the reverse S/H pattern in sê/hirê.
- Kurmanji ST- versus Zazaki EST-/AST-: stêr(k)/estare-astare.
- Zazaki A versus Kurmanji Î/Ê in present/future verb stems, with ez di-min-im/ez man-en-a, ew di-xwin-e/o wan-en-o and di-bê-n/di-bêj-in versus va-n-ê; plus hêk/hak and kitêb/kitab.
- Kurmanji H versus Zazaki Ş: guh/goş and têhnî/tihnî/tînî/tî versus teyşan.
- Kurmanji K versus Zazaki H: ker/her and kanî/henî, with the historical X comparison across Kurdish, Zazaki, Hewrami, Persian and older Iranian forms.
- Kurmanji S versus Zazaki Ş: rast/raşt.

## Important source caveats
- The section explicitly says shared sounds can have different distribution and use; do not imply that the varieties simply have different phoneme inventories.
- The source's claims about historical directions (e.g. V/W/M, RD/R, XW/W, J/C, G/V and X>K/H) are the author's explanations and should remain attributed to the source.
- The Zazaki pronoun xo/xwu is an exception to the XW > W comparison.
- The source gives both H deletion and H > S patterns and also the reverse S > H example in sê/hirê; these must not be collapsed into one unconditional rule.
- The printed p.400 heading “Kurmancî K / Zazakî H” is followed by a sentence that repeats Ş instead of H. This appears to be an internal editorial slip; verify the rendered payload flags or preserves the source wording rather than silently rewriting the source.
- The final comparison is only a few examples (rast/raşt) on p.401; do not expand it beyond the book.
