## Source coverage reviewed

The payload contains the main comparison topics and examples found in the source:

1. **Shared sounds, different lexical use (printed p.394):** preserves the author's distinction between phonetic inventory and how shared sounds are used in cognate words. It retains the aspirated/unaspirated Ç, K, P, T distinction and emphatic P/T variants.
2. **Kurmanji B / Zazaki V (p.394):** ba/va, baran/varan, berf-befr/vewre, bîst/vîst; also preserves the contrast with Sorani/Persian B and Hewrami/Southern Kurdish W, and the historical V/W > B account as the author's explanation.
3. **Kurmanji V / Zazaki M or W (pp.394–395):** nav/name, gav/gam, av/aw, şev/şew; correctly distinguishes inherited M > Kurmanji V from P > B > V/W in the şev/şew set. Footnote 287's dialect forms awe, awi, awk are retained.
4. **Zazaki RD / Kurmanji R (p.395):** berdene/birdin/birin, kerdene/kirdin/kirin, merdene/mirdin/mirin, pird/pird/pir, werdene/xwardin/xwarin. Footnote 288's semantic distinction for pir (“bridge”) is retained.
5. **Zazaki E / Kurmanji I (p.396):** berdene/birin, kerdene/kirin, merdene/mirin, wesar/bihar, berz/bilind, with the source's historical interpretation and qualification.
6. **Kurmanji XW / Zazaki W (pp.396–397):** weş/xweş, werdene/xwarin, wendene/xwendin-xwandin, way/xweh-xuşk; the exception for the pronoun xwe (Zazaki xo/xwu) is preserved.
7. **Kurmanji J / Zazaki C (p.397):** jin/cenî, roj/roc, jîn-jiyan/ciwayene and jî/zî. Footnote 289 about Vate's selection of roj is included with its stated reason.
8. **Kurmanji G / Zazaki V (p.397):** gul/vilî, gur(g)/verg, gotin/vatene, plus the author's historical comparison.
9. **Kurmanji H / Zazaki Ø and S (pp.397–398):** hestî/este, hejmar/amar, hêstir-hesp/estor-istor, havîn/amnan-hamnan; deh/des and bihar/wesar; the 11–19 forms and the exception sê/hirê are included.
10. **Kurmanji ST / Zazaki EST-/AST- (p.399):** stêr(k)/estare-astare and the source's comparison with other Iranian languages.
11. **Zazaki A / Kurmanji Î or Ê (p.399):** verb forms ez di-mîn-im / ez man-en-a, ew di-xwîn-e / o wan-en-o, ew di-bê-n-di-bêj-in / ê va-n-ê; and hêk/hak, kitêb/kitab.
12. **H/Ş, K/H and S/Ş (pp.400–401):** guh/goş and têhnî/teyşan; ker/her and kanî/henî; rast/raşt. The payload correctly identifies an internal source-heading/body inconsistency: the source's heading says “Kurmancî K / Zazakî H” and the examples support K/H, but the explanatory sentence immediately below mistakenly repeats H/Ş. The payload flags this as a source typo rather than silently treating it as a new H/Ş rule.

## Fidelity notes and limitations

- The payload retains the major comparison sets, dialect qualifications and the three relevant footnotes (287–289) visible in the source pages.
- Historical explanations are represented as the book author's interpretations, not independently asserted as settled linguistic fact.
- The payload is a structured lesson adaptation, not a diplomatic transcription. Exact word-for-word preservation of every sentence, punctuation mark, diacritic and typographic detail has not been certified.

- The source's K/H explanatory typo is explicitly disclosed; the payload's correction is supported by the heading, examples and following historical explanation.
