## Source coverage

### P — articulation, distribution and phonetic contrasts (printed pages 155–158)

The payload includes:
- P as a voiceless bilabial plosive, formed by closing and releasing both lips; IPA [p];

- the distinction between front/light and back/heavy P as allophonic, not phonemic, with vowel-conditioned tendencies and dialectal variation.

### P — historical development and alternations (printed pages 159–162)

The payload includes:
- retention of Proto-Indo-European P in *pê*, *pênc* and *pir*;
- P from historical W/V/U in *pêl*, *por(r)/pûrt*, *hesp* and *dilop*;
- initial B → P in colloquial borrowings such as *pisqilêt*, *paysikil*, *pas* and *patrî*;
- the source's examples of *bi* + pronoun becoming *pê*, and dialectal P forms of *bi* and verbal prefix *bi-*;
- medial/final P → B → V in Kurmanji and corresponding W forms in Sorani, with comparisons such as *av/aw*, *sêv/sêw* and *heft/hewt*;
- P → F through Arabic mediation in examples such as *fincan*, *fisteq*, *faris/farisî*, and the source's wider route of transmission for *fisteq*.

### Q — articulation, distribution and historical development (printed pages 163–167)

The payload includes:
- Q as a voiceless uvular plosive [q], with its place and manner of articulation and script representations;
- the distinction between Kurdish [q] and the differing values of the letter Q in several European orthographies;
- word-initial, medial and final examples and the cited 0.27% frequency in Kurmanji writing;
- the source's account of Q's absence as this phoneme in many older Indo-European and Iranian languages, alongside its prevalence in modern Iranian languages;
- influence from Semitic languages, especially Arabic, and Turkic languages;
- the source's Turkic K → Kurdish Q tendency conditioned by back vowels in the source-language form, with examples including *qazan*, *qazanc/qezenc*, *qere-*, *qonax/qûnax*, *qurm*, *qeytan*, *qalind*, *quzilqurt* and *qûm*;
- the source's qualifications and exceptions, including *kon/kûn* and *kol/qol*;
- Q/K alternations in Arabic loans, Q → X before certain voiceless consonants, and examples of Q in words with Iranian, Semitic or uncertain transmission histories;
- the Q consonant-chart placement and the historical Hawar orthography note distinguishing the earlier use of K and Q.

## Source-fidelity observations

1. The payload follows the source's separation of P's aspirated/unaspirated contrast (described as phonemic in Kurmanji and Zazaki) from light/heavy P (described as allophonic).
2. It preserves the source's positional and dialectal qualifications rather than turning tendencies into exceptionless rules.
3. The historical P discussion retains the source's distinct pathways: Proto-Indo-European retention, W/V/U correspondences, colloquial B → P, medial/final P → B → V/W, and Arabic-mediated P → F.
4. The Q section preserves the source's two major influence pathways (Semitic and Turkic) and the stated exceptions to the K → Q tendency.
5. The payload includes additional recap/summary sections that restate material already covered. These are not new source claims, but the rendered lesson should be reviewed for unnecessary repetition and heading hierarchy before release.
6. The printed page 155 contains an apparent IPA transcription error: it prints [b] in the sentence identifying P, although the section defines P as voiceless, the same page describes it as voiceless, and the consonant chart on printed page 162 places P in the voiceless row. The payload uses [p], which is the internally consistent value. This is an explicit correction of an apparent source typo, not an unmarked alteration of the source's phonological account.
