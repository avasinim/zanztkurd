The payload includes:

- the source's discussion of T as a past-stem marker in verbs, contrasted with present/future stems;

- the source's explanation that apparent T/Ş alternations in certain verbs are morphologically/historically conditioned, not a direct sound substitution;
- footnotes 145–148 and their limits.

## Source-fidelity observations

1. The lesson preserves the distinction between Ş [ʃ], a fricative, and T [t], a stop. It does not collapse their different manners of articulation.
2. The payload accurately reflects the author's warning that the T/Ş pattern in *firotin/firoş-* and *dotin/doş-* should not be described as a direct historical T ↔ Ş sound change.
3. The payload distinguishes T aspiration (described by the source as phonemic in Kurmanji and Zazaki) from light/heavy T (described as allophonic in Kurmanji). These are separate phonetic dimensions.
4. The payload retains the source's qualifications around etymology, dialect distribution and loanword aspiration rather than presenting every correspondence as exceptionless.
5. The lesson's declared printed-page range 187–202 matches the source boundary. It does not absorb the next V chapter, which begins on printed page 203.
6. The source's articulatory description of Ş on printed page 187 uses a closure-and-release explanation while naming it “xişok-vizok”. This is internally inconsistent for [ʃ], which is a voiceless postalveolar fricative, not an affricate. The payload explicitly flags the discrepancy and supplies the correct phonetic classification.
7. The source's historical reconstructions and borrowing pathways are represented as the author's analyses; this audit does not independently prove every etymology.

## Assessment

The source-page boundary is confirmed.
