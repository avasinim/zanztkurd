## Source-fidelity observations

1. The payload correctly distinguishes aspirated and unaspirated K and preserves the source's minimal example *ka*. It does not treat aspiration as a merely stylistic pronunciation difference.
2. The payload preserves the historical distinction between K retained from Proto-Indo-European and K that changed to S or Ç. The *kî/çi* contrast is explicitly explained.
3. The payload includes the source's caution that light and dark L may not be separate phonemes in Kurmanji; the *çil/çił* comparison is qualified because Ç differs in aspiration too.
4. The payload distinguishes dialectal L forms from standard/widespread N forms in *limêj/nimêj*, *livîn/nivîn*, and *loş/noş*.
5. The two forms/functions written *lê* are distinguished: the prepositional form related to *li wê/li wî/li wan* is not conflated with the conjunction meaning “but/however,” which the source relates to Arabic.
6. The payload reproduces the main tables and comparative examples in prose and structured tables. Some fine-grained source details are compressed, but the central historical and phonetic arguments are represented.
7. The frequency figures 3.8% for K and 2.1% for L are attributed to the book's cited research, not presented as new corpus measurements.
