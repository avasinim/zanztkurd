## Source-fidelity observations

1. The payload maintains the source's distinction between synchronic phonemic contrasts and historical sound correspondences. For example, it does not treat every C/Ç or Ç/J/Z relation as a productive rule.
2. The source's corpus percentages for B, C, Ç, and D are explicitly attributed to the author's earlier letter-frequency study rather than represented as newly measured data.
3. The payload distinguishes B/P voicing from P aspiration and emphatic quality; for Ç, it retains the source's claim that aspiration can distinguish two phonemes.
4. For D, the payload distinguishes the phonemic D/T voicing contrast from the non-phonemic light/emphatic T variation.
5. Historical etymologies and comparative reconstructions are framed as the book author's analysis. This audit checks correspondence to the book, not independent validity of every reconstruction.
6. The lesson includes a supplementary section for examples and footnotes, rather than reproducing all 36 printed pages line by line. A final rendered-page check is still needed to confirm that the supplementary section is visible and accessible in the lesson UI.
