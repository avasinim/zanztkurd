## Source structure observed

The source proceeds through:
1. definitions and examples distinguishing letter, sound, homophony, and polyphony;
2. whether homophony occurs in Kurdish writing, including source-language spellings, proper names, loanwords, R/RR, and U/W;
3. the prevalence of polyphony in Kurdish writing, including lists of single-sound and multi-sound consonant letters;
4. examples and descriptions of R, H, S, X, Z, L, W, Ç, K, P, and T.

The author distinguishes ordinary Kurdish spelling from rare or editorial alternative glyphs, and repeatedly qualifies dialectal or lexical variation.

## Payload comparison

The current payload follows the same broad sequence and includes all 11 consonant topics named in the source. It preserves several important distinctions:
- homophony is not the same as alternative spelling preferences such as R/RR;
- W can represent consonantal [w] generally and vowel-like [u] in limited environments such as -XW-;
- S and L quality cannot always be predicted from the following vowel;
- Ç and K aspiration contrasts, and P/T aspiration and quality distinctions, are presented as phonemic contrasts in Kurmanji and Zazaki where the source says so;
- rare P/T combinations are qualified as scarce or uncertain rather than presented as common patterns.
