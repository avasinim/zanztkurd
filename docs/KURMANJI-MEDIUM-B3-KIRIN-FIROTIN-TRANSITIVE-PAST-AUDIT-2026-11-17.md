# Kurmancî B3 — kirîn û firotin: distinct lexemes, stem alternations and transitive past (2026-11-17)

## Scope and safety
Documentation-only linguistic audit. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, migrations, production database, PR #3, or deployment was changed. The repository's existing 26 + 12 lesson inventory remains unchanged. The target remains Standard Kurmancî in Hawar-based Latin orthography; Sorani equivalents and regional variants are not merged into the core.

## Executive findings
- **kirîn** means “to buy”. It is not **kirin** (“to do/make”). The spelling difference is meaningful: **kirin** has present stem **k-**, past stem **kir-**; **kirîn** has present stem **kir-**, past stem **kirî-**.
- For **kirîn**, the present forms are **ez dikirim, tu dikirî, ew dikire, em/hûn/ew dikirin**. The simple-past transitive pattern is ergative: oblique agent, nominative direct patient, verb agrees with the patient. With a third-person singular patient, **Min ew kirî** (“I bought it”); with a plural patient, the cited conjugation table gives **min kirîn**. The form **kirî** is especially easy to mislabel: for this verb it is the past stem and also the past participle, so syntax and construction—not spelling alone—identify its function.
- **firotin** means “to sell”. Its present stem is **firoş-**, its past stem **firot-**. Present: **ez difiroşim, tu difiroşî, ew difiroşe, em/hûn/ew difiroşin**. Simple past: **min firot, te firot, wî/wê firot, me firot, we firot, wan firot** when the direct patient is third-person singular. The verb is transitive; a plural direct patient triggers plural agreement, but a full independently checked object-agreement paradigm was not located in the sources consulted.
- Both verbs follow Standard Kurmancî transitive past alignment: the agent is oblique; the direct patient is nominative and controls verbal agreement. The oblique pronoun in **min firot** is the agent, not the object.
- **kirî** for “bought” must not be confused with **kir** for “did/made”; nor should either be confused with **firot** “sold”.
- Evidence for **firotin** includes a published lexical entry with the present and past paradigms and a dated 1986 Firat Cewerî quotation in a perfect passive form **hatibûn firotin** (“had been sold”). That quotation is not a simple-past active example. No separate classical/literary active simple-past token was independently page-verified in this audit.

## 1. Kirîn — “to buy”, not kirin “to do”
**Infinitive:** kirîn  
**Present stem:** kir-  
**Past stem:** kirî-  
**Meaning:** buy.

### Keep the two verbs separate
| Infinitive | Meaning | Present stem | Past stem | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| kirin | do, make | k- | kir- | ez dikim; min kir |
| kirîn | buy | kir- | kirî- | ez dikirim; min kirî |

Morîstan's current Kurmancî stem table independently lists **kirin: k / kir** and **kirîn: kir / kirî**. Ekici's *Kurmanji Kurdish Reader* separately labels **kirîn** as “to buy” and gives the transitive past agreement construction.

### Present paradigm
- **Ez dikirim**
- **Tu dikirî**
- **Ew dikire**
- **Em dikirin**
- **Hûn dikirin**
- **Ew dikirin**

The present stem is **kir-**, with the regular present prefix **di-** and personal endings. The present is not built on the past stem **kirî-**.

### Simple past and object agreement
Ekici explicitly explains that transitive past clauses have an oblique agent, a nominative direct object, and verb agreement with the object. The reader gives:
- **Min ew kirî.** — I bought it.
- **Min kirî.** — I bought (a third-person singular object; subject-oriented table).
- **Te kirî.** — You bought (a third-person singular object).
- **Wî/wê kirî.** — He/she bought (a third-person singular object).
- **Me kirî.** — We bought (a third-person singular object).
- **We kirî.** — You (plural) bought (a third-person singular object).
- **Wan kirî.** — They bought (a third-person singular object).

The Êzdîxan grammar table also gives **min kirîn** as the plural-patient simple-past form, alongside **min kirî** for a singular patient. This supports the familiar object-agreement contrast:
- singular direct patient: **min kirî**
- plural direct patient: **min kirîn**

Use this contrast only with the direct object clearly identified. The agent pronoun remains oblique.

### The important participle overlap
Thackston's grammar states that a past participle is formed by adding **-î** to consonant-final past stems, but where the past stem itself ends in **-î**, the participle is identical to the stem. It gives **kirîn > kirî > kirî** (“bought”). Thus:
- **kirî** is the past stem used in finite simple past with a 3sg patient;
- **kirî** is also the participle used in perfect/passive constructions;
- **hatibûn kirîn** in a perfect/pluperfect passive context is not a simple-past active sentence.

Do not decide tense solely from the final letters of **kirî**. Read the whole clause and its auxiliary.

## 2. Firotin — “to sell”
**Infinitive:** firotin  
**Present stem:** firoş-  
**Past stem:** firot-  
**Meaning:** sell; transitive.

### Present paradigm
- **Ez difiroşim**
- **Tu difiroşî**
- **Ew difiroşe**
- **Em difiroşin**
- **Hûn difiroşin**
- **Ew difiroşin**

The alternation **firoş-** (present) versus **firot-** (past) is explicitly shown in the Kurmancî Wiktionary lexical entry and in the Kurmanji grammar stem-formation table. The form **difiroşim** is therefore correct; a mechanically derived *difirotim* is not the standard form documented by these references.

### Simple past
The lexical entry gives:
- **Min firot**
- **Te firot**
- **Wê/wî firot**
- **Me firot**
- **We firot**
- **Wan firot**

These are the agent-oriented forms when the direct patient is third-person singular. The construction is transitive/ergative, so the agent is oblique. If the direct patient is an overt plural noun or plural pronoun, the verb must agree with that patient; this is the general Standard Kurmancî rule. However, a complete independently verified object-oriented **firotin** paradigm was not located in this source audit, so the full person/number table should not be invented or presented as source-verified.

### Published usage
The Kurmancî lexical entry cites Firat Cewerî, *Girtî* (1986), with **Erdên vala ... hemû hatibûn firotin** (“The empty lands ... had all been sold”). This confirms the verb in published literary prose and its use in a passive pluperfect construction. It does **not** independently verify a simple-past active literary token. The same entry gives **Kirîn û firotin** (“buying and selling”) and the question **Tu dê tirimpêla xwe bi çendê bifiroşî min?** (“For how much will you sell me your car?”), useful lexical/context evidence but not simple past.

## 3. Negation and aspect: safe rules
- General Standard Kurmancî simple-past negation uses **ne-** before the past form: **min nekirî** (I did not buy it) and **min nefirot** (I did not sell it), assuming a third-person singular direct patient.
- The forms above are regular-rule illustrations, not direct quotations from the cited literary passage.
- Do not confuse **kirî** (buy) with **kir** (do/make).
- Do not confuse the past **firot** with the present stem **firoş-**.
- Do not call **hatibûn firotin** a simple past; it is a passive pluperfect construction.
- The present **difiroşim** is distinct from simple past **min firot**.
