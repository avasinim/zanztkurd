## 1. Scope and question

This audit checks what can safely be said about Standard Kurmanji `gotin` in the present perfect and past perfect (pluperfect), and whether the available sources justify a complete six-person paradigm.

The key result is deliberately narrow:

- Thackston (2006) directly attests the negative present-perfect form `negotiye` in an ordinary transitive sentence and gives the general transitive/ergative formation rule.
- Thackston directly explains the past participle formation rule and includes `negotî` ‘unsaid’ as a participle.
- Thackston gives the general transitive past-perfect rule and a complete illustrative paradigm for `dîtin`, not for `gotin`.
- Therefore `gotibû` is a morphologically motivated candidate for a 3SG Direct patient in a canonical transitive pluperfect, but a six-person `gotin` pluperfect table is not directly attested by the cited paradigm.
- A separate `Min gotim ku…` complement construction must not be mixed into the ordinary perfect paradigm.

## 2. Source-established principal part and participle

Haig & Öpengin (2018), Table 9, p. 184, list `gotin` with past stem `got-` and present stem written literally as `-bej-`. Their source spelling should be preserved when citing the table; any normalized `-bêj-` form must be labelled editorial normalization.

Thackston §19 (pp. 51–52) states that a past stem ending in a consonant forms its past participle by adding `-î`. The same section explicitly includes `tiştên negotî` ‘unsaid things’ as a negative participle example.

**Directly supported:**
- Past stem: `got-` (Haig & Öpengin, Table 9).
- Participial formation for consonant-final stems: past stem + `-î` (Thackston §19).
- Negative participle: `negotî`, directly present in Thackston’s example.

**Morphological inference, not a quoted full paradigm:** applying the stated participle rule to `got-` yields the positive participle `gotî`. The cited source does not present a dedicated six-person `gotin` perfect table.

## 3. Present perfect: direct evidence and argument roles

### 3.1 General formation rule

Thackston §20.1 (pp. 52–54) says the transitive present perfect is formed with agent pronouns plus the endings given for the perfect, and that the construction remains ergative, as in the simple past. The examples deliberately vary:
- agents while holding a 3SG patient constant; and
- patients while holding a 3SG agent constant.

The examples show that the person marking follows the Direct patient, not automatically the agent. For example, the source gives `wî ez dîtime` (‘he has seen me’) and `wî em dîtine` (‘he has seen us’), where the Oblique agent is held constant while the Direct patient and verbal agreement vary.

### 3.2 Directly attested `gotin` perfect example

Thackston §20.1 contrasts:
- `Wî tu caran tiştekî wilo negot.` — simple past, ‘He never said any such thing.’
- `Wî tu caran tiştekî wilo negotiye.` — present perfect, ‘He has never said any such thing.’

This is especially valuable because the same lexical predicate and near-identical sentence frame are used to contrast simple past and present perfect. The perfect is chosen when the non-occurrence remains relevant to the present or current topic; it is not merely a mechanical English “has + participle” translation.

The example also confirms that `negotiye` is a real attested form in this construction. It should not be generalized into an unverified six-person table.

### 3.3 What is not established

The source’s full transitive perfect table is for `dîtin`, not `gotin`. It supports the general argument-structure rule but does not directly certify each proposed `gotin` form (for example, forms with 1SG or plural Direct patients).

Do not infer that a bare form ending in `-ye` always identifies a 3SG agent. In the canonical transitive construction, identify the Oblique agent and Direct patient separately before assigning agreement.

## 4. Past perfect / pluperfect: formation and limits

Thackston §21.1 (pp. 55–56) states that the transitive past perfect is formed from the past stem plus `-i-` plus the past tense of `bûn`, following the ergative model. A vowel-final past stem attaches `bû` directly without the extra `-i-`.

The source’s full transitive paradigm is again for `dîtin`, including:
- `min dîtibû` (‘I had seen it’), and
- `wî ez dîtibûm` (‘he had seen me’).

These examples show that the Oblique agent remains distinct from the Direct patient, and that agreement follows the patient in the ergative construction.

### 4.1 Applying the rule to `gotin`

Since `got-` is consonant-final, the general rule predicts the sequence:

`got- + i + bû`

For a third-person singular Direct patient, `gotibû` is therefore a **rule-derived candidate form**. It is not presented here as a directly attested `gotin` paradigm row. The cited source’s explicit example `wî gotibû ku …` occurs in a narrative quotation at §21.1, p. 56, translated ‘he had said that …’; it is a directly attested token, but it is not a full paradigm and its complement context must be retained.

The narrative example is particularly useful but should be treated carefully: it establishes the form in context, not every person combination or every syntactic environment.

### 4.2 Meaning and discourse

Thackston notes that the Kurmanji past perfect is not always restricted to the strict English “had already” temporal relation. It can also occur independently in narrative for a more remote past meaning. Thus a course explanation must not reduce it to a one-to-one English tense substitution.

## 5. Critical construction boundary: `Min gotim ku…`

Thackston §18.5, p. 51, explicitly says ergativity may be lost for stylistic reasons in certain past transitive expressions such as ‘I said that…’ and ‘I saw that…’ when followed by a subordinate clause functioning as complement:

`Min gotim ku…` — ‘I said that…’

The same section contrasts this with canonical `min got`. This is a separate complement construction, not a normal present-perfect form and not evidence for assigning the 1SG ending to the agent in every transitive perfect.

Keep three analyses apart:
1. simple past `min got`;
2. special simple-past complement construction `Min gotim ku…`;
3. present perfect `Wî … negotiye`.

The similar-looking endings do not erase the differences in tense, case alignment, argument role, and complement structure.

## 6. Dialect and source-boundary warning

Haig & Öpengin’s discussion on p. 199 of past participles as main predicates and a present-perfect-like construction occurs in their description of **Şemzînan / Southeastern Kurmanji**, not as a direct statement of the Standard Kurmanji paradigm. It must not be imported into the Standard pathway without a clear dialect label.

The current audit uses Thackston’s explicitly described Standard-oriented reference grammar for the principal Standard transitive perfect and pluperfect rules. Haig & Öpengin is used here for the lexical past stem and for identifying the dialect boundary. Neither source licenses merging Central/Eastern colloquial forms with Standard Kurmanji.

## 8. Editorial recommendations — proposal only

1. Use the directly attested contrast `negot` vs `negotiye` to explain the difference between simple past and present perfect.
2. Explain present relevance/topic relevance as the semantic contrast, not a rigid English auxiliary translation.
3. Teach the canonical transitive alignment explicitly: Oblique agent, Direct patient, verb agreement with the Direct patient.
4. Present `negotî` as a participle/adjectival form, not as a finite perfect by itself.
5. Treat `gotibû` as directly attested only in the cited narrative complement context; do not manufacture a six-person table from the `dîtin` paradigm.
6. Keep `Min gotim ku…` in a separate note on complement clauses and stylistic loss of ergativity.
7. Label the Haig & Öpengin p. 199 participle-predicate pattern as Şemzînan/Southeastern Kurmanji and do not present it as Standard.
8. Require native Kurmanji editorial review of any new `gotin` example, including its valency, case marking, agreement and interpretation.
9. No learner-facing lesson, glossary, quiz, seed, or course content should be changed on the basis of this audit alone.
