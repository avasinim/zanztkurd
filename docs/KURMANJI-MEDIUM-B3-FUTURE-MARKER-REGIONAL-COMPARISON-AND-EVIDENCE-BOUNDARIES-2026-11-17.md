# Kurmanji Future Marker: Regional Comparison and Evidence Boundaries

Date: 2026-11-17  
Type: literature-based comparative audit; documentation only.

## Research question

How should the K-078 Şemdinli (Şemzînan) future-marker evidence be compared with the wider Kurmanji dialect continuum without treating one speaker's transcript as a dialect-wide paradigm?

## Evidence from the Manchester database and its comparative analysis

The Manchester dialect database identifies K-078 as Şemdinli in the Turkey (Kurmanji) sample group. Its questionnaire maps distinguish:
- 3.11.1: “Tomorrow it will rain”;
- 3.11.2: “Tomorrow I will go to town”;
- 3.11.3: combination of future-marker patterns.

The database documentation says entries derive from transcribed recorded interviews and can be searched by sample, location, structural tag, English elicitation phrase, or Kurdish text. These methodological facts matter: elicited questionnaire responses and connected free-speech extracts are related evidence types, but they are not interchangeable.

Haig and Öpengin's comparative discussion of the Manchester database characterizes an analytical future marker ê/dê/wê as a Kurmanji/Bahdini feature and points to maps 3.11.1–3.11.2. Their discussion also notes regional distribution patterns rather than a single invariant form. The available publication excerpt describes:
- a southern area around Qamishli/Nusaybin/Kızıltepe where a future marker is reported;
- the southeastern Kurmanji innovation zone centred in Duhok and extending northwards into Hakkari, Yüksekova, Van and Muş, among other areas;
- a separate area in northern Erbil where the analytical type is reported only sporadically, with wê also sporadic.

These are broad areal summaries from a comparative study, not direct attestations of every form in every locality. The published excerpt does not justify assigning a specific ê/dê/wê variant to an individual speaker without checking the corresponding database record or map feature.

## K-078 natural-speech evidence

The K-078 free-speech transcript contains multiple overt dê-marked prospective clauses in several discourse-time settings:
- intention relative to interview speech time;
- prospective events viewed from a remembered past situation;
- reported expectations and deliberation;
- reported plans and promises.

The same transcript contains the contextually future-oriented negative clause `em ceyranê girê nadeyn` alongside an overt dê-marked clause in the same narrative episode. This is evidence for the usage of this speaker in this recorded context, not a complete negative-future paradigm.

## Comparison matrix

| Evidence level | What is supported | What is not supported |
|---|---|---|
| K-078 free speech | Several dê-marked prospective clauses and one contextually future-oriented negative clause | A complete Şemzînan paradigm or a categorical negative-future rule |
| Manchester elicitation maps | The existence and prompts of future-marker questionnaire items 3.11.1–3.11.3 | Exact K-078 response forms until the sample's feature attributes are retrieved |
| Comparative dialectology | Analytical ê/dê/wê future marking is discussed as a Kurmanji/Bahdini feature with regional distribution | A claim that all Kurmanji varieties use one form uniformly |
| Regional distribution summaries | Broad areal tendencies and sporadic zones are reported | A speaker-level form or local rule inferred solely from a shaded map or summary paragraph |

## Methodological recommendations

1. Keep the K-078 transcript, questionnaire response, and comparative map evidence in separate columns in any future dataset.
2. Record the sample code, locality, survey item, exact Kurdish response, transcription convention, and source location for every elicited token.
3. Do not normalize ê, dê, and wê into one written form before establishing the local transcription and grammatical analysis.
4. Do not infer that the negative clause in K-078 proves that all negative futures omit dê.
5. When comparing another locality, use a locality-labelled recording or exact database response. Do not borrow a Behdinî, Duhok, Cizîrî, or northern Erbil form and label it Şemzînan.
6. Keep tense, prospective aspect, modality, and future-in-the-past as separate annotation dimensions where the discourse supports the distinction.

## Safe conclusion

The available evidence supports a careful two-level description: K-078 supplies direct natural-speech evidence for one Şemdinli speaker's dê-marked prospective clauses and a contextually future-oriented negative clause; comparative dialectology treats ê/dê/wê as an analytical future-marker family with regional variation across Kurmanji/Bahdini. The exact local distribution and K-078 elicited forms still require direct record-level retrieval. No new learner-facing rule should be adopted from this audit alone.
