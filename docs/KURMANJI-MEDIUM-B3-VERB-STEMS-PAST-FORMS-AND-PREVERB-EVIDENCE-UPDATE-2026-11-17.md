# B3 Kurmanji-Medium: Evidence Update on Verb Stems, Past Forms and Preverb Placement
Date: 2026-11-17
Scope: source audit only. This note refines the evidence status for daketin, derketin, raketin, hildan and hilgirtin. It does not authorize changes to learner-facing lessons.

## 1. Executive findings

1. **Daketin:** *Kurmanji Basic* lists **daketin, dakeve** as a compound/preverb verb and states that present indicative **di-** is normally inserted between the preverbal element and the verb stem. The same section directly attests **Ez ji vir derdikevim** for **derketin**, but does not give a finite **daketin** clause. Therefore **dakeve** is a dictionary/present citation form; a full finite paradigm such as *dadikevim* must remain provisional until a direct finite attestation is located.
2. **Derketin:** the same grammar directly attests **Ez ji vir derdikevim** (“I am leaving from here”) and lists **derketin, derkeve**. This is stronger than inferring the form from a general preverb rule.
3. **Raketin:** teaching sources converge on the present set **radikevim, radikevî, radikeve, radikevin**. These are useful corroborated teaching forms, but a scholarly reference grammar or edited primary text should be preferred before declaring the full paradigm canonical for this pathway.
4. **Hilgirtin:** Kurdîzan explicitly gives **hil-di-gir-im** (present 1sg), past stem **hilgirt**, and **Ez hilgirt** is not supplied as a full past clause. This is enough to establish the present morphological pattern and past stem in that pedagogical source, but not the complete past ergative paradigm.
5. **Hildan ≠ hilgirtin:** the verbs must remain separate lexical units. *Kurmanji Basic* lists **hildan, hilde** “to raise, lift”; Kurdîzan separately lists **hilgirtin** with present **hildigirim** and past stem **hilgirt**. Do not derive one verb's paradigm from the other.

## 2. Daketin: do not overstate the present paradigm

*Kurmanji Basic*, §4.7, lists **daketin, dakeve** and **derketin, derkeve** among compound verbs with preverbals. It explains that the present indicative marker **di-** is normally infixed between the preverbal and the verb stem. Its direct finite example is **Ez ji vir derdikevim** for **derketin**.

The source does not give a corresponding finite sentence or six-person paradigm for **daketin**. Therefore the following distinction is mandatory in B3 documentation:
- Attested infinitive: **daketin**.
- Dictionary/present citation form: **dakeve**.
- Morphological expectation from the general rule: the preverb **da-** remains before **di-**, but the exact finite surface forms need direct confirmation.
- Not yet approved for a learner paradigm: **dadikevim** and other person forms, unless corroborated by a finite example in a reliable source.

This caution is important: a productive-looking morphological rule is not a substitute for attestation of a specific verb, especially where spelling, stem allomorphy, and dialect differ.

## 3. Derketin: direct finite present and cautious past analysis

*Kurmanji Basic* provides:
- **derketin, derkeve** — to leave, emerge;
- **Ez ji vir derdikevim** — “I am leaving from here.”

The sentence directly attests the 1sg present indicative. Its segmentation follows the grammar's stated rule: **der-di-kev-im**. The dictionary stem **derkeve** is not itself a finite 1sg form.

For the past, the intransitive rule is independently established in the University of Arizona Kurmanji overview and in *Kurmanji Kurdish, Lesson 7*: intransitive verbs use the direct subject and personal endings on the past stem (e.g. **Ez hatim**, **Tu hatî**, **Ew hat**, **Em hatin**; **Ez çûm**, **Tu çûyî**, **Em çûn**). Removing the infinitive ending from **derketin** yields the expected past stem **derket**. However, this is a morphological derivation, not yet a direct quotation of a literary finite **derket** clause in this audit. Keep the past stem marked as morphologically regular/expected until a text attests it.

## 4. Raketin: present forms vs past forms

The present forms are supported by multiple pedagogical references:
- Kurdolingo gives **Ez nêzî saet dozdehan radikevim** and identifies **rakev** as the present stem.
- *Kurmanji Kurdish Lessons* gives **ez radikevim; tu radikevî; ew radikeve; em/hûn/ew radikevin**.
- The *Self-learning Kurmanji Kurdish* grammar provides a matching present table.

The evidence is consistent for the forms in the listed sources. Still, the B3 editorial policy should distinguish:
- repeated teaching-source agreement;
- an attested sentence;
- a paradigmatic table;
- a form confirmed by a scholarly reference grammar or edited literary text.

For past **raketin**, collaborative and teaching references list **raket** as the past stem and forms such as **ez raketim, tu raketî, ew raket, em raketin**. The sources inspected here do not elevate these to a fully verified standard paradigm. The verb is intransitive in the sources and must not be taught as a transitive past ergative example.

## 5. Hilgirtin and hildan: morphology and lexical identity

Kurdîzan's compound-verb lesson explicitly gives:
- **hilgirtin**;
- present 1sg **hildigirim**, segmented **hil-di-gir-im**;
- negative present **hilnagirim**;
- past stem **hilgirt**.

It explicitly warns learners to treat the full verb as a lexical unit, preserve its preverbal element, and learn the two stems and attested forms rather than mechanically assigning a meaning to a string of initial letters. This supports the present stem pattern and the past stem **hilgirt**. It does not, on its own, provide the full transitive past agreement paradigm. Since **hilgirtin** is transitive, a future past paradigm must demonstrate the oblique agent, direct object, and object-controlled agreement in actual clauses.

By contrast, *Kurmanji Basic* lists **hildan, hilde** “to raise, lift” and notes that some old compound verbs with preverbals do not take the ordinary **bi-** imperative prefix. The verbs **hildan** and **hilgirtin** therefore differ in their lexical stems and should not be collapsed into one word family for teaching purposes.

## 6. General alignment rule and caveats

The University of Arizona Kurmanji overview documents the core past transitive pattern:
- agent in oblique case;
- patient/object in direct (nominative) case;
- verb agreement controlled by the patient/object.

It gives a transitive paradigm in which the agent changes (**min, te, wê/wî, me, we, wan**) while **ew dît** remains constant for a third-person singular patient, and then changes the patient to show verbal agreement (**wî ez dîtim**, **wî tu dîtî**, **wî ew dît**).

The Zimanzan explanation gives the same general pattern and makes explicit that the intransitive past remains like the present in its direct subject and personal endings. A recent academic chapter on Kurdish verbal systems confirms that Kurmanji usually shows ergative constructions in past transitive clauses, while noting mixed patterns in some dialects. The B3 pathway should teach the canonical target variety and label deviations as regional/structural variation rather than erase them.

## 7. Editorial recommendations

- Keep **dakeve** as the citation form but do not publish a six-person **daketin** present paradigm yet.
- Use **Ez ji vir derdikevim** as the attested present example for **derketin**.
- Mark **derket** as an expected past stem derived by the regular intransitive pattern until a direct clause is found.
- Keep **raketin** present forms as corroborated pedagogical evidence, not as a substitute for a scholarly source.
- Record **hilgirtin → hildigirim / hilgirt** as a source-attested morphological pair, but do not create its full past paradigm without object-agreement examples.
- Preserve the lexical distinction **hildan** vs **hilgirtin**.
- Keep all regional and spelling variants in evidence notes until the project's written-standard policy is fixed.
