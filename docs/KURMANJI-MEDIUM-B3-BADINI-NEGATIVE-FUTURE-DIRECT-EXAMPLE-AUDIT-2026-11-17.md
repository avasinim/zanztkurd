# Badini Negative Future: Direct Examples and Dialect-Scope Audit

Date: 2026-11-17  
Scope: source-backed documentation only. No lesson or runtime changes.

## Direct example in published linguistic description

Haig's chapter “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî)” explicitly contrasts Standard Kurmanji with Behdinî:

- Standard Kurmanji: *Ez ē sibe bi wan re ne-çi-m.*
- Behdinî: *Ez sibe digel wan na-çi-m.*
- Translation: “I won't go with them tomorrow.”

The contrast is structural. Standard Kurmanji retains the future auxiliary and uses subjunctive negation *ne-*. The Behdinî example has no future particle; it uses the negative indicative form *na-çi-m*, with the future-time reading supplied by *sibe* “tomorrow” and the utterance context.

Source: Geoffrey Haig, “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî),” in *The Languages and Linguistics of Western Asia: An Areal Perspective* (2018), §2.3, p.129 in the printed chapter / PDF lines corresponding to the future section. The chapter states that in Behdinî there is “no dedicated negative future” and the negative indicative is used.

## Independent Badini-specific account

Christoph Unger (2014), “Badini Kurdish modal particles dê and da,” reports that the Badini future construction consists of *dê* plus a person/number-inflected present stem, without modal/aspectual prefixes; *bi-* is excluded from that construction. Unger also cites the same Standard Kurmanji vs. Behdinî negative contrast and provides Badini corpus examples of the affirmative *dê* construction. The article therefore supports keeping Badini *dê* separate from the Standard Kurmanji *wê/dê/ê* system.

## What the example proves—and what it does not

1. It directly attests the Behdinî negative clause *Ez sibe digel wan na-çi-m* in a published grammatical description.
2. It supports a contextually future-oriented negative indicative, not a dedicated morphological negative-future paradigm with *dê*.
3. It does not establish that every speaker or locality in Duhok, Zakho, Şemzînan, or northern Iraq uses an identical form.
4. Haig's chapter explicitly distinguishes its Standard Kurmanji sketch from Behdinî and notes that Behdinî is treated separately. Do not label this example as a generic Standard Kurmanji form.
5. No negative *dê* construction should be mechanically generated for Badini from the Standard Kurmanji pattern.
6. The source does not provide a direct Şemzînan negative-future sentence in this passage. The Behdinî example must not be relabeled as Şemzînan evidence.

## Editorial and Academy status

This is an evidence note, not an Academy standard decision. The Behdinî example is preserved in its source spelling and segmentation; no normalization to Hawar or Academy orthography is imposed. Existing lessons, course inventory, and the Kurmanji-medium pathway remain unchanged.

## Limitations

This audit records a published example and a source-specific grammatical generalization. It is not a substitute for a locality-tagged spoken corpus survey or speaker elicitation. Further claims about individual Badini or Şemzînan localities require separate primary evidence.
