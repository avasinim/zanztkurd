## 1. Purpose and source hierarchy

This audit extends the B3 verb evidence work from `gotin` to `dîtin` and `anîn`. Its narrow question is whether the sources provide enough evidence for present-perfect and pluperfect forms, and where the evidence stops.

The principal source is W. M. Thackston, *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, §§19–21.1, pp. 49–56. The online text was inspected at the actual Kurmanji grammar, not the similarly titled Sorani grammar. Geoffrey Haig and Ergin Öpengin (2018), Table 9, p. 184, independently list the principal parts of `dîtin` and help identify the broader Standard Kurmanji verbal-stem system.

**Important source distinction:** Thackston gives full transitive present-perfect and past-perfect paradigms for `dîtin`. He does not give a parallel full paradigm for `anîn`. The existence of irregular present forms for `anîn` does not license filling in its perfect forms by analogy.

## 2. Lexical stems and participial boundary

Haig & Öpengin (2018), Table 9, p. 184, list:
- `dîtin`: past stem `dît-`, present stem `-bîn-`, gloss ‘see’.
- `gotin`: past stem `got-`, present stem printed `-bej-`, gloss ‘say’.

Thackston §19 states that the past participle of a consonant-final past stem is formed by adding `-î`; where the past stem ends in `-î`, the participle is identical to the past stem. His examples include `hat > hatî`, `şust > şustî`, `kir > kirî`, and `kirî > kirî`.

Accordingly:
- `dît-` → `dîtî`: rule-based participle, consistent with the finite perfect examples below.
- For `anîn`, do not assume the past stem or participle solely from the infinitive spelling. The present-tense section explicitly identifies irregular present conjugation, but does not provide a perfect paradigm for this verb. Any `anî`/ `anî-` analysis needs a separate, source-attested principal-part check before use.

## 3. Present perfect of transitive `dîtin): directly attested paradigm

Thackston §20.1 (pp. 52–54) states that the transitive present perfect uses the agent pronoun plus the present-perfect endings, but remains ergative, like the simple past. He supplies two six-row blocks.

### 3.1 Different agents; third-person singular Direct patient

| Oblique agent | Direct patient | Attested verb form |
|---|---|---|
| min | ew | dîtiye |
| me | ew | dîtiye |
| te | ew | dîtiye |
| we | ew | dîtiye |
| wî | ew | dîtiye |
| wan | ew | dîtiye |

The constant 3SG Direct patient corresponds to the constant 3SG verb form, while the Oblique agent changes.

### 3.2 Third-person singular Oblique agent; different Direct patients

| Oblique agent | Direct patient | Attested verb form |
|---|---|---|
| wî | ez | dîtime |
| wî | em | dîtine |
| wî | tu | dîtiye |
| wî | hun | dîtine |
| wî | ew (singular) | dîtiye |
| wî | ew (plural) | dîtine |

This block is the clearest pedagogical evidence that the verb agrees with the Direct patient, not the Oblique agent. The singular and plural forms must be read with the pronoun’s referent in context; the repeated surface pronoun `ew` does not itself encode number.

### 3.3 Negative forms

The same section says negation is formed by prefixing `ne-` to the verb and prints the corresponding negative rows, e.g. `nedîtiye`, `nedîtime`, `nedîtine`. This is direct evidence for the negative paradigm, not a license to mix negative morphology into the affirmative stem inventory.

## 4. Past perfect of transitive `dîtin`: directly attested paradigm

Thackston §21.1 (pp. 55–56) states that the transitive past perfect is formed from the past stem + `-i-` + the past tense of `bûn`, on the ergative model. It supplies two six-row blocks.

### 4.1 Different Oblique agents; third-person singular Direct patient

| Oblique agent | Direct patient | Attested verb form |
|---|---|---|
| min | ew | dîtibû |
| me | ew | dîtibû |
| te | ew | dîtibû |
| hun | ew | dîtibû |
| wî | ew | dîtibû |
| wan | ew | dîtibû |

The source's order and rows are retained. The table demonstrates that the verb form stays constant when the agent changes but the Direct patient remains 3SG.

### 4.2 Third-person singular Oblique agent; different Direct patients

| Oblique agent | Direct patient | Attested verb form |
|---|---|---|
| wî | ez | dîtibûm |
| wî | em | dîtibûn |
| wî | tu | dîtibûyî |
| wî | hun | dîtibûn |
| wî | ew (singular) | dîtibû |
| wî | ew (plural) | dîtibûn |

Again, the verbal endings track the Direct patient. The past-perfect form is not simply a fixed `dîtibû` attached to every subject; the agreement changes when the Direct patient changes.

### 4.3 Meaning in discourse

Thackston explicitly cautions that the Kurmanji past perfect is not restricted to the English temporal sequence “had already.” In narrative it can independently convey a more remote past where English may use a simple past. The example `wî gotibû ku …` is translated with English ‘he said that’ in its narrative context. Course explanations must therefore describe Kurdish discourse use rather than force a mechanical English tense mapping.

## 5. What the sources establish about `anîn`

Thackston §14, pp. 32–34, calls `hatin` and `anîn` irregular in the present tense and prints their present conjugations. The `anîn` forms include `ez tînim`, `tu tînî`, `ew tîne`, and plural forms `em tînin`, `hun tînin`, `ew tînin`; negatives include `ez naynim`, `tu naynî`, `ew nayne`, and plural forms. These are present-tense forms only.

The cited sections on past participle, present perfect, and transitive past perfect do not give a complete `anîn) perfect paradigm. The six-person `dîtin) table cannot be copied mechanically to `anîn), even if the general ergative alignment rule applies to transitive past constructions.

**Evidence status for `anîn):**
- Irregular present conjugation: directly attested by Thackston §14.
- Full present-perfect paradigm: not supplied in the cited reference.
- Full transitive pluperfect paradigm: not supplied in the cited reference.
- Any derived participle or finite perfect row: requires a separate explicit stem and form source, followed by native-editor validation.

This is an evidence gap, not proof that the forms do not exist.

## 6. Construction and dialect controls

1. The paradigms in §§20.1 and 21.1 are transitive/ergative: the agent is Oblique; the patient is Direct; verbal agreement tracks the Direct patient.
2. Do not describe Oblique as synonymous with “object.” Here it marks the transitive agent in these past constructions.
3. Do not infer that all verbs share identical surface endings without checking stem class and the relevant paradigm.
4. Keep present-stem irregularity (e.g. `anîn`) separate from past-stem and perfect formation.
5. Do not combine the Standard-oriented paradigms with regional alternatives unless the variety is explicitly identified and independently sourced.
6. A complete paradigm is not a substitute for checking real lexical examples, valency, case marking and discourse.

## 8. Editorial recommendations — proposal only

1. Use `dîtin` as the evidence-backed model verb for teaching the transitive present perfect and pluperfect, with the exact two-block paradigms and case roles shown separately.
2. Explicitly teach the contrast between changing Oblique agents and changing Direct patients; avoid a single table whose columns obscure which argument changes.
3. Explain that `dîtibû` is the form with a 3SG Direct patient, while forms such as `dîtibûm`, `dîtibûyî`, and `dîtibûn` track the other Direct-patient persons/numbers in the source's paradigm.
4. Keep `anîn` in a “further evidence needed” register for perfect and pluperfect; do not publish an inferred six-person table.
5. Require a Kurmanji native-language editor to review every proposed lesson example for argument structure, naturalness, reference variety and context.
6. No learner-facing lesson, glossary, quiz, seed, or existing course content should be changed on the basis of this audit alone.
