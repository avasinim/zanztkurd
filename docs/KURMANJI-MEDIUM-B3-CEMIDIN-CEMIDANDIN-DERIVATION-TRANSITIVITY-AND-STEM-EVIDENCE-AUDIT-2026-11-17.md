# Cemidîn / cemidandin: derivation, meaning, transitivity, and stem-evidence audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only lexical and grammatical audit for the Kurmanji-medium research track. No learner-facing lesson is modified.

## Executive findings

The pair **cemidîn → cemidandin** is strongly attested in independent grammatical and lexical sources.

- **cemidîn**: “to freeze, become frozen, congeal”; intransitive.
- **cemidandin**: “to freeze something, cause something to freeze”; transitive and causative.
- Both are derived from the nominal root **cemed** (“ice/frost”).
- Husein Muhammed's *Kanîzar* article explicitly states that **-în** generally forms intransitive verbs and **-andin** forms transitive verbs; it lists *cemed → cemidîn / cemidandin* as an example.
- Samî Tan's Kurmanji grammar independently lists **Cemed – cemidîn – cemidandin** and explains the valency distinction between the two suffixes.
- Joyce Blau's dictionary gives both verb meanings and the imperative forms **bicemide** and **bicemidîne**.

The derivational relationship and valency contrast are well supported. A full present and past conjugation paradigm for these exact lemmas was not independently verified in the sources inspected, so this audit does not invent one.

## 1. Lexical and valency contrast

| Lemma | Meaning | Valency | Derivational base |
|---|---|---|---|
| cemidîn | freeze, become frozen, congeal | intransitive | cemed + -în |
| cemidandin | freeze something, cause to freeze | transitive | cemed + -andin |

The semantic distinction is important: *cemidîn* describes a substance becoming frozen, whereas *cemidandin* describes an agent causing something to freeze.

## 2. Direct grammatical evidence

Husein Muhammed, in *Kanîzar*, states that the suffix **-andin** forms transitive verbs, while **-în** generally forms intransitive verbs. He gives the exact derivations:

- **cemed → cemidandin**
- **cemed → cemidîn**

The article also explains the vowel alternation **e → i** in the second syllable: **cemed → cemidîn / cemidandin**. This is not a spelling variant to be normalized away; it is the attested derived form.

Samî Tan's *Waneyên Rêzimanê Kurmancî* independently presents the sequence **Cemed – cemidîn – cemidandin** in its section on compound/derived verbs, and explicitly assigns intransitive valency to **-în** and transitive valency to **-andin**.

## 3. Lexical evidence and imperative forms

Joyce Blau's *Kurdish-French-English Dictionary* records:

- **cemidandin (bicemidîne)** — French *geler*, English “to freeze”.
- **cemidîn (bicemide)** — French *se geler, se figer*, English “to freeze, to congeal”.

The reflexive French gloss for *cemidîn* and the transitive English/French gloss for *cemidandin* support the intransitive/transitive contrast. The imperative forms are reproduced as printed; the source's typography in the *cemidîn* entry is slightly degraded, so **bicemide** should be checked against a clearer edition before publication in a learner-facing paradigm.

Kurdi School independently teaches the same contrast in its causative lesson: **cemidîn (freeze) → cemidandin (freeze something)**.

## 4. Stems and paradigm limits

The grammatical sources establish the infinitives and their derivation, and Blau records the imperative forms. The Wiktionary verb-category lists *cemidandin* as an **-andin** verb, but the materials inspected did not provide a sufficiently clear, exact entry with a full present/past conjugation for both *cemidîn* and *cemidandin*.

Therefore:

- Do not infer a full paradigm solely from the generic pattern.
- Do not publish a past-tense patient-agreement table without direct examples.
- A likely present-stem pattern may be explored in a later audit, but it must be marked provisional until an exact conjugation or corpus example is located.
- Preserve the contrast between the intransitive event (*cemidîn*) and the transitive causative (*cemidandin*).

## 5. Teaching-safe summary

> **Cemidîn** واتە قەتیس بوون/سەهۆڵ بەستن؛ کردارێکی ناتێپەڕە. **Cemidandin** واتە شتێک قەتیسکردن یان بەسەهۆڵکردنی؛ کردارێکی تێپەڕە.

For a learner-facing example, use only after the surrounding noun and context are supplied and checked; this audit does not fabricate a sample sentence.
