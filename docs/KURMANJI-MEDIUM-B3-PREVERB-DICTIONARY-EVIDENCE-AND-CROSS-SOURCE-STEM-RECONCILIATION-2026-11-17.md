# Kurmanji B3 — Dictionary Evidence and Cross-Source Reconciliation for Preverb Verbs

Date: 2026-11-17  
Status: source-critical audit; no lesson approval implied  
Scope: source verification for selected lexicalized preverb verbs and a cross-publication check of the stem table used in recent research notes.

## 1. Executive result

The strongest directly accessible source in this audit is Haig's 2018 chapter on Northern Kurdish. It gives a general frequent-verb stem table and a separate account of preverb incorporation. It does **not** provide a complete present/past paradigm for every preverb verb named in the editorial candidate list. Consequently, the following must remain unresolved until dictionary entries and attested clauses are obtained: hildan, vekirin, derketin, daketin, and raketin (beyond the single inflected example ra-di-keve).

The source comparison also reveals small but important differences between the Haig 2018 chapter and the Haig & Öpengin 2018 chapter in the transcription of several present stems. These differences must not be silently harmonized.

## 2. Directly attested facts in Haig 2018

### 2.1 General stem table

Haig states that Kurmanji verbs have present and past stems, and that formation of one from the other is not fully predictable. His Table 12 includes the following:

|---|---|---|---|
| hatin | hat- | -(h)ê- / -wer- | come |
| hêlan/hiştin | hişt- | -hêl- | leave |
| girtin | girt- | -gir- | grasp, hold |
| gotin | got- | -bêj- | say |
| rûniştin | rûnişt- | -rûn- | sit |
| kirin | kir- | -k- | do, make |
| çûn | çû- | -ç- / -her- | go |
| ketin | ket- | -kev- | fall |
| xwarin | xwar- | -xw- | eat |
| avêtin | avêt- | -avêj- | throw |

This is a general Northern Kurmanji table, not a dedicated paradigm list for every preverb compound. Do not copy the bare-verb stems into a compound lemma without independent evidence.

Source: Geoffrey Haig, “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî): An Areal Perspective” (2018), §2.6, Table 12, author-hosted chapter PDF: https://multicast.aspra.uni-bamberg.de/data/pubs/Haig2018_Northern-Kurdish_Kurmanji.pdf

### 2.2 Preverb incorporation

Haig describes Standard Kurmanji preverbal particles such as hil, řā, and da as combining with verb stems to form new verbs. In Standard Kurmanji, the infinitive is usually written as one word, but indicative/imperfective and negative prefixes may be inserted between the preverb and the stem. He gives ra-di-keve ‘goes to sleep’ from raketin.

In Şemzînan (SEK), prefixes can precede the preverb, indicating a more fully lexicalized preverb+stem complex. Haig analyses di-helē-t as a contraction of di-hel-hē-t, and contrasts this with Standard Kurmanji hil-t-ê. He also reports the phenomenon in southernmost varieties around Midyat.

Important limit: ra-di-keve is an attested form and a glossed example, not a complete conjugation table for raketin. Likewise, di-helē-t supports the cited SEK analysis, not a generalized prefix-order rule for every variety.

## 3. Cross-publication discrepancies requiring editorial caution

A direct comparison with Haig & Öpengin (2018), “Kurmanji Kurdish in Turkey: structure, varieties, and status,” shows several present-stem transcription differences in the corresponding frequent-verb table:

| Infinitive | Haig 2018 chapter | Haig & Öpengin 2018 | Editorial treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| birin | -b- | -bi- | preserve source-specific transcription pending phonological/typographic reconciliation |
| gotin | -bêj- | -bej- | diacritic differs; do not treat as a distinct lexeme |
| kirin | -k- | -ki- | preserve source-specific form; check transcription conventions |
| çûn | -ç- / -her- | -çi- / -her- | vowel notation differs; check the stated variety and phonetic interpretation |
| dan | -d- | -di- | preserve source-specific transcription; do not normalize without checking the analysis |

These may reflect transcription, vowel representation, or presentation differences rather than genuinely distinct lexical stems. The publications are related in authorship and descriptive framework; they should not be counted as fully independent corroboration. The correct editorial action is to record the exact source form, dialect scope, and any stated phonetic realization.

Sources:
- Haig 2018, Table 12, https://multicast.aspra.uni-bamberg.de/data/pubs/Haig2018_Northern-Kurdish_Kurmanji.pdf
- Haig & Öpengin 2018, Table 9, https://www.uni-bamberg.de/fileadmin/aspra/06_Team/Publications_and_CV/HaigOepengin2018_Kurmanji_Kurdish_.pdf

## 5. Lexical evidence workflow

For each candidate, the next evidence pass must obtain:
1. A named dictionary headword with bibliographic edition/page or stable entry URL.
2. Infinitive and present/past stems as explicitly printed.
3. At least one finite clause for the present stem and one for the past stem, where available.
4. Dialect/locality metadata for each form.
5. Negative and indicative/subjunctive prefix placement from actual forms.
6. Valency and argument marking from full clauses.
7. A note distinguishing synchronically transparent meaning from historical etymology.
8. A record of orthographic variants without merging them prematurely.

A word-list gloss alone is insufficient to establish argument structure, agreement, or the complete paradigm.
