# Kurmancî Medium B3 — xistin, danîn, and lê xistin: lexical contrast, stems, and past agreement audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only lexical and grammatical audit. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, seed, database, migration, PR, or deployment changes.

## Executive findings

The core distinction is not “one verb with two spellings”; these are separate lexical items/constructions:

| Form | Core meaning | Valency | Present stem | Past stem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **xistin** | put/place; strike/knock; throw/cast in context | transitive | x- / xîn- (variant stems listed) | xist- |
| **danîn** | put/place, set down, locate; extended senses | transitive | da-în- | da-nî- |
| **lê xistin** | beat/strike someone; hit at/on someone | lexicalized verb + preposition construction | xistin stem within construction | xist- within construction |

**Do not merge xistin and danîn into one conjugation.** Their meanings overlap in some contexts, but their stem formations are different. Also, **lê xistin** is not simply the literal sum of “to it + put”: the academic grammar explicitly identifies it as a lexicalized verb-plus-prepositional-complement construction meaning “beat a person”.

## 1. xistin: lexical meanings and stems

Haig & Öpengin's academic overview lists:
- infinitive: **xistin**
- past stem: **xist-**
- present stem: **-x- / -xîn-**
- meaning: **strike, knock**

The beginner grammar *Kurmanji Kurdish for the Beginners* independently gives xistin with present stem **x-** and first-person-singular present **dixim / dixînim**, glossed “to drop, strike”. This supports a short and an expanded present-stem realization in teaching references; it does not authorize replacing the academically listed variants with a single universal form.

Kurdish Academy's conjugation table labels xistin “to put” and gives:
- ez **dixim**
- tu **dixî**
- ew **dixe**
- em/hûn/ew **dixin**

The table's past column gives **xist** for singular patient rows and **xistin** for plural patient rows. Read these as patient-agreement forms in a transitive past paradigm, not as a six-person agent conjugation: Kurmancî transitive past marks the patient on the verb.

The Kurdish-Kurdish dictionary *Ferhengên Kurmancî* gives the entry:
**xistin — avêtin, danîn, me hev girt. min ew xist. xistin ber xwe. xistin ser riya rast, ji riya rast xistin.**
It therefore records senses spanning throwing/casting, placing, taking/holding together, and expressions involving putting before oneself or putting someone on/off the right path. The attested short clause **min ew xist** is a useful transitive past example: oblique agent **min**, direct patient **ew**, verb **xist**.

The lexical range is broad and context-sensitive. Avoid defining xistin only as “put” or only as “hit”; select the appropriate sense and complement in each example.

## 2. danîn: separate transitive verb ‘put/place’

Wîkîferheng records **danîn** as transitive, with:
- present stem: **da-în-**
- present: **ez datînim**, **tu datînî**, **ew datîne**, **em/hûn/ew datînin**
- imperative: **daîne** (singular), **daînin** (plural)
- past stem: **da-nî-**
- simple past: **danî** (singular patient rows); plural patient form requires careful agreement interpretation from the table.

The Kurdish-English Kurmanji dictionary by Chyet records **danîn** as transitive “to put, place” and supplies authentic examples:
- **Azad ... datîne ser maseyê** — Azad puts his book on the table.
- **Nanê xwe danî ber xwe nan xwar** — He put his bread before himself and ate it.
- **Tembûra xwe danî ser coga xwe** — He put his tambour on his knee.

Chyet also records extended senses: “to name”, “to subside/abate”, “to land/alight/settle”, and constructions such as **serê xwe danîn** (“lay down one’s head”). These are dictionary senses and constructions, not all interchangeable with the physical placement sense.

A separate teaching grammar analyses danîn as **da- + anîn** (“bring”) and gives the present form **datînim**. The Wîkîferheng entry independently confirms the present-stem notation **da-în-**. This is a useful morphological analysis, but course material should teach the attested lexical forms, not overgeneralize preverb behavior to every compound.

## 3. xistin versus danîn

Both can translate as “put/place” in some English contexts, and the Kurdish-Kurdish dictionary lists **danîn** among xistin's near-equivalents. That does not make them identical.

Recommended editorial distinction:
- Use **danîn** as the ordinary neutral verb for placing/setting an object somewhere when that is the intended meaning.
- Use **xistin** where the source/context calls for its own lexical sense: throw/cast, strike/knock, put/bring into a position, or a fixed expression.
- Do not replace a source's xistin with danîn solely because both can be translated “put”.
- Do not infer that every xistin phrase can be rephrased with danîn without changing meaning or idiomaticity.

## 4. lê xistin: lexicalized construction

Haig & Öpengin explicitly state that Kurmancî creates additional verbs through lexicalization of a verb plus a prepositional complement, giving:
**lê xistin** — “beat (a person)”, literally “strike on him/her”.

This construction should be taught as a lexical unit with its own meaning and argument structure. Do not translate it mechanically as “put on” or treat **lê** as a freely optional decoration. The academic source's example establishes the lexicalized sense, while the dictionary gives related expressions such as **destê xwe lê xistin** (“to put one's hand on it / touch it / take hold of it”) and **destlêxistin**.

The exact person-by-person agreement paradigm for the lexicalized **lê xistin** construction was not independently established in the sources inspected. Apply the general transitive past alignment only as a grammatical framework; do not invent example forms and label them sourced.

## 5. Past alignment and the table-reading trap

Haig & Öpengin describe the Kurmancî agreement system: present-tense transitive verbs index the agent, while past-tense transitive verbs index the patient; intransitive verbs index their subject. The person suffixes include past **-Ø** for 3sg and **-(i)n** for plural.

Consequently, a table whose first column lists **Ez/Tu/Ew/Em/Hûn/Ew** next to past forms can be showing the grammatical patient, not the ergative agent. This matters for reading the Kurdish Academy xistin table:
- singular patient rows: **xist**
- plural patient rows: **xistin**

The dictionary sentence **min ew xist** illustrates the alignment clearly: **min** is the oblique agent, **ew** the direct patient, and the verb is in the 3sg-patient form **xist**.

## 7. Source and orthography cautions

The source materials are not all from the same editorial tradition or dialect. The academic overview describes Kurmanji variation and gives the stem alternation **x- / xîn-**; beginner materials and online tables may choose one surface form. The course should choose its standard only after the project's editor reviews the target variety. Preserve source spellings in evidence notes.

One online Kurdish Academy table includes a separate heading “Danîn - To put / To place” but lists present forms **dihêlim**. This conflicts with the independent Wîkîferheng, Chyet, and beginner-grammar evidence **datînim** for danîn and appears to be a mislabeled or copied paradigm (dihêlim belongs to hêlan/hiştin, “leave/allow”). Do not reuse that table's danîn row.
