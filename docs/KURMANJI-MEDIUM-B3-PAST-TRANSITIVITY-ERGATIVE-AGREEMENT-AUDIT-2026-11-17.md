# B3 Kurmanji-Medium: Past Transitivity, Ergative Alignment and Agreement Audit
Date: 2026-11-17
Scope: grammar audit for the attested verbs hildan, vekirin and daketin. Documentation only; no learner-facing lesson changes.

## 1. Core finding

The audit confirms that Kurmanji alignment must be taught through the interaction of transitivity, past-stem morphology, case and agreement—not as a simplistic rule that “the past is ergative” in every clause.

The central contrast in the sources is:
- Present/non-past transitive clauses: direct/nominative agent; oblique object; the verb agrees with the agent.
- Past-stem transitive clauses: oblique agent; direct/nominative object; the verb normally agrees with the direct object.
- Intransitive clauses: the sole argument remains direct/nominative and controls verbal agreement in both present and past.

Haig's overview of Northern Kurdish states that the ergative construction is used with past tenses of transitive verbs and otherwise syntax is accusative. The University of Arizona overview gives the pronoun contrast and examples **Min çivîkek dît** (“I saw a bird”) and **Ehmed ez dîtim** (“Ehmed saw me”), illustrating the oblique transitive agent and object-controlled agreement. The article by Yavaş & Kızıl (2020) explicitly cautions that deviations from the canonical pattern occur and deserve separate treatment.

## 2. Evidence and grammatical diagnostics

### 2.1 Canonical transitive pattern

The University of Arizona's Kurmanji overview describes:
- Present: direct subject + oblique object; agreement follows the subject.
- Past: oblique subject/agent + direct object; agreement follows the object.

Examples in that source:
- **Ez wî mirovî dibînim.** — “I see that man.” (present, direct agent **ez**, oblique object **wî mirovî**)
- **Min çivîkek dît.** — “I saw a bird.” (past, oblique agent **min**, direct object **çivîkek**)
- **Ehmed ez dîtim.** — “Ehmed saw me.” (past, direct object **ez**, verb suffix **-im** agrees with first-person singular object)

Haig's Northern Kurdish chapter (2018, §2.7.2) independently characterizes past transitive ergativity with oblique A and direct O, while retaining ordinary subject–object order. The 2002 Matras article defines transitivity in Kurmanji in terms of tense-sensitive alignment, but also notes that attested past clauses may deviate from the canonical construction. Thus the canonical pattern is a teaching baseline, not a claim that every variety and every clause is exceptionless.

### 2.2 Intransitive pattern

The *Ergative alignment in Kurmanji* study gives:
- **Ez di-rev-im.** — “I am running.” (present intransitive)
- **Ez revî-m.** — “I ran.” (past intransitive)
- **Em di-ç-in mal-ê.** — “We are going home.”
- **Em çû-n mal-ê.** — “We went home.”

The sole argument is direct and controls agreement in both tenses. This contrasts with past transitive clauses, where the oblique agent does not control the ordinary object agreement suffix.

### 2.3 Variation and limits

Yavaş & Kızıl (2020), *Ergativity in Kurmanji Kurdish and Deviations from the Canonical Pattern*, is particularly relevant because its stated subject is the non-canonical patterns. Matras (2002) likewise warns that past transitive text examples can deviate from the canonical alignment and that nominal case and verbal agreement can be partly independent in some varieties. The B3 pathway must therefore:
1. teach the canonical Standard Kurmanji pattern clearly;
2. mark dialectal/structural deviations as separate evidence;
3. avoid presenting a single simplified rule as exceptionless across all Kurmanji varieties.

## 3. Application to the three audited verbs

### Hildan — transitive

The Chyet dictionary examples previously recorded for **hildan** include past **hilda** in clauses meaning “he picked up his grain” and “the lad took the paper.” These attest lexical use and the singular past surface form. They do not, by themselves, establish a complete person/number paradigm.

Because **hildan** is transitive, a full learner-facing past example should identify:
- the oblique agent (e.g. **min**, **te**, **wî/wê**, **me**, **we**, **wan**);
- the direct object;
- whether the verb form agrees with that direct object.

The Kurdish Academy conjugation table lists singular **hilda** and plural **hildan**, but the table's labels and paradigm should be checked against a grammar's account of object agreement and ergative syntax before it is used as a complete teaching paradigm. The past form is now lexically attested; the full pedagogical paradigm remains a separate verification task.

### Vekirin — transitive

The cited pedagogical example **Min ew vekir** (“I opened it”) is consistent with the canonical past transitive pattern:
- **min** = oblique agent;
- **ew** = direct object;
- **vekir** = past form, with no overt suffix needed for a third-person singular direct object.

The present form **Ez vedikim** (as given in the audited grammar sources) is structurally different:
- **ez** = direct agent;
- **di** is placed after the preverbal element **ve-**;
- the present verb agrees with the agent.

Do not conflate **veke** (imperative), **bivekim** (subjunctive), **vedikim** (present indicative), and **vekir** (past). They belong to different inflectional environments.

### Daketin — intransitive

Chyet's examples previously recorded include **Kurê pîrê daket sûkê** (“The old woman's son went down to the market”) and **Gavan û jina xwe daketin nav gund** (“The cowherd and his wife went down to the village”). These are intransitive:
- the sole argument is the direct subject;
- there is no transitive object;
- the past verb form appears as **daket** in the singular example and **daketin** in the plural example.

This agrees with the general intransitive pattern in which the direct subject controls person/number agreement in both present and past. The two Chyet examples attest the singular/plural surface forms in context; they are not a complete conjugation table. The spelling variant **dakêtin** found in another teaching source remains a separate orthographic issue.

## 5. Recommended B3 teaching sequence (design recommendation only)

1. Introduce direct and oblique personal pronouns before presenting past transitive clauses.
2. Teach one present transitive example and its past counterpart side by side, explicitly marking agent and object.
3. Use **Min ew vekir** as the first compact past-transitive example, with the caveat that the object is third-person singular.
4. Contrast it with **Ez revîm / Ez di-revim** as an intransitive pair, showing that the sole subject remains direct in both tenses.
5. Introduce **hildan** only after learners can identify the oblique agent and direct object; do not rely on a bare conjugation chart.
6. Teach **daket / daketin** as an intransitive contrast, not as another ergative transitive.
7. Put non-canonical and regional patterns in an explicitly labelled extension, not in the core beginner explanation.
