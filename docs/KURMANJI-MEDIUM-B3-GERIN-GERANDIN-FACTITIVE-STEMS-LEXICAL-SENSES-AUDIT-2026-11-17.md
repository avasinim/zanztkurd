# Gerîn / gerandin: factitive meaning, stem alternation, and object-agreement evidence

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only audit of the Standard Kurmanji pair *gerîn / gerandin*. The note distinguishes the intransitive base from the transitive factitive, records multiple lexical senses of the derived verb, and avoids treating every attested gloss as a single English equivalent.

## Executive finding

The pair is explicitly documented by Haig and Öpengin as a Standard Kurmanji factitive derivation:

- **gerîn** — “walk, stroll”; intransitive base.
- **gerandin** — “lead”; transitive factitive.

The stems are:
- **gerîn**: present **ger-**, past **gerî-**.
- **gerandin**: present **gerîn-** (also written **gerên-** in one grammar source), past **gerand-**.

The stem alternation is not a guess from the infinitives. It is directly documented in Ch. Kh. Bakaev's Kurmanji grammar: *gerîn* has past stem **gerî-** and present stem **ger-**; *gerandin* has past stem **gerand-** and present stem **gerîn- / gerên-**.

The transitive verb *gerandin* has a wider lexical range than “lead”: sources also attest “turn/rotate”, “take for a walk”, “guide”, “drive”, “wrap”, “distribute/serve”, and “deceive” in particular constructions and contexts. These should be taught as context-dependent senses, not collapsed into one gloss.

## 1. Base verb: gerîn

Haig and Öpengin give **gerîn** “walk, stroll” as the intransitive base in their explicit factitive example **gerîn → gerandin**.

Kurdolingo independently records:
- infinitive: **gerîn**
- present stem: **ger-**
- example: **Em carinan li kolanan digerin.** (“Sometimes we stroll in the streets.”)

Bakaev records the stem pair:
- past: **gerî-**
- present: **ger-**

The Yazidi grammar tables source gives a regional description of *gerîn* with senses “search / go around” and explicitly labels it “trans./intrans. depending on meaning”. This supports a caution: *gerîn* can have extended senses and valency variation in some descriptions. For the Standard Kurmanji factitive teaching contrast, retain Haig and Öpengin's clear base sense “walk, stroll”; do not state that every possible use of *gerîn* is strictly intransitive.

## 2. Derived verb: gerandin

Haig and Öpengin explicitly cite:
> **gerîn** “walk, stroll” → **gerandin** “lead”

Chyet's *Kurmanji Lexicon* records:
- **gerandin gerîn- v.t.**
- meanings: “to turn, rotate; to take for a walk; to produce”.

The parenthetical **gerîn-** is the present stem, and **v.t.** marks transitivity.

Chyet's lexical examples and later dictionaries attest a broad semantic range:
- guide, lead, take someone for a walk;
- turn or rotate something;
- distribute/serve (for example, coffee);
- wrap or put something around;
- deceive, in the reciprocal expression **hevûdu gerandin**.

The exact sense depends on the object and construction. “Lead” is the clearest factitive gloss for the basic derivational contrast; the other senses belong in a lexical-semantic note rather than as interchangeable translations.

## 3. Stems: directly documented

| Verb | Past stem | Present stem |
|---|---|---|
| **gerîn** | **gerî-** | **ger-** |
| **gerandin** | **gerand-** | **gerîn- / gerên-** |

Bakaev's grammar explicitly states that the past stem of derived verbs is found by removing the infinitive ending **-in / -n**; the present stem of derived transitive verbs is formed from the past stem by removing its final **-d** and changing **a** to **î**. His examples give **gerandin → gerand- → gerîn- (gerên-)**.

The standard present examples independently agree:
- **Ez digerim.** — I walk/stroll/go around.
- **Rêber zarokan li bajêr digerîne.** — The guide shows/leads the children around the city.

A Kurmanji grammar exercise gives the simple past transitive example **Min ew gerand.** (“I led/took them around”), and Chyet gives further lexical examples with the derived verb.

## 4. Transitivity and past agreement

The base *gerîn* is presented as the intransitive “walk/stroll” verb in Haig and Öpengin's derivational example. The derived *gerandin* is explicitly transitive in Chyet (**v.t.**) and in Kurmanji grammar sources.

The attested sentence **Min ew gerand** shows:
- agent in oblique form: **min**;
- direct patient: **ew**;
- past verb form: **gerand**.

This is consistent with Kurmanji transitive past agreement, where the verb indexes the patient. The cited singular third-person patient example does not establish the complete object-agreement paradigm. Do not generate every person/number form from this one example.

## 5. Teaching-safe conclusion

> **gerîn** واتای «پیاسەکردن/گەڕان» دەدات؛ ڕەگی ئێستای **ger-** و ڕەگی ڕابردووی **gerî-** ـە. **gerandin** لە نموونەی هۆکارسازیی ستاندارددا واتای «ڕێبەریکردن/گەڕاندنی کەسێک» دەدات؛ کردارێکی تێپەڕە، ڕەگی ئێستای **gerîn-** و ڕەگی ڕابردووی **gerand-** ـە. ئەم کردارە واتای تریشی هەیە، وەک سووڕاندن، دابەشکردن و پێچان؛ واتاکە بە پێی تەواوکەر و چوارچێوە دیاری دەکرێت.
