# Şemzînan Same-Context Future Contrast: Negative Indicative and Positive *dê*

Date: 2026-11-17  
Type: documentation-only close reading of one locality-labelled natural-speech sample.

## Source

University of Manchester, *The Dialects of Kurdish*, sample K-078, “Şemdinli, Turkey.” The page provides an audio recording and transcript with English translation. The relevant passage is turns 14–17.

- Sample page: https://kurdish.humanities.manchester.ac.uk/k-078-semdinli-turkey-2/
- Recording: https://kurdish.humanities.manchester.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/K-078b_clip.mp3

The sample is explicitly locality-labelled Şemdinli (Şemzînan). The transcript is the source's own orthography; spellings below are reproduced without normalization.

## Turn-by-turn evidence

| Turn | Transcript excerpt | Published English translation | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | *gotin ceyran dê bo* | “they said the electricity will come” | Positive future with overt *dê*. |
| 15 | *gotin wiła em ceyranê girê nadeyn em dê çîn sibehê hêyn* | “they said we will not connect the electricity, we will leave and come back tomorrow” | Negative clause *em ceyranê girê nadeyn* is translated with future meaning; the coordinated following clause has overt *dê* and *sibehê* ‘tomorrow’. |
| 16 | *goti, em dê sibe hêyn ceyrana hingo çêkeyn* | “they said we will come tomorrow and make your electricity” | Positive future with overt *dê* and *sibe* ‘tomorrow’. |
| 17 | *Heta roja paştir hatin jî ceyrana me çêkirin* | “By the following day they came and connected our electricity.” | Narrative confirms the next-day realization. |

## Contrast in the same discourse

The negative clause in turn 15 is:

*em ceyranê girê nadeyn*  
Published translation: ‘we will not connect the electricity’

The following coordinated clause in the same turn is:

*em dê çîn sibehê hêyn*  
Published translation: ‘we will leave and come back tomorrow’

Turns 14 and 16 also show affirmative future clauses with overt *dê*. Thus, within the same narrative episode and the same locality-labelled sample, the speaker uses:
- negative indicative morphology *na-* in a clause translated with future reference;
- overt *dê* in adjacent affirmative future clauses.

The future interpretation of the negative clause is supported by the speaker's report of a plan, the immediately coordinated *dê* clause, the explicit ‘tomorrow’ reference, turn 16's repeated future promise, and turn 17's next-day fulfillment.

## Analytical caution

1. The website supplies an English translation, not a morpheme-by-morpheme gloss for *girê nadeyn*. The analysis ‘negative present indicative’ follows the published regional grammatical description and the visible negative form, but should not be represented as a gloss printed by the Manchester site.
2. The negative predicate itself contains no overt *dê* in the transcript. Do not insert one when quoting it.
3. The sentence is reported speech in a narrative, not a controlled minimal pair. It is strong contextual evidence, but not proof that every negative indicative in Şemzînan has future meaning.
4. The coordinated clause *em dê çîn sibehê hêyn* has overt *dê*. Do not claim that the entire turn lacks a future marker.
5. The source's spelling *nadeyn* should be preserved in quotation. Do not silently replace it with Standard Kurmanji *naçin* or infer an unattested paradigm.
6. This evidence is distinct from the Behdinî example *Ez sibe digel wan na-çi-m*, which remains labelled Behdinî.

## Safe conclusion

K-078 provides a particularly useful same-context contrast: a negative clause translated as future (‘we will not connect the electricity’) occurs beside affirmative future clauses marked with *dê* and ‘tomorrow’. This supports the analysis that the negative indicative can receive future interpretation contextually in this Şemzînan speaker's narrative. It does not establish a dedicated negative-future morpheme or a complete paradigm.
