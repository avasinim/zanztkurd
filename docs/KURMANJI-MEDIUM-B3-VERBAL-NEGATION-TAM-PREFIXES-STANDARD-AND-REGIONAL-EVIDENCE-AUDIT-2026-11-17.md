# Kurmanji Verbal Negation and TAM Prefixes — Standard and Regional Evidence Audit — 2026-11-17

## Purpose and scope

This audit distinguishes verbal negation markers from indicative, subjunctive and imperfective prefixes, and records the environments in which they occur. It is based primarily on Geoffrey Haig (2018), “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî)”, §2.6 and §3.4, with a specialist affix-ordering study by Songül Gündoğdu (2015), “Negation in Kurmanji”.

This is a research document only. It does not create learner content, alter existing lessons, import lexical data, or implement code/schema changes.

## 1. Do not treat all prefixes as negation

The forms have different grammatical jobs:

| Form | Function in Haig's Standard Kurmanjî sketch | Editorial classification |
|---|---|---|
| di- | Present indicative; also marks past imperfective/progressive in the relevant past construction | TAM/aspect, not negation |
| bi- | Present subjunctive / irrealis | Mood, not negation |
| na- | Negative present indicative; replaces the positive indicative prefix di- | Negation |
| ne- | Negative subjunctive; also negates simple past and past progressive | Negation |
| ni- | Lexically restricted negative present form with zanîn and karîn | Negation allomorph / lexically conditioned form |
| me-/mɛ- | Haig reports a distinct negative imperative marker in Western Kurmanjî | Regional imperative negation; not a general Standard K. rule |

The classification is essential: **di- and bi- are not negative prefixes**. They are positive TAM/mood markers whose slot can be blocked or replaced by negation in certain constructions.

## 2. Standard Kurmanjî: present stem

Haig §2.6 states that present-stem forms (simple present, subjunctive, imperative and future) take a single prefix in the ordinary pattern:
- indicative: di-;
- subjunctive: bi-;
- negative indicative: na-, replacing di-;
- negative subjunctive: ne-, replacing bi-.

Examples and contrasts:
- **di-bêj-im** — I say (indicative).
- **na-bêj-im** — I do not say (negative indicative; the negative marker replaces di-).
- **bi-xw-im** — that I eat / subjunctive.
- **ne-xw-im** — that I do not eat / negative subjunctive.

These are structural illustrations of the prefix opposition. A learner-facing paradigm should be checked against the chosen standard grammar and native editorial review before publication.

Haig notes that some imperative forms can lack a prefix. Therefore, do not mechanically add bi- or ne- to every imperative without checking the particular construction.

## 3. Lexically restricted ni-

Haig's footnote 7 explicitly identifies two verbs whose present stem is negated with **ni-**:
- **zanîn** “know”: **nizanim** — I do not know.
- **karîn** “be able”: **nikarim** — I cannot.

This is a restricted lexical pattern, not a free alternative that can be applied to any verb. Haig further notes that **şiyan**, used in Behdînî and Şemzînan, takes **ne-** in the cited negative form **neşêm**. This contrast must retain its dialect and lexical scope.

The 2015 study by Songül Gündoğdu also analyses n(a)-, n(e)- and ni- as distinct negative markers in a templatic affix system. Its abstract emphasizes blocking: the negation marker excludes certain prefixes such as progressive di- and subjunctive bi- from the same slot. This supports treating ni- as a morphologically conditioned member of the negative system, not as a universal present-tense replacement.

## 4. Standard Kurmanjî: past stem and past progressive

Haig distinguishes the simple past from the past progressive:
- the simple past is generally based on the past stem plus person agreement;
- the past progressive uses di- as an imperfective/progressive marker;
- Standard K. negates both simple and progressive past with **ne-**.

Attested schematic examples from Haig:
- **ne-hatin** — they did not come.
- **ne-di-hatin** — they were not coming.

Thus, a negative past progressive can contain both the negative prefix and di-, in that order. This is different from the present indicative, where na- replaces di- rather than combining with it.

Haig's footnote 8 records an important regional exception: in Western Kurmanjî around Adıyaman–Urfa, past progressive negation can use **na-**, as in **na-de-kir-in** “they were not doing it”. This is a variety-specific contrast and must not be generalized to Standard K.

## 5. Future and subjunctive negation

Haig notes that in Standard K. and contemporary written Kurdish, the future auxiliary can remain in negative sentences, with the subjunctive negation **ne-**. Behdînî differs: Haig says there is no dedicated negative future construction of the same type and the negative indicative is used.

Editorial consequence:
- do not assume that a future marker automatically changes the negative prefix;
- keep Standard K. and Behdînî future-negation patterns separate;
- record whether an example is an independent future clause or a subjunctive complement.

## 6. Preverbs and compound verbs: prefix placement is not uniform

Haig §3.4.1.2 describes an important contrast:
- In Standard K., inflectional prefixes such as negation and indicative/imperfective are inserted **between** a preverbal particle and the verb stem. His example is **ra-di-keve** from **raketin**.
- In Southeastern Kurmanjî (SEK), negation and imperfective prefixes can occur **before** the preverbal particle, reflecting a more fully lexicalized preverb+stem unit.

Haig gives a Şemzînan example:
- **ne-di-řa-westiya-m** — “I would not stop (working) until evening.”

He also reports that in some highly lexicalized and frequent light-verb constructions, the negative prefix may occur at the left edge of the entire verbal complex, even before non-verbal material.

This is a high-risk area for overgeneralization. The Academy's lexicon should record the actual construction, preverb, variety and source example. It should not impose one universal prefix-order rule on every compound verb.

## 7. Western Kurmanjî imperative negation

In §3.4.2.3, Haig reports a distinct negative imperative prefix **mɛ-** in Western Kurmanjî, with **mɛ-wē** “do not say (it)”. This is not presented as the general Standard K. imperative rule. Preserve the source's phonetic notation and variety label; do not silently normalize it to Standard K. **me-** without an editorial decision and orthographic evidence.

## 8. Editorial decision table for the Academy

| Candidate form | What can safely be said | What must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|
| na- | Negative present indicative in Standard K.; also WK past progressive in Haig's specified region | That na- negates every tense in every dialect |
| ne- | Negative subjunctive and Standard K. simple/past-progressive negation | That ne- is interchangeable with na- in all contexts |
| ni- | Lexically restricted with zanîn and karîn in Haig's account | That ni- is a free allomorph for any present verb |
| di- | Indicative present or past imperfective/progressive, depending on construction | That di- is a negation marker |
| bi- | Subjunctive / irrealis prefix | That bi- is negative |
| me-/mɛ- | Western Kurmanjî negative imperative form as reported by Haig | That it is the default imperative negator in Standard K. |

## 9. Required evidence fields for future entries

For each negative verb form, store:
1. lemma and sense;
2. exact surface form, including hyphens and diacritics;
3. stem type (present or past);
4. grammatical category of each prefix (NEG, IND, IPFV, SUBJ, IMP);
5. person/number and clause type;
6. variety and locality exactly as supplied by the source;
7. source, page, section, example/table number;
8. whether the form was checked against page image or only text extraction;
9. editorial normalization, if any, kept separately from the source form;
10. native-editor review status.

A form such as **ne-di-hatin** must not be stored as a single unanalyzed “negative prefix”. The record must retain both the NEG and IPFV layers.
