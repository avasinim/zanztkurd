# Xwendin: past perfect (pluperfect), object agreement, and negation audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only audit for the Standard Northern Kurmanji pathway.

## Executive finding

Kurmanji past perfect (pluperfect) is distinct from both the simple past and the present perfect. For a consonant-final transitive past stem, Thackston describes the pattern as:

**past stem + -i- + past-tense form of bûn**, with the transitive ergative case and object-agreement pattern retained.

For **xwendin**, the source-attested past stem is **xwend** (Lesson 7). Applying the documented formation rule yields the morphological construction **xwend-i-bû** for a third-person singular object. However, the sources consulted do not directly attest a complete **xwendin** pluperfect paradigm. Therefore **xwendibû** and plural-object **xwendibûn** are recorded here as transparent rule-based predictions, not direct quotations or fully verified course forms.

## 1. Do not conflate the three perfect/past forms

The Harvard/Uppsala *Kurmanji Kurdish* lessons distinguish:
- Simple past: **Min helbestek xwend.** — I read a poem.
- Present perfect: **Min helbestek xwendiye.** — I have read a poem.
- Past perfect/pluperfect: formed with the past stem plus the past-tense form of **bûn**, e.g. **Min nan xwaribû.** — I had eaten.

Thus **xwendiye** is a present-perfect example, not a past-perfect form. It must not be used as evidence for **xwendibû**.

## 2. Past perfect formation in transitive clauses

Thackston §21.1 states that transitive past perfect is formed from the past stem + **-i-** + the past tense of **bûn**, on the ergative model. He gives:
- **min dîtibû** — I had seen (it).
- **wî ez dîtibûm** — he had seen me.
- **wî em dîtibûn** — he had seen us.
- **wî ew dîtibûn** — he had seen them.
- **Îngilîzan zimanê hindiyan qedexe nekiribûn.** — The English had not banned the Indians' language.

The Harvard/Uppsala Lesson 8 independently gives:
- **Min nan xwaribû.** — I had eaten.
- **We ew pirtûk bi hev re kiribûn?** — Had you bought those books together?
- **Babê min em ligel xwe nebiribûn dukanê.** — My father had not taken us to the shop.

These examples show that the transitive past perfect retains oblique agents and agreement with the direct object. Plural direct objects can correspond to plural verb forms, as in **wî em dîtibûn**, **wî ew dîtibûn**, and **kiribûn** with plural **ew pirtûk**.

## 3. Applying the rule to xwendin: evidence status

Lesson 7 directly identifies:
- Infinitive **xwendin** (“read, study”).
- Past stem **xwend**.
- Simple-past singular-object example **Min helbestek xwend**.
- Simple-past plural-object example **Wî çirok xwendin** (“He read the stories”).

Thackston and Lesson 8 establish the pluperfect formation rule, but the exact **xwendin** pluperfect form was not found in the consulted text.

| Candidate | Analysis | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| **xwendibû** | xwend- + -i- + bû; third-person singular object | Rule-based prediction; exact xwendin form not directly attested |
| **xwendibûn** | xwend- + -i- + bûn; plural object | Rule-based prediction from plural-object agreement; exact xwendin form not directly attested |
| **nexwendibû** | ne- + xwendibû | Rule-based negative prediction; exact xwendin form not directly attested |
| **nexwendibûn** | ne- + xwendibûn | Rule-based negative plural-object prediction; exact xwendin form not directly attested |

These forms are not claimed to be ungrammatical. They are withheld from learner-facing use until directly checked with an attested xwendin example, a full paradigm, or a qualified speaker/editor following the Academy's approved variety.

## 4. Negation

Both Thackston and Lesson 8 document **ne-** as the negative marker in past perfect:
- Thackston: **Îngilîzan ... qedexe nekiribûn** (“The English had not banned…”).
- Lesson 8: **Babê min em ... nebiribûn** (“My father had not taken us…”).
- Lesson 8: **Min marekî ewqasî mezin qet nedîtibû** (“I had never seen such a big snake”).

This supports the general placement of **ne-** before the pluperfect verb form. It does not by itself directly attest the exact negative **xwendin** forms listed in section 3.

## 5. Teaching-safe decision

For the Standard Northern Kurmanji pathway:
1. Teach the contrast between simple past, present perfect, and past perfect explicitly.
2. Use directly attested examples **Min helbestek xwend**, **Min helbestek xwendiye**, and **Min nan xwaribû**, while explaining that they are three different forms.
3. Teach the general transitive pluperfect pattern from directly attested verbs such as **dîtin**, **xwarin**, and **kirin**.
4. Explain that the agent remains oblique and the verb agrees with the direct object.
5. Mark **xwendibû / xwendibûn / nexwendibû / nexwendibûn** as rule-based predictions pending direct verification, not as quotations.
6. Do not infer a complete person paradigm from these examples.
7. Do not modify learner-facing lessons until explicit approval.
