# Kurmancî Medium B3 — xapandin and xapîn: transitivity, voice, stems, and agreement audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only lexical and grammatical audit. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, seed, database, migration, PR, or deployment changes.

## Executive findings

The two verbs form a clear transitivity/voice contrast and must not be collapsed into one paradigm:

| Lemma | Core meaning | Valency | Present stem | Past stem | 1sg present |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **xapandin** | deceive, cheat, fool, trick | transitive | xapîn- (also xapên-) | xapand- | ez dixapînim / dixapênim |
| **xapîn** | be deceived, be fooled | intransitive | xapê- (dictionary citation form) | xapî (past stem inferred from regular infinitive pattern; do not extend without paradigm evidence) | ez dixapêm (lexicon gives present citation form xapê) |

The evidence for the active verb **xapandin** and the intransitive verb **xapîn** is strong across multiple independent lexicons. The complete simple-past agreement paradigm for xapandin beyond a third-person-singular patient has not been independently confirmed in a directly inspected verb-specific source; do not invent it.

## 1. xapandin: transitive active verb

The Kurmancî Wiktionary entry explicitly labels **xapandin** transitive and records the senses:
- to deceive, cheat, fool, trick;
- to make someone believe something false;
- to occupy or amuse oneself (reflexive/extended use in an example with **xwe**).

It lists two present-stem variants: **-xapîn- / -xapên-**.

Present paradigm:
- ez **dixapînim** / **dixapênim**
- tu **dixapînî** / **dixapênî**
- ew **dixapîne** / **dixapêne**
- em, hûn, ew **dixapînin** / **dixapênin**

Imperative:
- singular: **bixapîne** / **bixapêne**
- plural: **bixapînin** / **bixapênin**

Past stem: **xapand-**. The Wiktionary simple-past table lists **xapand** with each oblique-agent row, representing the third-person-singular patient citation pattern. That table does not establish every non-third-person patient agreement form.

A literary/example citation in the same entry is:
**... rojekê neyê we nexapîne.**
“... may it not deceive you one day.”
The example is a present/future-subjunctive form, not a past-tense model.

The lexical example:
**Ez carinan xwe bi kompûterê dixapînim, carinan jî bi xwendina romanan.**
“I sometimes occupy/amuse myself with the computer, sometimes with reading novels.”
This is an extended reflexive use and should not replace the basic transitive meaning in the beginner definition.

## 2. xapîn: intransitive ‘be deceived’

The independent lemma **xapîn** is listed as intransitive in:
- the Kurdish-English lexicon (Chyet): “to be deceived, to be fooled”;
- the Kurdish-English dictionary hosted by Kurdipedia: “to be deceived, to be cheated”;
- the Kurmancî Wiktionary-related entry and the large Kurmancî lexicon.

The lexicon records the citation form **xapê** for the present, while the infinitive **xapîn** follows the regular -în pattern in the reference grammar. The dictionary evidence establishes the intransitive lexical identity and present citation form, but a full six-person paradigm and directly verified simple-past forms were not found in the sources inspected for this audit.

Do not simply copy the transitive paradigm of xapandin and remove -andin. The two lemmas have different valency:
- **xapandin**: an agent deceives a patient.
- **xapîn**: a person is deceived / falls for a trick; the subject is the experiencer/patient and the verb is intransitive.

The English gloss “be deceived” is a lexical meaning, not necessarily a productive passive construction formed from xapandin in every context.

## 3. Past tense and split alignment

The general Kurmancî past-tense rule for transitive verbs is:
- agent in oblique case: min, te, wî, wê, me, we, wan;
- patient in direct/absolutive case;
- verb agreement tracks the patient.

For a third-person-singular patient, the simple past commonly appears without an overt agreement ending. The general rule is documented by Zimanzan and the academic reference grammar.

For **xapandin**, the lexical source confirms past stem **xapand-** and citation form **xapand**. However, this audit does not claim a complete verb-specific agreement paradigm such as “min te xapandî” or “wê ez xapandim” without direct independent attestations. Such forms may be morphologically expected under the general rule, but they must not be presented as sourced examples until verified.

For **xapîn**, because the lemma is intransitive, its past-tense subject pattern should follow the intransitive past rather than the transitive ergative pattern. Nevertheless, a verb-specific complete past paradigm was not independently established here. Do not manufacture one from a guessed past stem.

## 4. Stem and orthographic variation

The transitive present stem is listed as **xapîn-** and **xapên-**. Both are recorded variants, not an error to be silently corrected. The Kurdish encyclopedic dictionary also records present forms in the **xapîn-** series. The Chyet lexicon gives the headword xapandin with present stem xapîn-.

The intransitive lemma is written **xapîn**. A related dictionary records **xapîyan** as a cross-reference to xapîn. Treat this as a lexical variant/reference, not as evidence for a new tense or a new transitive verb.

## 5. Teaching recommendation and confidence

| Claim | Confidence | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| xapandin is transitive and means deceive/cheat/fool | High | Teach |
| xapandin present stems xapîn-/xapên- | High | Teach as recorded variants; choose house form only after editorial policy |
| xapandin past stem xapand- | High | Teach |
| xapîn is intransitive and means be deceived/be fooled | High | Teach as a separate lemma |
| xapîn full six-person present paradigm | Partial | Do not print a full table until a direct paradigm is verified |
| xapandin full past object-agreement table | Partial | Do not invent missing patient forms |
| xapîn verb-specific past paradigm | Unverified | Hold pending source evidence |

**Recommended lesson contrast:**
- **Kesek min dixapîne.** — Someone deceives me. (A constructed teaching illustration; not a quoted corpus attestation.)
- **Ez dixapêm.** — I am deceived / I get fooled. (Based on the intransitive lexicon citation form; verify local usage with a native editor before making it a fixed course example.)

To avoid teaching constructed forms as sourced evidence, label illustrative examples clearly and have the Standard Kurmancî editor confirm the preferred xapîn present form in the project's selected variety.
