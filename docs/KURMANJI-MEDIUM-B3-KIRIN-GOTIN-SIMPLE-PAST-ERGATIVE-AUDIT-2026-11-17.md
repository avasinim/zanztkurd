# Kurmancî B3 — kirin û gotin: simple-past ergativity and evidence audit (2026-11-17)

## Scope and safety
Documentation-only linguistic research. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, migration, production data, PR #3, or deployment was changed. The repository's 26 + 12 lesson structure remains untouched. The target is contemporary Standard Kurmancî in Hawar-based Latin orthography; historical and regional forms are recorded separately.

## Executive findings
- **kirin** (“to do/make”) and **gotin** (“to say”) are transitive in their ordinary lexical uses. Their simple past therefore follows the ergative pattern: the agent is oblique, the patient is nominative, and the verb agrees with the patient.
- The core past stems are **kir-** and **got-**. With a third-person singular patient, the standard examples are **Min kar kir** (“I worked/did work”) and **Min got** (“I said/told [it]”), depending on the lexical complement and construction.
- Agreement is not with the agent. If the patient is first-person singular, the form may be **Te min dîtim** (“You saw me”); if the patient is plural, the verb takes plural agreement: **Min ew dîtin** (“I saw them”). The same patient-agreement principle applies to kirin and gotin.
- **Gotin** has an important constructional exception: when followed by a subordinate content clause, Thackston explicitly records stylistic loss of ergativity, e.g. **Min gotim ku…** (“I said that…”), rather than the ordinary ergative **min got**. Do not teach this as the universal form for every object.
- A 2026 peer-reviewed study examines every use of **kirin** in Ehmedê Xanî's *Mem û Zîn*, including simple, derived and compound uses, and reports archaic/dialectal conjugations. This is useful historical evidence but must not be treated as a direct model of contemporary Standard Kurmancî inflection.
- A separate corpus-driven study describes **gotin** among Kurmanji ditransitive verbs (three-participant constructions). This confirms the need to teach recipient/addressee constructions separately from the basic transitive pattern.

## 1. Core morphology and ergative alignment

| Infinitive | Meaning | Past stem | Standard simple-past pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| kirin | do, make | kir- | oblique agent + nominative patient + kir/kir… |
| gotin | say, tell | got- | oblique agent + nominative patient/content + got/got… |

In the ordinary transitive simple past:
1. The agent takes the oblique form: **min, te, wî/wê, me, we, wan**.
2. The patient is nominative: a noun or direct pronoun such as **ez, tu, ew, em, hûn, ew**.
3. The verb agrees in person/number with the patient, not the oblique agent.

Illustrative agreement contrasts from Thackston's general ergative examples:
- **Min tu dîtî** — “I saw you” (2sg patient; verb 2sg).
- **Wî em dîtin** — “He saw us” (1pl patient; verb plural).
- **Te ez dîtim** — “You saw me” (1sg patient; verb 1sg).
- **Min ew dîtin** — “I saw them” (plural patient; verb plural).

These examples establish the agreement rule; they are not claimed as literary tokens of kirin or gotin.

## 2. The verb kirin
- Past stem: **kir-**.
- A simple transitive example with a third-person singular patient is **Min kar kir** (“I worked/did work”); the lexical noun **kar** is the patient/complement and the verb is singular.
- In open compound constructions such as **behs kirin** (“to discuss”), the extended complement controls agreement. Thackston gives **Wî ji min re behsa serpêhatiyên xwe kirin** (“He discussed his adventures with me”): the extended patient **serpêhatiyên xwe** is plural, so **kirin** is plural.
- Do not mechanically conjugate every expression ending in kirin as though it were a simple isolated verb. In extended compounds, identify the complete patient phrase first; its final relevant element can determine singular/plural agreement.

## 3. The verb gotin
- Past stem: **got-**.
- Ordinary transitive simple past follows the same ergative pattern: oblique speaker/agent + nominative content/patient + verb agreeing with that patient.
- **Gotin** also participates in ditransitive constructions, where the addressee/recipient may be expressed with an oblique form or an adpositional phrase. The syntactic role of the quoted content and the addressee must be distinguished.
- Important content-clause exception in Thackston §18.5: with a following subordinate clause, stylistic ergativity loss may occur: **Min gotim ku…** (“I said that…”), contrasted with the usual **min got**. Thackston also gives **Min dîtim ku…** (“I saw that…”). This is a documented constructional option, not a general replacement of ergative alignment.

## 4. Historical literary evidence: kirin in Mem û Zîn
Akgül and Açar (2026), *Bikaranîna Lêkera “Kirin”ê Di Mem û Zînê De*, Artuklu Kurdology 22, pp.24–48, explicitly analyzes the verb throughout Ehmedê Xanî's 17th-century *Mem û Zîn*. The article:
- classifies simple, derived and compound uses;
- discusses its role in compound-verb formation and integration of borrowed lexical material;
- reports archaic and dialectal conjugations that are not part of contemporary Standard Kurmancî;
- analyzes ditransitive properties and disputed passive-like readings.

The online abstract does not supply a full token-by-token table of the simple-past forms, and the PDF's detailed examples were not independently extracted in this pass. Therefore this audit does **not** claim that a specific modern form such as **min kir** or **min got** has been verified in a particular verse/page of *Mem û Zîn*. The article is cited as a scholarly corpus study and a source for a future close reading, not as proof of any unquoted token.

## 5. Present, simple past, participle and perfect — do not conflate
- Present **dikim / dibêjim** are present forms, not simple past.
- Simple past stems **kir- / got-** participate in ergative agreement with the patient.
- Past participles **kirî** (“done”) and **gotî** (“said”) are not automatically finite simple-past forms.
- Present-perfect constructions such as **min kiriye** are distinct from the simple-past **min … kir**. The same distinction must be made for forms built from **got-**; check the specific agreement and complement construction rather than extrapolating from the infinitive.
- The present stem of **gotin** is **bêj-** (e.g. **dibêjim**); do not create a false regular present stem *got-* by analogy with the past.

## 6. Teaching and evidence recommendations
1. Teach the ergative pattern before isolated verb tables: agent oblique, patient nominative, verb agrees with patient.
2. Use kirin first with a clear singular patient, then a plural patient in an extended compound, explicitly marking agreement.
3. Teach gotin with a simple nominal/content patient, then a separate lesson box for reported-speech subordinate clauses (**min gotim ku…**) and ditransitive addressees.
4. Label the 2026 *Mem û Zîn* study as historical/classical evidence. Do not import its archaic or dialectal forms into the Standard Kurmancî core without token-level comparison.
5. No lesson content has been edited or approved by this research note.
