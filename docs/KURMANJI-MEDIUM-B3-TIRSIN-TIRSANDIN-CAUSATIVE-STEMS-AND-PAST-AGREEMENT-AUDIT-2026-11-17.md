# Tirsîn and tirsandin: causative stems, transitivity, and past agreement audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only lexical and grammatical audit for the Kurmanji-medium research track. This file does not authorize or implement lesson changes.

## Executive finding

The pair **tirsîn → tirsandin** is directly attested as a Standard Kurmanji intransitive/causative-transitive pair:

- **tirsîn**: “to fear, be afraid”; intransitive.
- **tirsandin**: “to scare, frighten someone”; transitive and causative.
- The cited verb list gives the present stem of *tirsîn* as **tirs-**, with **ez ditirsim**, imperative **bitirse**, and past form **tirsî**.
- For *tirsandin*, the same list gives present stem **tirsîn-**, **ez ditirsînim**, imperative **bitirsîne**, and past stem **tirsand**.
- A Kurmanji grammar exercise independently gives the pair and the attested past example **Wî ez tirsandim** (“He scared me”).

These forms support teaching the lexical contrast and the cited example. They do not justify inventing a full past-tense paradigm for every person/object combination.

## 1. Lexical contrast and transitivity

| Infinitive | Meaning | Valency |
|---|---|---|
| tirsîn | to fear; be afraid | intransitive |
| tirsandin | to scare; frighten someone | transitive, causative |

The derivation is transparent in the cited learner grammar: **tirs- + -andin → tirsandin**. This is a useful illustration of the productive causative pattern, but it should be taught as an attested lexical pair rather than as a rule that mechanically predicts every verb.

## 2. Stems and attested forms

| Verb | Present stem | Present example | Imperative | Past stem/form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tirsîn | tirs- | ez ditirsim | bitirse | tirsî |
| tirsandin | tirsîn- | ez ditirsînim | bitirsîne | tirsand |

The listed forms are consistent with the standard present-tense pattern: **di- + present stem + personal ending**. The *-andin* infinitive type commonly has a present stem in **-în-**, as illustrated by other transitive verbs in the same grammar materials.

## 3. Past tense and agreement

The example **Wî ez tirsandim** is especially useful:

- **wî** is the oblique agent (“he”).
- **ez** is the direct object (“me”).
- **tirsandim** carries first-person singular agreement with the object.

This is consistent with the ordinary Kurmanji ergative pattern for transitive simple past: the agent is oblique and the verb agrees with the patient/object. The example establishes this one combination only. Do not extrapolate a complete paradigm from it without additional direct evidence.

For *tirsîn*, the intransitive past forms follow the intransitive pattern; Thackston lists **ez tirsîm**, **tu tirsiyî**, **ew tirsî**, **em tirsîn**, and **ew tirsîn**. These are distinct from the transitive causative forms of *tirsandin*.

## 4. Dialect and orthographic caution

The audit concerns the Standard Kurmanji forms shown in the cited references. Haig and Öpengin discuss dialectal differences in the extension of **-and**: in some Southern Kurmanji varieties, infinitives ending in **-andin** can occur without causative semantics and may retain the stems of the corresponding Standard Kurmanji verb. Therefore, the suffix should not be interpreted mechanically without checking the dialect and lexical item.

No Sorani forms are mixed into this entry. The Latin spellings are retained as attested in the Kurmanji sources.

## 5. Teaching-safe summary

A safe concise explanation is:

> **Tirsîn** واتە ترسان/ترسێک هەبوون و کردارێکی ناتێپەڕە. **Tirsandin** واتە کەسێک ترساندن و کردارێکی تێپەڕە. نموونە: **Wî ez tirsandim** — ئەو منی ترساند.

Keep the contrast between the intransitive experiencer verb and the transitive causative verb explicit. Do not present a full object-agreement chart for *tirsandin* until each form is independently sourced.
