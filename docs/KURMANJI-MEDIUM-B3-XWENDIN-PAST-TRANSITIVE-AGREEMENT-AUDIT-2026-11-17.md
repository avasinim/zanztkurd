# Xwendin: past transitive agreement and oblique-agent audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only grammar audit for the Standard Northern Kurmanji pathway.

## Executive finding

**xwendin / xwandin** (“to read; to study”) is transitive in the relevant use. In canonical Northern Kurmanji past transitive clauses:
1. The agent/subject is marked oblique.
2. The patient/direct object is in the direct case.
3. The finite verb agrees with the direct object, not the oblique agent.

A directly attested textbook example is:

**Min helbestek xwend.** — “I read a poem.”

Here **min** is the oblique 1sg agent, **helbestek** is the direct singular object, and **xwend** is the past form with no overt personal ending because the object is third-person singular.

This is not the same pattern as an intransitive past clause such as **Ez hatim** (“I came”), where the subject is direct and the verb agrees with it.

## 1. Evidence specific to xwendin

The Kurmanji textbook's Lesson 7 gives **xwendin → xwend** as the past stem and uses **Min helbestek xwend** as its transitive past example. It explicitly states that the past-tense personal endings on transitive verbs refer to the object, not the subject, and that the subject takes oblique case.

The same lesson supplies further examples with oblique agents (**min, te, wî/wê, me**) and a direct object. It also gives **Min ew dît** (“I saw him/her”) as a transparent parallel showing the object-pronoun construction.

Kurdîzan's lesson on the ergative construction independently teaches **xwendin** as its example verb: **Min xwend**, **Te xwend**, **Wî xwend**, **Me xwend**. It explains that with a third-person singular object the verb has no person ending; the next lesson treats agreement with other objects.

## 2. General rule: past transitive vs. past intransitive

| Construction | Agent/subject | Object | Verb agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present transitive | Direct case | Usually oblique | With subject |
| Past intransitive | Direct case | None | With subject |
| Canonical past transitive | Oblique case | Direct case | With direct object |

This is the canonical split-ergative alignment described in Haig & Öpengin's overview of Northern Kurdish. It is not a universal rule for every dialect or every discourse environment: the same grammar documents variation, including plural-agent agreement in certain contexts and double-oblique object marking in some Central Anatolian varieties. Such variants must not be silently generalized into the Academy's default pathway.

## 3. Reading the example

**Min helbestek xwend.**

- **Min**: oblique form of first-person singular “I”; marks the agent in this past transitive clause.
- **helbestek**: “a poem”; direct-case singular patient/object.
- **xwend**: past form of **xwendin**; no overt agreement ending because the object is third-person singular.

Do not explain **xwend** as agreeing with “I”. The agent is oblique and is not the agreement controller in this canonical construction.

## 4. Object agreement: safe teaching boundary

The textbook states that past transitive personal endings agree with the object and gives examples with object pronouns. Kurdîzan separates the introductory rule (third-person singular object has no ending) from the next lesson on agreement with other objects.

Therefore:
- It is safe to teach the rule that agreement tracks the direct object.
- It is safe to teach **Min helbestek xwend** as the third-person singular object example.
- Do not invent a complete xwendin paradigm or derive every suffix form from this one example.
- For a full paradigm, collect source-attested examples for first-, second-, and third-person direct objects and plural objects, then check the Academy's chosen dialect/orthography policy.
- Mark plural-agent agreement and double-oblique object patterns as documented variation, not as the default beginner rule.

## 5. Distinguish present and past

| Tense/construction | Example | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Present | **Ez dixwînim.** | Direct subject **ez**; present agreement with agent |
| Past transitive | **Min helbestek xwend.** | Oblique agent **min**; direct object; verb agrees with object |
| Past intransitive | **Ez hatim.** | Direct subject **ez**; verb agrees with subject |

The present and past forms must not be taught as though the same pronoun case and agreement rule applies in both.

## 6. Teaching-safe decision

For the Standard Northern Kurmanji pathway:
1. Introduce **Min helbestek xwend** as a sourced model sentence.
2. Explicitly label **min** as oblique agent, **helbestek** as direct object, and **xwend** as past verb.
3. State that in canonical past transitive clauses the verb agrees with the direct object.
4. Contrast with **Ez hatim** to show past intransitive agreement.
5. Do not overstate the rule as exceptionless across all Kurmanji varieties; keep documented regional patterns separate.
6. Do not create or alter any learner-facing lesson until the user explicitly approves lesson-content work.
