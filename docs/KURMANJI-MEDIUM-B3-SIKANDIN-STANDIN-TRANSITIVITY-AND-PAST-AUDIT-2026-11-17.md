# Kurmancî B3 — şikandin û standin/stendin: stems, transitivity and past agreement (2026-11-17)

## Scope and safety
Documentation-only linguistic audit. No lesson content, course inventory, runtime, CI, migrations, production database, PR #3, or deployment was changed. Course inventory remains 26 + 12. The target remains Standard Kurmancî in Hawar-based Latin orthography; dialect forms are explicitly labelled.

## Executive findings
- **şikandin** (“to break something; cause something to break”) is transitive. Standard present stem **şikîn-**, past stem **şikand-**. The documented 1sg present is **ez dişikînim**. A peer-reviewed study records **Min derê wan şikand** (“I broke their door”) from Muş Kurmanji. A separate Kurmanji grammar study gives **Min pace şikand** (singular object) and **Min pace şikandin** (plural object), directly showing object-number agreement in the past.
- Do not confuse **şikandin** (transitive “break [something]”) with **şikestin** (intransitive “break/be broken”). A recent 2026 comparative paper explicitly contrasts **Hesenî pênûs şikand** (“Hesen broke the pen”) with **Pênûs şikest** (“The pen broke”). The forms belong to distinct valency patterns.
- **standin** (“take, receive, obtain; take by force; in some contexts buy”) is transitive. Standard present stem **stîn-**, past stem **stand-**. The 1sg present is **distînim**, and the published paradigm gives **distînî, distîne, distînin** for the other persons. The simple past with a 3sg direct patient is **min stand**; the negative is **min nestand**.
- **stendin** is a well-attested infinitive variant of **standin**, not a separate unrelated verb. Its past stem is **stend-**, while its present stem remains **stîn-** in the standard pattern. **min stend / min nestend** are the documented simple-past forms. **istandin** is also reported as a regional/orthographic variant; do not silently promote it to the core standard spelling.
- In standard transitive past clauses, the agent is oblique and the direct patient controls agreement. For a third-person singular patient, the verb is unmarked (**şikand**, **stand/stend**). For a plural patient, **şikandin** is explicitly attested. No complete six-person patient-agreement paradigm for **şikandin** was independently established in this audit, so do not invent one.

## 1. Şikandin — “to break something”
**Infinitive:** şikandin  
**Present stem:** şikîn-  
**Past stem:** şikand-  
**Transitivity:** transitive; compare intransitive **şikestin**.

### Present
The Harvard/Uppsala beginner grammar explicitly lists **şikandin → şikîn-** as the present-stem pattern for verbs in **-andin**. A separate conjugation list gives **ez dişikînim**. The regular present endings allow a teacher to build the remaining persons, but this audit retains the directly located 1sg example as the fully explicit attestation rather than presenting a separately unverified full table.

- **ez dişikînim** — I break something.

### Simple past and object agreement
Two independent sources provide useful evidence:

1. Songül Gündoğdu's peer-reviewed study of Muş Kurmanji gives:
   - **Min derê wan şikand.** — I broke their door.
   - The study glosses **min** as 1sg oblique agent and **şikand** as past 3sg. It also explains that **şikandin** selects an oblique-marked object in the studied dialect; the object must not be replaced with an adpositional phrase in that construction.

2. Yaşar Eroglu's Kurmancî plural-agreement study gives:
   - **Min pace şikand** — I broke the window (one).
   - **Min pace şikandin** — I broke the windows (more than one).

The second contrast demonstrates that the past verb changes from **şikand** to **şikandin** with a plural direct patient. It is a particularly useful teaching pair because the agent remains **min**, while the patient number changes.

### Keep the intransitive verb separate
A 2026 comparative study explicitly contrasts:
- **Hesenî pênûs şikand.** — Hesen broke the pen. (transitive **şikandin**)
- **Pênûs şikest.** — The pen broke. (intransitive **şikestin**)

Do not teach **şikest** as a past form of transitive **şikandin**. The infinitive and argument structure differ.

### Negative and aspect caution
The ordinary transitive simple-past negation follows **ne-** before the past stem, but this audit did not locate a verb-specific independently checked negative **şikandin** example. Treat **neşikand** as a regular morphological prediction pending a direct source, not as an independently attested corpus quotation. Likewise, do not conflate simple past **şikand** with progressive or perfect constructions.

## 2. Standin / stendin — “to take, receive, obtain”
**Core infinitive:** standin  
**Variant infinitive:** stendin  
**Present stem:** stîn-  
**Past stem:** stand- (standin); stend- (stendin)  
**Transitivity:** transitive.

### Lexical forms and variation
Husein Muhammed's 2026 comparative, historical and dialectal study identifies three infinitive forms: **standin, stendin, istandin**. It describes **standin** and **stendin** as the two common variants, with **istandin** also found regionally. The study explains that the past stems are formed regularly:
- **standin → stand-**
- **stendin → stend-**

The present stem of both common variants is generally **stîn-** in most Kurmanji dialects and in the written standard. Some regional forms have **-stên-**; label these as regional rather than mixing them into the Standard Kurmancî core.

### Present paradigm
The Kurmancî Wiktionary entry for **standin** supplies the following standard forms:
| Person | Present |
|---|---|
| ez | distînim |
| tu | distînî |
| ew (sg.) | distîne |
| em | distînin |
| hûn | distînin |
| ew (pl.) | distînin |

A contemporary lexical source also lists **standin → -stîn- → distîne**. The Harvard/Uppsala beginner grammar lists **standin** as “receive” and notes **stendin** as a dialect variant.

### Simple past
The 2026 Zimannas study explicitly gives:
- **min stand** — I took / received.
- **min nestand** — I did not take / receive.
- **min stend** — variant spelling/infinitive stendin.
- **min nestend** — negative variant.

The source also separates simple past from past progressive:
- simple past: **min stand / min stend**
- past progressive: **min di-stand / min di-stend**
- perfect: **min standiye / min stendiye**

Do not use the progressive **di-stand** or perfect **standiye** as simple-past examples.

### Classical literary occurrence
Husein Muhammed quotes a couplet from Ehmedê Xanî's *Mem û Zîn*, section 41:
- **Pê nav-i ebed ji bo xwe bistîn.**

The form **bistîn** is an imperative (“take/acquire for yourself”), not a simple-past finite form. It is useful historical literary evidence for the present stem **stîn-**, but it must not be presented as a past-tense citation.

### Semantic caution
The modern lexical sources record meanings including taking by force, receiving/obtaining, and buying. The exact interpretation depends on context. The 2026 linguistic study argues against reducing the verb to “seize by force” only and documents a broader semantic range. Keep idiomatic constructions and regional usages separate from the basic classroom gloss “take/receive”.

| Past stem | şikand- | stand- / stend- |
| 1sg present | ez dişikînim | ez distînim |
| 3sg present | derived from regular endings; not separately quoted here | ew distîne |
| Simple past with 3sg patient | min şikand | min stand / min stend |

## 4. Teaching recommendations
1. Teach **şikandin** and **şikestin** as a transitive/intransitive lexical pair, not as two conjugations of one verb.
2. Use **Min pace şikand / Min pace şikandin** to demonstrate singular vs plural patient agreement.
3. Teach **standin** as the core infinitive and mention **stendin** as a common variant; retain each one's corresponding past stem.
4. Teach **distînim** and **min stand** as the basic present/simple-past contrast.
5. Keep **min di-stand** (past progressive) and **min standiye** (perfect) in their own aspect lessons.
6. Use Xanî's **bistîn** only as an imperative and historical stem attestation, not as a past-tense model.
7. Avoid asserting a complete object-agreement paradigm for either verb unless a source supplies the actual forms.
