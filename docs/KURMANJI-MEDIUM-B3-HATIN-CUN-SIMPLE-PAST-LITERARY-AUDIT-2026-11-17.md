# B3 Kurmanji: Hatin and Çûn Simple-Past Forms — Literary and Grammar Audit
Date: 2026-11-17
Branch: fix/kurmanji-ci-current-migration
Scope: source-led research documentation only; no lesson or application changes.

## Executive finding
This audit treats `hatin` (come) and `çûn` (go) as two intransitive verbs with different past-stem shapes: consonant-final `hat-` and vowel-final `çû-`. The core Standard Kurmanji simple-past paradigms are `ez hatim / tu hatî / ew hat / em-hûn-ew hatin` and `ez çûm / tu çûyî / ew çû / em-hûn-ew çûn`. A published Kurmanji play provides a compact literary dialogue with both `hatim` and `çûm`; an academic transcription study of an 1860 language-learning manuscript independently records both forms in its lesson exercises. Grammar references agree on the paradigms and on the direct-subject pattern for intransitives.

## 1. Direct literary evidence: both verbs in one dramatic passage
In the play *Memê Bê Zîn*, the dialogue includes the imagined future line spoken about the children: `Bavo va ez hatim. Dayê va ez çûm.` The two finite verbs are first-person singular simple-past forms. In the dramatic context, the lines are part of a projected scene in which the child announces arrival and departure; they are not a report of an event that has already occurred in the play's present timeline. They are nevertheless direct literary tokens of the forms and their person marking.
Source: *Memê Bê Zîn*, PDF p.126: https://www.kurdipedia.org/files/books/2024/576718.PDF

## 2. Historical documentary evidence: 1860 teaching manuscript
A 2021 peer-reviewed study transcribes Mela Mistefayî's *Rahênanên Zimanê Kurdî*, originally translated by Auguste Jaba and dated Erzurum 1860. The Latinized manuscript exercise list contains the standalone sentences `Ez çûm.` and `Ez hatim.` among elementary verb examples. This is unusually valuable historical evidence that both forms were taught/used in written Kurmanji materials by 1860. Because these are decontextualized exercise sentences, they attest the form but do not provide rich narrative semantics.
Source: Mikail Bülbül & Mustafa Öztürk, “Destnivîska Mela Mistefayî ya ‘Rahênanên Zimanê Kurdî’ (Nirxandin, Metin),” *Folklor û Ziman*, vol.2, no.2(1), April 2021, pp.124ff: https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1707295

## 3. Standard simple-past paradigms
### Hatin — past stem `hat-`
| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| Ez | `hatim` |
| Tu | `hatî` |
| Ew (singular) | `hat` |
| Em | `hatin` |
| Hûn | `hatin` |
| Ew (plural) | `hatin` |
### Çûn — past stem `çû-`
| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| Ez | `çûm` |
| Tu | `çûyî` |
| Ew (singular) | `çû` |
| Em | `çûn` |
| Hûn | `çûn` |
| Ew (plural) | `çûn` |
Thackston's *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, §18, prints the full six-person paradigms for `hatin` and the vowel-stem verb `bûn`, and gives `Tu bi pelikan çûyî jor` as a simple-past example for `çûn`. Kurdish Complete reproduces the same paradigm. Kurdîzan's lesson “Things that happened” explicitly contrasts `hat-` and `çû-`, and provides `Ez çûm`, `Tu hatî`, `Ew çû`, `Duh em hatin`.
Sources: Thackston excerpt https://www.academia.edu/5485402/Kurmanji_Kurdish_A_Reference_Grammar_with_Selected_Readings_by_W_M_Thackston ; Kurdish Complete https://kurdshop.net/content/upload/1/root/kurmanji-complete.pdf ; Kurdîzan https://kurdizan.net/ders/dema-bori

## 4. Intransitive agreement: keep direct subjects
Both verbs are intransitive in the cited examples. Their subjects remain direct-form pronouns: `ez`, `tu`, `ew`, `em`, `hûn`, `ew`. Do not write `min hatim` or `min çûm` for the intended simple-past meaning “I came / I went”. The oblique-agent construction belongs to transitive past clauses, where the verb agrees with the direct object. Zimanzan gives the contrast `Ez hatim` and `Ez çûm` versus `Min nan xwar` and `Wê ez dîtim`.
Source: https://zimanzan.com/kurmanji/grammar/past-tense

## 5. Negation
The ordinary negative simple past prefixes `ne-` to the past form: `ez nehatim`, `tu nehatî`, `ew nehat`, `em/hûn/ew nehatin`; and `ez neçûm`, `tu neçûyî`, `ew neçû`, `em/hûn/ew neçûn`. Thackston's paradigms give `néhatim`, `néhatî`, `néhat`, `néhatin`; Haig's overview explicitly identifies `ne-hatin` as past negation. Zimanzan gives `Ez neçûm`.
Sources: Thackston §18 https://www.academia.edu/5485402/Kurmanji_Kurdish_A_Reference_Grammar_with_Selected_Readings_by_W_M_Thackston ; Haig 2018 https://multicast.aspra.uni-bamberg.de/data/pubs/Haig2018_Northern-Kurdish_Kurmanji.pdf ; Zimanzan https://zimanzan.com/kurmanji/grammar/past-tense
Orthographic note: sources vary in whether the negative prefix is displayed with a hyphen or a spacing convention. For B3, follow the project's established Kurmanji orthography consistently; do not mix `ne-hatim`, `ne hatim`, and `nehatim` within one learning sequence without first documenting the chosen convention.

## 6. Tense/aspect contrasts that must not be collapsed
| Meaning | Hatin | Çûn |
|---|---|---|
| Simple past | `Ez hatim` | `Ez çûm` |
| Present perfect / past with current relevance | `Ez hatime` | `Ez çûme` |
| Past progressive / habitual | `Ez dihatim` | `Ez diçûm` |
| Earlier past / pluperfect | `Ez hatibûm` | `Ez çûbûm` |
Kurdîzan's completed-past lesson contrasts `Ez hatim / Ez çûm` with `Ez hatime / Ez çûme`, progressive `Ez dihatim / Ez diçûm`, and earlier past `Ez hatibûm / Ez çûbûm`. Haig notes the `di-` progressive contrast in past tenses. Therefore `hatime` and `çûme` must not be substituted for the simple-past forms.
Sources: https://kurdizan.net/ders/boriya-tedeyi ; https://kurdizan.net/ders/ciroka-boriya-tedeyi ; Haig 2018 above.

## 7. Dialect and historical caution for `çûn`
Haig's overview notes that `çûn` and `hatin` have suppletive stems and that present/imperative stems vary regionally. A collected folk-song source records variants such as `çom` and `çome` in a regional performance text. Those tokens should be preserved as source spellings and not silently normalized to Standard Kurmanji `çûm` / `çûme`. The B3 core should teach the agreed Standard Kurmanji forms while recording dialectal variants separately.
Sources: Haig 2018, https://www.academia.edu/17650650/Kurmanji_in_Turkey_structure_varieties_and_status ; Heciye Cindî, *Klamên Cimeta Kurdî*, p.74: https://www.ensiklopediyakurdi.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Heciye-Cindi-Klamen-cimeta-Kurdi.pdf

## 9. B3 editorial recommendations
1. Teach `hatin` with past stem `hat-` and `çûn` with past stem `çû-`; do not apply a single mechanical suffix rule without noting vowel-final stem behavior.
2. Use the *Memê Bê Zîn* dialogue as a paired literary example, clearly explaining its imagined dramatic setting.
3. Use the 1860 manuscript sentences as historical attestations of both first-person forms, not as evidence for all six persons.
4. Teach the full six-person paradigms from Thackston and Kurdîzan, while marking which persons have direct text attestations in this audit.
5. Keep the direct-subject intransitive pattern distinct from transitive ergative past clauses.
6. Keep simple past, present perfect, progressive past, and pluperfect in separate labeled sections.
7. Preserve dialectal `çom/çome` as quoted source variants only; do not mix them into the Standard Kurmanji core.
8. Existing lessons remain unchanged pending explicit approval.
