# K-078 Future Marker Concordance and Discourse-Time Audit

Date: 2026-11-17  
Type: source-based descriptive audit; documentation only.

## Scope

This note inventories clearly visible future-marker sequences in the published free-speech transcript for K-078, Şemdinli. It distinguishes the time of the narrated event from the speaker's viewpoint at the time of speaking. It does not claim to recover the separate questionnaire answers for maps 3.11.1 or 3.11.2.

## Concordance

The forms below are copied from the published transcript. English meanings are the project's own published translations or conservative context summaries.

| Turn | Transcript excerpt | Discourse function / time relation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `ez dê ... kem` | Speaker announces what he is going to tell now; prospective intention relative to speech time |
| 2 | `ez dê beḥsî wê kem` | Repeats the intention to tell about the village; prospective relative to speech time |
| 7 | `ridîna me dê derkewîtin` | Childhood memory: when they grew up, their beards would grow; future relative to a past viewpoint (future-in-the-past / prospective viewpoint) |
| 11 | `ʕesker dê hêne têre da`; `ʕesker dê hête têre da` | Reported expectation that soldiers would come; future relative to the reported past announcement |
| 11 | `em dê çi keyn`; `em dê kê ve çîn`; `Dê şito gundê xû hêlîn` | Questions and concern about what they would do, where they would go, and how they would leave the village; future/prospective viewpoint inside past narration |
| 15 | `em dê çîn sibehê hêyn` | Reported plan: they would leave and return tomorrow; future relative to the evening scene being narrated |
| 16 | `em dê sibe hêyn ceyrana hingo çêkeyn` | Reported promise/plan to come the next day and connect the electricity; future relative to the narrated scene |

Ellipses in turns 1 are editorial omissions here, not part of the transcript. The full original turn should be consulted for exact syntax.

## Interpretation

1. The transcript contains several distinct discourse settings for future reference:
   - speaker-time intention (turns 1–2);
   - a prospective event viewed from a remembered childhood/past situation (turn 7);
   - reported expectations and deliberation during the village-evacuation narrative (turn 11);
   - reported plans and promises in the electricity episode (turns 15–16).
2. Thus a raw count of `dê` tokens would combine different temporal anchors and discourse functions. They should not all be taught as one undifferentiated “future tense” use.
3. The negative clause `em ceyranê girê nadeyn` in turn 15 has a future-oriented interpretation in the published English translation (“we will not connect the electricity”), while the following coordinated clause `em dê çîn sibehê hêyn` overtly contains `dê`. This is a useful within-speaker contrast, but does not prove a general rule that `dê` is absent from all negative futures.
4. The transcript does not supply morpheme-by-morpheme glosses for these clauses. This note therefore avoids assigning a precise morphological segmentation to `dê`, `na-`, or the verb forms beyond the visible transcription.
5. The narrative's past setting is crucial: turns 7 and 11 are not simple predictions made at the interview's present time. They are prospective from a past reference point.

## Evidence boundary: free speech versus elicitation

The Manchester project describes its database as transcribed interview/questionnaire data searchable by sample, location, structural tag, English phrase, or Kurdish text. It separately publishes free-speech extracts with audio, transcription, and translation. The sample index identifies K-078 as Şemdinli in the Turkey (Kurmanji) group.

The prompts for maps 3.11.1 (“Tomorrow it will rain”) and 3.11.2 (“Tomorrow I will go to town”) are separately listed on the project's survey/maps pages. The exact Kurdish response for K-078 to either prompt remains unverified in this audit. The ArcGIS item identifiers recorded in the preceding audit identify the map items, not the K-078 response attributes themselves.

## Safe conclusion

K-078 provides direct natural-speech evidence for multiple `dê`-marked prospective clauses across different discourse-time settings, alongside the contextually future-oriented negative clause in turn 15. This strengthens the descriptive record for this one speaker/sample. It does not establish a complete paradigm, a categorical negative-future rule, or a uniform rule for all Şemzînan speakers.
