# B3 Amadiya and Sinjar Locality Evidence: Blau (1975) Source Audit — 2026-11-17

## Scope and decision

This note evaluates whether Joyce Blau's *Le kurde de ʿAmadiya et de Djabal Sindjar* (1975) can support locality-specific claims in the fully Kurmanji-medium pathway. It is a source-provenance and evidence-boundary audit, not a lesson draft and not a grammatical analysis of Blau's examples.

**Decision:** retain Blau (1975) as a high-priority primary fieldwork source for Amadiya (Amêdî) and Sinjar (Şingal), but do not yet cite it for any particular verb form, particle, case pattern, phonological rule, or sentence. The full book's relevant pages and examples have not been directly inspected in this audit.

## What is established

1. **Bibliographic identity.** Joyce Blau, *Le kurde de ʿAmadiya et de Djabal Sindjar: analyse linguistique, textes folkloriques, glossaires*. Paris: C. Klincksieck, 1975; 252 pages; series volume 8 of *Travaux de l'Institut d'études iraniennes de l'Université de la Sorbonne Nouvelle*. The 2025 bibliography by Martin van Bruinessen confirms the title, author, publisher and year (pp. 123–124). Google Books independently confirms the 252-page book and 1975 publication metadata.
2. **Fieldwork locations and dates.** Van Bruinessen's 2025 scholarly account states that Blau travelled to Amadiya in 1967 and made a second research trip to Sinjar in 1968; her field notes from both localities became the basis of the 1975 dissertation (pp. 117–118). This supports two distinct locality anchors, not one undifferentiated regional sample.
3. **Nature of the material.** The same account characterizes the dissertation as field linguistics: Blau recorded oral narratives, transcribed them, analysed features of each dialect and supplied translations. It explicitly says the two dialects differ considerably from standard Kurmanji (p. 118).
4. **Lexical evidence description.** A specialist review at Kurdish Academy of Languages describes the book as linguistic analysis plus folkloric texts with French translations and accompanying glossaries; it reports separate glossaries for Amadiya and Jabal Sinjar and says these include noun gender and present verb stems. The review characterizes the material as Southern Kurmanji collected in those particular localities. This is a secondary description, not a substitute for checking Blau's actual entries.
5. **Why this matters.** The source is potentially valuable precisely because it is locality-based and oral-text-based. Its Amadiya and Sinjar data should be kept separate from each other, from Zakho-specific evidence, and from Standard Kurmanji paradigms unless a comparison is explicitly demonstrated.

## Limits and cautions

- The available direct PDF result encountered during this search resolved to a different French-language work, not Blau's 1975 book. It was rejected and is not used as evidence for Blau's grammar.
- Google Books and catalogue records verify bibliographic metadata, not the content of the linguistic analysis.
- The 2025 obituary is a scholarly secondary source and is useful for fieldwork provenance and method; it does not provide the paradigms or primary examples needed for a lesson.
- The Kurdish Academy review is useful specialist commentary, but its summary cannot replace the original text, page references, transcription conventions or context.
- The label “Southern Kurmanji” in the review should not be silently converted into “all Badini” or “all Duhok speech.” Amadiya and Sinjar are separate sampling sites; a feature recorded at one site cannot automatically be generalized to the other or to Zakho, Barwari, Duhok city, Hakkari or the whole Badinan region.
- Do not infer that the 1967–1968 speech sample represents present-day usage unchanged. Date, speaker profile, genre, elicitation/recording circumstances and later language change need to be considered.
- Do not use Blau's local forms to overwrite the proposed Standard Kurmanji/Hawar pathway. If a local contrast is eventually included, mark the locality, source page, speaker/text context and its relationship to the standard.

## Recommended source-verification workflow

1. Obtain a complete, legible copy of the 1975 book from a library or authorized collection; confirm title page, contents, bibliography and pagination.
2. Inspect the phonology/linguistic-analysis sections and record the book's own transcription conventions before normalizing any forms.
3. Index every oral text by locality (Amadiya or Sinjar), text number/title, page range and genre. Keep the two corpora distinct.
4. For each candidate grammatical claim, capture the exact original sentence, page, morpheme segmentation, translation and the author's explanation. Do not reconstruct missing paradigms from isolated forms.
5. Compare only like-for-like structures across Blau's two localities and other sources. Label direct attestation separately from analysis and cross-dialect inference.
6. Have a qualified native Kurmanji editor familiar with the relevant locality review the transcription and interpretation before any learner-facing use.

## Project impact

- No existing lesson, course inventory, seed data, runtime, CI, migration, database or deployment was changed.
- No learner-facing example or paradigm was added.
- No grammatical claim about Amadiya or Sinjar has been promoted to “verified” on the basis of metadata or secondary summaries.
