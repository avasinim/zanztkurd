# Badini Locality Sampling and Evidence-Boundary Addendum
Date: 2026-11-17
Repository branch: `fix/kurmanji-ci-current-migration`
Scope: academic documentation only.

## Executive finding

The available sources do not justify treating Duhok, Zakho, Amadiya/Amêdî, Barwari, and Hakkari as one internally uniform “Badini” speech sample. They support a broad Southeastern Kurmanji (SEK) grouping that includes Duhok Province and Hakkari, while also warning that the empirical map is provisional and the sampling is too coarse for precise locality-level claims.

Accordingly, this addendum does **not** assign a single dê/da paradigm to every locality in the region. It establishes an evidence protocol for future locality-specific review.

## 1. What Öpengin & Haig (2014) actually sampled

The study explicitly states that an empirically grounded classification of Kurmanji dialects did not yet exist and that its own regional classification was provisional. Its five-region model is based on lexical, phonological and morphosyntactic data from five localities in southeastern Turkey.

Its Southeastern Kurmanji (SEK) region includes Hakkâri Province and Duhok Province, and includes what is traditionally called Badini. The representative SEK speaker in that study was from Şemdinli (Şemzînan), Hakkâri—not a multi-locality sample of Duhok or Amadiya.

The authors state that the regional boundaries are approximate; some areas were left unassigned because data were insufficient, and finer sampling would be required to resolve intersecting features. They also caution that speakers' speech can vary internally under mobility and contact with other varieties.

**Implication:** the SEK label is a useful comparative grouping, not proof that every town and village inside it shares every grammatical feature.

## 2. What Unger (2014) actually documents

Unger explicitly identifies the Badini variety under discussion as predominantly spoken in the Dohuk Governorate of Iraq, contrasting it with the Standard Kurmanji norm represented in Latin-script literature.

For the Badini examples, Unger reports that the examples are drawn from Unger (2012), and specifically identifies several recorded stories:
- Dostînî
- Sînem
- Xec
- Pira

The footnote identifies the recordings as made in Zakho, Iraq, in 1992, by Mela Nasir Zaxoyî and Loqman Nûredîn Hassan. This provides a clear locality anchor for those story examples: Zakho. It does not establish that the same distribution is identical in Amadiya, Barwari, all of Duhok, or Hakkari.

Unger describes:
- Badini future dê + person-inflected present stem without subjunctive bi-;
- Badini da + person-inflected present stem without modal/aspectual prefix, with counterfactual and distant-past habitual uses;
- the distinction between Badini dê and Standard Kurmanji wê/dê/ê as systematic, while presenting the semantic account as an analysis rather than a complete dialect atlas.

## 3. Amadiya / Amêdî: what can and cannot be claimed

Öpengin & Haig (2014) mention Blau (1975) as an earlier isolated study of Amadiya and Djabal Sinjar. This confirms that Amadiya has been the subject of separate dialect description, but the 2014 regional classification does not provide a sufficiently dense Amadiya sample to map every morphosyntactic feature there.

Therefore:
- It is legitimate to list Amadiya as a priority locality for a separate evidence search.
- It is not legitimate to transfer the Zakho dê/da examples to Amadiya without a locality-specific source.
- It is not legitimate to infer that Amadiya uses or rejects a particular particle solely because it is geographically grouped with Duhok/SEK.
- A separate Amadiya claim requires a traceable local source, speaker metadata, or reviewed elicitation.

## 4. Recommended locality-level evidence ledger

For any future Behdinî/Badini grammatical form, record the following before treating it as a learner-facing alternative:

| Field | Required record |
|---|---|
| Locality | Town/village and wider district/governorate |
| Variety label | Speaker's own label plus analytical label (e.g. SEK/Badini) |
| Source type | Published grammar, corpus, recorded narrative, elicitation |
| Speaker metadata | Number of speakers, locality, age/generation if supplied, mobility/contact notes if supplied |
| Exact form | Preserve source transcription; do not silently convert scripts |
| Morphological segmentation | Only what the source explicitly supports |
| Function/context | Tense, aspect, modality, clause type and discourse context |
| Evidence strength | Direct locality-attested / regional generalisation / hypothesis |
| Review | Native-speaker/editor confirmation and date |
| Teaching status | Core norm / labelled dialect note / research-only / unresolved |

## 5. Priority sampling plan (proposal only)

1. **Zakho:** retain Unger's recorded examples as directly locality-anchored evidence, while checking the original story/transcription where possible.
2. **Duhok city and surrounding localities:** seek independent sources or speaker-reviewed data rather than assuming Zakho forms generalise.
3. **Amadiya/Amêdî:** retrieve and inspect Blau (1975) and any newer local descriptions; record exact locality and grammatical coverage before drawing conclusions.
4. **Barwari / Barwar:** treat as its own locality cluster for evidence collection; do not collapse it into Zakho or Amadiya without comparative data.
5. **Hakkari / Şemzînan:** distinguish the Öpengin & Haig Şemdinli sample from Duhok-based Badini examples; compare only features directly represented in both datasets.

This is a research sampling plan, not a claim that these are internally uniform dialect blocks.

## 6. Editorial implications for Avasin Academy Pathway B

- Keep the provisional core norm as Standard Kurmanjî in Hawar-based Latin orthography, with a Cizîr/Botan orientation, pending owner approval.
- Label any Behdinî/Badini form with its actual locality and source where known.
- Do not label an example simply “Badini” if the source only attests Zakho or one named speaker.
- Do not add dê/da to the core Standard Kurmanjî paradigm as interchangeable forms.
- Do not invent locality-specific paradigms from the regional label SEK.
- Keep all current course lessons and seed payloads unchanged pending explicit approval.

## 7. Source limitations and review status

- Öpengin & Haig (2014) is an explicitly preliminary, coarse-grained classification based on five localities in southeastern Turkey; its own discussion warns that fine-grained sampling is needed.
- Unger (2014) is a focused semantic/morphosyntactic study of dê and da, not a comprehensive grammar of all Badini localities.
- Unger's cited story examples have a Zakho recording provenance; that provenance should not be broadened to all Duhok or Hakkari.
- Amadiya is mentioned as the subject of earlier separate work (Blau 1975), but that citation alone does not attest a particular dê/da form there.
- No native-speaker review for Avasin Academy is recorded in this document. All proposed teaching use remains pending qualified locality-aware review.
