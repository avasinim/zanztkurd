# Dengnasî source audit — Lesson 7 (2026-11-26)

## Scope and source evidence

Source: Husein Muhammed, *Dengnasî di kurdî de*, printed pages 56–67, corresponding to physical PDF pages 66–77. The source pages were reviewed against Lesson 7 authoring and its generated JSON/HTML previews.

## Findings and restored material

The lesson already explained the central orthographic principle, voicing assimilation, place/manner effects, and analogy. The comparison found a number of omitted footnotes, examples and cross-language tables. These have been restored in a supplementary source-notes section:

1. Footnotes 43–44: Turkish final-voicing exceptions (*ad/at*) and Kurmanji spoken variants (*leyîstin, lehîstin, axaftin*) contrasted with rejected spellings. Added *futbol/fudbol* and the compound *jinbab/jinbav → jimbab/jimbav* to clarify fast-speech effects versus written forms.
2. Suffix/compound detail: *dezgir, dezgeh, desmal, destpêk* and footnote 45's dialectal metathesis *dergistî*, preserving the distinction between *dezgir/dezgirtî* and *destgir/destgirtî*.
3. Nasal assimilation additions: *himban/hinban* and *duşem/duşembî* variants alongside the already-present examples.
4. Number analogy: the full cross-language comparison for “eight”, footnotes 46–47 on *heyşt* and dialectal H distinctions, and the “nine” comparison (*new, no, noh, neve-, neven, novem, neun, nine, nio, ennea, inn*). The author's proposed historical forms and the progressive/regressive distinction are retained as proposals, not universal facts.
5. Preposition comparison table across Kurmanji, Sorani, Hewrami, Persian, Pahlavi, Achaemenid and Avestan, with footnotes 48–55 on Sorani dialect distribution, blank cells, *bi/by*, *ender/hindir*, Avestan *heç*, and the author's explicitly tentative Zazaki/Armenian hypothesis.
6. The source's discussion of *bo*, Sorani Hewlêrî *lo*, dialectal *jo*, and footnote 56.
7. Postposition comparison table and footnotes 57–64, including dialectal *da/ra/ve/va*, Eastern Kurmanji distribution, Sorani Mukriyanî/Silemanî differences, and the source's glosses for Persian/Pahlavi/Achaemenid/Avestan forms.
8. Pronoun/adverb analogy details and the status of *bin/ban* as a tentative thought experiment rather than a demonstrated example.

The supplementary notes are synchronized in the authoring source, JSON payload and standalone HTML preview. The course validator now checks selected source terms and both comparison tables.

## Limits and caution

This is a source-alignment audit, not independent peer review of the book's historical or etymological claims. Dialectal forms and hypotheses remain attributed to the author. The validator was updated but has not been executed locally or by a newly observed GitHub Actions run. No merge to main, deployment, production database change, migration or course activation was performed.
