# Şemzînan Conditional Morphology: Direct Attestation and Dialect Scope

Date: 2026-11-17  
Scope: documentation-only grammatical evidence audit. No lessons, curriculum, runtime, CI, or production data changed.

## Source and dialect identification

Haig and Öpengin, “Kurmanji Kurdish in Turkey: Structure, varieties and status” (pre-publication draft 2015; later published chapter), §4.1, describe the Şemzînan variety of Southeastern Kurmanji (SEK). They identify Şemdinli (Kurdish: Şemzînan) as a district in Hakkari province, Turkey, and describe their Şemzînan data as representative of the eastern half of the dialect zone. They explicitly warn that the wider Badini/Hakkari region shows subdialectal variation.

## 1. Past conditional: a distinct suffixal construction

The authors contrast the Standard Kurmanji past conditional:

[bi- + past stem + (a) + person marker + a]

with the Şemzînan SEK construction:

[past stem + (i)ba + person marker]

Direct examples:

- *hat-i-ba* — come.PST-PTCP-SUBJ.PST.3SG, in “If the news of a killing would come from Iraq,”
- *got-i-ba* — say.PST-PTCP-SUBJ.PST, in the counterfactual antecedent “If you had said earlier,”
- *hat-i-ba-m* — come.PST-PTCP-SUBJ.PST-1SG, in “I too would have come / would come.”

The authors note that the *-i* preceding *-ba* is probably originally a past participle suffix. Their footnote proposes *-ba* may derive from contraction of past *bū-* ‘be’ plus conditional *-(y)a* (> *bū-ya* > *ba*). This etymology is explicitly presented as probable, not certain.

## 2. The Şemzînan particle *da* is multifunctional

The source identifies *da* as a modal particle absent from Standard Kurmanji and gives at least three functions:

1. Auxiliary in the apodosis of an imperfect counterfactual.
2. Repeated or customary action in the past, roughly “used to / would”; in this function the source says it can alternate with future auxiliary *dē*.
3. Past intentionality, with a bare present stem in the described construction.

Direct counterfactual example:

*te zū got-i-ba, ez ži da hat-i-ba-m*  
“If you had said earlier, I too would have come / would come.”

Therefore *da* must not be treated as a spelling variant of future *dê*, nor reduced to one single meaning. Its function must be read from construction and context.

## 3. Relation to negative future

Haig's areal chapter separately states that Standard Kurmanji can retain the future particle with a negated subjunctive, whereas the Kurmanji varieties spoken in Şemzînan and northern Iraq do not form negative future in that way; they use a negated indicative form.

The Behdinî example previously recorded in this repository is *Ez sibe digel wan na-çi-m* (“I won't go with them tomorrow”). This is evidence for Behdinî, not a directly transcribed Şemzînan sentence. The source's regional generalization includes Şemzînan, but the present audit does not relabel the Behdinî example as Şemzînan speech.

## 4. Additional Şemzînan evidence

The same §4.1 describes:
- present progressive negation: *axir tu yē na-xo-y* — “But you are not eating!”
- directional and aspectual constructions, and a distinct set of regional phonological and nominal features.

These details reinforce that Şemzînan is a specifically described variety, not simply a synonym for all Badini speech.

## Safe conclusions

1. The suffixal past conditional *-ba* is directly attested in the Şemzînan data and differs from the Standard Kurmanji *bi-* + past-stem pattern.
2. The examples *hat-i-ba*, *got-i-ba*, and *hat-i-ba-m* are source-attested; do not infer a complete verb paradigm from these few examples.
3. *da* has multiple documented functions. Do not merge it with future *dê*.
4. The source's negative-future generalization includes Şemzînan, but the cited direct negative-future clause is labelled Behdinî. No direct Şemzînan negative-future clause is asserted here.
5. The Şemzînan sample is described as representative of the eastern half of SEK; the authors note wider subdialectal variation. Do not generalize every detail to all Badini/Hakkari communities.
6. This is a research note, not an Academy standard decision. No lesson or pathway is changed.

## Evidence limitations

The article itself describes the Şemzînan variety and provides direct examples for past conditional and *da*. It gives a broader regional generalization for negative future, but not a directly labelled Şemzînan negative-future clause in the cited passage. Additional locality-specific corpus or elicitation data are needed before producing a complete Şemzînan paradigm or prescribing it as an Academy standard.
