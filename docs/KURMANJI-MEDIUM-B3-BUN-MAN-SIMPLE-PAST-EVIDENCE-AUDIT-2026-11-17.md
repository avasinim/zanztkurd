# Kurmancî B3 — bûn û man: simple-past forms and evidence audit (2026-11-17)

## Executive findings
- Both **bûn** (“to be; become”) and **man** (“to remain, stay”) are intransitive in the simple-past paradigms examined here. Their subject is direct, and the verb agrees with that subject.
- The reference paradigm in W. M. Thackston, *Kurmanji Kurdish: A Reference Grammar with Selected Readings*, §18, p.45, gives:
  - bûn: ez **bûm**, tu **bûyî**, ew **bû**, em/hûn/ew **bûn**.
  - man: ez **mam**, tu **mayî**, ew **ma**, em/hûn/ew **man**.
- The same paradigms and negatives are reproduced in the accessible Kurdipedia-hosted grammar scan and the Kurdshop-hosted complete grammar PDF. These are corroborating copies of the same grammatical presentation, not independent literary attestations.
- Literary evidence located for **bûn** includes the poetic text *Kurmancîkirina helbestên Ebdulla Peşêw*, p.12: “Dema zarok bûm” and repeated “Tu bûyî…”. This confirms forms in literary discourse, but some occurrences may be copular/identity or existential predicates; their exact semantics must be read in full context.
- For **man**, the grammar example “Hemû man bêdeng” (“They all remained silent”) is a pedagogical example, not a verified literary quotation. No independent literary occurrence of the target simple-past forms **mam / mayî / ma / man** was verified in this pass. Do not present the grammar example as corpus evidence.

## 1. Paradigms

| Person | bûn — simple past | man — simple past |
|---|---|---|
| ez | bûm | mam |
| tu | bûyî | mayî |
| ew (sg.) | bû | ma |
| em | bûn | man |
| hûn | bûn | man |
| ew (pl.) | bûn | man |

The repeated plural forms are expected in the cited paradigm. They are not evidence that the verbs have identical stems: **bû-** and **ma-** are distinct past stems.

## 2. Negation
The cited Thackston paradigm forms the negative with **ne-**:
- bûn: ez nebûm; tu nebûyî; ew nebû; em/hûn/ew nebûn.
- man: ez nemam; tu nemayî; ew nema; em/hûn/ew neman.

Orthographic rendering of the negative prefix varies across source conventions (for example, Thackston's marked **né-** versus unmarked **ne-** in other Kurmanji materials). Preserve the repository's chosen orthographic convention consistently; do not treat the accent mark as a separate tense marker.

## 3. Literary and documentary evidence

### bûn
*Kurmancîkirina helbestên Ebdulla Peşêw*, p.12, contains:
- “Dema zarok bûm” — first-person singular **bûm** in a temporal clause (“when I was a child”).
- “Tu bûyî…” — second-person singular **bûyî** repeated in a poetic address.

This is direct literary text evidence for the forms. It does not by itself settle every semantic use of **bûn**: the verb can express being, becoming, or serve as a copula depending on its complement and context.

A separate Kurmanji reference/teaching text gives “Ez bûm mamoste” and “Tu bûyî textor” as simple-past examples. These are grammatical examples, not literary corpus attestations.

### man
Thackston §18 gives “Hemû man bêdeng” as an illustrative sentence (“They all remained silent”). This demonstrates a plural form **man** with an adverbial/predicative complement, but it is explicitly a grammar example. It must not be counted as an independently sourced literary token.
No direct literary **mam**, **mayî**, **ma**, or **man** token with independently checked author/work/page was established in this audit. This is a bounded search result, not proof that such examples do not exist.

## 4. Simple past versus perfect and pluperfect
Keep the following contrasts clear:
- Simple past: **Ez bûm** (“I was / became”); **Ez mam** (“I remained”).
- Present perfect: **Ez bûme** is given in Kurmanji grammar sources as a distinct form; its interpretation is context-sensitive (“I have been/become”). Do not collapse it into **bûm**.
- Pluperfect: **Ez bûbûm** is documented as a compound past-in-the-past form in Kurmanji teaching materials; it is not the simple past **bûm**.
- For **man**, the paradigm's **mayî** is the second-person singular simple-past form in the cited table. Do not infer a full perfect paradigm from it without a source that explicitly supplies that tense and its agreement pattern.

## 5. Dialect and standardization cautions
A Hekarî-focused source reports regional forms such as **ez bîm / tu bî / ew bî / em bîn**, alongside the Standard-like **ez bûm / tu bûyî / ew bû / em bûn**, and separately lists perfect and narrative forms. These variants are useful dialect documentation, not replacements for the Standard Kurmancî core paradigm. Do not mix them into a single undifferentiated learner table.
A Zimannas discussion also distinguishes dynamic “become” from stative “be” through predicate type and word order. This is useful for semantic analysis, but it does not change the inflectional table above.

## 6. Recommended evidence status for future lesson review
- **bûn simple-past paradigm:** strong grammatical-reference support; literary tokens for bûm and bûyî located.
- **man simple-past paradigm:** strong grammatical-reference support; literary-token verification remains open.
- **Negatives:** grammatical-reference support; maintain orthographic consistency.
- **Perfect/pluperfect distinction:** supported for bûn; do not extrapolate unsupported perfect forms for man.
