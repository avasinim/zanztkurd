# Şemzînan Natural-Speech Attestation: Contextual Negative Future

Date: 2026-11-17  
Type: documentation-only primary-data attestation.

## Primary source and locality

The University of Manchester project *The Dialects of Kurdish* publishes a recording and aligned transcript for sample K-078, “Şemdinli, Turkey.” The page labels the sample by locality and provides an audio file as well as a multi-turn transcript. The speaker repeatedly refers to Şemzînan and recounts village life and events in 1984. This is natural narrative speech, not an elicited isolated grammar sentence.

Source page: https://kurdish.humanities.manchester.ac.uk/k-078-semdinli-turkey-2/  
Audio linked on the page: http://kurdish.humanities.manchester.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/K-078b_clip.mp3

## Direct negative-future passage

In transcript turn 15, the speaker reports what the electricity workers told the family:

*gotin wiła em ceyranê girê nadeyn em dê çîn sibehê hêyn*

The published English translation is:

“They said we will not connect the electricity, we will leave and come back tomorrow.”

The relevant negative clause is:

*em ceyranê girê nadeyn*  
1PL electricity-OBL connect-NEG.PRS.1PL (surface segmentation is not supplied by the webpage)  
‘We will not connect the electricity.’

The following coordinated clause contains an overt future auxiliary and tomorrow time reference:

*em dê çîn sibehê hêyn*  
1PL FUT go.PRS.1PL tomorrow come.PRS.1PL  
‘We will go and come back tomorrow.’

The whole passage is in a future-oriented reported plan: the first clause is translated with “will not connect,” while the following clause explicitly says “we will leave and come back tomorrow.” Thus this is a locality-labelled natural-speech example of a negative indicative form receiving a future interpretation from context.

## Interpretation and limitations

1. This is direct Şemdinli/Şemzînan narrative evidence, unlike the Behdinî sentence in the earlier comparative source.
2. The negative predicate *nadeyn* is not itself accompanied by a future particle in the transcript. The future interpretation is contextual and supported by the adjacent *dê* clause and “tomorrow” (*sibehê*).
3. Do not rewrite the negative clause as *dê ... ne-* or claim that it contains an overt future auxiliary.
4. The source webpage does not provide a morpheme-by-morpheme gloss for this exact clause. The segmentation above is a cautious descriptive analysis, not a source-supplied gloss.
5. The transcript is a published transcription of a recording; the page gives locality K-078 Şemdinli. Preserve the source's own orthography and do not normalize it into Standard Kurmanji without a separate phonological/editorial layer.
6. This one natural example supports contextual future interpretation of a negative indicative in this speaker's Şemzînan speech. It does not establish every speaker's usage or a complete paradigm.

## Relation to previous evidence

- *na-č-i* ‘Won't you go?’ remains a direct Şemzînan prospective interrogative example from Haig & Öpengin.
- *em ceyranê girê nadeyn* is a separate natural-speech declarative negative clause with future interpretation in context.
- *Ez sibe digel wan na-çi-m* remains the separately labelled Behdinî example and must not be relabelled as Şemzînan.
- Haig (2018), §2.3, provides the broader regional generalization that Şemzînan and northern-Iraq varieties use negated indicative forms rather than Standard Kurmanji future-particle + negated-subjunctive construction.

## Curriculum boundary

This attestation is research documentation only. It does not change existing lessons, the course inventory, dialect policy, or the Academy's standard-language decision. A teaching example should retain the reported-speech context and the explicit tomorrow clause rather than presenting *nadeyn* as an invariant future marker.

## Conclusion

A directly locality-labelled natural-speech declarative passage has now been located. The negative clause *em ceyranê girê nadeyn* is translated by the source with future meaning (“we will not connect the electricity”) in a context that explicitly continues with *em dê çîn sibehê hêyn* (“we will go and come back tomorrow”). This closes the specific evidence gap for at least one contextual negative-future declarative in the published Şemzînan sample, while leaving broader paradigm claims open.
