# Kurmancî Medium B3 — çandin and dirandin: stem, transitivity, and past-tense evidence audit

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Documentation-only lexical and grammatical audit. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, seed, database, migration, PR, or deployment changes.

## Executive findings

| Infinitive | Meaning | Transitivity | Present stem | Past stem | 1sg present | Simple past with 3sg patient |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| çandin | to plant, sow | transitive | çîn- | çand- | ez diçînim | min çand |
| dirandin | to tear, rip | transitive | dirîn- | dirand- | ez didirînim | min dirand |

Both verbs belong to the productive-looking -andin pattern in which the present stem is formed with -în, but this is a descriptive generalization, not a license to derive every verb without checking its lexical entry. The Beginner course explicitly lists çandin among verbs with present stems formed by replacing -andin with -în, while the Kurmanci.app table independently lists the stems çand-/çîn-.

## 1. çandin

### Stems and present forms

The Kurmancî Wiktionary entry marks çandin as transitive and gives present stem çîn-, past stem çand-, and the present forms:
- ez diçînim
- tu diçînî
- ew diçîne
- em/hûn/ew diçînin
- singular imperative biçîne; plural biçînin

The Kurmanci.app stem table independently lists past çand-, present çîn-, and 3sg present diçîne.

### Past-tense evidence

The Wiktionary entry gives the simple-past base çand with the oblique agents min, te, wî/wê, me, we, wan, i.e. the ordinary third-person-singular-patient shape. It also cites Mehmed Uzun, *Bîra qederê* (Nefel, 2002): “Meha adara 1951-an, ji hefta diduyan heta dawiya mehê, me tov çandin.” This is a dated literary prose attestation of the plural form çandin in a transitive clause. The noun tov may be number-neutral/collective in the cited sentence; therefore this token is not used here as the sole proof of object-number agreement.

The same entry cites Melayê Cizîrî (circa 1640) with the imperative biçîn, which is useful historical literary evidence for the present-stem family, but is not a simple-past token.

### Interpretation

Teach the secure contrast:
- present: di- + çîn- + present agreement ending
- simple past: çand- + past agreement, with oblique agent and direct patient

Do not claim a fully documented person-by-person object-agreement paradigm for çandin from the consulted lexical entry alone. The regular Kurmanji transitive-past rule is independently established by Haig & Öpengin and the general past-tense reference cited below, but verb-specific non-3sg patient examples remain unverified in this audit.

## 2. dirandin

### Stems and present forms

The Kurmancî Wiktionary entry marks dirandin as transitive and gives:
- present stem dirîn-
- past stem dirand-
- ez didirînim
- tu didirînî
- ew didirîne
- em/hûn/ew didirînin
- imperatives bidirîne / bidirînin

This verb means “to tear/rip”; the same entry also records a colloquial secondary sense “to beat/hit someone”. Keep the primary lexical sense distinct from the colloquial extension in teaching materials.

### Past-tense evidence

The lexical entry lists the simple-past form dirand with oblique agents min, te, wî/wê, me, we, wan, corresponding to a third-person-singular direct patient. It provides the example: “Bi xeletîve min nama wî dirand” (“I accidentally tore his letter”). This is a direct lexical example of the transitive simple past with oblique agent min and a singular patient.

The entry also gives “Eger tu careka dî hatiye vêrê, dê te dirînim” as a future/threat construction. It must not be misclassified as simple past: dirînim is based on the present stem and occurs under dê.

No independently verified literary simple-past token or verb-specific non-3sg patient agreement form for dirandin was found in this audit. Do not manufacture examples such as *min ew dirandim* unless a reliable source confirms the intended patient and form.

### Interpretation

Teach the secure contrast:
- present: ez didirînim (present stem dirîn-)
- simple past with a singular patient: min name dirand / min nama wî dirand (past stem dirand-)
- future/subjunctive construction: dê te dirînim (not past)

The general ergative rule predicts that a plural or first-/second-person direct patient controls past agreement, but the exact verb-specific paradigm needs further source verification before being included as a memorization table.

## 3. Alignment, negation, and aspect guardrails

For ordinary Standard Kurmancî transitive past clauses, the agent is oblique, the patient is direct, and the past verb agrees with the patient. Examples from general grammars include patterns such as *Min tu dîtî* and *Wê ez dîtim*. This rule applies to these transitive lexical verbs as a grammatical framework, but it does not replace the need to verify each claimed inflected token.

The normal past negator is ne- on the past form (e.g. *min name nedirand* as a transparent structural illustration); this audit does not present that constructed negative as a sourced quotation. For teaching, label it as a regularized illustration pending dialect/editorial review. The simple past must also be kept distinct from past progressive forms built with di- + past stem, as discussed in the academic grammar literature.

## 4. Evidence grading and teaching recommendation

- **High confidence:** both verbs are transitive; their stems and basic present paradigms are explicitly listed in lexical sources.
- **High confidence:** çandin has literary attestations in Melayê Cizîrî and Mehmed Uzun; the Uzun quotation is a simple-past clause.
- **High confidence:** dirandin has a sourced simple-past lexical example with oblique agent and singular patient.
- **Not established here:** complete verb-specific object-agreement tables for either verb; a literary simple-past token for dirandin; dialect-independent variation beyond the sources consulted.
- **Teaching recommendation:** introduce the stem contrast and 3sg-patient simple past first. Add non-3sg patient agreement drills only after checking an attested paradigm or a native-editor-reviewed example set. Keep the colloquial “hit/beat” sense of dirandin out of the core agriculture/verbs vocabulary sequence unless explicitly needed.
