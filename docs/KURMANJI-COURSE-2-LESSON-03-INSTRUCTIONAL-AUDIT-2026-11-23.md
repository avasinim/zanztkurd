## Scope
Reviewed the exact current seed entry `kurmanji-course-2-lesson-03.html`, title **ناو، ڕەگەزی ڕێزمانی و ژمارە**. This is a teaching-clarity audit, not a full source verification of every Kurmanji gender, plural, or ezafe paradigm.

## Current outline (verified)
1. Grammatical gender: says Standard Kurmanji distinguishes masculine/feminine in singular nouns and warns grammatical gender is not identical to natural sex.
2. Singular and plural: says plural formation depends on noun class/form and avoids a single universal rule.
3. Ezafe agreement: says ezafe forms reflect gender/number of the head noun, not merely the following word.
4. Method note: dialect differences should be checked with a specified dialect and source.
5. Review questions: grammatical gender, natural gender, and gender/number in ezafe.

## Strengths
- Avoids claiming grammatical gender is equivalent to biological sex.
- Cautions against generalizing one plural rule to all nouns.
- Correctly focuses ezafe agreement on the head noun as a teaching principle.
- Contains a dialect/source caution.

## Gaps and risks
- **No actual noun pairs:** Learners are not shown a masculine/feminine pair of nouns.
- **No plural examples or paradigms:** The statement that plural forms depend on noun type is accurate as a caution but gives learners no concrete pattern to observe.
- **Ezafe agreement is unillustrated:** There is no sample noun phrase or table demonstrating the forms; the claim is hard to retain without an example.
- **Review questions are conceptual only:** No model answers or applied identification task.
- **Terminology needs consistency:** “ڕەگەزی ڕێزمانی” should be defined once and distinguished from natural gender; use a consistent Kurdish equivalent for grammatical gender throughout.
- **Some wording is circular:** The singular/plural paragraph says number shows the difference between singular and plural but does not define the grammatical signals or teach how to recognize them.

## Conservative recommendation
Do not change lesson content yet. A later revision should add a small source-verified table with (a) one noun pair or gender contrast where appropriate, (b) a plural form with the noun class identified, and (c) two ezafe phrases showing agreement, each with Kurmanji, Kurdish gloss, segmentation where useful, and source. Avoid invented examples or overgeneralizing across dialects.
