# Kurmanji B3 — Preverb Verbs: Present/Past Stems, Inflectional Paradigms, and Dialect Audit

Date: 2026-11-17  
Status: source-critical research note; not approved lesson content  
Scope: lexical and inflectional verification of selected verbs containing preverbs or historically preverb-like material. No course lesson or production artifact is modified.

## 1. Main conclusion

A preverb-bearing infinitive does not reveal the full verbal paradigm. Every lexical record must independently establish:
- infinitive;
- present stem;
- past stem;
- inflectional prefix placement;
- agreement pattern and transitivity in relevant tenses;
- dialect/variety and source;
- attested clause(s), not only an English gloss.

The present/past stem alternations are often suppletive or otherwise non-transparent. A teacher or editor must not generate a present stem by stripping -in/-tin from the infinitive, nor infer the past stem by mechanically removing a preverb.

## 2. Primary source findings

### 2.1 Haig (2018): stem paradigms are independently lexical facts

Haig's Northern Kurdish chapter provides a table of frequent verbs with infinitive, past stem, present stem, and meaning. Examples include:
- hatin ‘come’: past hat-, present -(h)ê- / -wer- (dialectally variable);
- ketin ‘fall’: past ket-, present -kev-;
- rûniştin ‘sit’: past rûnişt-, present -rûn-;
- çûn ‘go’: past çû-, present -ç- / -her-;
- kirin ‘do/make’: past kir-, present -k-;
- dan ‘give’: past da-, present -d-;
- girtin ‘grasp/hold’: past girt-, present -gir-;
- hêlan/hiştin ‘leave’: past hişt-, present -hêl-;
- xistin ‘strike/knock’: past xist-, present -x- / -xîn-.

These are not all preverb verbs. They provide a baseline demonstrating why stem forms need their own lexical fields. Do not mistake the present-stem variants in the table for interchangeable forms in every dialect.

Source: Geoffrey Haig, “Northern Kurdish (Kurmanjî): An Areal Perspective,” The Iranian Languages (2018), §3.4 and tables of frequent stems; author-hosted PDF: https://multicast.aspra.uni-bamberg.de/data/pubs/Haig2018_Northern-Kurdish_Kurmanji.pdf

### 2.2 Haig: preverb morphology is dialect-sensitive

Haig describes Standard Kurmanji preverbs such as hil, řā, and da as combining with verb stems. In the infinitive they are generally written together as one item. In Standard Kurmanji, indicative/imperfective and negative prefixes may intervene between preverb and stem, e.g. ra-di-keve from raketin. In Şemzînan (SEK), inflectional prefixes can precede the preverb, as in di-helē-t, analysed as a contraction of di-hel-hē-t. Similar incorporation is reported in the southernmost Kurmanji varieties around Midyat.

The standard and dialectal forms must be stored separately; do not normalize both into one unlabelled paradigm.

### 2.3 Haig & Öpengin (2018): inflectional prefix competition

The authors describe present-stem forms as taking one of the relevant prefixes: indicative/imperfective di-, subjunctive bi-, or negative na-/ne- according to the construction. The negative marker replaces rather than simply stacks with certain TAM prefixes. Past-stem forms behave differently: simple past forms are generally based on the past stem plus agreement, while past progressive/imperfective uses di-. Past negation uses ne-, including forms such as ne-hatin and ne-di-hatin.

Source: Geoffrey Haig & Ergin Öpengin, “Kurmanji Kurdish in Turkey: structure, varieties, and status” (2018), author-hosted PDF: https://www.uni-bamberg.de/fileadmin/aspra/06_Team/Publications_and_CV/HaigOepengin2018_Kurmanji_Kurdish_.pdf

### 2.4 Gündoğdu (2015): negative/TAM blocking

Gündoğdu's chapter on negation argues that Kurmanji affix ordering is templatic: some affixes occupy the same position and cannot co-occur. Negation markers n(a)-, n(e)-, and ni- preclude certain prefixes such as progressive di- and subjunctive bi-. This is a strong warning against mechanically concatenating prefixes to preverb verbs.

Source: Songül Gündoğdu, “Negation in Kurmanji,” in Affix Ordering Across Languages and Frameworks (2015), pp. 154–172, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780190210434.003.0007

## 4. Why the stem distinction matters

Kurmanji agreement is sensitive to tense and transitivity. Haig's overview explains that finite verbs index a single core argument: the intransitive subject in all tenses, the transitive subject in present tenses, and the transitive object in past tenses. Therefore, a preverb verb's valency and past-stem paradigm cannot be inferred from its present form or from an English gloss.

For example, the ordinary paradigms of hatin and xwarin illustrate different intransitive/transitive past agreement patterns. These are baseline patterns, not templates to impose on every lexicalized preverb verb.

## 5. Required lexical evidence card

Before a B3 preverb verb is approved for a lesson or lexical database, record:

1. **Lemma:** exact infinitive, spelling, and source.
2. **Variety:** Standard Kurmanji, Şemzînan, Midyat, Behdînî, Badînî, or another explicitly named locality/variety.
3. **Present stem:** cited source and at least one finite form.
4. **Past stem:** cited source and at least one finite form.
5. **Prefix placement:** affirmative indicative, subjunctive, present negative, past progressive, and past negative only where attested.
6. **Agreement:** subject/object marking in relevant tenses; do not translate the clause into English and infer the case.
7. **Valency:** attested arguments and their marking.
8. **Meaning:** source-specific gloss plus a full sentence.
9. **Preverb status:** transparent, partly transparent, opaque, or uncertain; distinguish synchrony from historical etymology.
10. **Confidence:** direct attestation, cross-source corroboration, dialect-specific report, or unresolved.

## 6. Editorial rules

- Never derive a present or past stem mechanically from an infinitive.
- Never transfer the bare verb's paradigm to a preverb compound without evidence.
- Keep Standard Kurmanji, Şemzînan, Midyat, Behdînî, and Badînî records distinct.
- Treat ra-di-keve as a source-attested Standard Kurmanji example, not as proof of every prefix combination for all preverb verbs.
- Treat di-helē-t as a dialect-specific SEK form; preserve Haig's analysis and do not silently standardize it.
- Teach prefix competition and blocking; do not stack di-, bi-, na-, ne-, and ni- freely.
- Do not mark unknown stems as regular or fill missing paradigms by analogy.
