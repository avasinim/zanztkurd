# Kurmancî B3 — birin û girtin: transitive simple past, object agreement and perfect contrast (2026-11-17)

## Scope and safety
Documentation-only linguistic audit. No lesson text, course inventory, runtime, CI, migrations, production database, PR #3, or deployment was changed. Course inventory remains 26 + 12. The learner target remains Standard Kurmancî in Hawar-based Latin orthography; regional variants are not silently mixed into the core.

## Executive findings
- **birin** (“to take, carry”) has past stem **bir-** and present stem **-bi-** in Haig & Öpengin (2018). Deniz Ekici's *Kurmanji Kurdish Reader* provides a full simple-past object-agreement table:
  - **Wan ez birim** — They took me.
  - **Me tu birî** — We took you.
  - **We ew bir** — You (plural) took him/her/it.
  - **Te em birin** — You took us.
  - **Wî/wê hûn birin** — He/she took you (plural).
  - **Min ew birin** — I took them.
- **girtin** (“to catch, take, grasp, hold”) has past stem **girt-** and present stem **-gir-** in Haig & Öpengin. Ekici's simple-past paradigm includes **Wî/wê em girtin** (“He/she caught us”), a clear example where a plural direct patient controls plural agreement. The reader separately gives the perfect **Berfînê Ferho girtiye** (“Berfin has caught Ferho”); this must not be confused with simple past **girt**.
- For both verbs, the canonical Standard Kurmancî transitive past uses an oblique agent and direct patient; the verb agrees with the patient. When the patient is a third-person singular noun/pronoun, the verb has no person/number suffix (e.g. **min ew bir**, **min ew girt**).
- The past participle/perfect forms differ from finite simple past: **girtîye** is perfect (“has caught/taken”), while **girt** is simple past with a 3sg patient. Similarly, **bir** is simple past with a 3sg patient; **birî** is object agreement with a 2sg patient, not automatically a participle.
- The audited reader offers grammar paradigms and contemporary news prose, but no independently page-verified literary simple-past token for **birin** or **girtin** was established here. Do not claim literary attestation based on paradigms or perfect-tense examples.

## 1. Birin — stems and simple-past object agreement
**Infinitive:** birin  
**Past stem:** bir-  
**Present stem:** -bi-  
**Meaning:** take, carry

Haig & Öpengin list the two stems as **bir- / -bi-**. The present stem is not to be used in the simple past.

### Direct object controls agreement
Ekici, *Kurmanji Kurdish Reader*, simple-past section, gives the following full object-conjugation series:

| Oblique agent | Direct patient | Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wan | ez | birim | They took me. |
| Me | tu | birî | We took you. |
| We | ew | bir | You (plural) took him/her/it. |
| Te | em | birin | You took us. |
| Wî/wê | hûn | birin | He/she took you (plural). |
| Min | ew | birin | I took them. |

The same section gives the subject-oriented third-person-singular-patient forms:
- **Min bir** — I took (something / him / her / it).
- **Te bir** — You took (something / him / her / it).
- **Wî/wê bir** — He/she took (something / him / her / it).
- **Me bir** — We took (something / him / her / it).
- **We bir** — You (plural) took (something / him / her / it).
- **Wan bir** — They took (something / him / her / it).

The object-conjugation series is the stronger teaching evidence for person/number agreement. In particular, **birim, birî, birin** are controlled by the direct patients **ez, tu, em/hûn/ew**, not by the agents **wan, me, te, wî/wê, min**.

### Lexicalized preverb constructions
Haig & Öpengin note forms such as **jê birin** (“win; literally take from him/her”, also “erase”) as a verb-plus-prepositional-complement lexicalization. This is not simply the literal transitive verb **birin** with an ordinary direct object. Teach the construction as a separate lexical entry and preserve the preverb **jê**.

## 2. Girtin — stems, transitivity and agreement
**Infinitive:** girtin  
**Past stem:** girt-  
**Present stem:** -gir-  
**Meanings in the references:** catch, take, grasp, hold.

Haig & Öpengin give **girt- / -gir-** in their frequent-verb stem table. Ekici's simple-past object-conjugation overview gives:
- **Wî/wê em girtin** — He/she caught us.

Here **wî/wê** is the oblique agent, **em** is the direct plural patient, and **girtin** bears plural agreement with the patient. The same overview contrasts other transitive examples:
- **We ez dîtim** — You (plural) saw me.
- **Wan tu birî** — They took you.
- **Wî/wê em girtin** — He/she caught us.

This is useful evidence that the object-agreement pattern applies across different transitive verbs, not only to **dîtin**.

### A separate lexical example in the reader
Ekici's reading selection 30 includes the expression **rexne li ... neyêt girtin** (“criticism should not be directed at ...”), a passive/subjunctive construction. It is not a simple-past finite example and should not be used to illustrate the simple-past paradigm. The same selection includes **bi meqesê bibiranda**, a conditional/subjunctive form of **birin**, likewise not simple past.

## 3. Simple past vs perfect / participle
For **girtin**, the reader explicitly labels **girt** as the past stem and uses **girtîye** in its present-perfect paradigm:
- **Berfînê Ferho girtiye.** — Berfin has caught Ferho.
- **Min girt** — simple-past form with a third-person singular patient (a paradigm illustration, not a quoted sentence from the reading).

The grammatical difference is essential:
- **girt**: finite simple past; agreement follows the direct patient.
- **girtî**: past participle form based on consonant-final past stem + **-î**.
- **girtiye**: perfect construction; not a simple-past substitute.

For **birin**, the past stem is **bir-**; do not confuse it with the perfect/participle construction **birî** or forms built with auxiliary **bûn**. The reader's simple-past table explicitly uses **birî** as agreement with the second-person singular direct patient (**Me tu birî**), so context and syntax are required before labelling any **-î** form a participle.

## 4. Negation and aspect
The general Standard Kurmanji simple-past negative uses **ne-**:
- **Min ew nebir.** — I did not take him/her/them (interpret the direct pronoun by context; for a singular patient use the corresponding singular referent).
- **Min ew negirt.** — I did not catch/take him/her/it.

These are regular-rule teaching illustrations formed from the attested past stems and general negative rule, not independent literary quotations. The same sources distinguish past progressive constructions with **di-** from simple past; do not teach **dibir/digirt** as the simple-past forms.
