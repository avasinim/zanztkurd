# K-078 Future Reference and Modality: Contextual Annotation of Turns 7, 11, 14–17

Date: 2026-11-17  
Scope: documentation-only annotation of one speaker's locality-labelled Şemdinli/Şemzînan free-speech sample.

## Primary source

Matras, Yaron et al. 2016. *The Dialects of Kurdish*, University of Manchester, free-speech sample K-078, “Şemdinli, Turkey.” The page supplies a recording, transcript and English translation. The relevant evidence is turns 7, 11, and 14–17.

## Contextual inventory

| Turn | Source excerpt | Discourse function / published meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | *ridîna me dê derkewîtin* | Future-oriented imagined outcome: “we will grow a beard.” The speaker recalls children imagining adulthood. |
| 11 | *goti dê gundi derkevin ʕesker dê hête têre da* | Reported evacuation and expected military arrival: “They said you will leave the village and the military will come here.” |
| 11 | *em dê çi keyn, em dê kê ve çîn. Dê şito gundê xû hêlîn* | Future-oriented uncertainty and anticipated departure: “what are we going to do, where are we going to go; how are we going to leave our village behind.” |
| 14 | *gotin ceyran dê bo* | Reported prediction: “they said the electricity will come.” |
| 15 | *em ceyranê girê nadeyn* | Negative clause translated as future: “we will not connect the electricity.” No overt *dê* appears inside this negative clause. |
| 15 | *em dê çîn sibehê hêyn* | Coordinated affirmative future: “we will leave and come back tomorrow.” |
| 16 | *em dê sibe hêyn ceyrana hingo çêkeyn* | Reported promise: “we will come tomorrow and make your electricity.” |
| 17 | *êdî dê-b keywxûşî bîtin* | Future-oriented expectation in the narrator's retrospective account; the source translates the passage as “Just as we were saying that things will now become pleasant.” The transcript's exact segmentation should not be over-interpreted. |

## What the distribution supports

1. The speaker uses *dê* repeatedly for a range of future-oriented discourse functions: imagined future, reported prediction, anticipated action, uncertainty about what will happen, and a reported promise.
2. In turn 15, the negative clause *em ceyranê girê nadeyn* is translated with future reference, but it does not contain an overt *dê* in the transcript.
3. The same turn immediately coordinates that negative clause with *em dê çîn sibehê hêyn*, which does contain *dê* and an explicit ‘tomorrow’ reference.
4. The following turn repeats the tomorrow promise with *em dê sibe hêyn ...*, and turn 17 reports that the electricity was connected by the following day.
5. This is a discourse-level contrast from one speaker, not a controlled grammatical minimal pair. The negative clause's future interpretation is strongly supported by the reported plan and surrounding temporal context, but the source does not provide a morpheme-by-morpheme gloss for *nadeyn*.

## Analytical boundaries

- Do not claim that *na-* is itself a future marker.
- Do not silently insert *dê* into *em ceyranê girê nadeyn*.
- Do not treat every occurrence of *dê* as semantically identical: the discourse contexts include prediction, reported instruction/expectation, imagined outcome and promise.
- Do not infer a full negative-future paradigm from this single speaker's narrative.
- Keep the locality label attached to this sample only; it does not establish the distribution across all Şemzînan speakers.
- Preserve the source's transcription. The Manchester project notes that its free-speech samples are transcribed recordings and makes audio available; its transcription conventions aim at consistency but acknowledge dialectal pronunciation differences.

## Safe research conclusion

K-078 gives a useful within-speaker map of overt *dê* in several future-oriented contexts and one negative clause, *em ceyranê girê nadeyn*, translated with future reference but lacking overt *dê*. Its interpretation depends on the reported plan, the coordinated *dê* clause, the repeated next-day promise and the narrative outcome. This supports a carefully limited contextual account of negative future reference in this sample; it does not prove a dedicated negative-future construction or a general Şemzînan rule.
